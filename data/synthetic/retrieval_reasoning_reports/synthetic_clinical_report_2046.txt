### Explanation of Failure
The PRIORITY study faced considerable challenges, notably recruitment failures that limited enrollment to a mere 60 subjects, a fraction of the target population. This insufficiency undermined trial robustness, impairing the viability of statistical analyses to derive meaningful conclusions regarding rasagiline's efficacy in cognitive impairment related to advanced Parkinson's disease.

Moreover, strict eligibility criteria necessitating participants to present moderate cognitive decline negated opportunities for enrolling a broader patient demographic. This restriction could impede the extrapolation of findings to the general Parkinson's disease population exhibiting cognitive impairment, thus restricting interpretation of rasagiline's generalizability in this therapeutic context.

Finally, early reviews during the study revealed a lack of significant cognitive improvements, hindering optimism surrounding neuroprotective claims attributed to rasagiline based on prior evidence in other neurological domains. Coupled with adverse event reporting suggesting an elevated safety profile concern, the combination of these factors culminated in the decision to terminate the trial prematurely.
