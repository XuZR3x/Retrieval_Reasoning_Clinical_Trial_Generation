### Summary of the Failed Clinical Trial of Hydroxyurea
This study, which sought to explore the efficacy of hydroxyurea in patients suffering from chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), unfortunately met the criteria for termination. The termination stemmed from two primary issues: inadequate efficacy demonstrated by interim assessments as well as significant challenges in recruiting a sufficient number of eligible patients within the target demographic. Participants’ response to therapy was lower than expected, failing to show a meaningful impact on lymphocyte counts or quality of life metrics, reflected in the secondary outcomes. Additionally, the trial faced obstacles in patient accrual, which hindered the completion of critical endpoints necessary to validate the treatment’s effectiveness. Consequently, the lack of diverse population representation diluted the general applicability of the findings, and these compounded factors led to the conclusion of the trial before the completion of enrollment.
