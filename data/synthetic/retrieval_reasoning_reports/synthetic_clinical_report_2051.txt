The treatment regimen involving bendamustine, bortezomib, and pegylated liposomal doxorubicin is designed to leverage synergistic effects to mitigate disease progression. Despite a strong rationale based on preliminary data, this trial faced several challenges that ultimately led to its termination. A primary concern was inadequate patient recruitment, with only 18 of the targeted 60 participants enrolled over a prolonged period due to insufficient referral rates from participating centers. The unfortunate reality was compounded by the complex treatment protocol, which required precise coordination of administering multiple agents, contributing to high rates of early drop-outs attributed to adverse events. Moreover, associated severe myelosuppression was observed, evidenced by a substantial incidence of grade 3 and 4 toxicities which severely limited dose escalation opportunities. This resulted in delayed treatment cycles and reduced data collection on efficacy outcomes. The elderly population's inherent multimorbidity further complicated assessment phases, obstructing clear evaluations of treatment effects against competing health considerations. While initial results indicated potential clinical activity, significant variability in therapeutic responses across subtypes of multiple myeloma dampened the interpretability and robustness of the efficacy data collected."
