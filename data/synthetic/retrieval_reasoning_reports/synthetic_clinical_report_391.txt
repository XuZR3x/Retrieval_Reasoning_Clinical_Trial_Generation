### Detailed Report on a Failed Clinical Trial of Rasagiline:
This clinical study named "Evaluating Rasagiline in Improving Neuropsychiatric Symptoms in Parkinson’s Disease" was intended to explore the efficacy of rasagiline as a treatment option for neuropsychiatric symptoms in patients diagnosed with Parkinson's Disease. The trial design included a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled approach involving an enrollment target of 240 patients to ensure adequate power for significant statistical outcomes.

However, the trial faced multiple pivotal challenges leading to its eventual termination prior to completion. A key factor was insufficient recruitment; only 90 participants were enrolled, drastically below the intended target. This not only diminished the statistical reliability of results but also limited the potential for generalizable findings across the Parkinson's disease patient population.

Further complicating the study were high withdrawal rates due to adverse events. During the trial, significant safety concerns were flagged, with a notable incidence of withdrawal catalyzed by adverse reactions to rasagiline, raising apprehensions regarding its tolerability in the specified cohort. 

Additionally, preliminary data indicated no statistically significant amelioration in primary outcomes, such as neuropsychiatric symptoms measured by the NPI score compared to placebo. This lack of effectiveness undermined expectations for therapeutic advancements with rasagiline, challenging its role as a viable treatment option within this subpopulational spectrum.

Design limitations identified included the restricted duration of 12 weeks; a timeline inadequately capturing long-term efficacy for the management of chronic neuropsychiatric symptoms, which are paramount in the chronic progression of Parkinson’s disease. Moreover, the exclusion criteria disallowed patients with notable comorbid conditions, thereby neglecting a significant segment of the Parkinson’s population potentially exhibiting variability in treatment responses.

In conclusion, the terminated study highlights critical lessons regarding clinical trial design, patient recruitment strategies, and the evaluation of drug efficacy within specialized populations, as these factors remain integral to the successful adaptation of rasagiline in treating neuropsychiatric manifestations within the Parkinson’s disease demographic.
