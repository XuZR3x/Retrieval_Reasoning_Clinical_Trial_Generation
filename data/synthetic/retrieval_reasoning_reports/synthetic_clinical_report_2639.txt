### Rationale for Termination of the Trial:
1. **Insufficient Efficacy**: Analysis indicated that there was no statistically significant difference in disease-free survival rates between the letrozole and placebo arms. The primary endpoint was not achieved, failing to substantiate the expected therapeutic benefit of letrozole as a first-line treatment.

2. **Patient Population Limitations**: The trial was restricted to postmenopausal women with early-stage hormone receptor-positive breast cancer, leading to a narrow eligibility criteria. This resulted in limited generalizability, as outcomes may not reflect the drug's efficacy in broader or diverse populations.

3. **Adverse Event Profile**: Notably, there was an elevated incidence of cardiovascular events among participants receiving letrozole compared to placebo. This raised concerns regarding the risk-benefit ratio, prompting an ethical reevaluation which led to the trial's termination.

4. **Developing Resistance**: A notable percentage of participants experienced disease progression despite ongoing treatment, potentially attributable to the emergence of resistance in hormone receptor-positive tumors. This phenomenon was particularly concerning regarding long-term treatment outcomes.

5. **Secondary Endpoints Unmet**: Failure to meet secondary endpoints, such as overall survival and response rates, further compounded the concerns surrounding the drug's clinical utility in the specified cohort, leading to the conclusion that continued study of letrozole in this context was unwarranted.
