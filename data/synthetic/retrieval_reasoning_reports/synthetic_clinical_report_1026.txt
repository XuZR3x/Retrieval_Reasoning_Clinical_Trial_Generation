      "textblock": "This clinical trial aimed to assess the potential anti-thrombotic effects of clopidogrel in patients undergoing the implantation of cardiac devices such as implantable cardioverter defibrillators (ICDs). Given the known risk of thromboembolic events during cardiac procedures, we sought to investigate if the addition of clopidogrel to standard aspirin therapy could significantly reduce the incidence of complications related to device thrombosis."

    "detailed_description": {
      "textblock": "The hypothesis driving this study was that clopidogrel, a P2Y12 inhibitor, would provide additional anti-thrombotic benefits when administered in conjunction with aspirin post-cardiac device implantation. Following prior studies indicating a possible increase in thromboembolic events following such procedures, particularly in patients with underlying conditions that predispose to coagulation, we aimed to recruit a representative cohort from various centers.\r\n\r\nDespite robust preclinical data supporting clot prevention, we encountered substantial barriers to effective recruitment. The stringent eligibility criteria necessitated that participants be between 40-80 years old, have no significant bleeding disorders, and exhibit no contraindications to dual antiplatelet therapy. This resulted in a marked reduction in the pool of eligible patients, leading to only 110 out of the anticipated 500 being enrolled across 12 international centers. \r\n\r\nDuring the trial, we observed an alarmingly elevated incidence of bleeding complications within the clopidogrel group, ranging from gastrointestinal hemorrhages to procedural site bleedings. These adverse outcomes further intensified concerns regarding the safety profile of the drug in the demographic that predominantly comprises older adult patients, many with significant comorbidities such as hypertension and diabetes.\r\n\r\nAdditionally, due to a relatively short follow-up period of only six months, we could not ascertain long-term efficacy and safety outcomes associated with clopidogrel therapy, thereby failing to demonstrate the drug's role in reducing thromboembolic events in an adequately powered cohort."
