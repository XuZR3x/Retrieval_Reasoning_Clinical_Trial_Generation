### Detailed Rationale for Failure:

1. **Inadequate Enrollment**: Despite projections for participant recruitment, the study only enrolled three patients over three years. This abject under-enrollment halved potential statistical power, undermining the ability to derive valid conclusions about the treatment's efficacy.

2. **Restrictive Eligibility Requirements**: The stringent requirement for high ISG15 expression limited the candidate pool, restricting eligibility to a niche subset of patients with NSCLC, thus compounding recruitment difficulties and further dimming hopes for hypothesis validation.

3. **Lack of Preliminary Efficacy Data**: The absence of earlier comprehensive safety and efficacy results for this patient subgroup deterred potential participants. Patients often preferred well-documented standard therapies over experimental trials with uncertain clinical benefits.

4. **Competing Therapeutic Alternatives**: The advent and availability of novel agents in the oncological armamentarium rendered irinotecan less appealing to patients. With a host of emerging therapeutic options that promise better outcomes, the study's positioning appeared less favorable to prospective participants.

5. **Study Design Limitations**: As a non-randomized study, the design may have provoked skepticism among potential enrollees, who could perceive enhanced bias in treatment assignment and outcomes measurement compared to a randomized controlled trial structure, diminishing participant interest.
