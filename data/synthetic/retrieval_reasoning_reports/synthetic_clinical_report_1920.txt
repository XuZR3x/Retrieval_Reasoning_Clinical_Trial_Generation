### Rationale for Failure
**Insufficient Biological Activity**: The trial demonstrated that gefitinib, while hypothesized to inhibit tumor growth, failed to show significant biological activity within the highly resistant population of metastatic TNBC. The overall objective response rate fell short of clinical significance, undermining the drug's potential as a viable treatment option for this demographic.

**Tumor Resistance Mechanisms**: The presence of inherent resistance mechanisms against EGFR inhibitors such as gefitinib likely contributed to the treatment failure observed. Variants in signaling pathways and compensatory mechanisms in tumor biology, including activation of alternative oncogenes, limited gefitinib's efficacy in reducing tumor burden.

**Limited Patient Population and Heterogeneity**: The diverse genetic and molecular makeup of TNBC presented challenges in achieving a uniform therapeutic outcome. Dozens of unique tumor characteristics posed complications in response predictability and clinical monitoring, diluting the effectiveness of gefitinib as a single therapy in this heterogeneous cohort.

**Toxicity Profile and Adverse Events**: The adverse event profile associated with gefitinib was substantial enough to affect patient retention and compliance. The incidence of moderate to severe side effects prompted some participants to discontinue therapy, severely impacting the study's data integrity and overall efficacy outcomes.

**Clinical Trial Design Limitations**: The single-group assignment design did not allow for control comparisons. The lack of randomization potentially introduced selection bias, failing to adequately encompass the full variance in treatment response among eligible participants. This fundamentally limited the ability to ascertain the actual therapeutic efficacy of gefitinib on patient health outcomes when considered alongside existing treatment modalities.
