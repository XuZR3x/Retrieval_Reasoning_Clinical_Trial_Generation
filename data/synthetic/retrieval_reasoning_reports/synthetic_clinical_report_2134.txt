    "textblock": "This clinical trial aimed to assess the efficacy of montelukast as a treatment for patients with moderate to severe asthma, comparing its effects to a placebo over a 12-week period. Despite the existing literature supporting montelukast's use in asthma management, the trial faced significant enrollment challenges due to many potential subjects being previously treated with montelukast, which could have influenced study results."
  },
  "detailed_description": {
    "textblock": "Asthma exhibited considerable heterogeneity, with diverse pathophysiological subsets including Th2 and non-Th2 phenotypes. This variability can influence patient responses to treatment and complicate the assessment of drug efficacy in clinical trials. In this study, patients were randomized to receive either montelukast (10 mg daily) or placebo, with primary and secondary outcome measures focusing on spirometric indices and diary-recorded asthma symptom scores. The anticipated outcomes included changes in forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) and the frequency and severity of asthma symptoms over the treatment period.\n\nHowever, despite the rigorous study design and ethical approval, the enrollment was significantly lower than required. The recruitment efforts were hampered by the fact that many patients had previously used montelukast or other leukotriene receptor antagonists, leading to a risk of selection bias and treatment history affecting the treatment's efficacy perceptions. Further complicating the study, the heterogeneity of asthma phenotypes among the enrolled subjects diluted the outcomes, as individual responses varied based on the underlying immunological mechanisms involved in their disease presentations."
