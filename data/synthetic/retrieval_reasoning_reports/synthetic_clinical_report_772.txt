The investigational hypothesis was that patients receiving pegfilgrastim prophylaxis alongside chemotherapy would report a lower incidence of severe neutropenia compared to those receiving standard care.\n\nOBJECTIVES:\n  - To assess the reduction in the rate of severe neutropenia (ANC < 500 cells/mm³) in patients receiving pegfilgrastim during chemotherapy.\n  - To compare the frequency of febrile neutropenia episodes and the subsequent healthcare utilization.\n  - To evaluate the quality of life measures through validated scales (EORTC QLQ-C30).\n\nPROCEDURES:\nPatients were stratified based on their risk profiles as moderate to high risk for developing febrile neutropenia. Despite initial intentions, patient stratification was inadequate, leading to the inadvertent inclusion of a significant number of low-risk patients, thereby diminishing observed treatment effects. Variability in the dosing schedule resulted in a lack of standardization, leading to compromised outcomes and interpretations. Despite initial observations of reduced occurrences of neutropenia, the absence of a measurable improvement in overall survival rates posed critical questions about the clinical significance of the findings.\n\nSAFETY ANALYSIS:\nIncreased rates of adverse events, mostly hematologic in nature, emerged as a primary concern for patients receiving pegfilgrastim. Reports indicated exacerbated immune-related side effects and an elevated perception of treatment risk among the patient population. These unexpected outcomes, coupled with the existing economic constraints associated with pegfilgrastim, further influenced the reluctance of clinicians to enroll patients or embrace the treatment.\n\nECONOMIC CONSIDERATIONS:\nWith healthcare costs under scrutiny, the marginal benefits provided by pegfilgrastim in the context of febrile neutropenia prevention were significantly undermined. It became evident that the economic viability of incorporating pegfilgrastim as a standard prophylactic measure was questionable given its cost relative to the minimal clinical efficacy demonstrated.\n\nPROJECTED ACCRUAL:\nThe trial initially projected to enroll 400 patients. However, due to these cumulative factors, the actual enrollment reached only 245 patients before being terminated."
