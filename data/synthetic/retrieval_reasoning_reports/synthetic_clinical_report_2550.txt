### Summary of Failure:
This clinical trial was initiated with the goal of determining the efficacy of tiotropium as an adjunct therapy for asthma patients with existing comorbid conditions. The trial faced several obstacles that contributed to its failure to yield meaningful results.

1. **Insufficient Sample Size**: The enrollment of 75 participants may have limited the statistical power necessary to capture significant treatment effects on primary and secondary outcomes.

2. **Inadequate Definition of Primary Outcomes**: The primary outcome measure, change in FEV1, was assessed only at the end of the study, not accounting longitudinal fluctuations that could have obscured the understanding of tiotropium's potential benefits across its treatment period.

3. **Stringent Exclusion Criteria**: The rigorous exclusion criteria, particularly regarding comorbid conditions, may have resulted in a sample that was not fully representative of the broader asthma patient population, thus reducing the generalizability of findings.

4. **Study Design Limitations**: The parallel group design without sufficient accounting for potential confounding factors may have introduced biases, limiting the reliability of the data collected.

5. **Lack of Patient Engagement**: The modest number of participants suggests potential issues with participant recruiting strategies or engagement methodologies, ultimately limiting the trial's progress and results.
