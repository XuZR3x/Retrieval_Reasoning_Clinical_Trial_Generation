DPP-IV inhibitors, including sitagliptin, are thought to provide metabolic benefits by enhancing incretin hormone levels and thereby promoting glucose-dependent insulin secretion. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of sitagliptin on glycemic control within a demographic that presents an increased challenge to diabetes management due to renal impairment.\n\nThe design of this interventional study involves a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial with an estimated enrollment of 160 patients diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes and Stage 3 CKD over a 12-month period. Participants will be assigned to either the experimental group receiving sitagliptin 100 mg daily or the control group receiving a matching placebo, in parallel fashion. Primary efficacy outcomes will encompass changes in HbA1c levels as evaluated at baseline and every three months thereafter. Secondary outcomes will include the incidence of hypoglycemia, changes in renal function parameters, and quality of life assessments quantified by validated questionnaires.\n\nDespite the rationale underpinning the study, the anticipated therapeutic efficacy of sitagliptin may be compromised by complexities inherent to diabetic management in patients with concurrent CKD, such as altered pharmacokinetics.
