Efalizumab, an anti-CD11a monoclonal antibody, acts by modulating lymphocyte adhesion and migration to ameliorate the severity of psoriasis.\n\nThe trial will enroll 60 participants with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis, aiming to evaluate the synergy between efalizumab and NB-UVB therapy. The study comprises an initial 12-week phase where efalizumab is administered weekly alongside NB-UVB phototherapy three times a week. This is followed by a subsequent 12-week observation period of efalizumab monotherapy to determine sustained efficacy.\n\nRigorous screening and inclusion criteria ensure that participants have not received previous systemic immunotherapy, thereby establishing a baseline for treatment response. Efficacy will primarily be measured via the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) and supported by assessments of patient-reported outcomes, including the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI).\n\nThis study hypothesis posits that the sequential treatment strategy not only enhances lesion clearance but also results in improved quality of life for participants through comprehensive, well-structured assessments over the treatment period."
