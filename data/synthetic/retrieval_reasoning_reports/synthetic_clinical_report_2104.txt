Previous investigations have suggested that this combination may enhance overall response rates in elderly patients diagnosed with AML, a demographic often underrepresented in clinical trials. This study's design emphasizes patient selection to include only those aged 70 years and older who meet the specific eligibility criteria, thereby maximizing the likelihood of therapeutic response and minimizing unnecessary exposure to potentially toxic treatments.\n\nThe study employs a multi-step adaptive randomization model, allowing treatment assignment to be adjusted based on early observed outcomes, thus optimizing dosing regimens dynamically throughout the trial. Participants will receive a treatment regimen comprising clofarabine (20 mg/m² IV daily), fludarabine (30 mg/m² IV daily), and low-dose cytarabine (20 mg/m² subcutaneously daily) for 5 consecutive days, every 28 days for a maximum of 6 cycles. \n\nRigorous monitoring protocols are established to track both efficacy and safety, using frequent assessment of hematologic parameters, and disease progression via bone marrow biopsy and imaging techniques at defined intervals. This ensures timely identification of treatment responses and adverse effects, warranting prompt modifications to the treatment regimen if necessary. \n\nEnrolment is projected to include 60 patients across multiple centers, with a dedicated focus on continuous follow-up to assess long-term outcomes and potential relapse rates as part of the study's design."
