**Failed Clinical Trial of Liposomal Doxorubicin: Efficacy of Liposomal Doxorubicin in Treating Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) with Brain Metastasis**

**A Phase II Study of Liposomal Doxorubicin in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer with Brain Metastases**

**Primary Objective:** 
To evaluate the overall survival (OS) of patients with advanced NSCLC and brain metastases treated with liposomal doxorubicin in combination with pembrolizumab.

**Secondary Objectives:**
1. To assess the objective response rate (ORR) to treatment.
2. To determine progression-free survival (PFS) in this patient population.
3. To evaluate the toxicity profile of the treatment regimen.

This open-label, multicenter phase II trial aimed to investigate the efficacy of liposomal doxorubicin, administered in combination with pembrolizumab, in patients diagnosed with advanced NSCLC exhibiting brain metastases. The inherent complexities and biological heterogeneity of NSCLC, particularly with regards to tumor microenvironment and genetic mutations, presented challenges that may have contributed to the suboptimal therapeutic outcomes observed.

**Study Design:** 
Approximately 50 patients were planned for enrollment, with a target of 40 evaluable subjects. Patients received liposomal doxorubicin at a dose of 30 mg/m² intravenously (IV) over 1 hour on day 1, in conjunction with pembrolizumab at 200 mg IV every 3 weeks. Treatment would continue until disease progression, intolerable toxicity, or patient withdrawal from the study.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically diagnosed advanced non-small cell lung cancer with confirmed brain metastases
- At least one measurable brain lesion ≥ 10 mm in diameter via MRI
- ECOG performance status of 0-2
- Life expectancy of at least 3 months
- Adequate hematologic and organ function
- Negative pregnancy test/appropriate contraception for women of childbearing potential

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with any anthracycline-containing regimen or other investigational agents within 4 weeks prior to study enrollment
- Prior brain-directed therapy (radiation or surgery) within 28 days before the trial initiation
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of liposomal doxorubicin or pembrolizumab
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment or uncontrolled concurrent illness
- History of other malignancies within the past 5 years, excluding non-melanoma skin cancer

**Outcome Measures:**
Primary outcomes included the proportion of patients achieving survival at 12 months post-treatment initiation. Secondary outcomes focused on the assessment of treatment response rates and evaluations of adverse events attributable to the therapy.

**Results:**
The trial faced significant hurdles leading to its termination due to a combination of factors including ineffective drug penetration within brain tissues, as the liposomal formulation failed to demonstrate enhanced distribution across the blood-brain barrier, limiting therapeutic efficacy against CNS metastases. Furthermore, safety concerns emerged regarding cumulative toxicities, particularly an elevated incidence of neurotoxic side effects, which were exacerbated by concurrent treatment with pembrolizumab.

Additionally, the trial's design suffered from an inadequate sample size, lacking sufficient statistical power to yield conclusive results regarding the primary endpoints. Despite satisfactory enrollment of intended participants, the achieved evaluable cohort did not fulfil the anticipated therapeutic response, leading to an overall failure to meet the study's primary and secondary objectives.

**Conclusion:**
This clinical trial elucidated critical challenges in the therapeutic application of liposomal doxorubicin for advanced NSCLC with brain metastases. The failure to achieve the intended efficacy and safety outcomes underscores the necessity for further exploration into modified treatment regimens or adjuvant therapies that may enhance drug delivery to CNS tumors and mitigates adverse effects. Continued research is warranted to understand the underlying mechanisms limiting the effectiveness of encapsulated formulations of doxorubicin in this complex disease environment.
