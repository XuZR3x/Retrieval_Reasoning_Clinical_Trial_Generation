**Successful Clinical Trial of Bendamustine Hydrochloride: A Phase II Study of Bendamustine Hydrochloride in Patients with Refractory Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL)**

**Status:** Completed

**Background:**  
Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is a malignancy characterized by the accumulation of functionally incompetent lymphocytes. The standard treatment options include fludarabine, ibrutinib, and rituximab; however, a significant subset of patients exhibit refractory disease. Bendamustine hydrochloride, exhibiting a unique structural profile and mechanism of action, has demonstrated potential efficacy against lymphoid malignancies, including CLL. The purpose of this study is to evaluate the safety, pharmacokinetics, and antitumor efficacy of bendamustine hydrochloride in patients with refractory CLL.

**Methods:**  
A multicenter, open-label Phase II clinical trial was conducted to assess the efficacy and safety of bendamustine hydrochloride administered intravenously at a dose of 100 mg/m² over two consecutive days every 28 days for a maximum of 6 cycles. The patient population consisted of adults aged 18-75 years with histologically confirmed CLL who had failed at least one prior line of therapy. Inclusion and exclusion criteria were designed to minimize confounding factors and ensure an adequate evaluation of bendamustine’s therapeutical benefits.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients with histologically confirmed refractory CLL.  
- Age between 18 and 75 years.  
- Performance Status (PS) of 0-2 according to the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) criteria.  
- Adequate organ function, including a creatinine clearance ≥ 50 ml/min and total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal.  
- Provided written informed consent.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Presence of active infections or serious comorbidities (e.g., hepatic, renal, or cardiac conditions).  
- Prior treatment with alkylating agents or previous history of hypersensitivity to bendamustine.  
- Concurrent participation in other investigational drug protocols.   
- Known positive status for HIV or hepatitis.  

**Endpoints:**  
The primary endpoint was the overall response rate (ORR), measured by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) criteria, defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) after treatment. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), safety evaluations, and pharmacokinetic profiling of bendamustine hydrochloride.

**Results:**  
A total of 120 patients were enrolled in the trial, with 110 fulfilling the analysis criteria. The study observed a statistically significant ORR of 68% (75/110 patients), with 25% achieving a CR. The safety profile was consistent with prior studies, with manageable side effects, primarily hematologic toxicity (neutropenia and thrombocytopenia), addressed through appropriate dose adjustments and supportive care measures. Long-term follow up demonstrated a median PFS of 18 months.

**Discussion:**  
The favorable outcome observed in this trial supports the hypothesis that bendamustine hydrochloride’s distinct mechanism of action, coupled with its synergistic effects when administered as part of combination therapies, enhances antitumor activity in patients with refractory CLL. The careful patient selection criteria, as well as the diligent monitoring of safety and response rates, underscore the trial's design efficacy. 

**Conclusion:**  
Bendamustine hydrochloride has demonstrated considerable activity in patients with refractory CLL, offering a new therapeutic alternative for this challenging patient population. Further investigation into its role in newly diagnosed CLL or in combination with novel agents is warranted.
