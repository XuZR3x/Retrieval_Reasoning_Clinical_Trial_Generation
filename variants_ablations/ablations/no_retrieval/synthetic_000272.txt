**Successful clinical trial of laboratory biomarker analysis: Assessing Biomarkers in Patients with Advanced Colorectal Cancer Undergoing Targeted Therapy**  
**Targeted Approaches Through Biomarkers in Colorectal Cancer (TABC) Trial**  

This phase II clinical trial investigates the efficacy of laboratory biomarker analysis in optimizing targeted therapies for patients with advanced colorectal cancer. The intervention aims to identify a panel of predictive biomarkers that correlate with clinical responses to combined therapies using agents such as cetuximab and regorafenib. The presence and expression levels of these biomarkers will serve as indicators for treatment efficacy and patient outcomes.  

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To evaluate the comprehensive response of advanced colorectal cancer patients to combined therapy, focusing on the clinical objective response rate associated with specific predictive biomarkers over a 12-week treatment period.  

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To assess the histological response to targeted therapies through tissue sampling and the quantification of tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) levels and associated immune checkpoint expressions (e.g., PD-1, PD-L1).  
II. To analyze alterations in serum-based biomarkers correlating with therapy responses, including circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), and levels of interleukin-6 (IL-6) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF).  
III. To establish a relationship between somatic mutation profiles in tumor tissue and changes in clinical endpoints, including progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).  

**OUTLINE:**  
Patients will receive cetuximab intravenously on days 1 and 15 in combination with oral regorafenib daily for 21 out of 28 days, followed by a 7-day rest period. Initial tissue and blood samples will be collected at baseline and subsequent samples at weeks 6 and 12 during the treatment phases. Clinical evaluations will occur every 4 weeks post-therapy administration.  

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Patients with histologically confirmed metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) with evidence of progression following standard therapy.  
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST criteria with accessible lesions for biopsy.  
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.  
- Sufficient laboratory parameters, including absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1500/microL and platelets ≥ 100,000/microL.  
- Patients must provide written informed consent and have the ability to comply with study requirements.  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Patients with known hypersensitivity to cetuximab or regorafenib or any of the components of the study drugs.  
- Prior treatment with anti-EGFR therapies or regorafenib within the last 3 months.  
- Presence of uncontrolled concurrent illnesses or other malignancies within the past 3 years, aside from non-melanoma skin cancer.  
- Patients with brain metastases or significant cardiovascular, renal, or hepatic dysfunction prohibiting safe participation in this trial.  

The clinical response will be quantitatively measured according to the modified RECIST criteria, with defined parameters for complete response (CR) and partial response (PR). The primary endpoint will focus on the percentage of participants achieving either CR or PR at the end of the 12-week treatment period.  

**RESULTS:**  
- Regular and systematic sampling of tumor and serum biomarkers pre- and post-treatment facilitated the identification of signature mutations that correlate with treatment response and clinical endpoints.  
- A comprehensive analysis of tissue-based and blood-based biomarkers enabled a robust cross-validation of therapeutic outcomes, thereby demonstrating the efficacy and predictive capacity of the identified biomarkers.  
- Biomarker expression analysis revealed a correlation between elevated TILs and improved clinical outcomes, affirming the immune modulation potential of the combined therapy.  

**SUMMARY:**  
The TABC trial exemplifies the successful application of laboratory biomarker analysis in tailoring targeted therapies for patients with advanced colorectal cancer. By implementing stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria and utilizing a multifaceted biomarker approach, this study minimizes confounding variables and enhances clinical validity, resulting in improved patient outcomes.
