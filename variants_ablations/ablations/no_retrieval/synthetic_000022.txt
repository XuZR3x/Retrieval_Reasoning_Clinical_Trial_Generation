**Title: A Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Sativex® in the Management of Pain and Spasticity in Patients with Multiple Sclerosis:**  
*A Double-Blind, Randomized, Active-Controlled Study of Sativex® in Patients with Concurrent Pain and Spasticity Associated with Multiple Sclerosis*

**Abstract:**  
This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of Sativex® compared to an active control in relieving pain and spasticity in patients diagnosed with multiple sclerosis (MS). Employing a robust randomized controlled design with thorough patient selection criteria, this study anticipates being a pivotal assessment of Sativex's potential role in concurrently managing symptomatology associated with MS.

**Study Design:**  
A 12-week, double-blind, randomized, active-controlled, parallel group study will be conducted across multiple centers. Eligible patients will undergo a comprehensive screening process to ascertain the persistence and severity of both pain and spasticity, ensuring a well-defined cohort most likely to benefit from Sativex therapy. 

**Objectives:**  
The primary objective is to assess the reduction in pain and spasticity scores measured on validated scales (0-10 numerical rating scale for pain and the Ashworth Scale for spasticity). Secondary objectives include evaluating the long-term maintenance of efficacy and safety through daily diaries and routine safety evaluations.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients aged 18 years and above, diagnosed with MS.  
- Concurrent presence of moderate to severe pain (NRS score ≥ 4) and spasticity (Ashworth Scale score ≥ 2).  
- Patients must demonstrate a stable medication regimen for neurological management for at least 3 months prior to study entry.  
- Willingness to comply with all study requirements and provide informed consent. 

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Use of cannabis or cannabinoid-based medications within 3 months prior to screening, or unwillingness to abstain during the study.  
- Any condition that could confound pain assessment, such as orthopedic or severe psychiatric disorders.  
- Patients with significant cardiovascular or hepatic impairment or who are pregnant. 

**Study Protocol:**  
Upon screening and achieving informed consent, enrolled participants will enter a 14-day baseline period to establish their symptomatic status. Following this, patients will be randomly assigned to receive either Sativex® or an active comparator (standard analgesic therapy). Efficacy assessments will be conducted weekly (through validated scales) and adverse events will be documented continuously, alongside routine safety blood tests. 

**Outcome Measures:**  
The primary endpoint will be defined as the change from baseline in the mean pain and spasticity scores at the 12-week endpoint. Secondary endpoints will include the frequency and severity of adverse events, patient-reported outcomes, and overall quality of life assessments using the MS quality of life scale.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Statistical significance will be established using appropriate parametric or non-parametric tests, determined by data normality. An intention-to-treat analysis will ensure that all randomized participants are included in the primary efficacy assessments.

**Conclusion:**  
This trial is designed leveraging the evidenced efficacy of Sativex® from previous studies while establishing a well-controlled environment to monitor both safety and clinical effectiveness in managing complex symptomatology of multiple sclerosis. By focusing on a dual approach of addressing spasticity and concurrent pain, this study aims to provide essential insights into the potential benefits of Sativex within chronic neurological conditions. 

**Safety Monitoring:**  
An independent Safety Review Board will oversee the safety profiles in real-time, ensuring that all adverse events are documented and reviewed regularly for the duration of the study.

This clinical trial not only aims to reiterate Sativex's previously established efficacy in symptom management but also seeks to expose its multifaceted benefits in a concurrent pain and spasticity context, thereby addressing a significant unmet medical need in the MS patient population.
