**Study Title:** An Open-Label Study to Evaluate the Safety and Efficacy of Adalimumab in Patients with Moderate to Severe Hidradenitis Suppurativa: A Multicenter Approach

**Study Design:** This multicenter, open-label trial aims to assess the safety, tolerability, and clinical efficacy of adalimumab in a cohort of patients diagnosed with moderate to severe hidradenitis suppurativa (HS). The study seeks to confirm hypotheses regarding the therapeutic impact of adalimumab on disease activity and patient quality of life dimensions, utilizing both validated clinical measures and established patient-reported outcomes.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Males and females aged 18 years and older.
2. A confirmed diagnosis of moderate to severe hidradenitis suppurativa for a minimum duration of 6 months prior to screening, as confirmed through clinical examination.
3. Active HS characterized by at least 3 painful nodules, abscesses, or tunnels, per validated HS clinical scoring systems (e.g., Hidradenitis Suppurativa Clinical Severity Score - HiSCR).
4. Must have had inadequate response to at least two conventional systemic therapies or must have demonstrated intolerance to prior systemic treatments.
5. Capability and willingness to provide written informed consent and to adhere to the study protocol requirements.
6. Adequate metabolic function determined by baseline laboratory evaluations, ensuring comprehensive medical safety prior to treatment initiation.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of malignancy within the past 5 years, with the exception of adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancers.
2. Current or past significant infections, including but not limited to tuberculosis or opportunistic infections.
3. Past history of severe allergic reactions to monoclonal antibody therapies.
4. Individuals with active autoimmune conditions that may confound response to treatment.
5. Subjects who have received systemic biologic therapy within 8 weeks of enrollment.
6. History of significant cardiovascular events (e.g., myocardial infarction) within the last 6 months.
7. Women who are pregnant or lactating, or who are not utilizing effective contraceptive methods during the study duration.

**Objectives:**
- To determine the proportion of patients achieving clinical response defined by a ≥50% reduction in the number of draining sinuses or abscesses at Week 16.
- To evaluate the impact of treatment on patient-reported outcomes regarding disease-related quality of life, using the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) as a primary endpoint.
- To monitor the safety profile of adalimumab throughout the study, particularly the incidence of adverse events leading to discontinuation.

**Treatment Protocol:**
- Participants will receive adalimumab at a dosage of 40 mg subcutaneously every other week, commencing with an initial dose of 80 mg at Week 0.
- Regular follow-up will occur at Weeks 4, 8, 12, and 16, where clinical data will be systematically collected to assess disease activity and monitor the safety profile.
- Upon reaching Week 16, a follow-up visit will occur at Week 24 to evaluate long-term efficacy concerning remission and sustained improvement in the quality of life.

**Endpoints:**
- Primary endpoint: Proportion of patients achieving clinical response by Week 16, measured through reduction in HS activity scores.
- Secondary endpoint: Change from baseline in DLQI scores at each visit, alongside comprehensive clinical assessments and safety profile evaluation.

**Statistical Analysis:**
An intention-to-treat analysis will be employed to assess the primary effectiveness endpoint. Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline characteristics, while appropriate statistical tests (e.g., Chi-square tests) will compare categorical data. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) will be utilized to evaluate changes in continuous measures.

**Conclusion:**
Given the delineated structured approach to trial design through robust inclusion criteria, exclusion of confounding variables, adherence to patient management protocols, and systematic monitoring of adverse events, this study aims to conclusively demonstrate the efficacy and safety of adalimumab in managing moderate to severe hidradenitis suppurativa, potentially reinforcing its clinical utility in dermatological practice.
