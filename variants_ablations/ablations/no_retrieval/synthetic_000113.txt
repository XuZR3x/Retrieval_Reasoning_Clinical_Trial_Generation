**Failed clinical trial of infliximab: Evaluation of Infliximab Efficacy in Patients with Newly Diagnosed Moderate to Severe Ulcerative Colitis (Study IC-UC001) (TERMINATED)**

*Evaluation of Infliximab Efficacy in Patients with Newly Diagnosed Moderate to Severe Ulcerative Colitis: A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind Study*

This Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind clinical trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of infliximab in inducing clinical remission in patients with newly diagnosed moderate to severe ulcerative colitis (UC). Participants aged 18–75 years with a confirmed diagnosis of moderate to severe UC and a baseline Mayo score ≥6 were recruited for the study. The primary hypothesis was that treatment with infliximab would result in a significant reduction in the Mayo score at Week 12 compared to placebo.

Patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either infliximab 5 mg/kg at Weeks 0, 2, and 6 or placebo, followed by an additional 12 weeks of observation. Secondary objectives included evaluating the safety profile of infliximab and assessing quality of life measurements using the Inflammatory Bowel Disease Questionnaire (IBDQ) and changes in biochemical markers of inflammation.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18–75 years with a diagnosis of UC according to the standard criteria
- Patients with moderate to severe disease (Mayo score ≥6)
- No previous treatment with corticosteroids or immunosuppressants for UC
- Ability to provide informed consent

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with prior exposure to biologics targeting TNF-alpha
- History of significant comorbid conditions, including uncontrolled infections, malignancies, or chronic renal or hepatic failure
- Concurrent use of antibiotics or enzyme-inducing medications
- Pregnant or nursing women

**Results:**
The study was terminated early due to multiple factors that consistently aligned with reasons for failure in clinical trials assessing infliximab. 

1. *Inconsistent Disease Activity Profiles:* Variability in disease severity among participants at baseline was observed, contributing to disparate responses to infliximab. A significant proportion of patients had a Mayo score below 10 at enrollment, suggesting low disease activity that limited the observed efficacy of infliximab in this population.

2. *Timing and Protocol Variability:* The introduction of a variable treatment schedule was noted by the research team, where some participants inadvertently modified their regimen of concomitant therapies, leading to confounding results associated with the timing of infliximab administration.

3. *Patient Adherence and Retention:* Recruitment efforts were inadequate, resulting in low patient enrollment rates across study sites. Moreover, high dropout rates were noted in the initial treatment phase, primarily owing to the occurrence of adverse reactions and the complexity of maintaining protocol adherence.

4. *Insufficient Duration of Follow-Up:* While the primary endpoint was set for Week 12, patient follow-up was insufficient to ascertain long-term therapeutic responses and adverse effects. Preliminary findings suggested that chronic inflammatory conditions such as UC may require an extended observation period to gauge the complete impact of infliximab.

5. *Subclinical Disease Factors:* Variability in immune response to infliximab was noted, which was inadequately accounted for during participant selection. Patients who were later found to possess antibodies against infliximab were less likely to achieve clinical remission, further complicating outcome measures.

**Conclusions:**
Ultimately, the trial was deemed unsuccessful due to inadequate patient response and low retention rates, alongside complications arising from protocol deviations and variability in prior disease management. Consequentially, the anticipated therapeutic benefits of infliximab in the defined patient cohort were not successfully demonstrated, prompting the study's termination prior to reaching the full enrollment goal. 

Further investigation into optimizing treatment protocols and understanding patient-specific disease profiles is warranted to enhance future clinical outcomes with infliximab in similar patient populations.
