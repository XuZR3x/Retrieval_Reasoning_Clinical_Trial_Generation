**Failed Clinical Trial of Cixutumumab: A Study of Cixutumumab With or Without Atezolizumab in Patients With Unresectable Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

*Title: A Phase II Randomized Trial of Cixutumumab in Combination With Atezolizumab Versus Atezolizumab Alone in Patients With Unresectable Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer*

**Purpose:** This phase II study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of cixutumumab, an anti-IGF-1R monoclonal antibody, when administered in combination with atezolizumab in patients with unresectable advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have previously received standard treatments. Given the mechanism of action of cixutumumab, we hypothesized that the addition of cixutumumab to atezolizumab could enhance tumor response rates and prolong progression-free survival (PFS). However, recent findings suggest that heterogeneous tumor biology and compensatory growth pathways may restrict the efficacy of this combination.

**Primary Objective:**
I. To assess progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months in patients treated with cixutumumab plus atezolizumab compared to those receiving atezolizumab alone.

**Secondary Objectives:**
I. To evaluate overall survival (OS) rates across both treatment arms.
II. To document the objective response rates (ORR) in each treatment group.
III. To ascertain the safety profile and tolerability of cixutumumab in combination with atezolizumab.
IV. To analyze potential biomarkers of response including IGF-1R expression levels and associated signaling pathways through pre-and post-treatment biopsies.

**Outline:**
Patients are randomly assigned to one of two treatment arms.

- **ARM I:** Patients receive atezolizumab intravenously (IV) at 1200 mg on day 1 of each 21-day cycle and cixutumumab IV at a dose of 10 mg/kg on days 1 and 15 of each cycle.
- **ARM II:** Patients receive atezolizumab IV as per the identical schedule, without cixutumumab.

For both arms, treatment continues until progression of disease or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Patients are monitored for adverse events and are followed for response evaluations every 6 weeks for 6 months, then every 3 months thereafter.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed NSCLC, stage IV, and unresectable.
- Patients must have progressed after at least one line of standard systemic therapy.
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior therapy with atezolizumab or other anti-PD-1/PD-L1 agents.
- Clinically significant cardiovascular disease or concurrent uncontrolled illness.
- Poorly controlled diabetes mellitus or uncontrolled hyperglycemia.

**Endpoints:**
- PFS is measured from randomization to documented progression or death without progression.
- ORR is defined as the sum of complete and partial responses according to RECIST criteria.
- Safety assessments will include the incidence of adverse events categorized by severity and relationship to treatment.

**Results:**
The trial ultimately demonstrated a lack of significant improvement in PFS for patients receiving cixutumumab in combination with atezolizumab compared to those treated with atezolizumab alone. Despite the rationale for targeting IGF-1R in this cohort, the presence of compensatory pathways and heterogeneous tumor biology attenuated the expected benefits. Notably, subgroups with high levels of EGFR expression showed no additional benefit, potentially indicating pathway redundancy that undermined the efficacy of cixutumumab.

Furthermore, the regiment experienced significant adverse events leading to dose reductions and omissions in treatment. The lack of synergy between cixutumumab and atezolizumab was evident, supporting the hypothesis that engagement of the IGF-1R may not be sufficient to warrant continued investigation in the context of advanced NSCLC.

**Conclusion:**
The clinical trial failed to meet its primary endpoint of improved PFS in patients with unresectable advanced NSCLC, reinforcing concerns regarding insufficient target engagement and the influence of compensatory growth pathways in tumor biology. Future studies should consider refined patient stratifications and alternative therapeutic approaches to address underlying tumor heterogeneity. Careful evaluation of co-morbidities and molecular profiling of tumors may be necessary to enhance treatment outcomes in this challenging population.
