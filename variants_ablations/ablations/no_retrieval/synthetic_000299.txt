**Failed Clinical Trial of Bicalutamide: A Phase III Study Evaluating Bicalutamide in Combination With Radical Prostatectomy for High-Risk Locally Advanced Prostate Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** The pathophysiology of prostate cancer relies heavily on androgen receptor activation, which contributes to tumor progression. The utility of bicalutamide as a monotherapy has demonstrated inconsistent efficacy within high-risk populations, particularly those with locally advanced prostate cancer. This study aimed to elucidate the potential benefits of incorporating bicalutamide into a radical prostatectomy protocol, given its capacity to diminish circulating androgen levels.

**PURPOSE:** This randomized Phase III trial aimed to compare the rates of recurrence-free survival and overall survival between patients receiving radical prostatectomy alone and patients receiving radical prostatectomy with adjuvant bicalutamide therapy.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objective:** Compare the recurrence-free survival (RFS) rates at 5 years post-treatment between two cohorts: those undergoing radical prostatectomy plus bicalutamide versus those undergoing radical prostatectomy alone.
- **Secondary Objectives:**
  - Assess overall survival (OS) rates at 5 years.
  - Evaluate the impact of bicalutamide on quality of life metrics in high-risk prostate cancer patients post-surgery.
  - Analyze adverse event profiles associated with the inclusion of bicalutamide following radical prostatectomy.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, randomized study enrolled patients with high-risk locally advanced prostate cancer characterized by clinical stage T3 or higher, or a Gleason score > 8, with a pre-operative PSA level greater than 20 ng/mL. Enrolment criteria aimed to select patients who are fit to undergo surgical intervention without any contraindications to hormone therapy.

- **Arm I:** Patients received radical prostatectomy followed by adjuvant therapy with bicalutamide 150 mg daily for 6 months.
- **Arm II:** Patients underwent radical prostatectomy alone with no additional bicalutamide therapy.

Patients were stratified by initial PSA levels, clinical T-stage, and Gleason score. Subsequent follow-up assessments, including serum PSA levels, radiographic evaluations, and quality-of-life questionnaires, occurred post-operatively at 3-month intervals for the first two years and every 6 months thereafter for a total of 5 years.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 1,200 patients were anticipated to be enrolled over a span of 4 years.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically proven prostate cancer with high-risk features as defined by:
  - Clinical stage T3 or T4, or
  - Gleason score greater than 8, or
  - PSA levels greater than 20 ng/mL.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: ≥ 18 years
- Performance status: Zubrod 0 or 1
- Exclusions: Prior androgen deprivation therapy, other malignancies within the last five years, active cardiovascular disease, significant hepatic or renal impairment.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- No prior treatment with cytotoxic chemotherapy, hormones, or biological agents for prostate cancer was allowed.
- Patients must have recovered from any surgical procedures conducted prior to study entry.

**RESULTS:**
Despite initial hypotheses suggesting that the inclusion of bicalutamide would yield better RFS and OS rates due to its androgen-suppressive properties, the trial demonstrated that the addition of bicalutamide did not significantly improve clinical outcomes. The 5-year RFS rates were statistically similar between both treatment arms, and no substantial enhancement in OS was observed. Moreover, the toxicity profile associated with bicalutamide treatment led to significant treatment-related adverse events, including increased instances of gynecomastia and hepatotoxicity, contributing to reduced patient tolerability. 

Furthermore, the evaluation of biomarker levels, including PSA response pre- and post-treatment, indicated that bicalutamide failed to adequately modulate PSA levels in a predictive manner for successful treatment outcomes, thereby underscoring the initial hypothesis of ineffective androgen blockade in advanced disease presentations. 

**CONCLUSION:** The integration of bicalutamide into the radical prostatectomy regimen for high-risk locally advanced prostate cancer did not yield the desired efficacy. This trial illustrates the complexities involved in treating aggressive prostate cancer and serves as a critical reminder of the necessity for ongoing investigations into more effective treatment modalities. Future studies should focus on more targeted and combined approaches to enhance survival outcomes in this patient population.
