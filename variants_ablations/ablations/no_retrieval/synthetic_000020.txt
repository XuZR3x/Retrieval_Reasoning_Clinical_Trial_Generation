**Phase II Clinical Trial of Alisertib in Combination With Pembrolizumab for Patients With Relapsed/Refractory High-Grade Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma**

**Study Summary**: This Phase II study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of alisertib, a selective Aurora A kinase inhibitor, in combination with pembrolizumab, a PD-1 blocking antibody, in patients with relapsed or refractory high-grade non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL). The trial seeks to leverage the complementary mechanisms of action of alisertib and pembrolizumab to enhance anti-tumor efficacy in a patient population that historically has limited therapeutic options. 

**Interventions**: Participants will receive:
- Alisertib 40 mg, administered orally twice daily on days 1-7 of each 21-day cycle.
- Pembrolizumab 200 mg, administered intravenously on day 1 of each 21-day cycle.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Confirmed histological diagnosis of relapsed or refractory high-grade non-Hodgkin lymphoma, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and aggressive T-cell lymphomas.
2. Patients aged 18 years or older.
3. ECOG performance status of 0-2.
4. At least one prior line of therapy, which must include a CD20 targeting agent for B-cell lymphomas.
5. Adequate organ function as defined by laboratory parameters:
   - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1500/μL and platelet count ≥ 75,000/μL.
   - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN and ALT/AST ≤ 2.5 x ULN if not related to lymphoma.
6. Ability to understand the protocol and provide written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Patients who have undergone any other investigational agent within 4 weeks prior to randomization.
2. History of autoimmune disease or active immune-mediated disorders that require systemic treatment.
3. Known hypersensitivity to either alisertib or pembrolizumab.
4. Active central nervous system involvement by lymphoma.
5. Significant comorbidity limiting the ability to follow the protocol or understand consent forms.

**Study Design**: This is a multicenter, open-label, Phase II clinical trial. The primary endpoint is overall response rate (ORR) as defined by the criteria established by the International Workshop on Lymphoma (IWL). Secondary endpoints will include duration of response (DOR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) over a monitoring period of 24 months post-initiation of treatment.

**Efficacy Considerations**: Based on previous successful preclinical findings, combining alisertib's cellular mechanism of inhibiting Aurora A kinase, which plays a role in cell cycle regulation and tumor cell proliferation, with pembrolizumab's immune checkpoint inhibition, is hypothesized to produce a synergistic anti-tumor effect. The rigorous patient selection will focus on those with measurable disease and available treatment history optimizing treatment impact.

**Pharmacokinetics and Monitoring**: Robust pharmacokinetic analysis will be conducted to evaluate drug exposures at different intervals. Efficacy and safety will be monitored rigorously, including regular assessments of adverse events following the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Participants will also undergo molecular profiling to elucidate potential biomarkers of response to alisertib and pembrolizumab combination therapy, thereby enhancing personalized treatment protocols.

**Data Collection**: Data will be collected on treatment response through imaging studies at baseline, every two cycles, and at the end of therapy. Additional assessments will include clinical evaluations and laboratory tests focused on liver and renal function to tailor any necessary treatment modifications.

**Conclusions**: This Phase II trial aims to establish the efficacy of the combination of alisertib and pembrolizumab in overcoming resistant high-grade non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Should this trial demonstrate statistically significant improvement in ORR and PFS, this regimen might offer a novel therapeutic option for patients facing a dire prognosis with current treatment modalities.
