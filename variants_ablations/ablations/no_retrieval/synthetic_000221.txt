**Failed Clinical Trial of Docetaxel: Dual-Agent Chemotherapy with Docetaxel and Carboplatin in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Title:** A Randomized Phase III Study of Docetaxel Combined with Carboplatin Versus Carboplatin Alone in Patients with Stage IIIB or IV Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**RATIONALE:** Docetaxel has been established as a chemotherapeutic agent in the treatment of various malignancies, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). This study hypothesized that the combination of docetaxel with carboplatin would enhance tumor reduction and provide a more effective treatment regimen over carboplatin alone for patients with advanced NSCLC. However, in prior investigations, there were indications that the incremental benefits of docetaxel may be countered by a differential toxicity profile, adverse clinical outcomes, and insufficient biomarker utility in predicting treatment response.

**PURPOSE:** This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a dual-agent chemotherapy regimen utilizing docetaxel and carboplatin compared to carboplatin monotherapy in patients with stage IIIB or IV NSCLC, with a focus on overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS).

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, randomized controlled trial enrolled patients with histologically confirmed stage IIIB or IV NSCLC without prior treatment. Patients were stratified based on performance status (ECOG 0-1 vs. 2) and the presence of comorbidities affecting treatment tolerance.

**Primary Objective:**  
- To determine whether the combination of docetaxel and carboplatin improves overall survival (OS) compared to carboplatin alone in patients with advanced NSCLC.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
- To assess progression-free survival (PFS) between the two treatment arms.  
- To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR).  
- To analyze the safety and tolerability profiles of the treatment combinations.  
- To explore the correlations between gene expression biomarkers (e.g., RRM1, ERCC1) and clinical response to therapy.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
1. Patients aged ≥ 18 years with histologically confirmed stage IIIB or IV NSCLC.  
2. Non-receipt of prior chemotherapy or radiation for metastatic disease.  
3. Adequate organ function, specifically: ANC ≥ 1500 cells/μL, Platelet count ≥ 100,000 cells/μL, Bilirubin ≤ 1.5XULN, AST/ALT ≤ 1.5XULN.  
4. ECOG performance status 0-2.  
5. Patients must provide informed consent.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
1. Prior treatment with docetaxel or carboplatin for any malignancy.  
2. Evidence of central nervous system metastasis or other significant comorbidities limiting treatment tolerance.  
3. History of severe allergic reactions to platinum-based therapy.  
4. Clinically significant hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction.  
5. Pregnancy or lactation.

**TRIAL DESIGN:**  
Patients were randomized to receive either:  
- Arm 1: Docetaxel 75 mg/m² IV plus carboplatin AUC 6 IV q3 weeks.  
- Arm 2: Carboplatin AUC 6 IV q3 weeks as monotherapy.

Treatment continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal. Patients were evaluated for therapeutic response by imaging and serum tumor markers every 8 weeks, and survival data were collected through 2 years.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES:**  
The primary endpoint was overall survival at 12 months. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival, adverse event rates, and the determination of biomarker status to predict response.

**RESULTS:**  
The trial ultimately failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in overall survival rates between the docetaxel/carboplatin arm compared to carboplatin alone. Key reasons for failure included:

1. **Insufficient Efficacy in Advanced Stage Disease:** The expected synergistic effect of combining docetaxel with carboplatin did not yield improved endpoints in OS or PFS, with many patients displaying a similar pattern of disease progression as seen in monotherapy.

2. **Differential Toxicity Profile:** Patients in the dual-agent arm experienced increased toxicity, leading to a higher rate of treatment discontinuation and reduced compliance compared to the carboplatin alone groups, thus confounding efficacy assessments.

3. **Inadequate Biomarker Utility:** The failure to correlate changes in biomarker expression levels (e.g., RRM1 and ERCC1) with clinical outcomes highlighted the inadequately predictive nature of these markers within the studied cohort, suggesting a disconnect between molecular response and treatment efficacy.

4. **Patient Population Heterogeneity:** The diverse genetic backgrounds and baseline tumor characteristics among enrolled patients underscored the challenge in generating homogenous responses to docetaxel, potentially masking its effect.

5. **Interaction With Concurrent Therapies:** Several participants had undergone prior treatments that may have altered the effectiveness of docetaxel and its pharmacodynamics, further complicating the assessment of the combined therapy.

**CONCLUSIONS:** This trial elucidates the complexities surrounding the use of docetaxel in advanced NSCLC, revealing critical insights into its efficacy when combined with other agents and the profound impact of toxicity on treatment outcomes. Further research is needed to elucidate more effective combinations and identify patients who may benefit the most from docetaxel therapy.
