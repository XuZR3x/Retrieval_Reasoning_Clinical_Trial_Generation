**Failed clinical trial of prednisone: Prednisone in Patients with Severe Eosinophilic Esophagitis (EoE)**  
A Phase III Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind Study of Prednisone versus Placebo in Adults with Severe Eosinophilic Esophagitis Refractory to Standard Therapy  

**Abstract**  
The primary objective of this study was to assess the efficacy and safety of prednisone in adult patients diagnosed with severe eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) who had not achieved adequate control of symptoms with standard therapies, including elimination diets and topical corticosteroids. EoE is characterized by eosinophilic infiltration in the esophageal mucosa and resultant esophageal dysfunction, leading to dysphagia and feeding difficulties. This randomized controlled trial aimed to determine whether systemic corticosteroids, specifically prednisone, could improve clinical outcomes in this challenging patient population.  

**Study Design**  
This was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving participants from multiple tertiary care centers. Eligible participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either prednisone (30 mg/day) or placebo for a treatment duration of 12 weeks. The co-primary endpoints included the rate of symptomatic improvement, measured by a validated dysphagia symptom questionnaire (DSQ), and endoscopic mucosal healing, defined as an eosinophil count of ≤ 5 eosinophils per high-power field.  

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adults aged 18 years and older.  
- Documented diagnosis of eosinophilic esophagitis confirmed by esophageal biopsy revealing ≥ 15 eosinophils per high-power field.  
- Persistent symptoms of dysphagia despite standard therapy, including dietary changes and topical corticosteroids.  
- Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study protocols.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Prior treatment with prednisone within the last 6 months.  
- Known hypersensitivity to corticosteroids.  
- Esophageal strictures requiring dilation.  
- Active malignancy or other significant comorbidities (e.g., uncontrolled diabetes, severe heart disease).  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.  

**Results**  
The study enrolled 120 participants across five clinical sites. Baseline characteristics were balanced between the two groups. The trial concluded with no significant difference in the co-primary endpoints. The rate of clinical response in the prednisone group was 25%, while the placebo group achieved a 22% response, yielding a p-value of 0.63, indicating no statistical significance. Endoscopic evaluation demonstrated similar mucosal healing rates between both groups, with only 20% of those receiving prednisone exhibiting resolution of eosinophilia compared to 18% in the placebo cohort.  

The adverse event profile was consistent with known effects of systemic corticosteroid therapy. Notably, there was an increase in reported episodes of gastrointestinal discomfort (nausea and abdominal pain) in the prednisone group, and the withdrawal of steroid therapy led to a rebound in eosinophilic activity, suggesting immunogenic variability among the participants that was not fully addressed.

**Discussion**  
This trial underscores the challenges faced when administering corticosteroids in a population exhibiting variable immunological responses, particularly in cases of eosinophilic disorders. In addition, the study highlights the detrimental impact of previous therapy on the efficacy of systemic corticosteroids, as many participants had undergone extensive treatment prior to enrollment. Furthermore, the suboptimal efficacy observed indicates that while prednisone may act as a therapeutic adjunct, it fails to elicit a meaningful clinical response in this cohort, thereby questioning the role of such agents in managing refractory EoE without personalized therapeutic strategies.  

**Conclusion**  
The failure of this trial to demonstrate the efficacy of prednisone in adults with severe EoE presents a compelling case for re-evaluating steroid utilization in eosinophilic esophagitis, particularly when accounting for previous treatment exposure and responder variability. Future studies must consider tailoring treatment approaches based on individual immunogenic profiles to enhance therapy effectiveness in this complex condition.
