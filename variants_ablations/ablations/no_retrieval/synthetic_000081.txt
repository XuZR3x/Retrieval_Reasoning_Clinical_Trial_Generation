<clinicalTrial>
    <title>Evaluating the Efficacy of Rasagiline in Managing Cognitive Impairment in Patients with Parkinson's Disease: A Double-blind, Randomized, Placebo-controlled Study</title>
    <objective>The primary objective of this clinical trial is to assess the efficacy of rasagiline in improving cognitive function and reducing cognitive decline in Parkinson's Disease (PD) patients. A secondary objective is to evaluate the effects of rasagiline on motor symptoms, non-motor symptoms, and quality of life.</objective>
    
    <background>Cognitive impairment in PD is increasingly recognized as a significant challenge, affecting daily functioning and overall quality of life. Prior studies have demonstrated that rasagiline, a selective and irreversible MAO-B inhibitor, enhances dopaminergic activity without the adverse effects associated with amphetamine-metabolized therapies, thereby hypothesizing its potential cognitive benefits in PD patients.</background>
    
    <design>This study will follow a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter design enrolling 240 participants from various neurological centers. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either rasagiline 1 mg per day or placebo for a duration of 24 weeks. 

    <inclusionCriteria>
        <criterion>Male or female age 45-80 years.</criterion>
        <criterion>Clinical diagnosis of idiopathic PD according to UK Brain Bank criteria.</criterion>
        <criterion>Modified Hoehn and Yahr stage I-III.</criterion>
        <criterion>Stable on dopaminergic medication for at least 60 days prior to screening.</criterion>
        <criterion>Baseline score on the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) between 18-26.</criterion>
    </inclusionCriteria>
    
    <exclusionCriteria>
        <criterion>History of major psychiatric disorders that impede informed consent.</criterion>
        <criterion>Clinical diagnosis of dementia or Alzheimer's Disease as defined by standard criteria.</criterion>
        <criterion>Malignancy or significant systemic disease that affects cognitive function.</criterion>
        <criterion>Uncontrolled significant medical comorbidities.</criterion>
        <criterion>Use of other MAO inhibitors or cognitive enhancers within 30 days prior to screening.</criterion>
    </exclusionCriteria>
    
    <primaryOutcome>Change from Baseline to Week 24 in total MoCA score, assessing cognitive function across multiple domains.</primaryOutcome>
    <secondaryOutcomes>
        <outcome>Change in Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) Part III score to evaluate motor function.</outcome>
        <outcome>Impact on quality of life measured by the Parkinson's Disease Quality of Life questionnaire (PDQ-39).</outcome>
        <outcome>Assessment of non-motor symptoms using the Non-Motor Symptoms Scale (NMSS).</outcome>
    </secondaryOutcomes>

    <rationale>The study’s rationale is supported by the previously established efficacious mechanism of action of rasagiline, enhancing dopaminergic neurotransmission, potentially benefiting cognitive domains affected in early stages of PD. The favorable safety profile observed in prior trials reinforces the hypothesis of rasagiline providing cognitive benefit, while also improving motor and non-motor symptoms. The rigorous study design, including well-defined inclusion and exclusion criteria, serves to ensure population homogeneity, allowing for clearer insights into the therapeutic effects of rasagiline on cognitive impairment and quality of life in PD patients.</rationale>
    
    <significance>If successful, this trial may identify rasagiline as a multifaceted treatment option for cognitive decline, potentially enhancing the overall management of PD and significantly improving patient-centered outcomes.</significance>
</clinicalTrial>
