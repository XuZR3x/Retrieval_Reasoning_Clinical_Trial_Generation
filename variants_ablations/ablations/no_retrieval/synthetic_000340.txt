**Failed Clinical Trial of Plerixafor: Plerixafor in Combination with Azacitidine for Treatment-Resistant Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

**Phase I/II Study of Plerixafor and Azacitidine in Adults with Relapsed or Refractory Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

This clinical trial investigates the safety and preliminary efficacy of plerixafor combined with azacitidine in adult patients suffering from relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia (AML), specifically targeting those who have previously received at least one line of standard therapy. Despite laboratory findings suggesting that plerixafor could enhance the effectiveness of azacitidine by increasing the mobilization of leukemic cells and possibly facilitating their clearance, the study ultimately failed to demonstrate significant clinical benefits.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the safety profile of plerixafor when administered alongside azacitidine in the target population.
II. To evaluate the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) following treatment, hypothesizing an improvement over the historical 20% CR rate associated with azacitidine monotherapy.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To compare overall survival rates at 6 months and 1 year with the standard of care.
II. To measure pharmacokinetic parameters of plerixafor when co-administered with azacitidine, and to monitor treatment-related adverse events.
III. To identify changes in minimal residual disease (MRD) levels post-treatment utilizing flow cytometry.

**OUTLINE:**
Eligible participants will receive plerixafor via subcutaneous injection once daily for 5 days, starting on Day 1 of a 28-day cycle, with azacitidine concurrently administered intravenously on Days 1-5 of each cycle. Following completion of treatment, patients will be evaluated for response, including bone marrow assessments to determine cellularity and blast counts.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Adults aged 18 years or older with a confirmed diagnosis of relapsed or refractory AML per World Health Organization (WHO) criteria.
- Prior treatment with at least one course of chemotherapy.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate renal (creatinine clearance ≥50 mL/min) and hepatic function (bilirubin ≤1.5 times institutional upper limit of normal; AST/ALT ≤2.5 times institutional upper limit of normal).
- Signed informed consent.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- History of hypersensitivity to plerixafor or azacitidine.
- Presence of acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL) or any condition that would require immediate intervention prior to study initiation.
- Concurrent treatment with other investigational agents that may affect safety or efficacy outcomes.
- Central nervous system involvement by leukemia confirmed via MRI/CT.
- Pregnant or lactating women.

**TRIAL DESIGN AND CONCLUSIONS:**
The trial commenced with an initial cohort of 60 participants, emphasizing safety assessments and the determination of dosage tolerability. Unfortunately, the study faced significant barriers leading to its termination:

1. **Inadequate Efficacy in Combination**: Initial analyses indicated that the proportion of patients achieving a CR did not statistically differ from historical controls, evidencing plerixafor's ineffectiveness in enhancing azacitidine's therapeutic profile within this population.

2. **Suboptimal Trial Design**: Feedback highlighted insufficient preliminary data establishing a synergistic effect between plerixafor and azacitidine, prompting investigators to reassess the combination approach which lacked a solid scientific rationale and robust data.

3. **Pharmacokinetic Challenges**: The complexity of pharmacokinetic interactions revealed unpredictable clearance rates of plerixafor when combined with azacitidine, leading to complications such as increased hematologic toxicities. This detracted from both safety and efficacy experiences observed in early trials.

4. **Participant Selection Limitations**: The strict inclusion parameters inadvertently restricted participant enrollment diversity, resulting in a cohort that did not sufficiently represent the overall AML patient population. This lack of variability skewed the outcomes and limited the generalizability of results.

5. **Insufficient Biomarkers for Response**: There was a notable absence of validated biomarkers to reliably predict response to treatment, which complicated the assessment of plerixafor's potential benefit alongside azacitidine.

In conclusion, the trial was officially closed due to failure to meet therapeutic endpoints associated with effective treatment outcomes for adult AML patients. Future investigations may necessitate exploring alternative combinations or dosing regimens, as well as expanded biomarker studies to identify suitable patient populations for plerixafor use.
