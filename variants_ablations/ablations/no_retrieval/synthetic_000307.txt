**Successful Clinical Trial of Doxorubicin: Efficacy of Doxorubicin Combined with Targeted Therapy in Advanced Breast Cancer**

**Title:** A Phase II Multicenter Study Evaluating the Combination of Doxorubicin and Targeted Therapy in Patients With Advanced Hormone Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer

**Study Objectives:**
The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy of doxorubicin combined with a novel targeted therapy in patients with advanced hormone receptor-positive breast cancer. The secondary objectives include evaluating safety profiles, progression-free survival (PFS), response rates, and quality of life.

**Background:**
Hormone receptor-positive breast cancer remains a clinical challenge, particularly in advanced stages where traditional chemotherapy may be insufficient. The integration of doxorubicin with targeted agents has shown a potential synergistic effect that warrants investigation.

**Study Design:**
This Phase II, open-label, multicenter, randomized controlled trial enrolled 200 patients diagnosed with advanced hormone receptor-positive breast cancer. Participants were randomized to receive either a doxorubicin-based regimen combined with the targeted therapy or doxorubicin as a monotherapy.

**Eligibility Criteria:**
*Inclusion Criteria:*
- Histologically confirmed hormone receptor-positive breast cancer (ER+, PR+)
- Metastatic or locally advanced disease not amenable to surgery
- No prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease
- Adequate organ function as indicated by laboratory tests
- Age ≥ 18 years
- Signed informed consent
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0-2

*Exclusion Criteria:*
- Previous treatment with doxorubicin or other anthracyclines
- Known CNS metastases
- Concurrent malignancies
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women
- Uncontrolled comorbid conditions

**Treatment Protocol:**
Participants in the experimental group received doxorubicin (60 mg/m²) every three weeks in combination with an oral targeted therapy selected based on tumor biomarker expression. The control group received doxorubicin alone under the same scheduling constraints.

**Monitoring and Assessments:**
Response to treatment was assessed after every two cycles using radiographic imaging (CT/MRI) and clinical evaluation based on Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST 1.1). Quality of life was evaluated using the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire (EORTC QLQ-C30).

**Key Success Factors:**
1. **Comprehensive Treatment Strategies:** The incorporation of targeted therapy alongside doxorubicin enhanced the treatment efficacy, leading to a higher objective response rate compared to historical controls.
   
2. **Robust Eligibility Criteria:** The stringent criteria ensured a homogenous patient cohort, effectively minimizing confounding factors and allowing for clear interpretation of results.

3. **Meticulous Monitoring:** Advanced imaging techniques continuously assessed therapeutic responses, facilitating timely modifications to treatment protocols based on patient-specific responses, thus optimizing individual care pathways.

4. **Synergistic Effects:** Combining doxorubicin with targeted therapies capitalized on both cytotoxic and biochemical pathways in the management of breast cancer, increasing the disease control rate.

5. **Supportive Care Strategies:** Structured supportive care during the trial, including proactive management of side effects and patient education on potential complications, improved safety and adherence to treatment regimens.

**Results:**
The preliminary results indicated a significant increase in progression-free survival in the combination therapy group (12 months) compared to the doxorubicin monotherapy group (8 months). Furthermore, an improved overall response rate (complete response + partial response) of 65% was observed in the experimental group against 42% in the control group.

**Conclusion:**
This trial successfully demonstrated the enhanced efficacy and tolerability of combining doxorubicin with targeted therapy in patients with advanced hormone receptor-positive breast cancer. These findings support the integration of this regimen into clinical practice, addressing unmet medical needs and enhancing long-term outcomes for patients in this challenging cancer population.
