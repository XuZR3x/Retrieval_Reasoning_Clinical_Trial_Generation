**Failed Clinical Trial of Plerixafor: Evaluation of Plerixafor in Combination with Azacitidine for Patients with Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)**

**Phase I/II Study of Plerixafor and Azacitidine in Elderly Patients with Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

The objective of this clinical research study is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of plerixafor (AMD3100) in combination with azacitidine in elderly patients (>60 years) diagnosed with acute myeloid leukemia (AML). The study aims to determine the effects of plerixafor on the mobilization of hematopoietic stem cells and its potential to enhance the therapeutic efficacy of azacitidine.

**Study Drug Administration:** 
Plerixafor will be administered subcutaneously at a fixed dose of 240 µg/kg body weight on days 1-5 of a 28-day cycle, alongside azacitidine given intramuscularly at a dose of 75 mg/m² on days 1-7. The study is designed to assess patient tolerance, mobilization rates of CD34+ cells, and overall objective response rates in AML.

**Primary Objective:** 
I. To determine the safety profile and tolerability of the combination of plerixafor and azacitidine in elderly AML patients.

**Secondary Objectives:** 
I. To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) as defined by complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) per International Working Group criteria.  
II. To assess the mobilization efficacy measured by the number of CD34+ cells collected through leukapheresis after treatment.  
III. To determine the duration of response and overall survival in patients receiving the treatment regimen.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Diagnosis of previously untreated AML according to the World Health Organization (WHO) criteria.
2. Age >60 years.
3. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
4. Adequate renal and hepatic function as evidenced by total bilirubin ≤1.5 × institutional upper limit of normal (ULN) and normal aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) values.
5. Informed consent must be obtained prior to participation in the study.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients with acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL).
2. Prior exposure to any hypomethylating agents, including azacitidine, or prior chemotherapy for AML.
3. Significant cardiovascular events or uncontrolled infections.
4. Concurrent malignancy or significant comorbid conditions that may interfere with study participation or induce additional risks.

**Dose Limiting Toxicity (DLT):**
The DLT will be assessed during the initial treatment cycles, with particular attention to hematologic and non-hematologic adverse effects during the first 28 days of therapy. Toxicity will be graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) Version 5.0.

**Study Outcomes:**
The trial faced significant challenges, leading to its early termination. Key issues included:

1. **Inadequate Mobilization Efficacy:** Patients did not achieve the target mobilization of 5 x 10^6 CD34+ cells/kg within the prescribed timeframe, indicating poor response to the combined regimen compared to prior studies that evaluated plerixafor in heterogeneous populations.

2. **Suboptimal Drug Combination:** The pharmacodynamic interactions between plerixafor and azacitidine were not beneficial, with patients displaying unsatisfactory response rates that fell below the anticipated efficacy thresholds as per the pre-defined endpoints.

3. **Insufficient Dosing Adjustments:** The fixed dosing strategy of plerixafor did not account for individual tolerance levels in a diverse population of elderly patients with varying physiological responses. This rigidity contributed to adverse event profiles that limited further escalation of treatment.

4. **Patient Population Considerations:** The inclusion of patients with varying disease complexities overshadowed the specific therapeutic potential of plerixafor, leading to challenges in interpreting the mechanistic effects of the drug within the context of azacitidine treatment.

5. **Adverse Event Burden:** A considerable incidence of dose-related toxicities was noted, resulting in treatment discontinuation for a subset of participants. Adjustments in treatment protocol to mitigate toxicity were deemed insufficient to allow continuation of combination therapy.

**Length of Study:**
The planned duration for patient enrollment was initially set at 24 months, with follow-up aimed at evaluating response rates and survivorship. However, due to the reasons outlined, the study did not progress beyond the early enrollment phase.

This study is deemed investigational; azacitidine is an FDA-approved agent for myelodysplastic syndromes but not fully approved in combination with plerixafor for patients with untreated AML. The combination remains under investigation to elucidate potential therapeutic synergies or delineate the adverse profiles that necessitated this trial's early termination.
