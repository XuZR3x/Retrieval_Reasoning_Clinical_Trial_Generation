**Successful Clinical Trial of Radiotherapy: Assessment of LDFRT and Cisplatin in Pancreatic Cancer**

**Title**: A Phase II Study of Low-Dose Fractionated Radiotherapy Combined with Cisplatin as Induction Therapy in Patients with Locally Advanced Pancreatic Cancer

**Purpose**: This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of combining low-dose fractionated radiotherapy (LDFRT) with cisplatin in inducing tumor response in patients diagnosed with locally advanced pancreatic cancer.

**Rationale**: The integration of LDFRT is hypothesized to enhance the therapeutic effect of cisplatin by mitigating tumor radioresistance and increasing overall response rates, leveraging the established synergistic relationship between radiotherapy and chemotherapy.

### Primary Objectives:
1. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) at 12 weeks post-initiation of treatment.
2. To measure progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months following treatment initiation.

### Secondary Objectives:
1. To evaluate overall survival (OS) rates at 1 year post treatment.
2. To analyze the safety profile and adverse events associated with the combination therapy.
3. To explore the correlation between tumor biomarker expression (e.g., KRAS mutations) and treatment response.

### Methodology:
**Design**: This is a multicenter, single-arm, open-label Phase II clinical trial. 

**Treatment plan**:
- Patients will receive LDFRT (20 Gy administered in 10 fractions over 2 weeks) in conjunction with cisplatin (75 mg/m² administered intravenously on days 1 and 15 of the cycle) for a total of two cycles.

### Inclusion Criteria:
1. Adult patients aged ≥ 18 years with histologically confirmed locally advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma.
2. Patient must have measurable disease based on RECIST criteria.
3. ECOG performance status of 0-2.
4. Adequate organ function: creatinine clearance > 60 mL/min, liver function tests within normal limits.
5. Written informed consent from the patient or their legally authorized representative.

### Exclusion Criteria:
1. Previous treatment with chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or investigational agents for pancreatic cancer.
2. Pregnancy or lactation; females of childbearing potential must use effective contraception.
3. Active infection or serious concurrent medical conditions that may interfere with treatment.
4. History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to cisplatin or similar compounds.
5. Previous malignancy diagnosed within the past 5 years, excluding non-melanoma skin cancer.

### Tumor Volume Measurement:
Tumor dimensions will be assessed using CT scans at baseline and every 6 weeks post-initiation of therapy to determine changes in tumor volume for evaluating response.

### Follow-Up:
Patients will be monitored for treatment response and adverse events on a biweekly basis for the first 6 months, with subsequent follow-ups every 3 months thereafter, up to a maximum of 2 years. The primary endpoint of ORR will be analyzed after 12 weeks, while PFS and OS will be detailed at the conclusion of the study period.

### Expected Outcomes:
The trial is anticipated to demonstrate an enhanced ORR compared to historical data for chemotherapy alone, with a notable improvement in PFS and OS due to the synergistic effects of LDFRT and cisplatin. Furthermore, a comprehensive analysis of adverse events will contribute to understanding the safety implications of this combination regimen in this patient population.

### Statistical Analysis:
The ORR and PFS will be analyzed using Kaplan-Meier survival methods, with the significance of the results assessed using log-rank tests. Data will be presented as mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables and frequencies for categorical variables.

### Conclusion:
The proposed study intends to establish a new combination regimen that may improve outcomes for patients with locally advanced pancreatic cancer, addressing the challenges posed by tumor resistance and enhancing therapeutic efficacy through combined modality treatment.

The trial is registered and conducted following the principles of Good Clinical Practice and in compliance with local regulatory requirements.
