**Clinical Trial Report: PROMISE Trial – The Role of Azithromycin in Patients with Prolonged COVID-19 Symptoms**

**Study Title:** Evaluating the Efficacy of Azithromycin on Persistent Symptoms in Patients Recovering from COVID-19 (PROMISE Trial)

**Background:** Prolonged and lingering symptoms following COVID-19, commonly referred to as "Long COVID," can affect various organ systems and lead to considerable morbidity. The potential role of azithromycin, with its established anti-inflammatory properties alongside its antimicrobial effects, presents a compelling option for therapeutic intervention.

**Objective:** This study aims to assess the efficacy of azithromycin in alleviating persistent symptoms in patients recovering from COVID-19, primarily focusing on respiratory and fatigue-related outcomes.

**Design:** This is a multi-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial. Eligible participants will be randomly assigned to receive either azithromycin 500 mg orally once daily for 10 days or an identical placebo. 

**Hypothesis:** It is hypothesized that treatment with azithromycin will result in a statistically significant reduction in the prevalence and severity of prolonged COVID-19 symptoms compared to placebo.

**Setting:** The trial will be conducted at 10 hospitals across North America, with recruitment from outpatient settings and post-COVID clinics, ensuring broad patient representation.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years and older.
- Documented history of confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection (PCR positive).
- Persistent symptoms for at least 4 weeks after initial diagnosis, including respiratory symptoms, fatigue, and cognitive disturbances.
- Patients must provide written informed consent for participation.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Active bacterial infection requiring antibiotic treatment.
- Chronic pulmonary conditions (other than COVID-19) such as asthma or COPD that could confound results.
- Serious organ complications post-COVID, such as active heart failure or severe liver dysfunction.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

**Outcome Measures:** 
- Primary outcome is the change in the symptom severity score (via a validated 10-point scale) for respiratory symptoms and fatigue from baseline to 8 weeks post-treatment.
- Secondary outcomes include changes in quality of life metrics assessed through the EuroQol-5D questionnaire and analysis of hospitalization rates and additional medical care in both arms.

**Data Collection:** Outcome assessments will be performed at baseline, at 4 weeks, and 8 weeks post-treatment. A comprehensive data collection plan will involve objective measures such as spirometry for pulmonary function and biomarkers for inflammation.

**Randomization and Blinding:** The allocation of participants to either azithromycin or placebo will be performed using a computer-generated randomization list. Both participants and investigators will remain blinded to treatment allocation to eliminate bias in reporting and assessing outcomes.

**Methodological Rigor:** The study will utilize stringent inclusion/exclusion guidelines designed to target a representative patient population, mirroring real-life scenarios associated with COVID-19 recovery. The robustness of our design, coupled with rigorous data collection methods, will enhance the internal validity of our findings while ensuring adequate power to detect clinically meaningful differences.

**Contingency Plans:** If preliminary results indicate a significant effect of azithromycin on Long COVID symptoms, an extension phase may be incorporated, where all patients will receive active treatment after the study's initial conclusion to validate sustained benefits.

**Statistical Analysis:** A two-sided significance level of 0.05 will be used for analysis. Continuous variables will be assessed using t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests, and categorical variables will be analyzed using Chi-square tests.

In summary, the PROMISE Trial seeks to provide empirical evidence regarding the efficacy of azithromycin in ameliorating Long COVID symptoms, capitalizing on the drug's known pharmacological benefits while ensuring a rigorous methodological framework fostering generalizability and clinical applicability in managing post-viral sequelae.
