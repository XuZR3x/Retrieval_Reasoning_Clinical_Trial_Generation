**Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Tolvaptan in Management of Sodium-Dependent Edema in Patients with Class II and III Heart Failure

**Study Design:** A Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial

**Objective:** This trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of tolvaptan in reducing sodium-dependent edema in patients diagnosed with Class II and III heart failure and to assess the drug's safety profile over a 28-day treatment period.

**Intervention:** Participants will receive either tolvaptan 30 mg or matched placebo orally once daily in the morning for 28 consecutive days while continuing their standard heart failure therapies.

**Rationale:** Building on successful outcomes from previous trials indicating tolvaptan's significant efficacy in volume overload management, this study hypothesizes that tolvaptan will further enhance therapeutic outcomes by specifically targeting sodium retention mechanisms in heart failure. The study uses stringent inclusion criteria to select patients at risk of exacerbation who are perceived to benefit most from an aggressive diuretic approach.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Written informed consent obtained prior to protocol participation
- Men and women aged 18 to 80 years, inclusive
- Clinical diagnosis of Class II or III heart failure per New York Heart Association (NYHA) classification
- Documented history of persistent edema and/or dyspnea despite optimal diuretic therapy
- Current treatment with standard therapies, including ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, and loop diuretics

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with contraindications to systemic diuretics and hypersensitivity to tolvaptan or its analogues
- Declining kidney function (eGFR <30 mL/min at screening)
- Active liver disease or jaundice
- Patients dependent on chronic high-dose acute dialysis
- Severe electrolyte imbalances, defined as serum sodium <130 or >150 mEq/L at baseline
- History of hypervolemia due to conditions other than heart failure such as nephrotic syndrome or chronic liver disease
- Patients unlikely to comply with study protocols or scheduled visits

**Primary Endpoints:**
- Change in body weight from baseline measured weekly
- 24-hour urine output recorded daily during inpatient observation

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Quality of life assessment using the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ) at baseline and end of treatment
- Incidence of adverse effects associated with tolvaptan treatment

**Methodology:** 
Eligible patients will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either tolvaptan or placebo. Body weight, fluid intake, and urine output will be systematically monitored. Blood samples will be collected to assess serum electrolytes and copeptin levels at baseline and weekly throughout the study period to examine the pharmacodynamic response and potential predictive biomarkers for treatment efficacy.

**Statistical Analysis:** 
A sample size of 200 patients is estimated to provide 80% power to detect a significant difference in body weight changes between treatment groups, assuming a significance level of 0.05. All statistical analyses will follow an intention-to-treat approach.

**Conclusion:** This trial directly correlates the management of sodium-dependent edema in Class II and III heart failure patients using tolvaptan with patient-reported outcomes and aims to elucidate the relationship between copeptin biomarkers and therapeutic response, capitalizing on established safety and efficacy observed in prior studies. The outcomes are expected to broaden the therapeutic scope of tolvaptan in heart failure management, marking a step forward in personalized patient care in the congestive heart failure population.
