<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Phase 2 Multicenter Study of Enzastaurin in Combination with Novel Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Advanced Solid Tumors</Title>
    <Objective>
        This clinical trial aims to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of oral enzastaurin in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors in patients with advanced solid tumors who have failed prior therapies. The study is designed to exploit the synergistic effects of enzastaurin's inhibition of the Protein Kinase B (PKB) pathway alongside the immune modulation offered by checkpoint inhibitors, potentially enhancing clinical outcomes in a challenging patient population.
    </Objective>
    <StudyDesign>
        This is a randomized, open-label, multicenter Phase 2 clinical trial. Patients will be stratified into cohorts based on cancer type and prior treatment history. The trial will encompass a control group receiving standard immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy alone, compared to an experimental group receiving the designated combination therapy of enzastaurin and immune checkpoint inhibitors. The study will utilize both progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) as primary efficacy endpoints, supplemented by comprehensive safety monitoring.
    </StudyDesign>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>You must have a confirmed diagnosis of advanced solid tumors (e.g., melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer) with progression after at least one line of standard therapy.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients must have measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1 guidelines.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Age ≥ 18 years.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status of 0 to 2.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Prior immunotherapy must be completed with a washout period of at least 4 weeks before study enrollment.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients must provide informed consent and have the ability to adhere to study requirements.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>You must not have active central nervous system (CNS) metastases unless previously treated and stable for at least 4 weeks.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>You must not have uncontrolled infections or other serious comorbidities that may interfere with study participation.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>You must not have previously received treatment with enzastaurin or other investigational agents within 30 days of enrollment.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>You must not be pregnant or breastfeeding.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>You must not have any electrocardiogram (ECG) abnormalities that might compromise heart function.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <OutcomeMeasures>
        <Measure>The primary outcome measure is the overall response rate (ORR) assessed by an independent review committee after 24 weeks of treatment, as well as the incidence of adverse events to evaluate safety and tolerability.</Measure>
        <Measure>Secondary outcomes will include PFS, OS, and quality of life assessments through validated questionnaires.</Measure>
    </OutcomeMeasures>
    <Eligibility>
        <PatientPopulation>A targeted demographic including diverse ethnicities with an emphasis on inclusivity to ensure comprehensive data representation.</PatientPopulation>
        <PatientCompliance>Robust strategies will be implemented to ensure adherence, including frequent safety evaluations, patient education on potential adverse effects, and support systems for all participants.</PatientCompliance>
    </Eligibility>
    <Conclusion>
        This study is anticipated to enhance the understanding of enzastaurin's role when combined with novel immune therapeutics, leveraging its PKB pathway inhibition in a way that potentially synergizes with immune response modulation. The robust design informed by previous trials demonstrating enzastaurin's pharmacological activity and its safety profile aims to foster substantial improvements in patient outcomes and broaden therapeutic options for advanced cancer patients.
    </Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
