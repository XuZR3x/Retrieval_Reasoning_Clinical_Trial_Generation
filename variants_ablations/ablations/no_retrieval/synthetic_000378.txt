**Phase II Trial of Eribulin Mesylate as Monotherapy in Patients with Advanced or Metastatic Soft Tissue Sarcoma**

This multicenter, open-label, phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of eribulin mesylate in patients diagnosed with advanced or metastatic soft tissue sarcoma (STS) who have received previous chemotherapy. The underlying hypothesis for this study posits that the dual mechanism of action of eribulin mesylate will provide clinical benefits to this particular population, as it has demonstrated consistent antitumor activity in similar malignancies.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To estimate the objective response rate (ORR) of eribulin mesylate in patients with advanced or metastatic STS who have progressed following at least one prior systemic therapy.  
II. To evaluate the safety and tolerability of eribulin mesylate in the specified cohort.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To estimate the median progression-free survival (PFS) in patients treated with eribulin mesylate.  
II. To assess the overall survival (OS) rate at 6 months and 12 months.  
III. To characterize the toxicity profile of eribulin mesylate using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), thereby ensuring vigilant safety monitoring.

**OUTLINE:**
Eligible participants will receive eribulin mesylate intravenously at a dose of 1.4 mg/m² over 2-5 minutes on days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle. Treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal. Patients will be followed for response assessment via imaging every two cycles.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Patients must be aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed advanced STS of any subtype, excluding certain aggressive histologies such as dedifferentiated liposarcoma.
- Patients must have measurable disease per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v1.1, with at least one target lesion amenable to accurate measurement.
- Must have progressive disease following one to three prior systemic regimens for advanced disease.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function as defined by specified laboratory criteria.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Patients with a history of allergic reactions to halichondrin derivatives or similar agents.
- Patients currently receiving other investigational drugs, biosimilars, or any form of immunotherapy.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- Patients with symptomatic central nervous system involvement or uncontrolled intercurrent illness.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**
The primary measure for efficacy will be the objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of participants exhibiting a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) according to RECIST v1.1 criteria. Secondary measures will include the estimation of median PFS and OS, which will be calculated from the time of the first eribulin mesylate dose to the date of observed disease progression or death. 

**EXPECTED FINDINGS:**
Based on the established efficacy of eribulin mesylate in analogous malignancies, the trial anticipates a favorable response rate correlating with previously reported data while addressing the stringent selection criteria to enhance precision in patient recruitment. Expectations for both objective response and disease control rates are supported by previously acknowledged preclinical findings advocating eribulin's effectiveness in resistant histologies.

**SAFETY AND ADVERSE EVENTS MONITORING:**
Rigorous monitoring for treatment-related adverse events will be conducted throughout the study, implementing the CTCAE. Scheduling regular assessments will ensure timely identification and management of any emerging toxicities, thus promoting participant retention and resultant treatment adherence. 

**CONCLUSION:**
This clinical trial of eribulin mesylate in patients with advanced STS is designed to systematically evaluate its therapeutic benefit while concurrently emphasizing safety profiles, aligning with the reasons affirming the intervention's potential success. By incorporating stringent inclusion criteria and a detailed monitoring framework, this study seeks to bolster the body of evidence supporting eribulin as a viable treatment option in a challenging therapeutic landscape.
