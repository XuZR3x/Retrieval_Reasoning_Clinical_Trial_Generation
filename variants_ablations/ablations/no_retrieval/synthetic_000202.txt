**Failed Clinical Trial of Corifollitropin Alfa: A Phase 2 Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Corifollitropin Alfa in Inducing Monofollicular Growth with a Modified Dosing Regimen in WHO Group II Anovulatory Infertility (Trial ID: 009876)**

**Study Overview:**  
This Phase II, multicentric, randomized, controlled trial aimed to assess the efficacy of a novel modified dosing regimen of corifollitropin alfa (Org 36286) in inducing monofollicular growth in women diagnosed with WHO Group II anovulatory infertility. The proposed dosing strategy involved administration of corifollitropin alfa followed by an altered sequence of low-dose hCG or recombinant FSH, hypothesizing enhanced follicular response compared to previous trials.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
The primary objective was to determine the percentage of participants achieving a monofollicular response (defined as the presence of one follicle ≥18 mm without any follicles ≥15 mm at the time of hCG bolus injection) among the treated populations.

**Study Design and Methodology:**  
The trial consisted of two sequential stages (Ia and II) across a broader participant pool to improve statistical power. Stage Ia consisted of an open-label, uncontrolled assessment enrolling a total of 15 women to evaluate the modified dosing of corifollitropin alfa paired with a daily regimen of recFSH. Stage II followed, employing a randomized approach with a total of 100 participants divided into two groups: one receiving corifollitropin alfa followed by hCG and the other receiving corifollitropin alfa followed by recFSH.

Inclusion Criteria:  
- Age 18 to 40 years  
- Oligomenorrhea (menstrual cycles ≥35 days and <6 months) or amenorrhea (cycles ≥6 months)  
- BMI between 18 and 30 kg/m²  
- Normal serum FSH and estradiol levels at screening  
- Progestagen-induced withdrawal bleeding

Exclusion Criteria:  
- History of ovarian cysts or tumors of the reproductive organs  
- Prior use of corifollitropin alfa or investigational drugs within the past 90 days  
- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding or other clinically significant lab abnormalities  
- Hypersensitivity to treatment components  

**Results:**
The trial ultimately did not yield favorable results; only 15% of the participants achieved the primary endpoint of monofollicular response within the treatment group receiving corifollitropin alfa followed by hCG, contrasting with expectations. The cohort receiving recFSH fared similarly, reflecting insufficient therapeutic efficacy as outlined by previous failures. Resultant data demonstrated that the alterations in the dosing regimen did not substantially improve ovarian response.

**Key Issues Identified:**
1. **Insufficient Efficacy of Modified Dosing Regimen:** Contrary to the postulated hypothesis, the revised dosing failed to demonstrate a significant increase in monofollicular growth. The pharmacodynamics of corifollitropin alfa may not have been sufficient to elicit an adequately robust follicular development response across a varied patient demographic.

2. **Suboptimal Participant Generalizability:** Although the cohort was expanded, strict eligibility criteria continued to limit the diversity of the study population, creating challenges in extrapolating findings to broader populations with WHO Group II anovulatory infertility.

3. **Phase-Specific Dosage Limitations:** The experimental regimen did not provide the necessary physiological support for follicular development. The reliance on the modified protocol may have been inadequate in meeting the hormonal requirements needed for effective ovarian stimulation.

4. **Monitoring Challenges:** Insufficient monitoring of ovarian response in terms of biochemical markers, besides ultrasound assessments, may have limited timely intervention adjustments during the treatment course, leading to suboptimal trial outcomes.

**Conclusion:**  
The findings from trial 009876 underscore the complexities of pharmacological intervention in fertility treatments and highlight the need for more comprehensive strategies that encompass diverse characteristics of the target population. Ongoing research must address the gaps identified in dosing efficacy, participant diversity, and robust monitoring to potentially realize successful outcomes in future applications of corifollitropin alfa. Consequently, this trial reaffirms the necessity of judicious trial design in the pursuit of innovative reproductive therapies.
