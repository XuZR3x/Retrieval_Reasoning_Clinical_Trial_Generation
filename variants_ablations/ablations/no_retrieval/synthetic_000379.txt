**Successful Clinical Trial of Curcumin: Curcumin for Symptomatic Relief in Patients with Rheumatoid Arthritis**

**Curcumin for Symptomatic Relief in Patients with Rheumatoid Arthritis: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study**

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic inflammatory disorder characterized by symmetrical polyarthritis, resulting in significant morbidity and functional impairment. Conventional therapies, including non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs), often provide insufficient alleviation of symptoms and carry potential adverse effects. In light of the persistent need for adjunctive therapies to improve patient outcomes, curcumin—a polyphenol derived from the rhizome of *Curcuma longa*—has garnered interest due to its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, which may enhance the therapeutic effects of standard treatments.

**Study Design**

This is a 24-week, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving 100 patients diagnosed with moderate to severe rheumatoid arthritis, in accordance with the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) criteria. The trial will be conducted across multiple hospitals, ensuring a diverse patient population and enhanced generalizability of findings.

**Participants**

One hundred patients will be recruited from outpatient rheumatology clinics over a period of 2 years. Eligible participants will include adults aged 18-75 years with a confirmed diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis for at least 6 months, exhibiting persistently active disease with a Disease Activity Score (DAS28) greater than 5.1. All participants will provide written informed consent after thorough briefing on study protocols. The study will be conducted following approval from the local Institutional Review Board in adherence to ethical guidelines.

**Procedure**

All patients will continue their standard DMARD therapy, which will be maintained at a stable dose for at least 3 months prior to the trial. All participants will be randomly assigned to receive either curcumin (Curcuma Phytosome® 1500 mg/day, administered as two 750 mg softgel capsules twice daily) or placebo (identical softgel capsules containing inert ingredients) over a period of 24 weeks.

Outcome evaluation will occur at baseline, 8, 16, and 24 weeks, focusing on the primary endpoints of pain relief and functional improvement assessed by the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) and the Health Assessment Questionnaire-Disability Index (HAQ-DI), in addition to monitoring adverse events and laboratory parameters.  

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Aged 18-75 years, male or female
- ACR criteria met for rheumatoid arthritis
- DAS28 score > 5.1 at screening
- Stable DMARD therapy for 3 months prior to enrollment
- Provision of informed consent

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Concurrent tocilizumab or TNF-inhibitor therapy
- History of hypersensitivity to curcumin or any component of the study
- Significant hepatic or renal impairment
- Active gastrointestinal disorders impacting absorption
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women

**Outcome Measures**

The primary outcome will be the change in the VAS pain score from baseline to week 24. Secondary outcomes will include changes in the HAQ-DI, levels of inflammatory biomarkers (C-reactive protein (CRP) and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)), and the occurrence of treatment-related adverse events.

The efficacy data from this trial aims to evaluate whether curcumin supplementation, known for its favorable pharmacokinetic profile and enhanced bioavailability when combined with agents such as phosphatidylcholine, can significantly reduce inflammation and improve symptoms in patients with rheumatoid arthritis.

Statistical analyses will involve paired t-tests and ANOVA for repeated measures, considering a p-value less than 0.05 as significant.

Through rigorous methodological standards, including a well-defined participant pool and standardized dosing regimen, this study aims to validate the effectiveness of curcumin as a viable adjunct therapy to help manage rheumatoid arthritis symptoms, addressing the existing gaps in treatment efficacy while ensuring patient safety.
