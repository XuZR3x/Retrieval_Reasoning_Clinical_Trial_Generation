**Failed clinical trial of atorvastatin: Effects of Atorvastatin on Endothelial Function and Cognitive Decline in Vascular Dementia Patients.**

**A 52-Week, Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial Assessing the Efficacy of Atorvastatin 40 mg in Improving Endothelial Function and Cognitive Outcomes in Patients with Vascular Dementia.**

This study aimed to evaluate the effects of atorvastatin on endothelial function and cognitive decline in patients diagnosed with vascular dementia, hypothesizing that atorvastatin would facilitate endothelial improvements that subsequently translate into cognitive enhancements.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male and female subjects aged 50 to 85 years with a clinical diagnosis of vascular dementia according to NINCDS-ADRDA criteria.
- Patients must exhibit a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score of 10-24 at screening.
- Evidence of endothelial dysfunction, defined as an impaired flow-mediated dilation (FMD) of the brachial artery ≤ 6%.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of Alzheimer's disease or other primary dementia syndromes.
- Uncontrolled hypertension (blood pressure ≥ 160/100 mmHg) or diabetes (HbA1c > 8.0%).
- Cardiovascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, coronary artery bypass grafting) within the previous six months.
- Known hypersensitivity to atorvastatin or any statin medication.

**Study Design:**
Enrolled participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either atorvastatin 40 mg daily or a placebo for a duration of 52 weeks. Primary endpoints included the change in FMD from baseline at 26 weeks and cognitive assessment using the MMSE at baseline and 52 weeks. Secondary endpoints included analysis of cognitive domains (attention, memory, and executive functions).

**Complications Encountered:**
The trial prematurely ceased enrollment after 32% of screened participants were disqualified due to diverse ethnic backgrounds exhibiting significant genetic polymorphisms affecting statin metabolism. Specifically, participants displayed varied responses to atorvastatin, complicating the statistical efficacy analysis.

Furthermore, a high prevalence of uncontrolled comorbidities emerged within the screened population, particularly hypertension and diabetes, primarily due to the stringent inclusion criteria for clinical stability. These uncontrolled factors further obscured cognitive assessment results and influenced the overall interpretation of atorvastatin's efficacy.

Another identified concern was the trial's treatment duration, which may have been insufficient to observe meaningful improvements in cognitive functionality or endothelial biomarkers in a population already experiencing pronounced vascular damage. 

Lastly, reliance on FMD as a primary endpoint without considering wider clinical outcomes led to inadequate depiction of atorvastatin's potential influence, misrepresenting its therapeutic effect on vascular dementia.

**Conclusion:**
The study was terminated after the first interim analysis, which indicated a lack of significant changes in endothelial function in patients with vascular dementia treated with atorvastatin compared to the placebo group. Moreover, the cognitive measures revealed no improvement, reinforcing the decision to halt further participation in this clinical trial due to unfavorable outcomes and confounding variables.

**Date of Termination:** March 15, 2022.
