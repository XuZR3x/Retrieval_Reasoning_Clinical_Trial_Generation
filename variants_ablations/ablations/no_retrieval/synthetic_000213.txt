**GSK1120212 in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with BRAF V600E/K Mutation-positive Advanced Melanoma: A Phase II Randomized Study**

**Background:**
GSK1120212 (trametinib) demonstrates potent inhibition of MEK1/2, pivotal components of the RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK signaling pathway, which has been implicated in the progression of BRAF V600E/K mutation-positive melanoma. This study evaluates the efficacy and safety of GSK1120212 in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors, hypothesizing that the dual mechanism of tumor cell growth inhibition via MEK inhibition and immune modulation may yield synergistic benefits in overall response rates and progression-free survival (PFS).

**Study Design:**
This is a multicenter, double-blind, randomized Phase II trial that investigates the efficacy of GSK1120212 in combination with an anti-PD-1 antibody (nivolumab) compared to nivolumab alone in treating patients with advanced melanoma harboring BRAF V600E/K mutations.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed Stage IIIc or Stage IV malignant melanoma with documented BRAF V600E/K mutation.
- Provided written informed consent.
- Age ≥18 years.
- Measurable disease defined by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST 1.1).
- ECOG Performance Status of 0-1.
- No prior treatment with BRAF inhibitors or other MEK inhibitors.
- Adequate organ function: ANC ≥1,500 cells/mm³, platelets ≥100,000 cells/mm³, total bilirubin ≤1.5 times upper limit of normal (ULN), ALT/AST ≤2.5 ULN (≤5 ULN if liver metastases), creatinine <1.5 times ULN.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with any anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 agents or other immunotherapeutic agents.
- Presence of symptomatic brain metastases (except those treated and stable).
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment or conditions that impair immune function.
- Significant cardiovascular history including QTc ≥480 msec or uncontrolled arrhythmias.
- Severe pulmonary condition including interstitial lung disease.

**Intervention:**
Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive GSK1120212 plus nivolumab (Arm A) or nivolumab alone (Arm B). GSK1120212 will be administered orally once daily at an established effective dose, while nivolumab will be administered via IV every 2 weeks.

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint is progression-free survival (PFS), defined as time from randomization to first documented disease progression or death due to any cause. Secondary endpoints include overall response rate (ORR), duration of response, safety profile, and quality of life assessments.

**Statistical Approach:**
PFS will be analyzed using a two-sided log-rank test with a significance level of 5%. The sample size estimates approximately 240 patients will provide 90% power to detect a statistically significant difference in PFS between the two arms.

**Results Overview:**
Interim analysis is anticipated at 12 months, focusing on PFS and ORR. Enrollment of subjects exhibiting high levels of PD-L1 expression was also encouraged, leveraging the biochemical targeting of tumorigenesis coupled with immune modulation to likely enhance outcomes.

**Conclusion:**
This trial aims to establish a safety and efficacy profile for GSK1120212 in the neoadjuvant setting, supporting earlier therapeutic intervention and improving outcomes in patients with advanced BRAF V600E/K mutation-positive melanoma. Given the strategic design, including innovative combinations with immune checkpoint inhibitors and meticulous patient selection criteria, it aims to contribute significantly to the body of evidence surrounding MEK inhibition in the context of targeted and immunotherapeutic strategies. 

This integrated approach, alongside dual evaluation of efficacy and safety, is posited to further elucidate the potential role of GSK1120212 in multimodal treatment paradigms for melanoma, with the ultimate goal of improving overall patient outcomes while minimizing adverse events.
