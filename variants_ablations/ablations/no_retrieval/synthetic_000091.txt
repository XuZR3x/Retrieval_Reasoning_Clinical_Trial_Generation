**Study of Anastrozole, Nivolumab, and Chemotherapy vs Chemotherapy Alone in Treating Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**  
**A Phase II Randomized Study of Anastrozole in Combination With Nivolumab and Chemotherapy Compared With Chemotherapy Alone in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**  

**RATIONALE:**   
Anastrozole, an aromatase inhibitor, reduces estrogen levels, which could potentially impair cancer cell proliferation in estrogen-sensitive malignancies. Nivolumab, an immune checkpoint inhibitor, enhances anti-tumor immunity by blocking the PD-1 pathway. The current study aims to assess whether the combination of anastrozole and nivolumab with standard chemotherapy improves clinical outcomes in patients with advanced NSCLC compared to chemotherapy alone. Despite theoretical benefits, previous trials have indicated potential pitfalls, including inadequate efficacy of hormone-related interventions in lung cancer, tumor heterogeneity leading to variable response rates, and toxicity profiles affecting treatment compliance.

**PURPOSE:**  
This randomized phase II trial investigates the efficacy of anastrozole and nivolumab combined with chemotherapy compared to chemotherapy alone in improving progression-free survival (PFS) in patients diagnosed with advanced NSCLC.

**OBJECTIVES:**   
**Primary:** To compare PFS in patients treated with anastrozole, nivolumab, and standard chemotherapy versus those receiving standard chemotherapy alone.  
**Secondary:** To assess overall survival, overall response rates, and the adverse event profile associated with each treatment regimen.

**OUTLINE:**  
This is a multicenter, randomized controlled trial. Eligible patients will be stratified according to their prior treatment history (chemotherapy naïve vs previously treated) and tumor histology (squamous vs non-squamous). Participants will be randomly assigned to one of two treatment arms.  
- **Arm I:** Patients receive oral anastrozole once daily and nivolumab intravenously every two weeks in conjunction with chemotherapy (cisplatin and pemetrexed) administered every three weeks.  
- **Arm II:** Patients receive chemotherapy alone (cisplatin and pemetrexed) every three weeks.  
Administration of both arms repeats until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity is observed.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Histologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)  
- Unresectable, advanced stage as defined by the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC)  
- No known EGFR mutations or ALK rearrangements  
- Measurable disease defined as ≥ 1 unidimensionally measurable lesion  

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Age ≥ 18 years  
- ECOG performance status 0-1  
- No history of significant cardiovascular or thromboembolic events  
- Adequate renal and liver function (defined as AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 times upper limit of normal)  
- Not pregnant, nursing, or planning to become pregnant during the study  

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**  
- No prior systemic therapy for advanced NSCLC  
- More than 4 weeks since prior radiotherapy  
- No concurrent use of hormone replacement therapy or additional investigational agents targeting estrogen signaling  

**STATISTICAL CONSIDERATIONS:**  
The study aims to enroll 300 patients (150 per treatment arm). PFS is defined as the time from randomization to documentation of disease progression or death from any cause. Tumor response will be evaluated according to RECIST v1.1 criteria.  

**PROJECTED OUTCOMES:**   
The failure of this trial is anticipated based on several reasons:  
1. **Insufficient Efficacy of Anastrozole in NSCLC:** Prior studies indicated a lack of synergistic effects when combining hormone therapy with anti-PD-1 inhibitors in NSCLC, potentially due to the absence of estrogen sensitivity in this cancer type.   
2. **Tumor Heterogeneity:** The presence of molecular alterations in NSCLC may lead to inherent resistance to endocrine approaches, diminishing the expected therapeutic benefits of anastrozole when combined with nivolumab.    
3. **Adverse Events Impacting Patient Adherence:** Previously documented tolerability issues related to immunotherapy and chemotherapy co-administration could contribute to high rates of treatment discontinuation, impacting overall outcomes and efficacy results.

**RESULTS MEASUREMENT:**   
- **Primary Outcomes:** PFS within each treatment arm analyzed using Kaplan-Meier survival curves and log-rank tests.  
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Overall survival rates and objective response rates will be analyzed with appropriate statistical methods.  

After completion of study treatment, patients will be followed periodically for overall survival outcomes for up to 3 years. Due to the anticipated complications and limited efficacy observed in preliminary analyses, anastrozole's role in NSCLC remains a critical subject for further research.
