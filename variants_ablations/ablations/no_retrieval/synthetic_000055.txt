**Successful clinical trial of adalimumab: Efficacy and Safety of Adalimumab in Patients with Psoriatic Arthritis**

**A Phase 3, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Multi-Center Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Adalimumab in the Treatment of Adults with Active Psoriatic Arthritis**

**Objective:** This clinical trial aims to assess the efficacy and safety of adalimumab in inducing and maintaining clinical remission in adults diagnosed with active psoriatic arthritis (PsA) who have had an inadequate response to prior therapies, including conventional disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs).

**Study Design:** This is a Phase 3, multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial designed to rigorously evaluate the therapeutic impacts of adalimumab in the target population. The study will incorporate meticulous patient selection criteria, ensuring participants meet the defined inclusion and exclusion parameters, thereby aligning with prior successful clinical trials of adalimumab (as seen in the aforementioned trials for Crohn's Disease and Ankylosing Spondylitis).

**Methods:** Approximately 600 adult participants with a confirmed diagnosis of active PsA will be enrolled across 50 clinical sites. Following a 4-week screening period, eligible participants will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either adalimumab (80 mg at Week 0, followed by 40 mg every other week) or matching placebo over a 24-week treatment period. 

**Patient Cohort:** Patients will be meticulously selected according to the following inclusion criteria:
- Aged 18-75 years.
- Diagnosis of psoriatic arthritis as per the Classification Criteria for Psoriatic Arthritis (CASPAR).
- A BASDAI (Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index) score of at least 4, indicating moderate to severe disease activity.
- Prior inadequate response or intolerance to one or more conventional therapies.

Participants will be excluded based on criteria including recent corticosteroid injections, significant comorbidities, or prior exposure to any biologic therapies targeting TNF.

**Efficacy Assessments:** Efficacy will be evaluated using the ACR20 (American College of Rheumatology) response criteria, which encompasses measures across multiple domains including tender and swollen joint counts, patient-reported outcomes regarding overall well-being, and health assessment questionnaires. Participants will be assessed at Baseline, Week 12, and Week 24 to determine treatment responses.

**Safety Evaluations:** A comprehensive safety assessment will be performed throughout the study period, including the monitoring of adverse events (AEs), vital signs, physical examinations, and routine laboratory tests. Participants reporting serious adverse events will receive prompt attention and, if necessary, withdrawal from the trial will be considered.

**Study Duration:** Participants will be followed for a total of 48 weeks, which includes a 24-week treatment phase and an additional 24-week open-label phase where all participants will receive adalimumab (40 mg every other week) regardless of initial group allocation. 

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary endpoint is the proportion of participants achieving an ACR20 response at Week 24, defined as a 20% improvement in the ACR criteria. 

**Secondary Endpoints:** Secondary objectives include evaluating the rates of ACR50 and ACR70 responses, improvement on the Health Assessment Questionnaire-Disability Index (HAQ-DI), and use of concomitant therapies throughout the study duration.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Confirmed diagnosis of psoriatic arthritis per CASPAR criteria.
- Active disease defined by swollen and tender joint counts ≥ 3.
- Age between 18 and 75 years.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Recent systemic therapy with corticosteroids (> 10 mg/day).
- Prior treatment with any anti-TNF biologic therapies.
- Recent history of serious infections within the past 3 months.

**Evaluation of Outcomes:** 

Results will be analyzed using statistical approaches appropriate for the designated endpoints, enabling the identification of meaningful efficacy and safety signals associated with adalimumab therapy. The outcome measures will align with industry standards, and findings will contribute to understanding the long-term clinical benefits of adalimumab in managing psoriatic arthritis within the intended patient population.

**Conclusion:** This study's adherence to robust design principles, meticulous patient cohort selection, and comprehensive assessment strategies is anticipated to yield significant insights into the efficacy and safety of adalimumab in the management of active psoriatic arthritis, aligning with the favorable outcomes observed in earlier adalimumab trials for diseases such as Crohn's and ankylosing spondylitis.  

**End of Trial Report.**
