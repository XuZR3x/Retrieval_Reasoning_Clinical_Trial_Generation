**Title: Efficacy and Safety of PRO 140 in Combination with Long-Acting Injectable Antiretroviral Therapy in Treatment-Experienced Adults with CCR5-Tropic HIV-1**

**Background:**
This Phase 2b/3, multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial aims to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of PRO 140 in conjunction with long-acting injectable antiretroviral therapy (LAI-ART) in treatment-experienced adults with HIV-1 infection predominantly involving CCR5-tropic virus. The strategic design of this study not only allows for assessment in a well-defined population but also leverages existing treatment paradigms to maximize therapeutic impact.

**Study Design and Population:**
The study is designed to include treatment-experienced participants aged ≥ 18 years, enrolled at multiple sites. Eligible subjects must demonstrate evidence of HIV-1 replication despite ongoing therapy with documented resistance to at least one agent in three antiretroviral classes or to two classes with limited options. The inclusion of this robust patient population ensures that PRO 140 is evaluated under conditions characterized by therapeutic failure of existing regimens, allowing for a clearer demonstration of its impact.

The trial will consist of a 1-week double-blind treatment phase comprising daily subcutaneous administration of either PRO 140 or placebo, followed by a 25-week open-label extension where all participants will receive PRO 140 in conjunction with LAI-ART.

**Primary Objective:**
To assess the proportion of participants achieving a ≥ 1.0 log10 reduction in HIV-1 RNA from baseline at Week 1 in the double-blind phase when compared to placebo.

**Secondary Objectives:**
1. To evaluate the change in viral load from baseline to Week 25 of the open-label extension.
2. To assess the safety and tolerability of PRO 140 in combination with LAI-ART.
3. To investigate the effects on CD4+ T-cell counts and overall quality of life, as measured by approved QoL questionnaires.

**Intervention:**
Participants will be administered PRO 140 at a dose of 350 mg subcutaneously once daily for the duration of the 1-week blind phase, with subsequent weekly injections of LAI-ART combined with PRO 140 in the open-label phase. This dual-therapy approach, employing both traditional and innovative administration techniques, aims to optimize adherence and maximize therapeutic efficacy.

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint evaluates the change in viral load, captured through standardized HIV-1 RNA assays. Secondary endpoints include safety assessments through adverse event monitoring, changes in CD4+ T-cell counts, and patient-reported outcomes from validated questionnaires throughout the study duration.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Males and females aged ≥ 18 years.
2. Documented CCR5-tropic virus at Screening as verified by Trofile™ Assay.
3. Treatment-experienced HIV-1 infected patients demonstrating an ongoing viral load > 400 copies/mL despite current ART.
4. Evidence of resistance to at least one ART agent from three different drug classes or limited options as reflected in genotypic testing.
5. Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study protocols.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Documented CXCR4-tropic virus or dual/mixed tropic (R5X4) virus.
2. Patients currently on any experimental art or who have received PRO 140 previously.
3. Active opportunistic infections or malignancies requiring additional systemic therapy.
4. Any contraindications to the use of PRO 140 and/or LAI-ART, including known allergies to any study medication.

**Conclusion:**
Through a methodologically rigorous and scientifically sound design, this trial seeks to leverage the incorporation of PRO 140 with long-acting injectable ART to provide a clear advantage in the management of treatment-resistant HIV-1. The outcomes of this study have the potential to shape future therapeutic strategies, enhance adherence through optimization of treatment regimens, and ultimately contribute to improved patient quality of life. The combination of strategic patient selection, high methodological standards, and comprehensive endpoint evaluations positions this study to significantly impact the landscape of HIV management.
