**Title:** Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Mifepristone in Patients with Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) and Comorbid Metabolic Disturbances: A Phase II Randomized Controlled Trial

**Background:** Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) significantly affects patients’ quality of life and is often accompanied by metabolic disturbances, including insulin resistance and weight gain, often exacerbated by systemic inflammation. Accumulating evidence suggests that glucocorticoid receptor antagonism might play a pivotal role in mitigating the metabolic side effects observed in COPD patients, particularly in those receiving systemic corticosteroids.

**Objectives:** 
- Primary Objective: To determine the efficacy of mifepristone in reducing systemic inflammation and improving metabolic profiles in patients with COPD and concurrent metabolic disturbances.
- Secondary Objectives: To evaluate the safety, tolerability, and patient quality of life following mifepristone administration compared to placebo.

**Design:** This phase II multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled trial will enroll 200 patients diagnosed with moderate to severe COPD, aged 40-75 years, who exhibit signs of metabolic syndrome. Participants will be randomized to receive either mifepristone or placebo for a duration of 12 weeks. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 40-75 years with a diagnosis of moderate to severe COPD (Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease [GOLD] Stage II or III)
- Presence of metabolic syndrome, defined by the presence of at least three of the following: abdominal obesity (waist circumference > 102 cm in men, > 88 cm in women), elevated triglycerides (≥ 150 mg/dL), decreased HDL cholesterol (< 40 mg/dL in men, < 50 mg/dL in women), elevated blood pressure (≥ 130/85 mmHg), and fasting glucose level ≥ 100 mg/dL
- Ability to provide written informed consent
- Stable doses of COPD medications for at least 3 months prior to enrollment

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of severe psychiatric disorders or current use of antipsychotic medications
- Significant cardiovascular, renal, hepatic, or endocrine disorders
- Use of systemic glucocorticoids within the past 3 months
- History of recent (within 6 months) significant respiratory exacerbations requiring hospitalization
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding

**Intervention:** Participants assigned to the mifepristone group will receive an oral dose of 300 mg daily, while the placebo group will receive an identical-looking capsule for 12 weeks. 

**Endpoints:**
- Primary Endpoint: Change in high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) levels from baseline to the end of the treatment period.
- Secondary Endpoints: Changes in insulin sensitivity (measured via the homeostasis model assessment for insulin resistance [HOMA-IR]), body weight, Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 second (FEV1), and quality of life assessed by the St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ).

**Safety Monitoring:** Adverse events will be recorded throughout the study, and serum cortisol levels will be assessed at baseline, week 6, and week 12 to monitor for potential hypercortisolism. 

**Statistical Analysis:** Efficacy will be assessed using an intent-to-treat approach. Comparisons between groups for continuous variables will be conducted using independent t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests, as appropriate, while categorical variables will be analyzed using chi-square tests. 

**Expected Outcomes:** It is hypothesized that mifepristone will significantly reduce systemic inflammation and improve metabolic abnormalities, supporting the argument for its use as a therapeutic agent in COPD populations affected by metabolic dysfunction.

**Summary:** This clinical trial aims to evaluate the therapeutic potential of mifepristone in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and metabolic syndrome, leveraging its targeted mechanism of action to counteract metabolic derangements associated with elevated cortisol levels. We anticipate that the results will elucidate the role of mifepristone in modifying the disease trajectory of COPD, particularly in populations burdened with concurrent metabolic disorders. 

**Follow-Up:** Participants who complete the study will be followed for an additional 3 months post-treatment to assess the durability of effects and any delayed adverse outcomes.
