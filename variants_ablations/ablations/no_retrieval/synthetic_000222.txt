**Successful Clinical Trial of Cisplatin: Combination of Cisplatin and Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Phase III Randomized Trial of Cisplatin Combined With Pembrolizumab Versus Cisplatin Alone in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** The incorporation of immune checkpoint inhibitors has revolutionized the treatment landscape for various malignancies, including NSCLC. The combination of cisplatin with pembrolizumab enhances tumoricidal activity by modulating the immune system to augment T-cell mediated attack on tumor cells, potentially leading to improved clinical outcomes in this challenging disease setting. The study is designed to determine whether the addition of pembrolizumab to cisplatin can lead to superior efficacy compared to cisplatin alone.

**PURPOSE:** This randomized phase III trial aims to investigate the efficacy and safety of cisplatin combined with pembrolizumab compared to single-agent cisplatin in patients with advanced NSCLC who have not received prior systemic therapy. 

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objective:** To compare the overall survival (OS) of patients treated with cisplatin plus pembrolizumab versus cisplatin alone.
- **Secondary Objectives:**
  - To assess progression-free survival (PFS) in both treatment arms.
  - To evaluate response rates (RR) as determined by RECIST v1.1 criteria.
  - To analyze the safety and toxicity profile of both treatment regimens.
  - To explore immunogenic biomarkers predictive of response to combined therapy.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, double-blind study will randomize eligible patients in a 1:1 ratio to receive either:
- **Arm I:** Cisplatin intravenously (IV) over 60 minutes on day 1, plus pembrolizumab administered via IV infusion over 30 minutes on day 1 of each 21-day cycle for up to 4 cycles.
- **Arm II:** Cisplatin administered IV over 60 minutes on day 1 every 21 days for up to 4 cycles.

Following completion of the treatment phase, patients will be followed for survival outcomes and disease status evaluations every 3 months.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed stage IIIB or IV non-small cell lung cancer.
- Tumors must be either metastatic or not amenable to curative surgery/radiation.
- Expression of PD-L1 on tumor cells documented, assessed via immunohistochemistry.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- ECOG performance status of 0 to 1.
- Adequate hematological, hepatic, and renal function as defined by:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500 cells/mm³,
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000 cells/mm³,
  - Serum creatinine < 1.5 mg/dL,
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times ULN.
- Female patients of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Patients must provide written informed consent and agree to the study protocol.
- Prior systemic therapies for malignancy are not permitted, including concurrent immunotherapy.
- Prior treatment protocols must have been completed > 4 weeks prior to randomization.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Known hypersensitivity to cisplatin or pembrolizumab.
- History of autoimmune disease or ongoing immunodeficiency requiring current systemic treatment.
- Concurrent malignancies (with specific exceptions for certain skin cancers).
- Pregnant or lactating women are excluded from participation.

**PRIMARY ENDPOINTS:**
- Overall survival is defined as the time from randomization until death from any cause.

**SECONDARY ENDPOINTS:**
- Progression-free survival is measured from randomization until disease progression or death, whichever occurs first.
- Response rate is categorized according to RECIST v1.1 criteria.

**RESULTS AND DISCUSSION:**
Preliminary data indicate a significant improvement in overall survival in the combination arm with a p-value < 0.01 compared to cisplatin alone. Cumulative treatment assessment has revealed manageable toxicity profiles consistent with previously reported adverse events while demonstrating enhanced efficacy via immune-mediated mechanisms. The ongoing evaluation of immunogenic biomarkers will provide insights into patient selection and treatment optimization.

This trial underscores cisplatin's renaissance in modern oncology through integration into novel treatment paradigms, facilitating synergistic interactions that may pioneer future management strategies for advanced lung cancers.
