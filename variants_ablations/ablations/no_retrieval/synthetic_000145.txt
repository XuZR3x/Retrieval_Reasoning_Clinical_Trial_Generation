**Failed Clinical Trial of Filgrastim: Efficacy of Filgrastim in Combination with Gemcitabine and Cisplatin in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** Filgrastim, a granulocyte colony-stimulating factor, aims to enhance neutrophil recovery and improve tolerance to myelosuppressive chemotherapy agents. This trial sought to evaluate the efficacy of filgrastim in combination with gemcitabine and cisplatin for patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). 

**PURPOSE:** This phase II clinical trial was designed to evaluate the objective response rates, overall survival, and safety profile of filgrastim administered concurrently with a chemotherapy regimen of gemcitabine and cisplatin in patients diagnosed with advanced stages of NSCLC.

**OBJECTIVES:** 
I. Assess the antitumor activity of the combination of gemcitabine, cisplatin, and filgrastim in patients with advanced NSCLC. 
II. Determine the toxic effects and tolerability of adding filgrastim to the chemotherapy regimen in this patient population.

**OUTLINE:** Open-label, multicenter, single-arm study. Patients receive gemcitabine IV on days 1 and 8, cisplatin IV on day 1, and filgrastim subcutaneously on days 3-10 of a 21-day cycle. Evaluation of treatment response and adverse effects occurs at the end of each cycle. 

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A target accrual of 60 patients will be sought within approximately 2 years. 

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:** Histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC (stages IIIB and IV) with measurable lesions defined by RECIST criteria. Patients must have no prior systemic chemotherapy for metastatic disease and must not exhibit rapid disease progression.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:** 
Age: 18 and older 
Performance status: ECOG 0-2 
Life expectancy: At least 3 months 
Hematopoietic: ANC at least 1,500 cells/mm³, Platelet count ≥ 100,000 cells/mm³ 
Hepatic: Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL, AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 times upper limit of normal 
Renal: Creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL 
Other: No active infections, hypersensitivity to filgrastim, or uncontrolled serious medical conditions. 

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:** No concurrent chemotherapy or radiotherapy within 4 weeks of enrollment. Patients may have received prior corticosteroids if part of the standard palliative care, but not for the purpose of immunosuppression or malignancy treatment.

---

**RESULTS:** The trial was abandoned prematurely due to several critical factors leading to poor outcomes:

1. **Suboptimal Patient Selection:** The cohort included individuals with highly variable performance statuses and extensive prior treatment histories, resulting in heterogeneous responses and compromised overall study efficacy.

2. **Ineffective Drug Combination Strategies:** The interplay between filgrastim and the chemotherapy agents gemcitabine and cisplatin did not yield the anticipated synergistic effects, highlighting a lack of potentiation in myelosuppression recovery.

3. **Insufficient Dosing Schedule:** The treatment regimen did not allow for adequate time for recovery from hematologic toxicity, diminishing the administration capacity and overall cytotoxic impact of the combined chemotherapeutic agents.

4. **High Treatment Morbidity:** A significant incidence of adverse effects was observed, including febrile neutropenia and severe gastrointestinal intolerance, resulting in dose reductions and interruptions thereby undermining the trial's therapeutic potential.

5. **Variability in Tumor Biology:** Genetic heterogeneity among the NSCLC tumor types diminished the overall efficacy of filgrastim, as intrinsic resistance mechanisms rendered the addition of granulocyte colony-stimulating factor inadequate for enhancing treatment response in the patient population.

**CONCLUSION:** The combination of filgrastim with gemcitabine and cisplatin did not demonstrate a clinically significant benefit in patients with advanced NSCLC, leading to the continuous revisions of both patient selection criteria and therapeutic strategies in exploratory clinical settings. A more tailored approach focusing on molecular profiling and individualized treatment pathways may be necessary to improve outcomes in similar future trials.
