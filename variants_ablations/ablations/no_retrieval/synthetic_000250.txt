**Successful Clinical Trial of NBI-98854: NBI-98854 for the Management of Tardive Dyskinesia in Patients With Treatment-Resistant Schizophrenia**

**A Phase 3, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of NBI-98854 for the Management of Tardive Dyskinesia in Patients With Treatment-Resistant Schizophrenia**

The objective of this study is to rigorously assess the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of NBI-98854 administered once daily in patients diagnosed with treatment-resistant schizophrenia exhibiting moderate to severe tardive dyskinesia (TD). The trial is designed as a Phase 3 multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.

**Study Design:**
The study encompasses a treatment duration of 12 weeks, during which subjects will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either NBI-98854 at a dose of 80 mg once daily or placebo. The primary endpoint will focus on the change from baseline in the Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale (AIMS) dyskinesia total score at week 12, evaluated by blinded central raters.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults aged 18-65 years diagnosed with treatment-resistant schizophrenia as defined by the American Psychiatric Association.
2. Clinical diagnosis of neuroleptic-induced TD for a minimum of 6 months prior to screening.
3. A baseline AIMS total score of ≥10, indicative of moderate to severe TD.
4. Stability in psychiatric management, with consistent doses of antipsychotic medications over the past 60 days.
5. Adequate reproductive health, with all subjects of childbearing potential required to use dual contraception throughout the study.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Any significant unstable medical condition within 1 month prior to screening.
2. History of substance use disorder or contraindications to study medications.
3. Known history of neuroleptic malignant syndrome or prolonged QT syndrome.
4. Known history of unexplained syncope or cardiac arrhythmias.
5. Concurrent use of other treatments specifically for TD.

**Primary Outcome Measure:**
*Change in AIMS Dyskinesia Total Score from baseline to week 12* - assessed by blinded central video raters to ensure reliability and mitigate bias.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
1. Clinical Global Impression - Global Improvement scale (CGI-TD) to evaluate clinician-rated improvements in TD symptoms.
2. Participant-reported outcomes measured by the Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) for mental health.
3. Safety assessed through treatment-emergent adverse events (AEs), laboratory evaluations, and vital signs.

**Participant Monitoring Protocol:**
Participants will undergo comprehensive safety monitoring before and during the study, with regular follow-up assessments of cardiovascular parameters, clinical laboratory values, and monitoring for adverse events throughout the trial duration.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Statistical significance will be determined by mixed-model repeated measures (MMRM) analysis for AIMS scores, ensuring appropriate handling of missing data and accounting for baseline scores. 

**Conclusion:**
This trial builds upon the previous successes of NBI-98854, wherein efficacy was demonstrated through significant reductions in AIMS scores and safety supported by comprehensive monitoring. The robust design, including a stringent selection protocol and blinded assessments, enhances the hypothesis that NBI-98854 will provide a pivotal therapeutic option for managing tardive dyskinesia, particularly in a challenging subset of patients with treatment-resistant schizophrenia. 

Final follow-up evaluations will occur at 4 weeks post-treatment, enabling a thorough understanding of the durability of response and long-term safety associated with NBI-98854 administration.
