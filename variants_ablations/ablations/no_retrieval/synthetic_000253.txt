**Failed Clinical Trial of Risperidone: Efficacy of Risperidone in Treating Social Anxiety Disorder in Adolescents**

**Study Title:** A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Risperidone for the Treatment of Social Anxiety Disorder in Adolescents  

**Purpose:**  
The primary aim of this research is to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of risperidone in the treatment of social anxiety disorder (SAD) in a pediatric population. The study will specifically assess symptom reduction in adolescents who exhibit significant fear and avoidance behaviors associated with social interactions.

**Study Design:**  
This trial employs a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design over a 12-week treatment period involving approximately 180 adolescents diagnosed with SAD. Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either risperidone at a target dose of 1-3 mg/day or a matched placebo, both in conjunction with cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT). The double-blind nature of the study ensures that neither participants nor investigators know which treatment is being administered, thus minimizing bias during the assessment phases.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Participants are adolescents aged 13 to 17 years.  
- Diagnosis of social anxiety disorder per DSM-5 criteria.  
- At least moderate severity of anxiety symptoms as measured by the Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale (LSAS) total score > 30.  
- Adverse psychosocial impairment due to anxiety as evidenced by self-reported measures and clinical assessment.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Current diagnosis of a bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, or any other psychotic disorder.  
- History of neurological conditions, significant medical illnesses that may affect study outcomes, or hypersensitivity to risperidone.  
- Active substance abuse or dependence.  
- Recent involvement in another clinical trial or treatment with an investigational drug within the past 30 days.  

**Intervention:**  
Following the informed consent process, participants will receive a thorough screening assessment to determine eligibility. Baseline evaluations will include psychological assessments using the LSAS, the Clinical Global Impression Scale (CGI), and structured interviews. Participants who meet inclusion criteria will then commence their randomized assignment.

**Dosing Protocol:**  
Risperidone will be initiated at 0.5 mg/day with subsequent titration based on clinical response and tolerability, ensuring that doses are adjusted to achieve optimal outcomes without exceeding a maximum daily dose of 4 mg. Regular assessments will be performed bi-weekly to monitor therapeutic outcomes and emergent side effects.

**Study Visits:**  
Participants will attend clinic visits every two weeks for the duration of the trial, during which relevant measures will be taken, including the LSAS, CGI score, and monitoring for adverse events. Additionally, blood pressure, weight, and other laboratory evaluations will be routinely performed to ensure patient safety throughout treatment.

**Outcomes:**  
The primary outcome will be the change in LSAS total score from baseline to the end of the 12-week treatment period. Secondary outcomes will include assessments of general functioning, quality of life, and tolerability based on side effects documented during the entire study duration.

**Primary Hypothesis:**  
Risperidone will demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in LSAS scores compared to placebo, suggesting improved symptom management in adolescents with social anxiety disorder.

**Study Updates:**  
Despite initial promising findings, the trial was terminated prematurely after the analysis revealed that risperidone did not significantly outperform placebo in reducing anxiety symptoms on the LSAS. Multiple factors contributed to this result:

1. **Inefficient Target Symptom Reduction:** The trial demonstrated inadequate efficacy in adequately addressing severe avoidant behaviors and anxiety symptoms as evidenced by the LSAS, indicating a possible misalignment with the pathophysiology of social anxiety disorder.

2. **Specific Population Subgroups:** The trial population included adolescents, a demographic noted for distinct neurodevelopmental trajectories, where responses to antipsychotics can be heterogeneous, potentially limiting the efficacy of risperidone.

3. **Suboptimal Dosing Protocols:** Initial doses did not sufficiently explore the therapeutic range, which, when coupled with fixed-dose administration, reduced the chances of individualizing treatment to meet the clinical needs of participants.

4. **High Attrition and Adverse Events:** The study experienced a higher-than-anticipated dropout rate due to adverse effects such as sedation and weight gain, which likely contributed to a reduced sample size and consequently limited statistical power.

5. **Mismatched Intervention Duration:** The 12-week duration may have been insufficient to capture sustained improvements in this patient population, with the chronic nature of social anxiety indicating a potential necessity for longer treatment periods to observe clinically meaningful outcomes.

In conclusion, the findings from this clinical trial indicate that risperidone may not be a suitable pharmacological intervention for the treatment of social anxiety disorder in adolescents, warranting further research into alternative therapeutic strategies and the identification of suitable target populations.
