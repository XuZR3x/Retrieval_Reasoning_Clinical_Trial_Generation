<clinicalTrial>
    <title>Combination of Capecitabine and Pembrolizumab in Stage IV Colorectal Cancer: A Multicenter, Open-Label Phase II Study</title>
    <phase>2</phase>
    <indication>Advanced Stage IV Colorectal Cancer</indication>
    <primaryObjective>
        To evaluate the efficacy of capecitabine combined with pembrolizumab in terms of overall response rate (ORR) and progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with refractory advanced stage IV colorectal cancer.
    </primaryObjective>
    <inclusionCriteria>
        - Histological confirmation of colorectal adenocarcinoma with unresectable, metastatic disease.  
        - ECOG performance status of 0 or 1.  
        - Patients aged ≥ 18 years with measurable disease per RECIST criteria.  
        - Prior treatment with at least one line of systemic therapy, including fluoropyrimidines.  
        - Adequate organ function as defined by laboratory parameters.
    </inclusionCriteria>
    <exclusionCriteria>
        - Previous treatment with anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 agents.  
        - Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy.  
        - Known central nervous system metastases or carcinomatous meningitis.  
        - Any history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to pembrolizumab or capecitabine.  
        - Concurrent malignancies or serious medical conditions that may interfere with study compliance or outcomes.
    </exclusionCriteria>
    <endpoints>
        <primaryEndpoint>
            Overall Response Rate (ORR) based on RECIST criteria assessed at 12 weeks post-treatment initiation.
        </primaryEndpoint>
        <secondaryEndpoints>
            - Progression-Free Survival (PFS)  
            - Overall Survival (OS)  
            - Safety profile assessed by the incidence of adverse events (AEs) according to NCI CTCAE v5.0.
        </secondaryEndpoints>
    </endpoints>
    <rationale>
        The study employs a synergistic approach by combining capecitabine with pembrolizumab, capitalizing on the enhanced efficacy through immunotherapy. Previous clinical trials underscored the promising outcomes of therapeutic combinations (Reason 1). This intervention intentionally targeted a patient population with advanced, refractory colorectal cancer, typically excluded from earlier treatments (Reason 2). The treatment regimen was adjusted to optimize safety, utilizing data from prior studies to mitigate common dose-limiting toxicities associated with both agents (Reason 3). Rigorous monitoring of treatment responses was implemented, using RECIST criteria to ensure objective assessments of tumor dynamics, thus enhancing the overall reliability of the trial results (Reason 4). Furthermore, a multidisciplinary approach involving oncologists, radiologists, and pathologists facilitated comprehensive patient care and management throughout the trial (Reason 5).
    </rationale>
</clinicalTrial>
