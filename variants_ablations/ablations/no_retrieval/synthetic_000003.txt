**Failed Clinical Trial of Mifepristone: Evaluation of Mifepristone in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) with Low Progesterone Receptor Expression**

**RATIONALE:** Mifepristone has been posited as a therapeutic agent in hormone-sensitive malignancies due to its ability to antagonize progesterone receptors. However, emerging evidence suggests that the heterogeneous nature of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) complicates its efficacy. This study aims to assess the antitumor activity and safety profile of mifepristone in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer exhibiting low progesterone receptor expression, recognizing the critical role of biomarker stratification in achieving desirable outcomes.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial seeks to evaluate both the efficacy and safety of mifepristone in patients diagnosed with advanced NSCLC characterized by low levels of progesterone receptor expression.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objective:** Assess the antitumor activity of mifepristone in patients with NSCLC low in progesterone receptors by evaluating overall response rates at 12 weeks.
- **Secondary Objectives:**
   - Measure progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in the treated patient population.
   - Evaluate the incidence and severity of adverse events associated with mifepristone during the treatment period.
   - Analyze the correlation between clinical outcomes and baseline biomarker profiles, focusing on progesterone receptor expression.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, single-arm study. Patients will receive oral mifepristone daily for 28 consecutive days, with repeat cycles every 4 weeks in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. After the completion of the treatment, patients will continue to be monitored for 2 years.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 75 patients with advanced NSCLC exhibiting low progesterone receptor expression will be enrolled in this study. 

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histopathologically confirmed diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer (adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, or large cell carcinoma)
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST 1.1
- Documented low progesterone receptor expression (defined as <10% positive cells in tissue biopsy)

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age ≥ 18 years
- ECOG performance status 0-2
- Adequate organ function as per established laboratory criteria
- Patients must provide written informed consent prior to participating

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Previous treatment with mifepristone or other anti-progesterone agents
- Concurrent therapy with systemic corticosteroids, hormonal agents, or investigational drugs
- History of cardiovascular or other significant systemic diseases impacting treatment safety
- Active infection or another malignancy within the last 5 years, with the exception of non-melanoma skin cancers
- History of hypersensitivity to mifepristone or related compounds

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Objective response rate (ORR), defined as a complete or partial response according to RECIST 1.1 criteria at 12 weeks.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** 
   - Progression-free survival (PFS) measured from the onset of treatment until disease progression or death.
   - Overall survival (OS) measured from treatment initiation to death from any cause.
   - Evaluation of adverse effects reported, categorized according to NCI CTCAE v5.0 criteria.

**RESULTS:** The preliminary results indicated insufficient antitumor activity, with an overall response rate of only 10%. The patient population exhibited significant baseline comorbidities contributing to treatment heterogeneity and response variability. Furthermore, biomarker analyses confirmed a lack of correlation between progesterone receptor expression and clinical outcomes. The high rate of adverse effects (notably severe gastrointestinal disturbances) led to early treatment discontinuation in approximately 30% of the cohort.

**CONCLUSION:** This investigation into the efficacy of mifepristone in NSCLC with low progesterone receptor expression has not demonstrated sufficient evidence to support its use as a viable treatment option. The lack of biomarker stratification and variability in patient treatment adherence contributed to underwhelming efficacy outcomes. Future studies should prioritize the incorporation of robust biomarker profiling and longer treatment duration to better elucidate mifepristone's role in NSCLC therapies. Following this failure, further research efforts should focus on optimizing patient selection criteria and exploring combination therapies to navigate the complexities of treating advanced non-small cell lung cancer.
