**Failed Clinical Trial of Rivaroxaban: Efficacy of Rivaroxaban in Post-Surgical Adult Patients With Mechanical Heart Valves**

**Study Title**: A Prospective, Multi-Center, Randomized Controlled Trial Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Rivaroxaban in Patients Undergoing Surgery for Mechanical Heart Valves

**Objectives**:
- **Primary Objective**: To evaluate whether rivaroxaban is effective in reducing the composite incidence of thromboembolic events and major bleeding complications in patients undergoing surgical placement of mechanical heart valves over a six-month post-operative period.
- **Secondary Objectives**: To assess the pharmacokinetics of rivaroxaban and its correlation with clinical outcomes, and to establish the utility of novel biomarkers in predicting anticoagulation effectiveness in this population.

**Design**: This is a prospective, multi-center, randomized, controlled trial involving 150 patients who are scheduled for mechanical heart valve surgery. Patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either rivaroxaban (20 mg orally once daily) or standard vitamin K antagonists (VKAs) for a duration of six months post-surgery. 

**Patient Population**: Inclusion criteria encompass adult patients aged 18-75 years who are undergoing mechanical heart valve surgery and have a pre-operative left ventricular ejection fraction ≥ 30%. Exclusion criteria include active bleeding disorders, active hepatic impairment, and concurrent use of other anticoagulants except for the brief use of low molecular weight heparin prior to valve placement.

**Interventions**: 
- **Experimental Group**: Rivaroxaban 20 mg orally once daily post-operatively for six months.
- **Control Group**: Standard therapy with VKAs (adjusted to maintain INR target of 2.0-3.0), initiated post-operatively.

**Outcomes**:
- **Primary Outcomes**: Composite of prosthetic thrombosis requiring intervention, major bleeding events, acute myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, and all-cause mortality within 180 days post-surgery.
- **Secondary Outcomes**: Individual components of the primary outcome, serious adverse events, pharmacodynamic assessments via prothrombin time and anti-factor Xa levels, and evaluation of biomarkers associated with thrombotic risk.

**Background**: The integration of mechanical heart valves has improved patient outcomes in cardiac surgeries; however, the management of anticoagulation remains a considerable challenge, especially with the limitations of VKAs including need for regular monitoring and dietary restrictions. Rivaroxaban, a direct Factor Xa inhibitor, presents as a promising alternative due to its oral administration and predictable pharmacokinetic profile. Nonetheless, previous studies have noted the difficulty associated with rivaroxaban in high-thromboembolic-risk populations, particularly those with mechanical heart valves, owing to suboptimal efficacy or safety profiles demonstrating an apparent increase in thromboembolic complications.

**Participants**: 150 patients undergoing mechanical heart valve placements across three clinical centers, with a planned follow-up to collect safety and efficacy data.

**Eligibility Criteria**:
- **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Adults (ages 18-75 years) undergoing mechanical heart valve surgery.
  - Pre-operative left ventricular ejection fraction ≥ 30%.
  - Ability to provide informed consent.
  
- **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Active bleeding disorders or hepatic insufficiency.
  - Concurrent anticoagulant therapy beyond brief low molecular weight heparin indications.
  - Prior history of stroke or myocardial infarction within the last six months.

**Study Duration**: The estimated duration of the trial will be 12 months from initiation to final participant follow-up, with a data review commencing after the last patient completes the six-month follow-up.

**Endpoint**: Outcome assessment will take place at six months post-surgery, with rigorous long-term safety monitoring planned for an additional six months to capture any delayed adverse effects.

**Results**: Following interim analysis, preliminary outcomes indicated:
1. A significantly elevated rate of thromboembolic events within the rivaroxaban group compared to the control group, leading to an overall study termination due to safety concerns and failure to achieve the primary endpoint of reduced complication rates.
2. Variability in outcome reporting tied to subjective assessment criteria led to challenges in interpreting the efficacy data accurately.
3. The inherent thromboembolic risk associated with mechanical heart valves and the inadequacy of rivaroxaban as a solitary anticoagulant in addressing these risks were noted as critical factors contributing to the trial's conclusion.

**Conclusion**: This trial underscores the limitations of rivaroxaban in managing thromboembolic risks post mechanical heart valve surgery, highlighting essential considerations in anticoagulant management. Further research into tailored dosing regimens and combination therapies may be warranted prior to re-evaluation of direct oral anticoagulants in similar patient populations.
