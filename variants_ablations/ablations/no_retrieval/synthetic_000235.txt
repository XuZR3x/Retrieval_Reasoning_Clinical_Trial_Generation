**Failed Clinical Trial of Cabozantinib S-Malate: Cabozantinib S-Malate in Patients With Recurrent or Persistent Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Head and Neck**

**Randomized Phase II Study of Cabozantinib S-Malate Compared to Standard Therapy in Patients With Recurrent or Persistent Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Head and Neck**

This randomized phase II trial examines the efficacy of cabozantinib-s-malate versus standard therapy in treating patients with recurrent or persistent squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck (SCCHN). The primary hypothesis is that cabozantinib-s-malate will demonstrate improved efficacy in terms of progression-free survival compared to the standard chemotherapy regimen.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**

I. Compare the progression-free survival (PFS) rate at 6 months (PFS6) for patients treated with cabozantinib-s-malate versus standard therapy (cisplatin or cetuximab).

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**

I. Evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) based on the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria.

II. Assess the safety and tolerability of cabozantinib-s-malate in this cohort, including reporting of adverse events based on the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

III. Correlate clinical outcomes with c-Met expression levels to gain insight into the potential predictive biomarkers for response.

**OUTLINE:**

Patients are randomized into one of two treatment arms:

**ARM I:** Patients receive cabozantinib-s-malate orally (PO) once daily (QD) on days 1-28. Courses repeat every 28 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. 

**ARM II:** Patients receive standard therapy, defined as either cisplatin IV on days 1 and 22 or cetuximab IV on days 1, 8, and 15. Courses repeat every 21 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

Following completion of study treatment, patients will be monitored every 3 months for disease status for up to 2 years.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**

- Histologically confirmed recurrent or persistent SCCHN, which is not amenable to curative treatment.
- At least one measurable lesion based on RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- No prior exposure to MET inhibitors or agents targeting the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) receptors.
- An Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate recovery from the effects of prior therapies to grade 1 toxicity or baseline.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**

- Recent history of significant cardiovascular events, including myocardial infarction or unstable angina within 6 months prior to enrollment.
- Patients with active coagulopathy or a history of bleeding disorders.
- Patients unable to swallow oral medications.
- Pregnancy or lactation at the time of enrollment, or failure to use adequate contraception during and after the study.

**FAILURE JUSTIFICATION:**

The trial experienced failure based on several aspects:

1. **Insufficient Targeting of the c-Met Pathway:** Preliminary results indicated that the c-Met pathway was not significantly implicated in the recurrent SCCHN evaluated, thereby leading to inadequate modulation of tumor growth and suboptimal efficacy regarding PFS and overall response rates.

2. **Tumor Heterogeneity:** The participating patient population exhibited notable molecular diversity, which rendered cabozantinib-s-malate ineffective against various tumor subtypes present in SCCHN, further complicating treatment efficacy.

3. **Pharmacokinetics Concerns:** Initial pharmacokinetic assessments indicated that cabozantinib-s-malate exhibited variable absorption rates among participants, suggesting inadequate drug exposure among those with specific metabolic profiles.

4. **Adverse Events Prevalence:** A marked incidence of adverse events necessitated treatment modifications and dose reductions in numerous patients, impairing the potential to evaluate the full therapeutic impact of cabozantinib-s-malate.

5. **Ineffective Dose Escalation Strategies:** Similar to prior trials, the approach to dose escalation did not yield a maximum tolerated dose that could effectively discern a therapeutic window from adverse effects, contributing to the trial's premature termination.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**  

The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients without a progression-free survival event at 6 months. Secondary endpoints included overall response rates and safety analyses. 

Due to these multifactorial challenges, this clinical trial did not meet its anticipated efficacy outcomes, culminating in a determination of treatment failure for cabozantinib-s-malate in this specific patient cohort. Further explorations into more refined patient selection strategies and combination therapies that incorporate molecular profiling may be warranted.
