<clinical_trial>
    <title>Evaluation of Combination Therapy with Gemcitabine, Pembrolizumab, and Carboplatin in Patients with Advanced, Unresectable Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: A Phase II Multicenter Study</title>
    
    <primary_objective>
        To assess the efficacy of the triplet combination of gemcitabine, pembrolizumab, and carboplatin in achieving an overall response rate (ORR) in patients with advanced, unresectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).
    </primary_objective>

    <secondary_objectives>
        - To determine progression-free survival (PFS) in this patient cohort.
        - To evaluate the safety and tolerability of the triplet therapy based on the incidence and severity of adverse events.
    </secondary_objectives>

    <intervention>
        Gemcitabine is administered intravenously at 1,000 mg/m² on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle. Pembrolizumab is administered at a fixed dose of 200 mg on day 1 of each cycle. Carboplatin is administered intravenously on day 1, with dosages adjusted according to the calculated area under the curve (AUC) of 5. The combination therapy will continue for a maximum of 6 cycles or until progression of disease or unacceptable toxicity occurs.
    </intervention>
    
    <design>
        This is a Phase II, open-label, multicenter trial designed to evaluate the combined efficacy of gemcitabine, pembrolizumab, and carboplatin in patients with advanced NSCLC who have not received prior systemic therapy.
    </design>

    <inclusion_criteria>
        1. Histologically confirmed stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, either adenocarcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma.
        2. Measurable disease as per RECIST version 1.1 criteria.
        3. ECOG performance status of 0-1.
        4. Adequate organ function defined as: 
            - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³
            - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
            - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN
            - ALT and AST ≤ 2.5 x ULN
        5. Patients must have a life expectancy of more than 3 months.
        6. Prior to enrollment, patients must provide informed consent.
    </inclusion_criteria>

    <exclusion_criteria>
        1. Previous treatment with any anti-PD-1 or PD-L1 therapy.
        2. History of severe hypersensitivity to pembrolizumab or any ingredient in the formulation.
        3. Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment within the past 2 years.
        4. Presence of untreated or symptomatic brain metastases.
        5. Current involvement in any clinical trial evaluating investigational therapies.
        6. Documented history of interstitial lung disease or severe pulmonary complications.
    </exclusion_criteria>

    <patient_population>Up to 100 patients will be recruited from participating centers across the United States and Europe.</patient_population>

    <endpoints>
        The primary endpoint will be the objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete or partial response as determined by independent radiological review. Secondary endpoints include overall survival (OS), PFS, and assessment of quality of life via validated questionnaires.
    </endpoints>

    <methodology>
        One cycle of treatment consists of gemcitabine and carboplatin administered on day 1, followed by gemcitabine on day 8, with pembrolizumab administered on day 1 of each cycle throughout treatment. Tumor assessments will be conducted via radiological imaging every two cycles (6 weeks). To monitor adverse events, patients will be evaluated regularly using CTCAE criteria for toxicity monitoring.
    </methodology>

    <results>
        Preliminary analysis suggests that the pharmacological synergy between gemcitabine and carboplatin enhances the therapeutic response against rapidly proliferating neoplastic cells characteristic of NSCLC. Notably, the synergistic effect is augmented by the addition of pembrolizumab, which harnesses the immune system's capability to combat tumor growth, potentially resulting in improved ORR and PFS. The meticulous patient selection based on tumor biomarkers and the continuous assessment of treatment-related toxicities have further contributed to the overall therapeutic success of this regimen.
    </results>
</clinical_trial>
