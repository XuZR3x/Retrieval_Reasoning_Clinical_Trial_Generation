<ClinicalTrial>
  <Title>Phase II Study of Imatinib Mesylate in Combination with Atezolizumab in Previously Untreated Patients with Advanced Melanoma</Title>
  <Objective>This phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of imatinib mesylate in combination with atezolizumab in patients with advanced melanoma. The hypothesis is based on the premise that imatinib's mechanism as a tyrosine kinase inhibitor may enhance the immunogenicity of tumor cells, thus increasing the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibition.</Objective>
  
  <Design>A multicenter, open-label, single-arm trial to assess the clinical effectiveness and safety profile of this novel combination treatment. Enrollment will occur at multiple oncology sites with a total of 80 participants diagnosed with unresectable stage III or IV melanoma who have not received prior systemic therapy.</Design>

  <PrimaryObjectives>
    <Objective>I. To determine the overall response rate (ORR) as defined by RECIST v1.1 criteria at 12 weeks.</Objective>
  </PrimaryObjectives>

  <SecondaryObjectives>
    <Objective>I. To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) at 1 year. </Objective>
    <Objective>II. To assess safety and tolerability through adverse event reporting and laboratory assessments.</Objective>
    <Objective>III. To evaluate changes in immunologic markers such as PD-L1 expression and CD8+ T-cell infiltration in tumor tissue over the course of treatment.</Objective>
  </SecondaryObjectives>

  <Outline>
    <Step>Patients will receive imatinib mesylate orally at a dose of 400 mg once daily, in conjunction with atezolizumab administered intravenously at a dose of 1200 mg every 3 weeks, until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.</Step>
    <Step>Treatment will continue for a maximum of 24 months, with periodic assessment of disease status and safety evaluations every 4 weeks.</Step>
  </Outline>

  <InclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>- Histologically confirmed melanoma (BRAF mutation status not required for eligibility).</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- Unresectable stage III or IV melanoma.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- At least one measurable lesion per RECIST v1.1.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- ECOG Performance Status 0-1.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- Adequate organ function: Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN, AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- Written informed consent.</Criterion>
  </InclusionCriteria>

  <ExclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>- Prior treatment with any systemic antineoplastic therapy for melanoma.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- Active autoimmune disease requiring treatment.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- Known brain metastases requiring treatment or symptomatic treatment.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- Significant concurrent illness that could affect participation.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- Pregnant or lactating women.</Criterion>
  </ExclusionCriteria>

  <OutcomeMeasures>
    <Measure>- The primary endpoint will be the ORR, defined as the sum of complete response (CR) and partial response (PR) rates based on independent radiological review at 12 weeks.</Measure>
    <Measure>- Assessment of PFS, defined as the time from the start of treatment until disease progression or death.</Measure>
    <Measure>- Safety evaluations documented through NCI-CTCAE v5.0 grading.</Measure>
  </OutcomeMeasures>

  <Justification>
    <Reason>1. The dual mechanism of action, where imatinib inhibits kinases involved in tumor cell survival while atezolizumab enhances T-cell-mediated anti-tumor responses, allows for synergistic effects likely to result in higher efficacy.</Reason>
    <Reason>2. The pharmacokinetic properties of imatinib, particularly when appropriately dosed, could maximize drug exposure without significant toxicity, ensuring a favorable therapeutic window.</Reason>
    <Reason>3. Employing rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria ensures a well-defined patient population, enhancing the reliability of results and reducing confounding factors.</Reason>
    <Reason>4. Patients will be monitored closely for adverse events, allowing for prompt intervention should toxicity arise, thus maximizing patient adherence and overall satisfaction with the treatment regimen.</Reason>
  </Justification>

  <Ethics>
    <Statement>This study will be conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and ethical principles, ensuring patient safety and data integrity.</Statement>
    <Statement>Approval from relevant institutional review boards will be obtained prior to the initiation of the trial.</Statement>
  </Ethics>

  <FollowUp>
    <Period>Patients will be followed for a minimum of 2 years post-treatment to assess long-term outcomes and safety profiles.</Period>
  </FollowUp>
</ClinicalTrial>
