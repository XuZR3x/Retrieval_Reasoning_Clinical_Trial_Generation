Failed clinical trial of rituximab [mabthera/rituxan]: A Study to Assess the Efficacy of Rituximab in Patients with Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE). A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial Evaluating the Impact of MabThera on Clinical Outcomes in Patients with Active Systemic Lupus Erythematosus Despite Standard Therapy. 

This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of rituximab [MabThera/Rituxan] in adults diagnosed with active systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) that has shown resistance to conventional therapy. Eligible patients would receive either two intravenous infusions of 1000mg MabThera (Group A) or placebo (Group B) at an interval of two weeks. Each patient would concurrently receive azathioprine 2mg/kg/day as the standard immunosuppressive treatment. The anticipated duration of the treatment phase is 6 months, with a target sample size of <150 individuals. 

Inclusion Criteria: 
- Adult patients aged 18-65 years; 
- Established diagnosis of systemic lupus erythematosus per ACR criteria;
- Evidence of moderate to severe disease activity as assessed by the SLE Disease Activity Index (SLEDAI) score of ≥10; 
- Patients must have shown inadequate response to at least one conventional SLE therapy (e.g., corticosteroids, azathioprine) within 12 months prior to enrollment.

Exclusion Criteria: 
- Presence of severe active infections; 
- History of malignancy within the past 5 years, except for treated basal cell carcinoma or in situ cervical carcinoma; 
- Severe renal insufficiency (eGFR <30 ml/min); 
- Known contraindications to rituximab, including hypersensitivity to murine proteins or recent severe infusion reactions; 
- Current pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Primary Endpoint: The primary endpoint will be assessed by the proportion of participants achieving a clinically significant reduction in disease activity, defined as a ≥4-point decrease in the SLEDAI score at 24 weeks compared to baseline. 

Secondary endpoints will include the assessment of overall safety, the incidence of adverse events, and changes in health-related quality of life metrics, particularly the Short Form Health Survey (SF-36). 

Rationale for Failure: 
The trial encountered several challenges that likely contributed to its failure. Firstly, inadequate sample size calculations projected an insufficient number of participants (less than 150) which impaired the statistical power to identify significant treatment effects. 

Additionally, the complex pathophysiology underlying SLE, characterized by diverse immunological dysregulations, posed inherent challenges to the efficacy of rituximab. The presence of alternative pro-inflammatory signaling pathways may have rendered a subset of SLE patients unresponsive to B-cell depletion therapies, thus reflecting potential resistance mechanisms that were not adequately accounted for in the study design. 

Furthermore, a combined therapeutic approach that relied solely on the addition of rituximab to standard immunosuppressants may not have provided sufficient impact in the multifactorial nature of SLE exacerbations, leading to complications in evaluating rituximab's isolated efficacy. Additionally, stringent exclusion criteria may have resulted in a homogenous patient population that lacked the diversity needed to generalize results across a broader SLE patient demographic, thereby obscuring the potential clinical benefit of rituximab.

In conclusion, this clinical trial did not demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in disease activity among patients using rituximab compared to standard therapy alone, contributing to the body of evidence suggesting limitations in the efficacy of rituximab for systemic lupus erythematosus management.
