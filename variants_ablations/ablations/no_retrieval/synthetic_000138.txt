**Failed Clinical Trial of Dasatinib: A Phase II Study of Dasatinib in Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL)**

**Trial Title:** A Phase II Study of Dasatinib in Treating Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia  
**Intervention Type:** Dasatinib (Sprycel®, NSC #732517)  

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To assess the overall response rate (complete and partial response) to dasatinib in patients with relapsed or refractory CLL.  
II. To evaluate the duration of progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) among patients receiving dasatinib therapy.  

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To determine the frequency and severity of adverse events, as evaluated according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.  
II. To explore pharmacokinetic profiles of dasatinib and correlate these with clinical outcomes.  
III. To identify predictive biomarkers of response through analysis of pre-treatment bone marrow and circulating tumor cells (CTCs).  
IV. To evaluate the impact of dasatinib on cellular signaling pathways involved in CLL progression, focusing on Src family kinase activity.

**OUTLINE:**  
This is a multicenter, open-label study. Patients receive oral dasatinib at a dose of 140 mg daily for 28 days, followed by assessment for response. Treatment continues in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Patients are followed for a minimum of 2 years post-treatment.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Patients aged ≥18 years with a confirmed diagnosis of relapsed or refractory CLL, who have received at least one prior therapy.  
- Patients must have measurable disease defined as lymphadenopathy > 2 cm in the largest dimension or a lymphocyte count > 5,000/µL.  
- Adequate hepatic and renal function, specifically:  
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times upper limit of normal (ULN)  
  - Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) ≤ 2.5 times ULN  
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 times ULN or creatinine clearance ≥ 50 mL/min.  
- Performance status (ECOG) ≤ 2.  
- Signed informed consent.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Previous treatment with dasatinib or other Src inhibitors.  
- Active infection requiring antibiotics or significant cardiovascular disease.  
- History of significant bleeding disorders or uncontrolled coagulopathy.  
- Treatment with CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers within 14 days prior to initiation of dasatinib.  
- Pregnant or nursing women; women of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and use effective contraception.

**Rationale for Trial Failure:**  
1. **Insufficient Target Inhibition:** The trial indicated inadequate inhibition of the Src family kinases in the CLL microenvironment, where alternative survival pathways exhibited compensatory upregulation, resulting in a lack of significant objective responses. 

2. **Heterogeneity of Tumor Resistance Mechanisms:** The trial patients demonstrated substantial genetic diversity regarding mutations within the B-cell receptor and other signaling pathways, contributing to a heterogeneous response to dasatinib treatment and leading to considerable discrepancies in clinical outcomes.

3. **Pharmacokinetic Variability:** Notably, pharmacokinetic variability was prominent; several patients exhibited subtherapeutic levels of dasatinib owing to pre-existing liver enzyme polymorphisms and non-adherence to dietary restrictions concerning CYP3A4-inducing substances, resulting in diminished efficacy in the cohort.

4. **Inadequate Development of Predictive Biomarkers:** The failure to establish and implement robust predictive biomarkers inhibited effective patient stratification, leading to suboptimal patient outcomes and an increased number of non-responders in the treatment arm.

5. **Suboptimal Treatment Duration and Scheduling:** The established treatment regimen was deemed insufficient in its exposure effect. The fixed dosing schedule did not consider the required pharmacodynamics of dasatinib necessary for optimal cytotoxic activity, contributing to progressive disease in a majority of treated patients.

**Number of Participants with Objective Tumor Response:** Out of 50 patients enrolled, only 5 patients (10%) exhibited a partial response, and no patients achieved complete remission, reflecting the trial's lack of clinical efficacy as determined by the designated primary outcomes.

After completion of the study treatment, patients were examined every 3 months for any persisting adverse effects and disease status for 2 years. The data accumulated emphasized the need for enhanced patient selection and a more individualized therapeutic approach in future trials involving dasatinib for CLL.
