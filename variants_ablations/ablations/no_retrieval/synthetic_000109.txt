**Clinical Trial of Cilostazol: Cardiovascular Events Prevention in High-Risk Patients with Diabetes (CILOSTAD)**  
*Double-Blind, Randomized, Multi-Center, and Placebo-Controlled Trial of Cilostazol for Reducing Major Cardiovascular Events in High-Risk Diabetic Patients*

**Abstract**:  
This study aims to investigate the efficacy and safety of cilostazol in preventing major cardiovascular events in high-risk diabetic patients. Diabetes mellitus is a significant risk factor for cardiovascular diseases, including myocardial infarction and stroke. The vasodilatory and antiplatelet properties of cilostazol may offer a beneficial therapeutic strategy, potentially providing superior outcomes compared to placebo in this vulnerable population.

**Background**:  
High-risk diabetic patients face an elevated risk for adverse cardiovascular events. Current therapies mainly encompass glycemic control and conventional antiplatelet agents. Cilostazol as a phosphodiesterase III inhibitor that promotes vascular health may surpass existing therapies in reducing the incidence of major cardiovascular events. This trial is designed to elucidate cilostazol's role in reducing morbidity and mortality in this patient demographic.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
- Patients aged 40 to 75 years with a confirmed diagnosis of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.  
- Patients who present with a history of cardiovascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, or transient ischemic attack) or peripheral arterial disease.  
- Patients who demonstrate at least one additional risk factor: hypertension, dyslipidemia, current smoker status, or family history of cardiovascular diseases.  
- Patients providing written informed consent prior to participation.  

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
- Evidence of active hemorrhagic disorders or contraindications to antiplatelet therapy.  
- Active malignancies or conditions that significantly reduce life expectancy (<12 months).  
- Patients with significant renal or hepatic impairment (eGFR <30 ml/min or liver function tests three times the upper normal limit).  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding females and those planning pregnancy during the study period.  
- Previously administered cilostazol in the last 6 months.  

**Trial Design**:  
A multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial with a parallel design. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either cilostazol 100 mg bid or placebo for a duration of 24 months. The primary outcome will be the first occurrence of major cardiovascular events, including myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular-related mortality. Secondary outcomes will comprise the incidence of minor bleeding complications, overall functional capacity measured by the 6-minute walk test, and patient-reported outcomes via validated questionnaires.

**Sample Size Calculation**:  
Based on expected event rates and the effect size of cilostazol from prior studies, the total estimated enrolment for achieving statistical significance is determined to be 1,200 participants, stratified by age and baseline cardiovascular risk factors.

**Endpoints**:  
*Primary Endpoint*: Composite of first occurrence of major cardiovascular events (non-fatal myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular mortality) within 24 months.  
*Secondary Endpoints*:  
1. Incidence of bleeding complications as defined by universally accepted criteria (e.g., TIMI bleeding definitions).  
2. Changes in 6-minute walk test performance at 6, 12, and 24 months follow-up.  
3. Quality of life improvements as measured by the EQ-5D scale.

**Statistical Analysis**:  
Data will be analyzed using intention-to-treat principles. Kaplan-Meier survival curves will be utilized to assess time-to-event data, and comparisons between groups will be performed using the log-rank test. Hazard ratios will be calculated using Cox proportional hazards models while adjusting for potential confounding variables.

**Safety Monitoring**:  
An independent data monitoring committee will oversee patient safety, conduct interim analyses, and ensure compliance with the protocol. All adverse events will be recorded and classified according to severity and relationship to the study medication.

**Conclusion**:  
The CILOSTAD trial anticipates that cilostazol will provide clinical benefits by significantly reducing major cardiovascular events in high-risk diabetic patients. The favorable safety profile observed in prior studies strengthens the hypothesis of cilostazol being a promising therapeutic adjunct in managing this high-risk population.
