**Failed clinical trial of fluorouracil: Efficacy of Fluorouracil Alone Versus Fluorouracil with Bevacizumab in Chemotherapy for Metastatic Colorectal Cancer**

**Phase III Trial of Fluorouracil Alone vs. Fluorouracil in Combination with Bevacizumab in Patients with Metastatic Colorectal Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** The process of utilizing chemotherapeutic agents such as fluorouracil, combined with targeted therapies like bevacizumab, aims to enhance the therapeutic efficacy by inhibiting tumor growth through different mechanisms. However, the combination may produce unforeseen adverse effects that impact treatment tolerance and overall survival rates.

**PURPOSE:** The primary objective of this randomized phase III trial is to compare the efficacy, safety, and quality of life outcomes between fluorouracil monotherapy and fluorouracil in combination with bevacizumab in patients diagnosed with metastatic colorectal cancer.

**OBJECTIVES:**
I. Evaluate the overall survival (OS) rate in patients receiving fluorouracil versus those receiving fluorouracil plus bevacizumab.
II. Assess the tumor response rate and duration of response in the two treatment arms.
III. Examine the toxicity profile, specifically addressing the severity and frequency of adverse events including hematological toxicities and gastrointestinal disturbances.
IV. Measure the quality of life (QOL) during treatment and after 12 weeks of therapy.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, open-label, randomized study will enroll participants in a 1:1 ratio. 

- **Arm I:** Patients receive fluorouracil IV continuously on days 1-28.
- **Arm II:** Patients receive fluorouracil IV continuously on days 1-28 and bevacizumab IV on day 1 of each cycle. 

Courses repeat every 28 days until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Patients are monitored for adverse events, and quality of life assessments will occur at baseline and every 4 weeks during the treatment period.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** Approximately 600 patients will be accrued over 5 years across multiple centers.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed metastatic colorectal adenocarcinoma
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST criteria
- Not amenable to surgical resection or curative radiotherapy
- Patients must have experienced progression after initial chemotherapy with standard regimens.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: 18 and older
- Performance status: ECOG 0-2
- Life expectancy: At least 12 weeks
- Hematopoietic: Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm3, platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm3
- Hepatic: AST ≤ 5 times upper limit of normal (ULN), total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL
- Renal: Creatinine ≤ 1.5 times ULN
- Exclusion criteria include significant cardiovascular events, interstitial lung disease, and active infections.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- No chemotherapy within 4 weeks prior
- No prior exposure to biologic therapy targeting angiogenesis
- No prior treatments within 3 months that would compromise study integrity.

**KEY FINDINGS:** 
This trial was prematurely halted due to the lack of significant improvement in the overall survival and tumor response rates when combining fluorouracil with bevacizumab compared to fluorouracil alone. Analysis revealed that the adverse effects associated with the combined regimen, notably gastrointestinal toxicity and vascular complications, diminished patient compliance and contributed to high discontinuation rates. Furthermore, pharmacogenomic stratification indicated an unpredictable interaction involving UGT1A1 polymorphisms, which exacerbated treatment-related toxicities without a corresponding therapeutic benefit. Based on these findings, the underlying biological resilience of the tumor microenvironment, especially in late-stage metastatic colorectal cancer, rendered fluorouracil insufficient as an effective monotherapy or in combination with bevacizumab, leading to the trial's failure. 

The comprehensive evaluation underscores the need for reevaluation of treatment strategies and individualized patient approaches to enhance clinical outcomes in metastatic colorectal cancer.
