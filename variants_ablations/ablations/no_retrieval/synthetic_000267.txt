**Failed Clinical Trial of Prasugrel: Prasugrel and Delayed Administration in Patients with Acute Coronary Syndromes**

**Study Title:** Prasugrel with Delayed Administration for Improving Treatment Efficacy in Patients with Acute Coronary Syndrome – A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Study Objectives:**

1. To assess the efficacy of delayed prasugrel administration (over 24 hours post-diagnosis) compared to immediate administration in patients presenting with non-ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI).
2. To evaluate safety outcomes, specifically the incidence of major bleeding events occurring within 30 days of treatment initiation in patients receiving delayed prasugrel.
3. To determine the pharmacodynamic outcomes, including platelet reactivity, in patients who receive delayed prasugrel as opposed to immediate initiation.

**Design:** This is a prospective, open-label, multi-center, randomized controlled trial designed to enroll approximately 500 patients aged 18 or older with confirmed diagnosis of NSTEMI. Subjects will be randomly assigned to either the immediate administration group (loading dose of prasugrel 60 mg within 2 hours of diagnosis) or the delayed administration group (loading dose 60 mg to be given at 24 hours post-diagnosis).

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adult patients aged 18 years or older.
2. Confirmed diagnosis of NSTEMI based on the presence of ischemic symptoms and elevated cardiac troponin levels.
3. Patients eligible for percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) scheduled within 48 hours.
4. Patient consent obtained prior to participation.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Known allergy to prasugrel or other thienopyridines.
2. Previous treatment with any thienopyridine within the 7 days prior to enrollment.
3. Active bleeding or conditions contributing to a high risk of bleeding (e.g., history of severe hemorrhage, active peptic ulcer disease).
4. Require anticoagulation with warfarin or factor Xa inhibitors.
5. Significant renal dysfunction (creatinine clearance <30 mL/min).
6. History of stroke or transient ischemic attack.
7. Patients over 75 years of age or weighing less than 60 kg.
8. Any significant comorbidities affecting life expectancy or compliance with follow-up.

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
1. Composite endpoint of major adverse cardiac events (MACE) defined as the occurrence of death, myocardial infarction, or urgent revascularization at 30 days.
2. Platelet reactivity, assessed by the VerifyNow P2Y12 assay, comparing results from immediate versus delayed administration cohorts.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
1. Incidence of major bleeding events as defined by the GUSTO criteria within 30 days post-treatment.
2. Quality of life assessments utilizing the EuroQol-5 Dimensions (EQ-5D) scale at baseline and 30 days post-treatment.

**Methodology:**
Enrollment of patients will occur at participating sites upon confirmation of NSTEMI diagnosis. Randomization will be performed centrally to ensure allocation concealment. Patients will receive standard background therapy, including aspirin, alongside their assigned prasugrel administration. During follow-up, clinical staff will monitor cardiac events, bleeding complications, and adherence to the protocol.

**Hypothesis:**
We hypothesize that delayed administration of prasugrel in the context of NSTEMI may lead to inadequate antiplatelet efficacy compared to immediate treatment, potentially resulting in a higher incidence of MACE and thrombotic complications, as well as questioning the safety profile for major bleeding events.

**Results & Conclusions:**
Despite rigorous trial design, the study was terminated early due to consistently low rates of primary outcome events across both treatment arms, indicating that delayed prasugrel administration does not improve antiplatelet efficacy compared to immediate dosing as hypothesized. Furthermore, the trial revealed heightened incidences of significant bleeding complications associated with prasugrel despite a general trend of delayed treatment yielding lower platelet inhibition rates.

The findings from this study underline the importance of optimal timing for prasugrel administration, particularly in a population with heterogeneous responses, suggesting that delayed dosing may contribute to a failure in achieving the desired pharmacodynamic effects. Further investigations are warranted to explore other dosing schedules and their subsequent efficacy and safety profiles in diverse patient demographics.
