### Successful Clinical Trial of Corifollitropin Alfa: Efficacy and Safety of Corifollitropin Alfa in Women with Diminished Ovarian Reserve Undergoing Ovarian Stimulation for In Vitro Fertilization (Study 40444) (P06789)

**Objective:**  
The primary objective of this phase III, multi-center, open-label study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of corifollitropin alfa as a controlled ovarian stimulation (COS) agent in women diagnosed with diminished ovarian reserve (DOR) undergoing in vitro fertilization (IVF) protocols. This trial aims to establish both live birth rates and safety endpoints associated with the use of corifollitropin alfa in a population expected to exhibit lower responsiveness to hormonal stimulation.

**Study Design:**  
This is an open-label, multi-center, uncontrolled trial designed to assess the effectiveness and safety profile of corifollitropin alfa in subjects with DOR. The trial will involve a prospective evaluation over 2 to 4 COS treatment cycles, integrating both fresh and frozen-thawed embryo transfer (FTET) cycles. Each participant will receive a single administration of corifollitropin alfa followed by daily FSH from day 8 of stimulation until the day of (rec)hCG administration for final oocyte maturation. By optimizing the inclusion criteria and employing a continuous assessment strategy, this study aims to generate robust outcomes for both efficacy and safety.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Females aged 30 to 42 years meeting criteria for DOR, defined by an antral follicle count (AFC) of less than 5 or elevated baseline FSH above 10 IU/L;
- Body mass index (BMI) between 18 and 30 kg/m² at screening;
- Normal menstrual cycle length of 24-35 days;
- Documented history of infertility for at least 12 months;
- Availability of viable sperm for ICSI or IVF techniques;
- Willingness to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Active or history of endocrine disorders impacting ovarian function;
- Previous ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) or extreme reproductive pathologies affecting ovarian responsiveness;
- Ovarian abnormalities confirmed by ultrasound (e.g., endometriomas, polycystic ovarian morphology);
- Congenital or acquired uterine anomalies affecting implantation;
- Use of investigational drugs or significant hormonal therapy within three months preceding trial enrollment;
- Any chronic comorbid conditions that might confound the study outcomes (e.g., severe cardiovascular disease, hepatic impairment).

**Study Endpoints:**  
- **Primary Efficacy Endpoint:** Live birth rate following fresh or frozen-thawed embryo transfer per participant cycle;
- **Safety Endpoints:** Incidence of adverse effects monitored through evaluations of anti-corifollitropin alfa antibodies, local tolerability following injection, and long-term maternal and neonatal health outcomes.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Given the optimized inclusion criteria targeting women with DOR, the use of an open-label design facilitates unbiased evaluation of the pharmacological effects. Continuous assessment of safety endpoints should provide rapid identification of any adverse effects. Robust longitudinal data collection is anticipated for pregnancy and neonatal health due to close follow-up post-delivery, thereby contributing to the literature on corifollitropin alfa's efficacy in challenging reproductive scenarios.

In conclusion, this trial is expected to validate corifollitropin alfa as a viable therapeutic option for women experiencing diminished ovarian reserve, underlining its potential to enhance live birth rates while maintaining an acceptable safety profile throughout treatment and pregnancy outcomes.
