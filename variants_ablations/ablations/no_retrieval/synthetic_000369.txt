  <TrialID>DBGR-2023-02</TrialID>
  <Title>Dabigatran Compared to Rivaroxaban in Preventing Thromboembolic Events in Patients with Non-Valvular Atrial Fibrillation: A Multicenter Randomized Failure Trial</Title>
  <Design>
    <Phase>3</Phase>
    <Type>Multicenter, Randomized, Open-Label</Type>
    <Objective>The primary aim of the study was to evaluate the efficacy of dabigatran compared to rivaroxaban in preventing thromboembolic events in patients diagnosed with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF).</Objective>
    <Endpoints>
      <PrimaryEndpoint>Incidence of composite thromboembolic events (ischemic stroke, systemic embolism) at 12 months.</PrimaryEndpoint>
      <SecondaryEndpoints>
        <Endpoint>All-cause mortality</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Incidence of major bleeding events</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Quality of life measures via EQ-5D</Endpoint>
      </SecondaryEndpoints>
    </Endpoints>
  </Design>
  <Methodology>
    <PatientPopulation>
      <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Adults aged 65 years and older with a documented history of NVAF.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>CHA2DS2-VASc score ≥ 2.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Informed written consent obtained.</Criterion>
      </InclusionCriteria>
      <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Significant renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min).</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Active bleeding or hemorrhagic disorders.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>History of major thromboembolic events requiring aggressive anticoagulation.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients currently using clopidogrel for dual antithrombotic therapy.</Criterion>
      </ExclusionCriteria>
    </PatientPopulation>
    <Randomization>Patients were assigned randomly 1:1 to receive either dabigatran or rivaroxaban via a computerized randomization process.</Randomization>
    <FollowUp>Routine follow-up visits were scheduled at baseline, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months.</FollowUp>
  </Methodology>
  <Intervention>
    <Arm>
      <Treatment>Dabigatran 150 mg twice daily.</Treatment>
      <Complications>Patients experienced significantly higher rates of minor bleeding events, with a notable incidence of gastrointestinal hemorrhage which raised safety concerns.</Complications>
    </Arm>
    <Arm>
      <Treatment>Rivaroxaban 20 mg once daily.</Treatment>
      <Complications>Rivaroxaban maintained a consistent profile with fewer adverse events reported compared to dabigatran.</Complications>
    </Arm>
  </Intervention>
  <Results>
    <Findings>
      <PrimaryOutcome>The primary endpoint of the incidence of composite thromboembolic events revealed no significant difference in efficacy between dabigatran and rivaroxaban, signaling the intervention’s inability to surpass rivaroxaban’s performance in the studied cohort.</PrimaryOutcome>
      <SecondaryOutcomes>Risk of major bleeding events was not significantly different; however, a trend toward increased minor bleeding with dabigatran was observed, corroborating previous concerns regarding dabigatran’s safety profile.</SecondaryOutcomes>
    </Findings>
  </Results>
  <Conclusion>
    <Outcome>This trial failed to demonstrate the superiority of dabigatran over rivaroxaban for the prevention of thromboembolic events in patients with NVAF. The results highlighted concerns regarding dabigatran's efficacy in thrombus reduction and the impact of variability in patient response on overall outcomes, aligning with findings noted in previous investigations. Furthermore, dabigatran's renal function dependency posed significant limitations, complicating patient eligibility and therapeutic effectiveness.</Outcome>
    <Implications>The observed limitations necessitate a re-evaluation of dabigatran’s role in this therapeutic context, emphasizing the need for precision in selecting anticoagulants based on patient-specific factors to optimize clinical outcomes.</Implications>
  </Conclusion>
