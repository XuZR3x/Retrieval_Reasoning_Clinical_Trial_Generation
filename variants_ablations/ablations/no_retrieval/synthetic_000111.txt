**Successful clinical trial of sorafenib (nexavar, bay43-9006): A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Sorafenib in Combination with Immunotherapy in Patients with Advanced Solid Tumors**

**Objective:**
This Phase II, multicenter, open-label study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of sorafenib in combination with a novel immune checkpoint inhibitor in patients presenting with advanced solid tumors, particularly focusing on survival outcomes, progression-free survival (PFS), and the assessment of pharmacodynamic responses.

**Primary Endpoint(s):**
- Overall survival (OS)
- Progression-free survival (PFS)

**Secondary Endpoint(s):**
- Objective response rate (ORR) according to RECIST (Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors)
- Patient-reported outcomes assessed via validated questionnaires
- Evaluation of the pharmacokinetic profile of sorafenib in combination with immunotherapy

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years and older with histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced solid tumors, excluding colorectal cancers
- Patients must have received prior systemic treatment and have disease progression after at least one line of therapy
- At least one measurable lesion per RECIST criteria
- An ECOG Performance Status of 0 or 1
- Life expectancy of at least 12 weeks
- Adequate bone marrow (hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL, ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelet count ≥ 100,000/µL), hepatic (ALT and AST ≤ 2.5 times ULN), and renal function (serum creatinine < 1.5 times ULN)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with prior therapy including immunotherapy administrated within 4 weeks prior to enrollment
- Active central nervous system metastases
- Significant cardiovascular events within the last 6 months, including myocardial infarction and unstable angina
- History of severe allergic reactions or known hypersensitivity to sorafenib or any excipients
- Patients with severe concurrent medical conditions that could interfere with trial participation or safety assessments

**Sample Size:**
The study anticipates enrolling approximately 120 patients to provide sufficient power to evaluate the defined endpoints effectively.

**Study Design:**
Following the initial screening and enrollment, patients will receive sorafenib at a starting dosage of 400 mg orally twice daily combined with immunotherapy as per the manufacturer’s guidelines. The combination will be continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent.

**Safety Assessments:**
Adverse events will be monitored continuously throughout the trial using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) to establish a robust safety profile. Moreover, pharmacodynamic assessments to investigate the effect of sorafenib on tumor microenvironment changes associated with immunotherapy will be conducted.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Statistical analyses will be performed utilizing Kaplan-Meier survival estimates for OS and PFS outcomes, alongside a Cox proportional hazards model for multivariate analyses correlating biomarkers with clinical responses.

**Conclusion:**
This study is designed to explore the potential of sorafenib in synergistic combination with immunotherapeutic agents in treating advanced solid tumors. The expectation is that the collaborative impact on the immune tumor microenvironment alongside sorafenib’s established efficacy in tumor angiogenesis will enhance clinical outcomes, address treatment resistance mechanisms, and provide much-needed therapeutic strategies for patients with limited options. Successful outcomes will broaden the therapeutical horizon for the management of advanced malignancies while ensuring patient safety and tolerability throughout the treatment course.
