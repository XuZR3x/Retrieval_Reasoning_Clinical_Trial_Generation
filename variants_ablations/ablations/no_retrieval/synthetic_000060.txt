<ClinicalTrial>
  <Title>Evaluation of Cyclosporine Combined with Tofacitinib in Patients with Moderate to Severe Atopic Dermatitis: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study</Title>
  
  <StudyDesign>This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of cyclosporine in combination with tofacitinib compared to placebo in subjects with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis inadequately controlled with topical therapies. The study will incorporate a parallel-group design to allow an optimal assessment of the drug interactions and combined effects on skin barrier integrity and inflammation.</StudyDesign>
  
  <Participants>
    <InclusionCriteria>
      <Criterion>Adults aged 18 to 65 years diagnosed with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis as assessed by Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) score ≥ 16.</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Documents an inadequate response to topical corticosteroids or other topical therapies within the past 3 months.</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Willing to comply with study visits and protocols, providing informed consent.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
      <Criterion>Active systemic infections or chronic infections such as tuberculosis or hepatitis B/C.</Criterion>
      <Criterion>History of malignancy within the past 5 years (excluding localized basal cell carcinoma).</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Social circumstances that could interfere with compliance or safety regarding the treatment regimen.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
  </Participants>
  
  <Endpoints>
    <PrimaryEndpoint>
      <Description>Change from baseline in EASI score at Week 16.</Description>
    </PrimaryEndpoint>
    <SecondaryEndpoints>
      <Endpoint>Safety profile assessed through the incidence of serious adverse events (SAEs) and adverse event rates at Week 16.</Endpoint>
      <Endpoint>Proportion of patients achieving EASI-75 (≥75% reduction from baseline) at Week 16.</Endpoint>
      <Endpoint>Improvement in quality of life measured by the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) score.</Endpoint>
    </SecondaryEndpoints>
  </Endpoints>
  
  <Outcomes>
    <Outcome>Patients will undergo a 16-week treatment period, with assessments of efficacy and safety recorded at baseline, 4 weeks, 8 weeks, and 16 weeks.</Outcome>
    <Outcome>Biochemical analyses will include check-ups of renal function and liver enzymes, as well as targeted evaluations for drug interactions.</Outcome>
  </Outcomes>
  
  <StatisticalAnalysis>
    <Description>Analysis will be conducted using appropriate statistical methodologies, including intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis. The effects of treatment will be evaluated using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) to adjust for any baseline confounders.</Description>
  </StatisticalAnalysis>
  
  <Rationale>
    <Reason>The selection of a population with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis and the comprehensive inclusion criteria aims to limit variability and enhance the interpretability of results, as seen in successful trials evaluating cyclosporine in other settings.</Reason>
    <Reason>Utilizing comparative frameworks to assess the efficacy of combined therapies, harmonized dosages of cyclosporine and tofacitinib, provides insight into optimizing treatment while possibly minimizing adverse effects.</Reason>
    <Reason>Rigorous endpoint monitoring, focusing on EASI scores and adverse events, establishes a robust safety profile consistent with established references for cyclosporine use in dermatological applications.</Reason>
    <Reason>The integration of advanced statistical techniques and multi-center approaches bolsters the reliability of findings, thereby enhancing the generalizability of clinical implications for future therapeutic strategies.</Reason>
    <Reason>Synergistic effects of the combination therapy may yield improved clinical management outcomes, as demonstrated in previous research focusing on immunosuppressive treatments.</Reason>
  </Rationale>
</ClinicalTrial>
