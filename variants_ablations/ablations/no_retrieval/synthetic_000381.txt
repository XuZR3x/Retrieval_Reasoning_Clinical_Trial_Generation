SUCCESSFUL CLINICAL TRIAL OF CYTARABINE: A Multi-Center Phase III Study of Cytarabine in Combination With Targeted Therapy in Patients With Newly Diagnosed Acute Myeloid Leukemia and High-Risk Features

RATIONALE: The use of cytarabine in conjunction with targeted therapies has demonstrated a synergistic effect, particularly for patients with high-risk acute myeloid leukemia (AML). This regimen aims to concurrently inhibit multiple oncogenic pathways while effectively reducing leukemic burden.

PURPOSE: This Phase III trial is designed to assess the efficacy and safety of cytarabine in combination with the FLT3 inhibitor gilteritinib in treating patients with newly diagnosed AML harboring FLT3-ITD mutations. The primary endpoint is overall survival (OS), while secondary endpoints assess complete remission rates, duration of remission, and assessment of adverse events.

OBJECTIVES:
Primary:
- To evaluate the overall survival rates in patients treated with cytarabine and gilteritinib as compared to standard cytarabine-based chemotherapy.

Secondary:
- To determine the rate of complete remissions achieved,
- To evaluate the duration of remission,
- To assess the occurrence of adverse events and toxicities associated with the combination therapy.

OUTLINE: This is a randomized, multi-center study. Eligible patients are stratified based on age (<60 years vs ≥60 years) and performance status (ECOG 0-1 vs ECOG 2).

- Induction Therapy: 
Patients receive cytarabine infusion at 1 g/m² continuously for 7 days alongside gilteritinib at a daily oral dose of 120 mg, commencing on day 8 of the induction.

- Consolidation Therapy:
Consolidation treatment will involve high-dose cytarabine (3 g/m² every 12 hours for 6 doses on days 1, 3, and 5) administered with gilteritinib (120 mg daily).

- Stem Cell Transplantation: 
Patients achieving complete remission may proceed to allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation based on donor availability, following appropriate conditioning regimens inclusive of cytarabine.

- Follow-Up: Patients will be monitored for relapse and toxicities, with follow-up assessments every 3 months for the first 2 years post-remission and biannually thereafter.

DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:
- Newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukemia defined according to 2016 WHO classification.
- Patients must possess the FLT3-ITD mutation confirmed by molecular diagnostics.

PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:
- Age ≥18 years,
- Adequate organ function (serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL, bilirubin ≤ 2 mg/dL, and AST/ALT ≤ 2x upper limit of normal),
- Ejection fraction > 50% via echocardiogram,
- Absence of prior malignancies except non-melanoma skin cancers.

EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Presence of GUM disease or active uncontrolled infections,
- History of central nervous system leukemia,
- Allergies to study drugs or severe concurrent medical conditions that could compromise participation.

SUCCESS CRITERIA:
The study was deemed successful based on the achieved overall survival rate, which exceeded the predetermined threshold of 60% at a median follow-up of 24 months. The complete remission rate reached 78%, with a median duration of remission of 16 months. Toxicity profiles indicated a manageable incidence of adverse effects, primarily grade 1-2, allowing for continued treatment scalability.

This integration of cytarabine with gilteritinib exemplifies a promising leap forward for high-risk AML patients, affirming the hypothesis of a multi-faceted therapeutic approach by inhibiting critical survival pathways while harnessing cytotoxic efficacy.
