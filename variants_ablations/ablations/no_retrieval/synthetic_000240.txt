Failed clinical trial of bevacizumab: Efficacy and Safety of Bevacizumab in Combination With High-Dose Interleukin-2 for Advanced Melanoma A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Phase III Clinical Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Bevacizumab in Combination With High-Dose Interleukin-2 Versus Placebo Plus High-Dose Interleukin-2 in Patients With Advanced Melanoma. This study aims to assess whether the incorporation of bevacizumab, an anti-VEGF monoclonal antibody, into a high-dose interleukin-2 (IL-2) regimen can improve overall survival and response rates in patients with advanced melanoma compared to placebo. Prior studies in various malignancies suggested that combining anti-angiogenic therapies with immunotherapy might enhance treatment efficacy. However, significant limitations may lead to the failure of this trial. Inclusion Criteria: - Adults aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed unresectable stage III or IV melanoma - Patients must have had at least one prior systemic therapy for advanced disease and be considered eligible for high-dose IL-2 treatment - Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2 - Adequate organ function as defined by standard laboratory parameters - Written informed consent obtained before any study-specific procedures Exclusion Criteria: - Active central nervous system metastases - Prior anti-angiogenic therapy more than 28 days before study enrollment - Concurrent participation in another clinical trial - History of thromboembolic events or significant bleeding diathesis - Known hypersensitivity to bevacizumab or any component of the commercial formulation - Pregnant or lactating patients - Clinically significant cardiovascular or pulmonary disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or active infection - Prior history of gastrointestinal perforation, fistulae, or severe proteinuria - Other malignancies requiring active intervention within 5 years, except for non-melanoma skin cancer and localized prostate cancer Objectives: The primary objective is to determine the overall survival (OS) of patients receiving bevacizumab in combination with high-dose IL-2 compared to placebo in conjunction with high-dose IL-2. Secondary objectives include assessing progression-free survival (PFS), objective response rate (ORR), and characterization of the adverse event profile associated with treatment. Methodology: This multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III trial will enroll up to 300 participants across diverse sites. Eligible patients will be randomized (1:1) to receive either bevacizumab (15 mg/kg) or placebo every 3 weeks, administered concurrently with high-dose IL-2 (600,000 IU/kg) via continuous intravenous infusion for up to 14 doses, with cycles repeated every 21 days. Randomization will occur within 14 days post screening and following confirmation of eligibility. Bevacizumab treatment will continue until progression of disease or unacceptable toxicity. Statistical analyses will be performed using an intention-to-treat approach. Expected Outcomes: The study aims to provide insight into the interplay between bevacizumab and IL-2, although factors such as limited target efficacy due to the heterogeneous angiogenic profiles of melanoma, the delayed introduction of treatment escalating beyond optimal timing, interference with high-dose IL-2 immunogenicity, discrepancies in patient population regarding performance status and comorbidities, as well as a distinctive adverse event profile of increased vascular complications may culminate in trial failure. Potential risks could induce treatment discontinuations, compromising overall survival and effectiveness. The trial will conclude with longitudinal follow-up assessments to monitor treatment responses and adverse events for a total duration of five years.
