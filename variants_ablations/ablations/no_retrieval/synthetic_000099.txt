**Successful clinical trial of daclizumab:** _A Randomized, Multi-Center Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Daclizumab in Combination with Conventional Immunosuppressants for Patients Undergoing Hepatic Transplantation_

**Background:** The rising incidence of acute rejection episodes in hepatic transplant recipients necessitates innovative immunosuppressive strategies. Daclizumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting the interleukin-2 receptor, has demonstrated potential in enhancing immunosuppression when used concomitantly with established agents. This study aims to evaluate the impact of daclizumab on acute rejection rates and long-term graft survival.

**Objective:** To compare the incidence of acute rejection episodes and assess the long-term safety and efficacy of daclizumab combined with standard immunosuppressive therapy versus standard immunosuppressive therapy alone in liver transplantation recipients.

**Study Design:** This is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving a defined population of naïve hepatic transplant recipients. The two arms of the study will include: 

- **Group A:** Patients receiving daclizumab in conjunction with mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) and tacrolimus.
- **Group B:** Patients receiving placebo alongside standard therapy with MMF and tacrolimus.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients (18-65 years) undergoing their first liver transplantation.
- Patients with no history of significant kidney or cardiac disease.
- Consent must be provided voluntarily and competently by the participant prior to enrollment.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior solid organ transplantation.
- Active infections or malignancies within the past 5 years.
- Previous therapy with immunosuppressants that may confound results.
- Women who are pregnant, breastfeeding, or not utilizing effective contraception during the study.

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary measure will be the incidence of acute cellular rejection episodes defined by biopsy-proven rejection graded per the Banff classification within the first 6 months post-transplant.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Renal function as assessed by glomerular filtration rate (GFR) at 12 months.
- Incidence of adverse events and serious adverse events related to both daclizumab and standard immunosuppressive therapy.

**Study Procedures:** Patients will undergo liver transplantation, with post-operative follow-up involving regular monitoring for liver function, renal function, and any incident adverse effects. Daclizumab or placebo will be administered for 12 weeks post-transplant, with further immunosuppressant therapy maintained according to standard protocols. 

**Monitoring:** Adverse events will be monitored systematically through scheduled visits at weeks 1, 4, 12, 24, and 52. Patients will also undergo laboratory assessments, including complete blood counts, liver function tests, and appropriate imaging studies as necessary.

**Results:** The expected outcomes include a statistically significant reduction in acute rejection episodes in the daclizumab group compared to the placebo group, supported by long-term graft survival rates demonstrating enhanced renal function (GFR) at the 12-month mark. With stringent monitoring for adverse events, we anticipate a favorable safety profile for daclizumab which, in conjunction with conventional immunosuppressants, may offer substantial benefits to the transplant population.

This study emphasizes the necessity of meticulous inclusion criteria, as well as rigorous monitoring protocols, ensuring a homogeneous patient population and enhancing the reliability of findings, consistent with the successes elucidated in prior clinical trials involving daclizumab in other transplant settings.
