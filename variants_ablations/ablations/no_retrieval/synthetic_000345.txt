**Failed Clinical Trial of Exemestane: A Study of Exemestane Plus Olaparib vs Exemestane Alone in Hormone Receptor-Positive, BRCA-Mutated Metastatic Breast Cancer**

**Randomized, Open-Label Trial of Exemestane in Combination with Olaparib Compared to Exemestane Alone in Patients with Hormone Receptor-Positive, BRCA-Mutated Metastatic Breast Cancer.**

The objective of this study was to assess the efficacy and safety of oral exemestane in combination with olaparib, a poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor, versus oral exemestane monotherapy, in postmenopausal women with hormone receptor-positive (HR+) metastatic breast cancer harboring BRCA mutations who had previously progressed on non-steroidal aromatase inhibitors.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed HR+ metastatic breast cancer with documented BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation.
- Patients must be postmenopausal (as defined by amenorrhea for ≥12 months or prior oophorectomy).
- Patients should have documented disease progression after treatment with letrozole and/or anastrozole.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of ≤1.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with PARP inhibitors or systemic chemotherapy for metastatic breast cancer.
- Presence of symptomatic central nervous system metastases.
- Concurrent use of strong CYP3A4 inducers or inhibitors.

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS), measured as the time from randomization to the first documentation of disease progression or death due to any cause. Secondary endpoints included overall response rate (ORR), safety and tolerability, and quality of life assessments.

**Study Design:**
This was planned as a multicenter, randomized, open-label trial with a 1:1 allocation to receive either the combination of oral exemestane (25 mg daily) and olaparib (300 mg twice daily) or exemestane alone. Patients would undergo treatment cycles of 28 days and be followed for clinical response evaluations every 8 weeks.

**Results:**
The trial was terminated prematurely due to inadequate patient recruitment and an unexpectedly high rate of withdrawal attributable to participant concerns over potential efficacy and perceived toxicity of the treatment regimen. Furthermore, there was marked variability in patient responses attributed to diverse tumor biology in the enrolled cohort, which resulted in insufficient power to meet the primary endpoint. As per interim analyses, anticipated pharmacokinetic interactions between olaparib and exemestane were not thoroughly explored, potentially impacting efficacy outcomes. Patient feedback indicated hesitation to participate in trials involving combination treatment regimens, stemming from preconceived notions about adverse events observed with PARP inhibitors in other malignancies.

**Conclusion:**
The study's failure to meet its recruitment goals compounded by the variability in individual tumor characteristics, limited understanding of drug interactions, and potential biases established by prior treatment narratives ultimately resulted in the trial's discontinuation. These findings underscore the need for more tailored selection criteria and enhanced education around investigational therapies for hormone receptor-positive breast cancer patients with BRCA mutations.
