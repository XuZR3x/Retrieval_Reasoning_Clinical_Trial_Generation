**Study Title**: A Phase II Study of Carboplatin Combined with Nivolumab and Pembrolizumab for Treatment of Metastatic Triple-Negative Breast Cancer

**RATIONALE**: Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is characterized by aggressive behavior and limited treatment options. The combination of carboplatin with immune checkpoint inhibitors, nivolumab and pembrolizumab, aims to exploit the potential synergistic effects of carboplatin-induced immunogenic cell death and enhanced T-cell activation through PD-1 pathway blockade. This innovative approach is posited to improve clinical outcomes, including overall survival and progression-free survival, by targeting both the tumor microenvironment and the cancer cells.

**PURPOSE**: This phase II trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a combination regimen consisting of carboplatin, nivolumab, and pembrolizumab in patients with chemotherapy-naïve metastatic TNBC.

**OBJECTIVES**:
- **Primary**: 
  - Evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) as assessed by RECIST 1.1 criteria in patients receiving carboplatin, nivolumab, and pembrolizumab.
- **Secondary**: 
  - Assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).
  - Characterize safety and tolerability profiles, with emphasis on immune-related adverse events.
  - Analyze changes in biomarkers, including PD-L1 expression and tumor mutational burden (TMB), correlating them with response rates.

**OUTLINE**: Patients will receive carboplatin intravenously (IV) on day 1 of a 21-day cycle, alongside nivolumab IV on day 1 and pembrolizumab IV on day 1. Treatment continues for 6 cycles, followed by maintenance therapy with nivolumab and pembrolizumab administered every 4 weeks for up to 1 year in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Participants are followed for 24 months after conclusion of therapy.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL**: A total of 80 patients will be enrolled over 24 months.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS**: 
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC).
- Stage IV disease with measurable lesions.
- Patients must be chemotherapy-naïve for metastatic disease.
- No active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment, or previous treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS**: 
- Age: 18 years and older.
- Performance status: ECOG 0-1.
- Adequate hematologic and organ function:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³.
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³.
  - Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).
  - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 times ULN.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY**: 
- No prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease.
- No concurrent radiotherapy.
- Patients must not have received any other investigational agents within 30 days prior to study entry.

**EFFICACY MONITORING**: Regular assessments will occur every 6 weeks for the first 6 months, then every 3 months thereafter. A thorough review of patient responses will be conducted, focusing on response rates, survival metrics, and incidence of adverse events, particularly those of autoimmune origin.

This trial is predicated upon a rigorous understanding of the pharmacodynamics of carboplatin alongside immune modulation through PD-1 inhibition. Preliminary studies have indicated enhanced cytotoxicity of carboplatin in immunocompetent hosts, establishing an encouraging rationale for this combination therapy. The structured follow-up and careful patient selection are anticipated to maximize the potential benefit of this innovative treatment strategy for metastatic TNBC.
