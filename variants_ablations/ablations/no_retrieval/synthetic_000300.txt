**Successful clinical trial of eltrombopag: ELTPHASE 2017**

**Multicentre Randomised Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of Eltrombopag in Patients with Chronic Hepatitis C Undergoing Antiviral Treatment – ELTPHASE 2017**

The objective of this clinical trial is to assess the efficacy and safety of eltrombopag in increasing platelet counts and minimizing treatment-related thrombocytopenia in patients with chronic hepatitis C (HCV) undergoing antiviral therapy. This study aims to evaluate the proportion of patients achieving a sustained virologic response (SVR) while receiving optimal doses of pegylated interferon and ribavirin without treatment interruptions.

**Background:** Chronic hepatitis C infection is known to be associated with thrombocytopenia, which significantly affects treatment adherence and outcomes. Eltrombopag has been recognized as a thrombopoietic agent that can facilitate the management of thrombocytopenic patients, thereby ensuring uninterrupted antiviral treatment.

**Study Design:** This is a phase III, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial designed to comprise approximately 800 adult participants diagnosed with chronic HCV infection. Eligible patients will be randomized to receive either eltrombopag or a matching placebo for a duration of 24 weeks, concurrently with pegylated interferon and ribavirin therapy.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years and older with laboratory-confirmed diagnosis of chronic HCV infection
- Candidates suitable for pegylated interferon and ribavirin combination therapy
- Baseline platelet count of <75,000/µl
- No significant renal impairment (creatinine clearance >50mL/min)
- Ability to provide informed consent and comply with protocol requirements

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of non-response to previous antiviral therapy with pegylated interferon and ribavirin for reasons other than thrombocytopenia
- Any history of decompensated liver cirrhosis or other significant comorbid conditions impacting liver function
- History of thrombosis or thromboembolic events, including portal vein thrombosis
- Requirement for any anticoagulation that cannot be safely managed

**Outcome Measures:** The primary endpoint will be the proportion of subjects achieving SVR, defined as undetectable HCV RNA at 24 weeks post-treatment. Secondary endpoints will include safety assessments, adverse event profiles, and analysis of thromboembolic events (TEEs) occurring during the study.

**Rationale for Success:** The successful design of the ELTPHASE 2017 trial incorporates robust measures aligned with previous successful interventions in enhancing treatment integrity:
1. By utilizing a randomized, placebo-controlled methodology, the study is poised to deliver high reliability in interpreting outcomes, mirroring proven effectiveness observed in the ENABLE trials.
2. Eltrombopag’s unique ability to elevate platelet counts directly addresses the detrimental impact of thrombocytopenia, thereby enabling patients to adhere to requisite dosing regimens, thus potentially increasing the likelihood of achieving SVR.
3. Thoroughly developed inclusion criteria ensure the selection of a patient population with a high propensity for benefit, akin to successful protocols utilized in preceding trials.
4. The implementation of stringent post-treatment follow-up plans will facilitate comprehensive risk assessments for TEEs and long-term health outcomes, addressing concerns raised from previous interventions.
5. The collaboration between the participating centers and data management entities will ensure the fidelity and accuracy of data collection, paralleling successful strategies observed in the ENABLE series.

**Anticipated Results:** ELTPHASE 2017 is hypothesized to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in SVR rates in the eltrombopag arm compared to the placebo, thus substantiating eltrombopag's role as an integral adjunct in the management of hepatitis C treatment-related thrombocytopenia, while maintaining a favorable safety and tolerability profile.

**Conclusion:** The ELTPHASE 2017 trial is positioned to further elucidate the role of eltrombopag in enhancing treatment efficacy for chronic hepatitis C, overcoming previous barriers associated with thrombocytopenia, and addressing patient safety through refined inclusion criteria and observational protocols, thereby contributing valuable insights into future clinical practice and guidelines in HCV management.
