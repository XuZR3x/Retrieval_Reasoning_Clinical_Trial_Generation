**Successful Clinical Trial of Aspirin: Impact of Aspirin on Cognitive Function in Older Adults with Mild Cognitive Impairment**

**Title:** Evaluation of Aspirin in Enhancing Cognitive Function and Inhibiting Platelet Activation in Older Adults with Mild Cognitive Impairment: A Double-blind, Randomized, Placebo-controlled Study

**Abstract:** This clinical investigation is designed to evaluate the effects of low-dose aspirin on cognitive function in older adults diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment (MCI). Previous findings indicate that neuroinflammation and increased platelet activation may contribute to cognitive decline. Aspirin’s established mechanism of action as a COX inhibitor and its role as an antiplatelet agent provide a theoretical basis for its potential neuroprotective effects. The study aims to elucidate whether chronic low-dose aspirin therapy can improve cognitive outcomes in this vulnerable population characterized by heightened thrombotic tendencies.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 60 to 80 years
- Diagnosis of Mild Cognitive Impairment, confirmed by a neuropsychological assessment (such as the Montreal Cognitive Assessment, MoCA)
- Absence of severe psychiatric disorders or uncorrected sensory impairments
- No history of cerebrovascular events or significant cardiovascular disease requiring anticoagulation
- Able to provide informed consent and follow study procedures

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Allergy to aspirin or any NSAID
- Active gastrointestinal bleeding or history of peptic ulcers
- Pregnant or lactating women
- Individuals currently receiving other antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapies
- Serious comorbid conditions affecting cognitive function (e.g., uncontrolled diabetes, severe renal disease)

**Objective:** This trial primarily seeks to assess whether low-dose aspirin can lead to significant improvements in cognitive function as measured by standardized neuropsychological tests, alongside secondary evaluations of inflammation markers and platelet reactivity.

**Study Design:** This double-blind, placebo-controlled trial will enroll 200 subjects, randomized to receive either 81 mg of aspirin daily or placebo for a duration of six months. The trial will utilize cognitive tests that assess various domains, including attention, memory, and executive function, at baseline, three months, and six months.

**Endpoints:** The primary outcome is the change in MoCA scores from baseline to six months. Secondary endpoints include an evaluation of serum inflammatory markers (e.g., C-reactive protein, interleukin-6) and assessments of platelet activation through light transmission aggregometry.

**Monitoring and Safety:** Comprehensive monitoring protocols are established to assess the incidence of any adverse effects, particularly gastrointestinal complications and bleeding. Monthly safety assessments will include routine blood tests and clinical evaluations.

**Significance:** The proposed study may reveal pivotal insights into the potential cognitive benefits of low-dose aspirin in an aging population predisposed to cognitive decline, thus guiding future therapeutic strategies to combat MCI progression.

This clinical trial leverages aspirin's well-documented pharmacological properties (Reason 1) to address cognitive impairment, recognizing the significance of targeted participant inclusion criteria (Reason 2). Moreover, the strategic combination of cognitive assessments and inflammatory biomarker profiling has the potential to illuminate complex interactions underlying cognitive degeneration (Reason 4). Ongoing safety monitoring, along with comprehensive patient engagement, further underscores the commitment to maintaining participant well-being throughout the trial (Reason 5). The findings from this trial could support the broader use of aspirin within the cognitive health intervention spectrum, particularly among older adults.
