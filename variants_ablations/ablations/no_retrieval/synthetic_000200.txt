<ClinicalTrial>
    <TrialTitle>
        Phase II, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-Controlled Study of Interferon Beta-1a for Patients With Ulcerative Colitis
    </TrialTitle>
    <Objective>
        The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of interferon beta-1a in inducing and maintaining remission in patients diagnosed with moderate to severely active ulcerative colitis (UC), assessing its impact on clinical outcomes and patient quality of life. 
    </Objective>
    <StudyDesign>
        This was a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled study involving 100 patients diagnosed with ulcerative colitis. Patients were randomized to receive either subcutaneous interferon beta-1a (22 mcg three times a week) or placebo for a period of 24 weeks, followed by a 24-week observation phase.
    </StudyDesign>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        - Adults aged 18-65 years with a confirmed diagnosis of ulcerative colitis as per the Montreal Classification.
        - Patients presenting with an endoscopic Mayo score of 3 or higher at screening.
        - Patients with a clinical Mayo score of 5 to 9 (inclusive) at baseline.
        - Signed informed consent obtained from all participants.
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        - Patients with concurrent inflammatory bowel disease or other chronic inflammatory disorders.
        - Patients previously treated with biologic therapies within 6 months prior to randomization.
        - History of psychiatric disorders or suicidal ideation as assessed by the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale.
        - Active infections or history of life-threatening infections.
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <PrimaryEndpoints>
        - The primary efficacy endpoint was defined as the proportion of patients achieving clinical remission, defined by a clinical Mayo score of 2 or lower, and no individual subscore greater than 1 at the end of the treatment phase (Week 24).
    </PrimaryEndpoints>
    <SecondaryEndpoints>
        - Assessment of quality of life via the Inflammatory Bowel Disease Questionnaire (IBDQ).
        - Assessment of safety and tolerability measured through the incidence of adverse events throughout the study duration.
        - Evaluation of endoscopic improvement as defined by a decrease of at least 2 points on the endoscopic Mayo score from baseline at Week 24.
    </SecondaryEndpoints>
    <Results>
        The trial concluded with a total of 100 participants, of which 50 received interferon beta-1a and 50 received placebo. At Week 24, only 10% of patients in the interferon beta-1a group achieved clinical remission compared to 8% in the placebo group (p=0.72). Notably, the results indicated a failure to meet the predefined efficacy criteria, raising concerns over the limited mechanistic justification for interferon beta-1a in the context of UC.
    </Results>
    <Discussion>
        The lack of significant difference in remission rates suggests insufficient treatment duration and possibly inadequate therapeutic response attributable to the complex inflammatory pathways involved in ulcerative colitis that interferon beta-1a may not adequately modulate. Furthermore, high dropout rates (30%) were observed due to adverse effects including flu-like symptoms and injection site reactions, significantly reducing applicable data from patients who may have benefited from longer treatment exposure.
    </Discussion>
    <Conclusion>
        Given the challenges associated with patient selection, treatment duration, and the demonstrable efficacy measured in meaningful clinical outcomes, this trial indicated a potential failure of interferon beta-1a as a therapeutic option for ulcerative colitis, necessitating future investigations to explore alternative immunomodulatory strategies that could provide a more favorable profile for managing this complex disease.
    </Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
