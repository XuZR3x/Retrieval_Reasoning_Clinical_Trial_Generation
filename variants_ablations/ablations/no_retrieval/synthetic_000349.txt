  <trial_title>A Study of Bevacizumab (Avastin) in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer</trial_title>
  <study_design>A Randomized, Open-label Study to Compare the Efficacy of Bevacizumab Plus Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors versus Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors Alone in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer</study_design>
  
  <objectives>
    The primary objectives of this trial were to evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in patients receiving bevacizumab in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors compared to those receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors alone. Secondary endpoints included safety, tolerability, and quality of life assessments.
  </objectives>
  
  <methodology>
    Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive either bevacizumab (15mg/kg iv q3weeks) in conjunction with either pembrolizumab (200mg iv q3weeks) or nivolumab (3mg/kg iv q2weeks), or the immune checkpoint inhibitor monotherapy alone. The anticipated duration of study treatment was until disease progression, and the target sample size was set at 400 individuals.
  </methodology>
  
  <inclusion_criteria>
    - Adult patients, >=18 years of age
    - Confirmed diagnosis of advanced non-small cell lung cancer 
    - At least one measurable lesion according to RECIST v1.1 criteria
    - Good performance status (ECOG 0-1) 
    - Adequate organ function, including liver, renal, and bone marrow parameters.
  </inclusion_criteria>
  
  <exclusion_criteria>
    - Previous systemic treatment for advanced non-small cell lung cancer
    - Clinically significant cardiovascular disease
    - Prior history of autoimmune diseases requiring systemic treatment
    - Major surgical procedure or significant trauma within 30 days prior to study enrollment
    - Use of other investigational agents within 30 days prior to starting study treatment.
  </exclusion_criteria>
  
  <endpoint_descriptions>
    The primary endpoint for evaluation of progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from randomization until the first occurrence of documented disease progression or death. This was assessed bi-monthly using the RECIST v1.1 criteria, with progressive disease defined as at least a 20% increase in the sum of the longest diameters of target lesions, relative to the smallest sum recorded since the treatment commenced. Secondary endpoints focused on overall survival (OS) metrics and quality-of-life outcomes as measured by validated questionnaires at baseline and intervals through the duration of treatment.
  </endpoint_descriptions>
  
  <findings>
    The study did not demonstrate statistically significant improvements in PFS or OS when combining bevacizumab with immune checkpoint inhibitors compared to the control arm receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors alone. Much of the therapeutic effectiveness was impeded by the inherent aggressiveness of lung cancer and potential immunosuppressive effects attributed to bevacizumab, which could have compromised anti-tumor immune responses. Additionally, discordance in biological pathways led to suboptimal therapeutic outcomes, with difficulty in enhancing the immune-mediated response in advanced malignancies. 
  </findings>
  
  <conclusion>
    In conclusion, the addition of bevacizumab to immune checkpoint inhibitors did not yield the anticipated clinical benefits in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer. The study highlights the necessity for further investigation into the mechanisms underpinning failed synergistic effects seen in prior trials involving bevacizumab, particularly concerning its effects on tumor immune microenvironments and biological response pathways.
  </conclusion>
