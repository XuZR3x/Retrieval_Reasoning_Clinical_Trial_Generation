<ClinicalTrial>
    <StudyTitle>Impact of Atorvastatin on Cognitive Function in Older Adults with Dyslipidemia: A Randomized Controlled Trial</StudyTitle>
    <Objective>
        This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of atorvastatin in improving cognitive function and reducing the risk of dementia in older adults diagnosed with dyslipidemia. We hypothesize that atorvastatin will enhance cognitive performance and correlate with cerebrovascular health indicators.
    </Objective>
    <StudyDesign>
        This is a multicentric, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial. A total of 500 older adults aged 65 years and above with a diagnosis of dyslipidemia will be enrolled. Participants will be stratified based on cardiovascular risk levels and history of statin intolerance. They will be randomly assigned to receive either 40 mg atorvastatin daily or a matched placebo for 24 months.
    </StudyDesign>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        1. Adults aged 65 and older
        2. Diagnosis of dyslipidemia defined as LDL-C levels ≥130 mg/dL (3.37 mmol/L)
        3. Ability to provide informed consent
        4. Cognitively intact as assessed by Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score >24
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        1. Previous history of serious statin-related adverse effects
        2. Presence of significant neurological disorders (e.g., Alzheimer disease, vascular dementia)
        3. Uncontrolled diabetes or chronic kidney disease (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²)
        4. On concomitant therapy with medications interacting with statins (e.g., cyclosporine, gemfibrozil)
        5. Prior statin use within the past year
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <Endpoints>
        Primary Endpoint: Change in cognitive function as measured by the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive Subscale (ADAS-Cog) from baseline to month 24. 
        Secondary Endpoints: 
        1. Changes in LDL-C levels, HDL-C levels, and triglycerides.
        2. Changes in brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) levels as a biomarker of neuroplasticity.
        3. Assessment of changes in neuroimaging markers from baseline to month 24, including volumetric MRI analysis of hippocampal and cortical regions.
        4. Incidence of new-onset dementia or mild cognitive impairment as assessed through follow-up at month 24.
    </Endpoints>
    <StatisticalAnalysis>
        Statistical significance will be evaluated using a mixed-effects model to handle the longitudinal data, incorporating baseline characteristics as covariates. An intent-to-treat approach will be utilized, with all participants analyzed according to assigned treatment regardless of adherence.
    </StatisticalAnalysis>
    <ExpectedOutcomes>
        Results are anticipated to reveal significant improvement in cognitive function in the atorvastatin group relative to placebo, paralleling reductions in LDL-C and improved cerebrovascular health markers. The diverse patient population and stratified selection criteria will ensure robust evaluation of atorvastatin’s efficacy, as echoed in previous studies that highlighted its multifaceted benefits across various disease states. 
    </ExpectedOutcomes>
    <Significance>
        The findings of this trial could provide critical insights into atorvastatin’s potential role in cognitive preservation and prevention of dementia in the aging population, thereby outlining novel therapeutic strategies in managing dyslipidemia-associated cognitive decline. 
    </Significance>
</ClinicalTrial>
