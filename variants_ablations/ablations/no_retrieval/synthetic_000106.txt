**Ranibizumab Treatment for Diabetic Macular Edema: A Novel Approach Utilizing Genotype-Driven Protocols and Tailored Follow-Up Strategy**

A Phase III, Multicenter, Double-Blind, Randomized Controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Genotype-Driven Dosing of Ranibizumab in Patients With Diabetic Macular Edema

**Objective:**  
This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of ranibizumab in treatment-naive patients with diabetic macular edema (DME), employing a genotype-driven treatment protocol alongside a structured follow-up regimen to optimize visual outcomes.

**Study Design:**  
This phase III, multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled trial will enroll 240 patients from 20 clinical sites. Enrolled subjects will undergo genomic profiling to identify specific genetic markers associated with treatment response (e.g., VEGF polymorphisms). Patients will then be assigned to either a standard dosing group receiving intravitreal ranibizumab (0.5 mg) monthly for three consecutive months followed by a personalized treatment interval defined by patient-specific parameters or a genotype-optimized dosing group receiving intravitreal ranibizumab adjusted according to their genetic profile.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adults aged 18 years or older with a confirmed diagnosis of type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus.  
- Visual acuity impairment attributed exclusively to DME, with a baseline BCVA of 78 to 24 letters (20/32 to 20/320) in the study eye.  
- Documented central retinal thickness greater than 300 microns on OCT at screening.  
- Patients must be treatment-naive, having no prior anti-VEGF therapy in the study eye.  
- Agreement to comply with study follow-up and assessments.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Pre-existing ocular conditions that could interfere with assessment or treatment outcomes (e.g., proliferative diabetic retinopathy).  
- History of significant intraocular procedures within 6 months of screening, including cataract surgery or retinal laser interventions.  
- Presence of uncontrolled systemic conditions (e.g., uncontrolled hypertension).  
- Other conditions that, in the opinion of the investigator, may compromise patient safety or compliance.

**Treatment Protocol:**  
Patients in the standard dosing group will receive three consecutive monthly intravitreal injections of ranibizumab followed by a bimonthly follow-up schedule, allowing assessment of retinal fluid dynamics through OCT. The genotype-optimized group will receive dosages adjusted according to the identified genetic markers, with follow-up intensified based on evolving OCT findings. Early responsive patients may be transitioned to extended treatment intervals adhering to the principles of individualized patient care.

**Outcome Measures:**  
The primary outcome will be the mean change in Best Corrected Visual Acuity (BCVA) from baseline to 12 months, analyzed using mixed-effects models to account for the potential variability in treatment response attributed to genetic factors. Secondary outcomes will include changes in central retinal thickness, patient-reported outcomes, and safety profiles evaluated through adverse event monitoring during the follow-up period.

**Rationale for Design:**  
The intervention is underpinned by the structured monitoring regimen inherent in both treatment protocols, allowing prompt identification of therapeutic efficacy and rapid adjustment of treatment plans to enhance visual acuity according to patient-specific demography (Reason 1). Moreover, the stringent inclusion/exclusion criteria ensure a homogeneous population that can provide reliable efficacy data (Reason 2), while the novel approach in utilizing genotype-driven dosing takes into account individual variability in the therapeutic response (Reason 3) enhancing the precision of outcomes. The integration of sophisticated imaging techniques via OCT will provide real-time feedback around treatment efficacy and inform adjustments in follow-up regimens (Reason 4). Furthermore, the provision for adjunctive therapies further supports a multidisciplinary approach in managing diabetic macular edema (Reason 5).

**Expected Outcomes:**  
This innovative trial is anticipated to demonstrate that genotype-informed dosing of ranibizumab can significantly improve visual outcomes in diabetic macular edema patients while also establishing a compelling safety profile. Findings will contribute to evolving treatment paradigms in diabetic eye diseases and refine dosing strategies to maximize therapeutic effectiveness in diverse patient populations.
