**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy and Safety of Telbivudine in Combination with Interferon for Chronic Hepatitis B**

**Study Title:** Evaluation of Telbivudine in Combination with Interferon in Adults with Chronic Hepatitis B: A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind Study

**Objective:** This clinical trial aims to assess the efficacy and safety of a combination therapy involving telbivudine at 600 mg/day and interferon-alpha in comparison to interferon-alpha monotherapy in adults with chronic hepatitis B (CHB).

**Design:** This is a prospective, multicenter, randomized, double-blind clinical trial designed to minimize bias and enhance the reliability of results. Participants have been randomized to receive either telbivudine plus interferon-alpha or interferon-alpha alone for a total of 48 weeks. The trial has been designed with a follow-up period extending to an additional 24 weeks post-treatment cessation to observe long-term effects and outcomes.

**Patient Population:** Participants will be adults aged 18 years and older diagnosed with chronic hepatitis B, specifically those with evidence of moderate to severe liver fibrosis, as determined by liver biopsy. The inclusion criteria are stringent, ensuring enrollment of a homogenous cohort predisposed to benefit from intervention strategies targeting active liver inflammation and viral replication.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Documented chronic hepatitis B infection characterized as follows:
  - Positive HBsAg for at least 6 months.
  - Serum HBV DNA levels ≥ 2,000 IU/mL at screening.
  - Elevated alanine transaminase (ALT) levels between 1 to 10 times upper limit of normal (ULN).
  - Moderate to severe fibrosis (METAVIR score F2 or higher).
  
**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Co-infection with hepatitis C virus (HCV), hepatitis D virus (HDV), or HIV.
- Previous treatment with nucleoside/nucleotide analogs or interferon within the last 12 months.
- Current or planned pregnancy and breastfeeding individuals.
- Presence of other significant hepatic or extrahepatic diseases impacting study integrity.

**Endpoints:**
Primary endpoints include the proportion of patients achieving HBV DNA levels < 300 copies/mL (51 IU/mL) at the 48-week visit. Secondary endpoints will focus on the rates of normalization of ALT levels, improvement in Child-Turcotte-Pugh scores, and safety profiles including occurrence of adverse events related to the intervention.

**Duration:** The treatment phase spans 48 weeks, followed by a 24-week follow-up period. Data collection will be comprehensive, considering both clinical responses and adverse events reported during the trial period.

**Data Analysis:** Statistical analyses will utilize intention-to-treat principles, with significance set at p < 0.05. Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline characteristics, while inferential statistics will evaluate primary and secondary efficacy outcomes.

**Expected Outcomes:** It is hypothesized that the combination of telbivudine and interferon will yield superior antiviral efficacy compared to interferon alone, as evidenced by a significantly higher proportion of participants achieving viral suppression and improved clinical parameters. The study design capitalizes on telbivudine's favorable pharmacokinetic profile and sustained viral suppression noted in earlier trials, thereby maximizing the likelihood of achieving clinically meaningful results over the treatment duration.

**Conclusion:** This study stands to enhance the understanding of telbivudine's effectiveness in chronic hepatitis B management, particularly its role within combination therapies. Leveraging the robust methodologies and rigorous patient selection criteria well-defined in this protocol promises to yield significant insights into the therapeutic landscape for chronic hepatitis B treatment.

**Status:** Currently recruiting participants across multicenter sites, aligned with regulatory and ethical standards as governed by authoritative health authorities.
