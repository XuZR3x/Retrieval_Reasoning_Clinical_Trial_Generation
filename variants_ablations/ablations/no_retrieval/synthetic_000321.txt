**Title**: A Phase II Clinical Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of RAD001 in Patients with Advanced Cholangiocarcinoma

**Study Identifier**: RAD001-CC-2023

**Purpose**: The primary aim of this clinical study is to assess the efficacy of RAD001 (everolimus) in patients diagnosed with advanced cholangiocarcinoma, a malignancy associated with significant morbidity and poor prognosis. We hypothesize that the targeted mechanism of action of RAD001 will inhibit the mTOR pathway, which is known to be upregulated in cholangiocarcinoma, thereby reducing tumor growth and improving patient outcomes.

**Background**: Cholangiocarcinoma is characterized by a high rate of progression and a dismal overall survival, emphasizing the necessity for innovative therapies. Recent findings on the mTOR signaling pathway have underscored its role in cholangiocarcinoma pathogenesis, supporting the rationale for utilizing RAD001 in this patient population.

**Study Design**: This is an open-label, multi-center, dose-ranging, Phase II clinical trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of RAD001 in combination with standard palliative treatments in patients with advanced or unresectable cholangiocarcinoma.

**Primary Objective**:
1. To determine the progression-free survival (PFS) rate at 6 months in patients treated with RAD001.

**Secondary Objectives**:
1. To evaluate overall survival (OS) at 12 months.
2. To assess the objective response rate (ORR) as per RECIST criteria.
3. To characterize the safety profile of RAD001 through adverse event monitoring.

**Clinical Hypothesis**: We postulate that the incorporation of RAD001 into the treatment regimen for advanced cholangiocarcinoma will lead to improved PFS and ORR due to its targeted action against the mTOR signaling pathway, thereby enhancing therapeutic efficacy.

**Study Population**: We plan to enroll up to 80 patients with histologically confirmed advanced perihilar or intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. 

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Histologically confirmed advanced cholangiocarcinoma not amenable to surgical resection.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST.
- Adequate organ function: 
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³.
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³.
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN.
  - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN.
- Willingness to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Prior treatment with mTOR inhibitors (including RAD001).
- Known hypersensitivity to RAD001 or its components.
- Presence of other malignancies within the past 5 years.
- Significant uncontrolled medical conditions that may interfere with treatment outcomes.
- Pregnant or lactating women, and those not using effective contraceptive measures.

**Methods**: 
Patients will receive RAD001 at a starting dose of 10 mg orally once daily, continuously until disease progression or intolerable toxicity occurs. All patients will also receive supportive care and symptomatic therapy according to standard practice. 

**Safety Monitoring**: Toxicities will be graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0, and regular safety assessments will be conducted biweekly. 

**Outcome Measures**: 
The primary outcome will be evaluated through imaging studies every 8 weeks, and the results will be correlated with toxicity assessments to ensure maintaining an optimal safety profile, thus supporting the prolonged usage of RAD001.

**Conclusion**: By leveraging RAD001's unique pharmacological properties and its targeted mechanism of action, this trial aspires to provide significant advancements in the management of advanced cholangiocarcinoma, a condition currently lacking effective systemic therapeutic options.
