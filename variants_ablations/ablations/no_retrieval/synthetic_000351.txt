**Failed clinical trial of mifepristone: A Randomized Study of Mifepristone in Patients with Advanced Cushing's Syndrome due to Adrenal Tumors**

**A Phase II, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Mifepristone in Patients with Advanced Cushing's Syndrome Due to Adrenal Tumors**

Mifepristone, a glucocorticoid receptor antagonist, has been postulated to ameliorate the hypercortisolism associated with Cushing's syndrome. This study aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of mifepristone in patients diagnosed with advanced Cushing's syndrome secondary to adrenal tumors inadequately controlled by prior therapies. Despite initial promising preclinical findings, this trial ultimately did not achieve its primary endpoint of demonstrable efficacy in the target population.

**Study Objectives:**  
The primary objective of this clinical trial was to evaluate the reduction of serum cortisol levels and associated clinical symptoms in patients undergoing treatment with mifepristone compared to placebo. Secondary objectives included the assessment of quality of life metrics and the incidence of adverse events related to mifepristone treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Male and female patients aged 18 years and older  
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of Cushing's syndrome due to an adrenal tumor  
- Elevated serum cortisol level (≥ 10 µg/dL) measured during the screening phase  
- Persistent Cushing's syndrome symptoms despite prior surgical, medical, or radiotherapy interventions  
- Ability to provide informed consent 

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- History of hypersensitivity to mifepristone  
- Severe underlying medical conditions precluding safe participation  
- Significant hepatic or renal impairment as defined by laboratory tests  
- Pregnancy or lactation  
- Use of medications known to extensively interact with CYP3A4 substrates within stipulated timeframes 

**Adverse Events Monitoring:**  
All participants who received at least one dose of mifepristone were monitored for adverse events throughout the study period, in conjunction with laboratory assessments to gauge hepatic, renal, and metabolic function.

**Results:**  
The study enrolled a total of 200 patients, randomized 1:1 to receive mifepristone or placebo alongside standard care. Despite a robust sample size, analyses conducted post-hoc did not reveal statistically significant reductions in cortisol levels in the mifepristone group compared to placebo. The pharmacokinetics of mifepristone exhibited considerable variability; notably, significant doses were required to achieve therapeutic effects. This variability, coupled with insufficient stratification of patients by potential responsiveness, likely contributed to the inadequate demonstration of efficacy. 

Excessive treatment-related adverse events, with reported rates of medication discontinuation at 25%, were attributable to the underestimated tolerability profile of mifepristone, particularly among patients experiencing endocrine dysregulation. The absence of predictive biomarkers for identifying likely responders may have resulted in a population that demonstrated a generalized lack of responsiveness to the intervention, further complicating interpretations of clinical efficacy.

The overall conclusion indicated that mifepristone, despite its initial promise, failed to produce clinically meaningful outcomes in this patient population, underscoring the need for more comprehensive pharmacogenomic analyses to inform future therapeutic approaches. Furthermore, the rigid inclusion criteria may have excluded a subset of participants who could have benefited from mifepristone treatment, demonstrating a necessity for more inclusive patient recruitment strategies in subsequent trials.
