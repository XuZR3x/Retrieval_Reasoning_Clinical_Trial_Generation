**Successful Clinical Trial of NBI-98854:**

**NBI-98854 for the Treatment of Tardive Dyskinesia in Patients with Schizophrenia or Schizoaffective Disorder: A Phase 3, Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of NBI-98854 in Managing Symptoms of Tardive Dyskinesia**

**Objective:**  
The objective of this study is to rigorously evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of two doses (25 mg and 50 mg) of NBI-98854 administered once daily (q.d.) over a treatment duration of 24 weeks in patients diagnosed with tardive dyskinesia (TD) secondary to schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder. 

**Design:**  
This is a Phase 3, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study. Subjects will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either NBI-98854 (at the designated dosages) or placebo for 24 weeks, followed by a 12-week open-label extension phase. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 to 65 years (inclusive) with a clinical diagnosis of schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder.
- A confirmed diagnosis of neuroleptic-induced tardive dyskinesia, meeting criteria defined in DSM-5 for a minimum of 6 months prior to the screening.
- Baseline AIMS total score of ≥10, indicating moderate severity of TD symptoms.
- Must be on a stable dose of antipsychotic medications for at least 30 days prior to enrollment.
- Good general health, with a BMI ranging from 18 to 40 kg/m².
- Females of childbearing potential must agree to use dual contraceptive methods throughout the trial.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Active clinically significant medical conditions, including but not limited to cardiovascular irregularities or substance use disorder within the previous 3 months.
- Known hypersensitivity or allergy to any component of NBI-98854.
- A history of neuroleptic malignant syndrome or significant risk of suicidal or violent behavior.
- Concurrent severe psychiatric illnesses that could interfere with completing the trial procedures.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
Change from baseline in the AIMS total score at week 24 compared to placebo, evaluated by blinded raters using standardized video assessments to ensure objective scoring.

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
- Improvement as measured by the Clinical Global Impression - Improvement of TD (CGI-TD) scale at weeks 12 and 24.
- Assessment of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and long-term safety through comprehensive vital sign monitoring and laboratory evaluations throughout the study period.
  
**Rationale:**  
This study is structured with rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria to ensure a well-defined patient population, thereby reducing variability in treatment responses, thereby maximizing the assessment of NBI-98854's efficacy. The open-label extension phase provides comprehensive longitudinal monitoring of long-term safety with a focus on tracking treatment-emergent adverse events. The comprehensive assessment of TD symptoms using established tools such as the AIMS and CGI-TD scales ensures that the evaluation is both thorough and scientifically robust.

**Enrichment of Results:**  
The targeted patient population suffering from neuroleptic-induced tardive dyskinesia and the inclusion of multi-dimensional assessment modalities allow for a more holistic understanding of NBI-98854's efficacy and safety profile. Moreover, the diligent follow-up protocols in the open-label phase enhance the validity of long-term safety data and ensure patient safety through continuous clinical oversight.

**Number of Participants:**  
Approximately 200 eligible subjects will be enrolled and monitored across multiple clinical sites, providing a substantial dataset for analysis and ensuring the robustness of the findings.

This Phase 3 study is pivotal in further delineating NBI-98854's therapeutic profile and could potentially set a new standard of care for patients suffering from TD resulting from antipsychotic treatments.
