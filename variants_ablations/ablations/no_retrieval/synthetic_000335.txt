**Rasagiline for Cognitive Dysfunction and Depression Treatment in Alzheimer's Disease Patients: ADAPT Study**

The primary objective of the ADAPT study is to rigorously evaluate the efficacy of rasagiline in improving depressive symptoms and cognitive function in patients diagnosed with mild to moderate Alzheimer’s disease who exhibit co-morbid depression. This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial is designed to elucidate the potential therapeutic benefit of rasagiline compared to a placebo over a treatment duration of 12 weeks.

The trial's primary endpoint evaluates clinical response based on changes from baseline in cognitive function assessed using the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive Subscale (ADAS-Cog) and changes in depressive symptoms measured via the Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS). In light of previous studies which highlighted rasagiline's lack of significant efficacy in treating depression and cognitive decline in other neurodegenerative disorders, careful attention has been devoted to participant inclusion criteria and study design metrics to delineate potential outcomes effectively.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male and female participants aged ≥65 and <90 years.
- Established diagnosis of probable Alzheimer's disease according to the National Institute on Aging-Alzheimer's Association criteria, exhibiting mild to moderate severity (Clinical Dementia Rating scores of 1 to 2).
- Scoring ≥5 on the GDS indicating moderate to severe depressive symptoms.
- Patients compliant with stable treatment regimens for at least 8 weeks prior to baseline, with no changes in dementia medications that could confound results.
- Provision of informed consent by the participant or a legally authorized representative, with capacity to comprehend the information.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Significant cognitive impairment as assessed by a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score <15.
- History of major depressive episodes or bipolar disorders within the last year per DSM-V criteria.
- Active or recent severe medical conditions (e.g., cardiovascular diseases, renal failure) that could compromise participant safety or study outcomes.
- Patients on concurrent treatments that are known to significantly interact with drug metabolism of monoamine oxidase inhibitors, specifically other antidepressants or central nervous system stimulants.
- History of hypersensitivity reactions to rasagiline or related compounds.

The primary objective of the ADAPT study is to determine the efficacy of rasagiline, administered at a dosage of 1 mg/day, in ameliorating symptoms of depression and cognitive dysfunction among Alzheimer’s disease patients, through analyses of changes in ADAS-Cog and GDS scores at the conclusion of the 12-week treatment period.

**Outcome Measures:**
The primary outcome measures are changes in the ADAS-Cog total score and GDS score from baseline to Week 12. Secondary outcomes will evaluate the safety and tolerability of rasagiline and its effects on measures of global clinical change, caregiver burden, and quality of life as assessed by the Quality of Life in Alzheimer's Disease (QoL-AD) scale.
