**Successful Clinical Trial of Radiotherapy**  
**Combination of Radiotherapy and Immunotherapy in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) - A Phase II Study**

**Study Overview:**  
This clinical trial aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of combining radiotherapy with a novel immune checkpoint inhibitor, Pembrolizumab, in patients diagnosed with advanced-stage non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The primary endpoint is to determine the overall response rate (ORR), with secondary endpoints including progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).

**Background:**  
NSCLC accounts for approximately 85% of lung cancer cases and presents significant treatment challenges, particularly in advanced stages. Conventional regimens often lead to suboptimal response rates and high recurrence. Recent studies have illustrated that combining radiotherapy with immunotherapy enhances the immune system's ability to target tumor cells, leveraging the synergistic effects of radiation in augmenting immunogenicity of tumor antigens.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Adults aged greater than 18 years with histologically or cytologically confirmed locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC (Stage IIIB or IV, excluding those with leptomeningeal disease).
2. Patients must have measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
3. ECOG performance status of 0-2.
4. Adequate organ function, evidenced by:
   - Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1500/mm³.
   - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³.
   - Liver function tests: total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 × ULN; ALT/AST < 2.5 × ULN.
   - Creatinine ≤ 1.5 × ULN.
5. Provision of informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Prior treatment with systemic therapy or radiation for NSCLC.
2. Active infection uncontrolled by antibiotics.
3. History of severe hypersensitivity to pembrolizumab or any excipients.
4. Presence of other malignancies within the last 5 years, excluding non-melanoma skin cancers or in situ cervical cancers.
5. Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Study Design:**  
This is a single-arm, multicenter, open-label Phase II trial. Patients will receive stereotactic radiotherapy (SBRT) directed at the primary tumor site in combination with Pembrolizumab administered intravenously every 21 days for 4 cycles followed by continuation as monotherapy for responding patients. The planned dose for SBRT will be 60 Gy delivered in three fractions.

**Primary Objectives:**  
I. Determine the overall response rate (ORR) following the combination of radiotherapy and Pembrolizumab.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
I. Assess progression-free survival (PFS) and median overall survival (OS).  
II. Evaluate the safety profile of the combination therapy through adverse event monitoring based on NCI CTCAE criteria (version 5.0).  
III. Investigate biomarkers of treatment response, including PD-L1 expression levels and T-cell receptor repertoire analysis from circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA).

**Rationale for Study Design:**  
This trial capitalizes on previously demonstrated success of integrating multimodal therapies, which have been shown to enhance therapeutic outcomes in advanced malignancies, particularly through the synergistic effects of radiotherapy to increase immunogenicity, as exemplified by clinical advancements in NSCLC via enhanced antigen presentation. Defining inclusion criteria that focus on maintaining optimal laboratory thresholds, ensures reduced complication rates while addressing individual patient physiology further strengthens overall treatment effectiveness, as previously highlighted in trials employing tailored approaches in chemotherapeutic regimens.

Following the treatment, patients will be monitored for radiographic responses via CT scans every 8 weeks according to RECIST v1.1 for up to 2 years, with additional follow-up assessments including quality-of-life questionnaires to evaluate treatment tolerability and long-term effects post-therapy.

This study represents a promising advancement in the management of advanced NSCLC and embodies the transformation towards personalized oncology strategies aimed at improving the efficacy and sustainability of treatment outcomes in an otherwise challenging patient population. The results from this trial may provide a successful framework for integrating immunomodulatory approaches alongside conventional radiotherapy in lung cancer treatment paradigms.
