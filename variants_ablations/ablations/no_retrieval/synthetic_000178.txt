**Failed Clinical Trial of Avastin: A Phase II Study of Avastin in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Trial Title:** A Phase II Study of Avastin (Bevacizumab) in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**Primary Objective:** To assess the 6-month progression-free survival (PFS) rate of patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer who are treated with a combination of Avastin and pembrolizumab as compared to historical controls receiving pembrolizumab alone.

**Secondary Objectives:**
1. Evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) and overall survival (OS) in participants receiving the combination therapy.
2. Assess the safety and tolerability of Avastin when administered in conjunction with pembrolizumab.

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm Phase II study. The trial aims to enroll 80 patients diagnosed with advanced (stage IIIB or IV) non-small cell lung cancer who are not candidates for curative treatment.

**Methods:**
- Patients will receive Avastin at a dose of 15 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks and pembrolizumab at a dose of 200 mg IV every 3 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- Tumor assessment will be performed every 8 weeks until 24 weeks and then every 12 weeks thereafter, using RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- Adverse events will be graded according to the NCI CTCAE version 5.0.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients aged ≥ 18 years.
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with measurable disease.
- ECOG performance status of 0-1.
- No prior treatment with anti-VEGF or immune checkpoint inhibitors.
- Adequate organ function as per protocol-defined criteria.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease.
- History of hypertensive crisis or poorly controlled hypertension (>150/100 mmHg).
- Prior history of arterial thromboembolic events (e.g., myocardial infarction or stroke) within the last 6 months.
- Current or recent therapeutic anticoagulation therapy.
- Any contraindications to Avastin as per established guidelines.

**Patient Enrollment and Treatment:**
A total of 80 patients were enrolled over 15 months. Treatment continued until evidence of disease progression per RECIST criteria, unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal of consent, or initiation of alternative therapies.

**Results:**
Despite initial optimism regarding the combined targeting of VEGF and PD-1 pathways, the trial demonstrated significant shortcomings:
1. **Suboptimal Efficacy**: The 6-month progression-free survival rate was notably low at 25%, failing to meet the anticipated threshold of 40%. The synergistic hypothesis regarding enhanced immune response from pembrolizumab upon tumor vascular normalization by Avastin was not realized.
   
2. **Unacceptable Toxicity Profiles**: The trial recorded a high incidence of grade 3-4 adverse events, particularly hypertension, proteinuria, and gastrointestinal toxicities. Notably, 38% of the patients experienced severe adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation, exceeding the pre-specified safety margin.

3. **Patient Selection Bias**: The inclusion criteria may have inadvertently limited the patient population to individuals with specific genetic profiles or those without significant comorbidities, creating a homogenous group that poorly represented the broader NSCLC population, thus affecting generalizability.

4. **Pharmacogenomic Variability**: Genetic testing revealed polymorphisms in VEGF receptor pathways, contributing to variability in treatment response among participants, highlighting the potential for differential efficacy attributable to hereditary factors.

5. **Inherent Tumor Resistance Mechanisms**: Examination of tumor biopsies revealed alternative angiogenic pathways that compensated for the inhibition of VEGF, undermining the efficacy of Avastin and contributing to the observed resistance mechanisms.

**Conclusion:**
The combination of Avastin with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer resulted in suboptimal therapeutic efficacy, accompanied by a concerning toxicity profile and intrinsic tumor resistance mechanisms. Further investigation into patient selection, genetic factors, and potential biomarkers is warranted, to enhance the likelihood of successful outcomes in future therapeutic strategies involving anti-angiogenic and immunotherapeutic agents.
