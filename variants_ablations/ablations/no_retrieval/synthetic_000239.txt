**Failed clinical trial of infliximab**: Evaluation of Infliximab Monotherapy in Pediatric Patients With Severe Asthma and Eosinophilia

**Background**: Severe asthma in pediatric patients is characterized by complicated airway inflammation, predominantly involving eosinophilic infiltration. Accumulating evidence suggests that TNF-α plays a critical role in the pathogenesis of inflammation in severe asthma. Given these observations, infliximab, a chimeric monoclonal antibody targeting TNF-α, was hypothesized to effectively attenuate eosinophilic inflammation and improve clinical outcomes in this population. However, the multifactorial nature of asthma pathophysiology, including the dominant role of other inflammatory mediators, raised concerns regarding the efficacy of TNF-α inhibition alone.

**Study Principle**: A multi-center, open-label, uncontrolled trial was conducted to ascertain the efficacy of infliximab monotherapy in pediatric patients suffering from severe asthma associated with marked eosinophilia, defined as eosinophil counts exceeding 500 cells/µL. 

**Study Design**: The trial enrolled patients aged 6-18 years diagnosed with severe uncontrolled asthma, incorporating post-bronchodilator FEV1 ≤ 60% predicted. Serial administration of infliximab (10 mg/kg at weeks 0, 2, 6, and 10) was intended to evaluate changes in asthma control and eosinophilic inflammation, measured via sputum eosinophilia and lung function tests.

**Rationale for this Trial**: A substantial subset of exacerbation-prone children exhibits a TH2 predominant cytokine profile that may have coexisting pathways contributing to asthma pathophysiology. While infliximab has exhibited efficacy in related disorders, such as Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis, the unique immune profile of severe asthma with eosinophilic dominance underscores the necessity for tailoring therapy to address various immunologic factors.

**Purpose of this Study**: The primary purpose was to investigate the changes in asthma control scores (ACQ-6) and to quantify eosinophilic reductions in sputum post-treatment with infliximab compared to baseline. Secondary outcomes included FEV1 measurements and frequency of exacerbations during the trial period.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
- Pediatric patients aged 6-18 years with severe asthma.
- Documented eosinophilia (≥ 500 cells/µL).
- History of frequent exacerbations necessitating systemic corticosteroids.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
- Presence of other underlying chronic respiratory disorders (e.g., cystic fibrosis, bronchopulmonary dysplasia).
- Active infections or history of malignancy.
- Evidence of significant autoimmunity such as SLE or prior treatment with agents targeting TNF-α.

**Results**: The trial failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in primary outcomes, with only a minority of subjects achieving the required reduction in asthma control scores (ACQ-6 improvement < 1.0). Furthermore, sputum eosinophilia persisted in several participants, indicating that alternative pathways believed to be involved in the pathophysiology of asthma, such as IL-5 signaling and other eosinophil-promoting cytokines, were not being effectively targeted by infliximab.

**Contributing Factors**:

1. **Inadequate Efficacy Against Pathophysiological Mechanisms**: Although TNF-α levels were noted to be elevated in patients, other inflammatory cytokines, particularly IL-5, were not inhibited by infliximab, leading to persistent eosinophilic activity and inflammation.

2. **Selection Bias in Patient Populations**: The heterogeneity in asthma phenotypes among pediatric patients complicated the interpretation of results; the trial inadvertently included patients with non-eosinophilic asthma, subsequently diluting potential effects.

3. **Therapeutic Window and Dosage Issues**: The dosing regimen and administration intervals of infliximab may not have achieved the therapeutic concentrations necessary to elicit the desired immunological effects, thus limiting the drug's capacity to modulate eosinophilic inflammation adequately.

4. **Adverse Effects and Treatment Interruption**: Several patients experienced infusion-related reactions, leading to treatment discontinuation. The incidence of upper respiratory infections following infliximab treatment further impeded the ability to assess treatment efficacy over the trial duration.

**Conclusion**: Infliximab monotherapy did not yield the anticipated clinical benefits in the management of severe asthma with eosinophilia in pediatric patients. The trial outcomes underscore the multifactorial nature of asthma and the necessity for innovative therapeutic strategies that encompass a broader range of cytokine modulation, thus warranting further investigation into combination therapies or alternative biologics targeting different inflammatory pathways.
