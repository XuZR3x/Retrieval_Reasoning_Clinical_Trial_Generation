**Failed Clinical Trial of Interleukin-2: A Phase II Study of Subcutaneous Interleukin-2 Combined with Nivolumab for Refractory Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Study Title:** A Phase II Study of Subcutaneous Interleukin-2 in Combination with Nivolumab in Patients with Refractory Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**Study Objective:** The primary objective of this trial is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of subcutaneous administration of interleukin-2 (IL-2) in conjunction with nivolumab in inducing an objective response rate in patients diagnosed with refractory non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Additionally, the study aims to investigate the potential immunomodulatory effects and overall survival associated with this therapeutic combination.

**Background:** Nivolumab is a programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitor demonstrated to enhance T cell-mediated antitumor immunity. The theoretical framework for this combined intervention rests on the assumption that IL-2 can amplify T cell responses and potentially address the suboptimal immune activation that is often observed with single-agent checkpoint inhibitors. However, previous studies have indicated that insufficient pharmacodynamic stimulation and the likelihood of Treg expansion may limit the efficacy of IL-2, leading to concerns regarding its combination with checkpoint inhibitors in such disease contexts.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with measurable disease.
- Patients must have received at least two prior therapies, including chemotherapy and at least one immune checkpoint inhibitor.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status of 0 or 1.
- Life expectancy of ≥ 3 months.
- Adequate organ function as defined by standard laboratory criteria.
- Informed consent obtained from the patient.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior systemic treatment with IL-2 or nivolumab.
- Presence of active or symptomatic central nervous system metastases.
- Autoimmune disorders or medical conditions requiring chronic immunosuppression.
- Severe cardiac or pulmonary conditions that would limit participation.
- Recent infections requiring systemic antibiotics.

**Methodology:** Patients will be stratified into two cohorts: one receiving subcutaneous IL-2 (4 million IU/m²) three times weekly for 8 weeks combined with nivolumab (3 mg/kg) administered intravenously biweekly for the same duration. The primary endpoint will be the Objective Response Rate (ORR) assessed at 24 weeks, while secondary endpoints will include progression-free survival (PFS) and safety profile evaluation.

**Primary Outcome Measure:**
- The primary outcome is the Objective Response Rate (ORR) at week 24 according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
- Progression-Free Survival (PFS) measured from the start of treatment until progressive disease or death.
- Analysis of immune biomarker response via peripheral blood immune profiling pre- and post-treatment.

**Study Design:** This study is designed as a multi-center, randomized, open-label trial. The projected sample size is 280 patients. Randomization will occur in a 1:1 ratio, allocating participants to either the treatment arm (IL-2 + nivolumab) or control (nivolumab alone).

**Trial Duration:** Estimated trial duration: 36 months with a proposed enrollment period of 12 months.

**Expected Challenges and Risk of Failure:**
1. **Suboptimal Synergistic Effect:** Despite the hypothesized immunologic synergy between IL-2 and nivolumab, historical data indicates potential lack of synergistic enhancement within immune response parameters, raising the risk of overall trial failure.
2. **Inadequate Patient Selection:** The heterogeneity of the NSCLC patient population concerning prior therapies and varying disease stages could lead to inconsistent treatment responses that undermining the study's efficacy and integrity.
3. **Immunomodulatory Imbalance:** Expansion of regulatory T cells (Tregs) due to IL-2 administration may counteract the anticipated therapeutic benefits by fostering an immunosuppressive environment, thus complicating interpretation of therapeutic outcomes.
4. **Limited Pharmacodynamic Response:** The theoretical immune activation from IL-2 may not sufficiently stimulate T cell expansion and functionality, contributing to the lack of efficacy seen in similar combination studies in refractory cancers.
5. **Insufficient Dose Optimization:** Dosing regimens for IL-2 may not have adequately achieved therapeutic thresholds required for robust systemic immune modulation, reflecting concerns regarding achieving a clinically significant response.

**Conclusion:** The current study of IL-2 combined with nivolumab aims to investigate a potential therapeutic paradigm in NSCLC; however, several risk factors identified from previous studies suggest a significant likelihood of failure to achieve the hypothesized endpoints. Ongoing analysis and careful monitoring of patient responses will be essential to understanding the complex interplay of these immunomodulatory agents.
