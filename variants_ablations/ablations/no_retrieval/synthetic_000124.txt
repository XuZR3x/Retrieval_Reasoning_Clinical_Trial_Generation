**Successful Clinical Trial of Laboratory Biomarker Analysis: Targeted Genomic Profiling in Patients With Advanced Stage Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**A Phase II Study of Laboratory Biomarker Analysis for Targeting Therapy in Patients With Advanced Stage Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

This phase II trial assesses the efficacy of utilizing laboratory biomarker analysis through targeted genomic profiling in identifying actionable mutations in patients with advanced stage non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which may inform treatment decisions and improve clinical outcomes.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. **To determine the overall response rate (ORR)** of targeted therapies in patients with advanced stage NSCLC whose tumors harbor actionable mutations identified through laboratory biomarker analyses. ORR will be defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete or partial response as per RECIST 1.1 criteria.

II. **To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS)** in patients after receiving therapy based on laboratory biomarker analysis results.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. **To assess the toxicity profile** of identified targeted therapies using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

II. **To explore the correlation between biomarkers** and clinical outcomes such as overall survival (OS).

III. **To evaluate patient-reported outcomes (PROs)** related to treatment tolerability and quality of life using validated questionnaires.

**OUTLINE:**
Patients will undergo comprehensive genomic profiling of tumor biopsy specimens and plasma samples to identify mutations, including but not limited to EGFR, ALK, ROS1, and KRAS. Identified mutations will guide the selection of targeted therapies administered in a precision medicine approach.

- Patients without progression are treated with a targeted agent based on mutational analysis for an initial duration of 12 months, with the possibility of extension based on clinical benefit and tolerability.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced stage [Stage IIIB or IV] non-small cell lung cancer.
- Availability of formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue and plasma samples for genetic testing.
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST 1.1.
- Prior chemotherapy for NSCLC is permissible; however, previous treatment with targeted agents directed against actionable mutations is excluded.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate hematological and metabolic parameters, including:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) > 1,500 cells/mm³.
  - Platelets > 100,000 cells/mm³.
  - Creatinine clearance > 30 mL/min.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Patients with active, uncontrolled inter-current conditions or those requiring concurrent use of immunosuppressive therapies.
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the study drug or previous treatment with the investigational agent.
- Patients with severe cardiac illness or any other concurrent malignant conditions (except adequately treated non-melanomatous skin cancer).

**PROGRESSION-FREE SURVIVAL:**
PFS will be defined as the time from initiation of targeted therapy until the first documented disease progression or death from any cause. Patients will be monitored bi-monthly for tumor assessment, and clinical status reports will encompass adverse events reported through CTCAE criteria alongside PRO measures.

**FOLLOW-UP:**
Patients will be followed for a period of 2 years post-treatment discontinuation, or until documented disease progression. Outcomes will include ongoing evaluation of genetic markers and their relationship with treatment efficacy as well as overall survival statistics.

This study aims to advance the field of precision oncology through robust methodologies of biomarker identification, thereby aligning patient-specific treatment regimens with distinct tumor genomic profiles. Enhanced follow-up strategies to monitor long-term outcomes and tolerability perspectives contribute to a comprehensive understanding of therapeutic effectiveness in the context of individual variability in cancer presentation.
