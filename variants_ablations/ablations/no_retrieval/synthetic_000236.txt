**Title**: A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Entinostat in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

**Study Design**: This is a multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind Phase II trial investigating the therapeutic efficacy of entinostat in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer who have progressed following at least one line of systemic therapy and express PD-L1 in at least 50% of tumor cells.

**Objectives**: 
- **Primary Objective**: To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of entinostat in combination with pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab plus placebo in an advanced NSCLC cohort.
- **Secondary Objectives**: 
   1. To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) in both treatment arms.
   2. To assess safety and tolerability through adverse event (AE) monitoring.
   3. To establish biomarker correlations related to clinical outcomes and study treatment response.

**Study Population**: 
Inclusion criteria permit adults aged ≥ 18 years with histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC, measurable disease, documented PD-L1 expression, and an ECOG performance status of 0-1. Exclusion criteria encompass prior treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors, ongoing systemic immunosuppressive therapy, and active CNS metastases.

**Treatment Regimen**: Participants are randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either entinostat (administered orally at 10 mg on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle) in conjunction with pembrolizumab (administered intravenously at 200 mg every 3 weeks) or pembrolizumab plus placebo.

**Assessment and Follow-up**: Patients are evaluated for response using RECIST criteria every 8 weeks for the first 24 weeks and then every 12 weeks thereafter. Safety evaluations, including laboratory assessments and quality of life metrics, are performed at each cycle.

**Data and Analysis**: The ORR will be calculated as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR). Statistical comparisons will be made using Fisher's exact test. PFS will be analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method with differences between arms assessed via log-rank tests.

**Results**: The trial was designed to enroll 150 patients; however, patient enrollment was significantly impeded by the following factors:

1. **Limited Efficacy in Combination Therapies**: Preliminary results indicated an absence of synergistic effects when entinostat was administered alongside pembrolizumab, mirroring previously established patterns seen with other combination therapies. The expectation for improved therapeutic response was unmet, resulting in early discontinuation in a significant proportion of participants.

2. **Poor Tolerability and Adverse Event Profiles**: There was an alarming rate of adverse events leading to treatment interruption. Specifically, the combination regimen resulted in elevated incidences of immune-related adverse effects alongside hematological toxicities, prompting safety concerns that outweighed potential therapeutic benefits.

3. **Inadequate Patient Selection**: Strict eligibility criteria inadvertently restricted enrollment. The resultant patient population displayed unexpected homogeneity concerning tumor mutational landscape and co-morbidities, ultimately compromising the study's ability to yield meaningful, generalizable conclusions.

4. **Lack of Distinct Biomarker Correlations**: The anticipated biomarkers required to identify responsive subgroups were not validated within this population, leading to ineffective patient stratification and obfuscating the systemic understanding of entinostat’s mechanistic role in conjunction with immune modulation.

5. **Progression of Disease Despite Treatment**: Many enrolled patients exhibited rapid disease progression due to advanced cancer stages and prior treatment histories that rendered them less responsive to the entinostat-pembrolizumab combination, demonstrating that the therapy was insufficient to counteract the aggressive nature of their malignancy.

**Conclusion**: The initial findings from this Phase II trial indicated a failure to demonstrate clinical efficacy, compounded by significant safety concerns, rendering entinostat's use in combination with pembrolizumab ineffective for advancing treatment of patients with NSCLC. Future investigations must address underlying biological indicators and facilitate more nuanced population selections to improve outcomes in prospective trials.

**Follow-Up**: Participants who completed the trial were monitored for further clinical outcomes and were advised about alternative therapies following the disappointing results. The study ceased enrollment early due to these concerning trends, and patients were de-briefed regarding the findings at their last visit. 

This study underscores the necessity for re-evaluation of combination strategies in targeted cancer therapies and highlights the challenges involved in translating preclinical observations into successful clinical applications.
