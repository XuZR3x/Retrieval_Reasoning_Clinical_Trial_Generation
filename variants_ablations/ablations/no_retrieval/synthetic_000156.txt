**Title**: Cabozantinib S-Malate in Combination With Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors (ICI) for Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC): A Randomized Phase II Study

**Study Protocol Identifier**: CABO-ICI/NSCLC-002

**Objective**:
To evaluate the efficacy and safety of cabozantinib s-malate in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors compared to immune checkpoint inhibitors alone in treatment-naïve patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

**Background**:
Despite evidence supporting the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., pembrolizumab, nivolumab) in NSCLC, a significant proportion of patients remain resistant or experience disease progression post-treatment. This trial seeks to explore cabozantinib s-malate, which targets the MET and VEGF pathways, in combination with ICI, potentially enhancing therapeutic outcomes. Nevertheless, reasons documented in previous trials foreshadow potential challenges, such as inadequate target engagement in the unique microenvironment of NSCLC and exaggerated adverse effect profiles.

**Primary Objective**:
To compare progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months between patients receiving cabozantinib s-malate in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors versus immune checkpoint inhibitors alone.

**Secondary Objectives**:
1. Evaluate overall survival (OS) of patients at 12 months.
2. Assess the frequency and severity of adverse events using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).
3. Analyze the objective response rates using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria.
4. Correlate biomarkers of MET expression with treatment response.

**Study Design**:
This randomized, controlled multicenter phase II trial will enroll treatment-naïve patients with advanced NSCLC, stratified by PD-L1 expression levels and smoking history.

**Outline**:
Patients will be randomized to one of two arms:
- **ARM I**: Patients receive cabozantinib s-malate orally once daily on days 1-28 and an immune checkpoint inhibitor (pembrolizumab or nivolumab) intravenously on days 1 and 15 of each 28-day cycle. 
- **ARM II**: Patients receive a comparable immune checkpoint inhibitor intravenously on days 1 and 15 of each 28-day cycle.

Courses will repeat every 28 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Following treatment, patients will be monitored for disease progression, safety, and quality of life assessments.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Histologically confirmed advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)
- At least one measurable lesion per RECIST criteria
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2
- No prior systemic therapy for NSCLC
- Adequate organ function
- Informed consent obtained

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Prior treatment with cabozantinib or any immune checkpoint inhibitors
- Previous history of severe adverse reactions to the components of cabozantinib or immune checkpoint inhibitors
- Uncontrolled hypertension or significant cardiovascular disease
- Active autoimmune disease or concurrent malignancy
- Significantly impaired gastrointestinal function, potentially affecting absorption

**Expected Outcomes**:
The trial aims to determine if cabozantinib s-malate can enhance the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors among patients with NSCLC, improving PFS and OS compared to standard immune therapy alone. However, considerations from previous trials indicate challenges, such as inadequate target engagement resulting from tumor resistance mechanisms and the potential for exaggerated toxicity due to combination therapy, which could significantly impact patient enrollment and retention.

**Statistical Analysis**:
PFS will be analyzed using Kaplan-Meier estimates. A log-rank test will be utilized to compare the two arms across PFS. Adverse events will be summarized descriptively. Biomarker correlations will be evaluated using multivariate analyses.

**Monitoring**:
Data safety monitoring will occur regularly. Any significant adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation will necessitate a review of patient safety protocols based on toxicity profiles established in earlier trials with cabozantinib.

**Ethical Considerations**:
This study will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the applicable Institutional Review Boards. Informed consent will be obtained from all participants.

---

**Conclusion**: This trial is designed to rigorously assess the efficacy and safety of cabozantinib s-malate in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors in advanced NSCLC patients. However, historical challenges, including resistance mechanisms, suboptimal dosing strategies, and toxicity profiles, necessitate cautious interpretation of the results.
