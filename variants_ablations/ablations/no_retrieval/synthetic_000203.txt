**Failed Clinical Trial of Risperidone: Risperidone in the Treatment of Mixed Anxiety and Mood Disorder in Adolescents**

**Study Title:** A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Risperidone in Adolescents with Mixed Anxiety and Mood Disorder

**Study Overview:**  
This clinical trial aimed to assess the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of risperidone in adolescents aged 12 to 18 diagnosed with Mixed Anxiety and Mood Disorder (MAMD). The primary objective was to determine if risperidone would yield a statistically significant reduction in mixed mood and anxiety symptoms compared to placebo over a treatment period of 10 weeks. Despite the potential for risperidone to mitigate symptoms of MAMD based on its pharmacologic profile, this study ultimately failed to demonstrate clinically meaningful benefits.

**Background:**  
Risperidone, an atypical antipsychotic, has demonstrated efficacy in the treatment of schizophrenia and bipolar disorder; however, its role in the management of MAMD has been primarily unexplored. This study sought to evaluate its effects on reducing anxiety while mitigating mood symptoms in a vulnerable adolescent population, a specified demographic with underrepresented clinical trials.

**Methods and Procedures:**  
This multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 150 subjects, targeting those with MAMD characterized by significant anxiety symptoms accompanied by fluctuating mood states. Subjects were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive risperidone or placebo over a period of 10 weeks. The initial dose of risperidone was set at 0.5 mg/day with titrations permitted based on clinical response and tolerability, not to exceed 3 mg/day.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Age between 12 and 18 years.
2. Diagnosis of MAMD as defined by DSM-5 criteria.
3. At least a score of 15 on the Hamilton Anxiety Scale (HAM-A), indicating moderate anxiety severity.
4. Informed consent obtained from the participant and their legal guardian.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of psychotic disorders or bipolar disorder.
2. Current use of any psychotropic medication.
3. Known hypersensitivity to risperidone.
4. Significant medical comorbidities affecting study feasibility.

**Outcomes:**  
Efficacy was primarily assessed using the HAM-A, along with the Young Mania Rating Scale (YMRS) to track mood fluctuations. Secondary outcomes included treatment-emergent adverse event monitoring.

**Results:**  
Upon completion, data analysis indicated no statistically significant difference between the risperidone and placebo groups in reducing overall anxiety symptoms and mood instability. The failure of risperidone to engage effectively in the proposed neurobiological mechanisms underlying MAMD raised concerns regarding its applicability in such a heterogeneous patient population. 

**Discussion of Factors Contributing to Failure:**
1. **Ineffective Pharmacological Mechanism:** The trial outcomes indicated insufficient engagement of key neurotransmitter pathways implicated in anxiety and mood regulation, demonstrated by a lack of significant symptom alleviation among participants.
   
2. **Secondary Effects on Symptomatology:** A notable rate of withdrawal due to undesirable side effects, including sedation, metabolic disturbances, and extrapyramidal symptoms, complicated participant adherence and likely contributed to the observed inefficacy.

3. **Inappropriate Patient Population:** The variable presentation of mixed anxiety and mood symptoms in the adolescent cohort led to inconsistent individual responses. Many subjects had comorbid conditions which may have obscured the specific impact of risperidone.

4. **Short Duration of Treatment:** The 10-week study period may have been inadequate to observe tangible changes in chronic emotional dysregulation, necessitating longer observation periods to fully evaluate potential long-term benefits of treatment.

5. **Complexities of Concurrent Conditions:** The recruitment of a diverse patient population with overlapping psychiatric diagnoses introduced additional variability that diluted potential therapeutic outcomes with risperidone.

**Conclusion:**  
This clinical trial achieved its objectives of evaluating risperidone in a potential new treatment paradigm for MAMD among adolescents; however, it ultimately failed to yield the expected reduction in symptoms, highlighting the complexities involved in utilizing risperidone for this indication. Future investigations into pharmacological agents for MAMD should consider more homogenous inclusion criteria, longer treatment durations, and tailored doses conducive to individual patient responses.
