**A Phase II Clinical Trial of Lenalidomide in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Relapsed/Refractory T-cell Lymphoma**

**Study Overview:**
This Phase II, open-label, multicenter study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of lenalidomide in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with relapsed or refractory T-cell lymphomas (TCL). Given the pharmacological profile of lenalidomide as an immunomodulatory agent, we hypothesize that it may enhance the tumor-killing action of pembrolizumab, a monoclonal antibody that inhibits the PD-1 pathway, thereby improving response rates in this patient population.

**Rationale:**
1. **Mechanisms of Action:** Lenalidomide promotes T-cell and NK cell-mediated responses, which, in conjunction with the immune checkpoint inhibition provided by pembrolizumab, has the potential to mount a more robust anti-tumor immune response in TCL.

2. **Patient Selection:** The inclusion criteria are specifically designed to recruit patients exhibiting measurable disease who have documented progression despite prior therapies, ensuring that the studied population reflects those most in need of effective novel treatment modalities.

3. **Safety Profile:** Prior safety data indicate that lenalidomide is well tolerated, even in patients with multiple prior therapies, therefore providing a reassuring context for its combination with pembrolizumab, an agent with its own established safety profile.

4. **Study Design:** The study employs a prospective and controlled design with clearly defined response evaluation metrics, allowing for the detection of both efficacy and safety signals. Use of pre-defined response criteria based on Chesson criteria will lend both precision and clarity to results.

5. **Standardized Regimen:** Patients will receive lenalidomide at a standard therapeutic dose of 25 mg PO daily on Days 1-21 of each 28-day cycle, coupled with pembrolizumab (200 mg IV) on Day 1 of each cycle, thereby ensuring consistent drug exposure throughout the trial.

**Background:**
T-cell lymphomas are traditionally resistant to standard therapies, necessitating the exploration of novel therapeutic combinations. Lenalidomide has shown promising activity in B-cell malignancies, prompting investigation into its utility in TCL. Pembrolizumab has demonstrated durable responses in various lymphomas; hence its co-administration with lenalidomide may synergistically enhance immune-mediated tumor control mechanisms.

**Primary Objective:**
To assess the overall response rate (ORR) in subjects with T-cell lymphoma treated with lenalidomide combined with pembrolizumab.

**Secondary Objectives:**
To evaluate the complete response rate (CR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety profile in patients receiving this treatment combination.

**Study Design:**
This is a prospective, open-label, Phase II clinical trial across five oncology centers in Canada.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients must have histologically confirmed T-cell lymphoma (subtypes including but not limited to peripheral T-cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma, and anaplastic large cell lymphoma).
- Measurable disease as defined by the revised Cheson criteria.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Patients must have received at least one prior therapy with an acceptable performance status.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Known hypersensitivity to lenalidomide or pembrolizumab.
- Concurrent malignancies except for non-melanoma skin cancers.
- Pregnant or lactating individuals.
- Active autoimmune disorders or history of severe hypersensitivity reactions.

**Recruitment and Monitoring:**
Subject recruitment will occur at multiple sites with appropriate oversight to assure compliance with all regulatory requirements. A Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will review safety data at regular intervals, particularly following accrual of the first 25 patients to assess early indications of therapeutic efficacy or safety concerns.

**Statistical Analysis:**
The sample size will encompass 80 patients to assess the objective response rates with a significance level set at 0.05. Power calculations indicate that if the true ORR is 30%, the study will achieve 80% power to detect this effect.

**Outcomes:**
The primary outcome will be measured as the proportion of patients achieving CR or partial response (PR), while secondary outcomes will assess PFS and OS, as well as incidences of adverse effects throughout treatment.

**Conclusion:**
This clinical trial of lenalidomide combined with pembrolizumab represents a novel therapeutic approach for patients with relapsed or refractory T-cell lymphoma. Enhanced immune responses enabled by lenalidomide, along with the checkpoint blockade provided by pembrolizumab, holds promise for improving patient outcomes in an area of significant unmet clinical need. Results of this investigation could provide a basis for expanded approaches to combination immunotherapy in T-cell lymphomas.
