**Clinical Trial Title:** Safety, Pharmacokinetics, and Efficacy of Pomalidomide in Combination with Novel Immunotherapeutic Agents in Patients with Relapsed and Refractory Multiple Myeloma: A Phase 1, Multicenter, Open-label, Dose-escalation Study

**Background:**  
Pomalidomide (POM), an immunomodulatory agent, has demonstrated significant activity in patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma (MM). The pharmacokinetic profile of pomalidomide, particularly when paired with novel immunotherapeutics, suggests the possibility of synergistic interactions that could enhance therapeutic efficacy. Given the urgency for innovative solutions in this patient population, this trial aims to explore the combination of pomalidomide with investigational immunotherapeutic agents, guided by strategic selection criteria.

**Objective:**  
The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of pomalidomide in combination with novel immunotherapeutic agents in subjects with relapsed or refractory MM. Secondary objectives include assessments of safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics (PK), and preliminary efficacy.

**Study Design:**  
This phase 1 trial will enroll subjects across multiple centers. It will utilize a 3 + 3 dose-escalation design to identify the MTD of pomalidomide in conjunction with the investigational agents. Safety evaluations will occur at regular intervals, with comprehensive biomarker assessments conducted throughout the treatment course to facilitate correlation with clinical responses.

**Participants:**  
Participants will include adults aged ≥18 years with a documented diagnosis of relapsed or refractory MM, having received at least two prior lines of therapy, including both lenalidomide and bortezomib. The population will be defined by measurable disease, with documented disease progression within 60 days after the last treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients must be ≥18 years old at the time of informed consent.
2. Documented diagnosis of multiple myeloma and measurable disease as defined by serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP).
3. History of relapse or refractory disease after at least two different lines of anti-myeloma treatment, which must include both lenalidomide and bortezomib.
4. Ability to provide informed consent and adhere to study protocols.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Active second malignancies (excluding certain skin cancers) or history of prior malignancies unless in complete remission for ≥5 years.
2. Known hypersensitivity reactions to pomalidomide or any components of the investigational regimen.
3. Current treatment with systemic corticosteroids exceeding the equivalent of 10 mg/day of prednisone.
4. Significant comorbid conditions or laboratory abnormalities placing participants at undue risk.

**Endpoints:**  
*Primary Endpoints:*
- Determination of MTD for the pomalidomide-immunotherapy combination.
- Incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs).

*Secondary Endpoints:*
- Evaluate response rates according to the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) criteria.
- Assess pharmacokinetics of pomalidomide and associated agents.
- Correlate biomarker assessments with treatment outcomes to identify predictive markers of efficacy.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
A descriptive statistical approach will be utilized to summarize patient demographics and treatment outcomes. The MTD will be estimated based on the occurrence of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) during the first treatment cycle. Efficacy assessments will employ a Kaplan-Meier method for survival analyses.

**Ethical Considerations:**  
The trial will be conducted following the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. Informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to inclusion.

---

This clinical trial builds upon the successful elements identified in previous assessments of pomalidomide, incorporating a multicenter design to enhance robustness and participant diversity, and leveraging detailed biomarker evaluations to elucidate treatment mechanics, ultimately fostering improvements in response rates for patients facing relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma.
