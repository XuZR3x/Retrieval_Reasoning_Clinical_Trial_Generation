**Successful Clinical Trial of Sarilumab: A Multinational Phase III Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Sarilumab in Combination with Non-MTX DMARDs in Patients with Active Rheumatoid Arthritis (SARIL-RA-GLOBAL)**

**A Randomized, Double-blind, Multicenter Study to Evaluate the Clinical Efficacy and Safety of Sarilumab in Combination with Non-MTX Disease-Modifying Antirheumatic Drugs in Patients with Active Rheumatoid Arthritis**

**Primary Objective:**  
To evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety of sarilumab administered in conjunction with stable doses of non-methotrexate (non-MTX) DMARDs in patients diagnosed with moderately to severely active rheumatoid arthritis.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
- To assess the change in disease activity as measured by the Disease Activity Score 28 (DAS28-ESR) at 24 and 52 weeks.  
- To evaluate patient-reported outcomes (PROs), specifically the Health Assessment Questionnaire Disability Index (HAQ-DI) and the Rheumatoid Arthritis Quality of Life (RAQoL), at various time points during the study.  
- To monitor the incidence and type of adverse events occurring throughout the treatment period.

**Total Study Duration:**  
Up to 64 weeks: including a 4-week screening period, a 52-week randomized treatment phase, and a 8-week follow-up period for safety assessments.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) based on ACR/EULAR classification criteria, with a disease duration of at least three months.  
- Patients exhibiting moderately to severely active RA defined as:  
  - At least 6 of 68 tender joints and 6 of 66 swollen joints at screening.  
  - hs-CRP ≥ 3 mg/L or ESR ≥ 28 mm/h at screening.  
- Eligible participants must have received non-biologic DMARDs other than MTX for a minimum of 12 weeks prior to randomization at a stable dose for at least 6 weeks.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Individuals under 18 years of age.  
- Prior treatment with any biologic DMARDs or targeted synthetic DMARDs, including anti-IL-6 therapies, without appropriate washout periods prior to screening.  
- Current or recent (>4 weeks before screening) treatment with intra-articular or systemic glucocorticoids.  

**Study Design:**  
This phase III study employed a multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled design across multiple international sites. Patients were randomized to receive sarilumab or placebo added to stable doses of non-MTX DMARDs. The strategic design of the study, including robust statistical methodologies and predefined outcomes, ensured a rigorous assessment of both the short-term and long-term effects of sarilumab therapy on this carefully selected patient population.

**Endpoints:**  
- **Primary Endpoint:** Change from baseline in DAS28-ESR at Week 52.  
- **Secondary Endpoints:**  
  - Changes in HAQ-DI and RAQoL scores at Weeks 24 and 52.  
  - Safety profile evaluated through the incidence of adverse events throughout the duration of the study.  

**Results:**  
The results demonstrated that sarilumab significantly improved DAS28-ESR scores compared to placebo by Week 52, yielding clinically meaningful benefits in terms of reduction in joint inflammation and improvement in overall disease control. Additionally, the therapeutic combination was well-tolerated, with adverse events reported at rates comparable to the placebo group. Patient-reported outcomes indicated notable enhancements in quality of life and functional status, thus supporting the inclusion of sarilumab as a vital component in the therapeutic armamentarium for managing RA.

**Conclusions:**  
The findings from the SARIL-RA-GLOBAL study underscore the efficacy and safety of sarilumab added to non-MTX DMARDs in patients with actively symptomatic rheumatoid arthritis. The well-defined inclusion criteria, rigorous endpoint assessments, and thorough long-term monitoring contributed to the success of this clinical trial, thereby validating sarilumab as an effective therapeutic option for this patient population. This study paves the way for further research into the long-term benefits and broader applications of sarilumab in rheumatology.
