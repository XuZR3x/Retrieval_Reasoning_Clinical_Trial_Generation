<clinical_trial>
  <title>Longitudinal Evaluation of Rosiglitazone's Impact on Metabolic Health and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes and Elevated CVD Risk: The ROSIME Study</title>
  <abstract>
    The ROSIME study aims to assess the long-term effects of rosiglitazone on metabolic health and cardiovascular outcomes in patients with type 2 diabetes exhibiting multifactorial cardiovascular disease risk profiles. We hypothesize that rosiglitazone treatment will significantly enhance insulin sensitivity, lower inflammatory markers, and improve cardiovascular health over a 52-week intervention period as compared to standard diabetes therapy including metformin.
  </abstract>
  
  <introduction>
    Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is increasingly recognized as a condition with a strong cardiovascular component. Recent meta-analyses indicate a concerning prevalence of cardiovascular events among diabetic populations, necessitating effective interventions to address both glycemic control and cardiovascular risk factors simultaneously. Rosiglitazone, a potent PPAR-γ agonist, enhances insulin sensitivity across various tissues while exerting favorable effects on inflammatory cytokine profiles and adipokine secretion profiles. This study will examine rosiglitazone’s capability to mitigate cardiovascular risk alongside improving glycemic control.
  </introduction>
  
  <methods>
    <design>Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.</design>
    <population>
      <inclusion_criteria>
        - Adults aged 45-75 years with a diagnosis of type 2 diabetes.
        - Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) levels ≥ 7% and ≤ 10%.
        - Patients exhibiting at least one prevalent cardiovascular risk factor, including hypertension or dyslipidemia.
      </inclusion_criteria>
      <exclusion_criteria>
        - History of myocardial infarction or stroke within the last 12 months.
        - Current therapy with any thiazolidinedione.
        - Renal dysfunction (creatinine > 1.5 mg/dL) or hepatic diseases.
        - Pregnant or lactating women.
      </exclusion_criteria>
    </population>
    <intervention>
      <treatment_group>
        - Rosiglitazone (4 mg/day) combined with standard diabetes care.
      </treatment_group>
      <placebo_group>
        - Placebo combined with standard diabetes care.
      </placebo_group>
    </intervention>
    <duration>52 weeks</duration>
  </methods>

  <outcome_measures>
    <primary_outcome>
      - Change in HbA1c from baseline to week 52.
    </primary_outcome>
    <secondary_outcomes>
      - Change in inflammatory biomarkers (hs-CRP, IL-6) from baseline to week 52.
      - Change in adipokines (leptin, adiponectin).
      - Assessment of insulin sensitivity using euglycemic-hyperinsulinemic clamps.
      - Incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) during the trial period.
    </secondary_outcomes>
  </outcome_measures>

  <results>
    The preliminary findings demonstrate significant reductions in HbA1c (average reduction of 1.3%) in the rosiglitazone group compared to placebo (average reduction of 0.5%). Furthermore, post-treatment levels of hs-CRP decreased by 25% and IL-6 by 20%, indicating an effective modulation of the inflammatory response. Notably, there was a notable elevation in adiponectin levels (29%) alongside a decrease in leptin (15%), suggesting favorable changes in adipokine profiles associated with improved metabolic dynamics in the rosiglitazone cohort.
  </results>
  
  <conclusion>
    The ROSIME study supports the therapeutic efficacy of rosiglitazone in enhancing glycemic control while concurrently attenuating inflammatory markers and improving cardiovascular risk profiles in type 2 diabetes patients with comorbidities. The utilization of advanced metabolic monitoring tools fortified the analytical rigor of the findings. Future studies are warranted to corroborate these promising outcomes and elucidate the long-term cardiovascular benefits associated with rosiglitazone therapy.
  </conclusion>
