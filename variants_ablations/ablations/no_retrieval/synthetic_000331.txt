**Failed Clinical Trial of Bevacizumab: Evaluation of Bevacizumab in Combination with Chemotherapy in Patients with Triple-Negative Breast Cancer**

**A Phase II Trial of Bevacizumab Combined with Weekly Paclitaxel in Patients with Recurrent or Metastatic Triple-Negative Breast Cancer**

**RATIONALE**: Bevacizumab is a monoclonal antibody that inhibits vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), which plays a key role in tumor angiogenesis. In prior studies, the combination of bevacizumab with various chemotherapeutic agents has shown conflicting results, particularly in advanced malignancies. In this trial, the efficacy of bevacizumab in conjunction with weekly paclitaxel was explored in patients with triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), characterized by a lack of estrogen and progesterone receptors and HER2 expression.

**PURPOSE**: This phase II trial aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of bevacizumab in combination with weekly paclitaxel in patients with recurrent or metastatic TNBC, measuring progression-free survival (PFS) and overall response rate (ORR).

**OBJECTIVES**: 
- **Primary**: 
  - To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) of patients receiving weekly paclitaxel combined with bevacizumab compared to historical controls. 
- **Secondary**: 
  - To assess the overall response rates (ORR) to the treatment.
  - To evaluate the safety profile of the combined regimen.

**OUTLINE**: This multicenter study enrolled patients who received bevacizumab intravenously over 30-90 minutes on day 1, in conjunction with paclitaxel administered weekly for 12 weeks. Treatment cycles were repeated every 3 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurred. Following completion of study treatments, patients were monitored periodically for disease status.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA**:
1. Histologically or cytologically confirmed triple-negative breast cancer.
2. Locally recurrent or metastatic disease, with measurable disease defined as at least one lesion ≥ 20 mm by conventional techniques or ≥ 10 mm by spiral CT scan.
3. ECOG Performance Status 0-2.
4. Adequate organ function as demonstrated by:
   - ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³.
   - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³.
   - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL.
5. Patients must provide written informed consent prior to study entry.
6. Life expectancy > 3 months.
7. No prior treatment with bevacizumab or taxane-based chemotherapy.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA**:
1. Known central nervous system metastases.
2. Uncontrolled hypertension not managed with medications.
3. Significant comorbidities including cardiovascular disease or severe pulmonary disease that may interfere with treatment.
4. Current use of anticoagulants that may increase bleeding risk.
5. Severe proteinuria evident on urinalysis (≥ 2+).
6. Any history of gastrointestinal perforation or fistula within the past year.
7. Inadequate recovery from prior surgeries or uncontrolled infections.
8. Prior history of adverse reaction to bevacizumab or paclitaxel.

**RESULTS**: The trial was discontinued due to a lack of sufficient efficacy in terms of progression-free survival. Data analysis revealed that the majority of patients exhibited either stable disease or disease progression soon after treatment initiation, suggesting a potential lack of efficacy in this specific patient population that may have intrinsic mechanisms of resistance to both bevacizumab and paclitaxel. Compounding factors included the adverse effects of treatment, which led to discontinuation in several cases and possibly impacted the overall response rates.

**CONCLUSION**: The results of this trial underscore the importance of careful consideration in patient selection, particularly regarding known resistance mechanisms in TNBC. The findings indicate that the combination of bevacizumab and chemotherapy may not yield the anticipated therapeutic benefit in patients with advanced triple-negative breast cancer. Further research is warranted to explore alternative combinations and identify predictive biomarkers for sensitivity to anti-VEGF therapies in this challenging malignancy.
