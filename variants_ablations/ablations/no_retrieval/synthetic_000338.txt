**Failed Clinical Trial of Vismodegib: A Phase II, Multicenter, Open-label Study of Vismodegib in Patients with Locally Advanced Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Skin**

**Background**: This trial was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vismodegib in patients diagnosed with locally advanced squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the skin who were not candidates for curative surgery or radiation therapy. Despite initial preclinical evidence suggesting activity against skin malignancies, the trial was terminated due to several factors contributing to inadequate treatment response.

**Study Design**: This Phase II, open-label study enrolled patients from multiple centers and included a primary endpoint of overall response rate (ORR) assessed through the rate of complete and partial responses after an 8-week treatment period. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety profile evaluation.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
- Patients aged ≥ 18 years with histologically confirmed diagnosis of locally advanced SCC, specifically targeting cutaneous manifestations.
- Patients must not have received prior therapy targeting the Hedgehog pathway.
- Life expectancy of at least 3 months.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status of 0-2.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
- History of other malignancies within 3 years, excluding non-melanoma skin cancers.
- Concurrent chemotherapy or radiation therapy.
- Hormonal therapies or investigational drugs within 28 days preceding the study medication.
- Significant uncontrolled comorbidities potentially impacting treatment tolerability.

**Treatment Administration**: 
Patients received vismodegib at a dose of 150 mg administered orally once daily. Treatment continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal.

**Endpoints**: 
- Primary endpoint focused on the ORR, defined as complete response (CR) plus partial response (PR) assessed via RECIST criteria.
- Secondary endpoints included measurement of PFS and OS alongside safety evaluations through adverse event monitoring and laboratory assessments.

**Study Results**: 
A total of 50 patients were enrolled, with the median age of participants being 64 years. The study results demonstrated that the ORR was a disappointing 18%, with only 2 patients achieving a CR and 6 a PR. The high rate of disease progression and treatment discontinuation due to adverse effects, including severe pruritus, muscle spasms, and dysgeusia, contributed to the low efficacy results. Notably, pharmacogenetic screening indicated a significant variability in Hedgehog pathway modulation among the tumor samples analyzed, highlighting the heterogeneity of tumor biology that affected treatment responses. This provided evidence that resistance mechanisms were likely at play, contributing to poor clinical outcomes.

**Adverse Events**: 
Documentation of treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) was significant, with grade 3 and 4 toxicities reported in 30% of participants leading to treatment discontinuation. The majority of adverse events were manageable, but their prevalence underscored a high burden that led to inadequate compliance and ultimately influenced the trial's overall efficacy.

**Conclusion**: 
This Phase II trial of vismodegib in patients with locally advanced squamous cell carcinoma of the skin failed to meet its primary endpoint of overall response rate. Factors contributing to this failure included significant heterogeneity in tumor biology, limited patient selection criteria, and a frequency of resistance mechanisms inherent to distinct SCC tumors. High rates of treatment-related toxicities further limited patient compliance, leading to an inability to establish sufficient drug exposure to ascertain effective outcomes. The findings highlight the necessity for more nuanced patient stratification and perhaps combination therapies to address the diverse and complex nature of advanced skin malignancies.
