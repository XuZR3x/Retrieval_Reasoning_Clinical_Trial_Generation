**Failed Clinical Trial of Azacitidine: Azacitidine in Combination with Pembrolizumab for High-Risk Neuroblastoma**  
A Phase II Study to Assess the Efficacy of Azacitidine Coupled with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Relapsed or Refractory High-Risk Neuroblastoma   

This clinical trial investigates the combined effect of azacitidine, a demethylating agent, and pembrolizumab, a checkpoint inhibitor, in children diagnosed with relapsed or refractory high-risk neuroblastoma. Despite expectations surrounding the synergistic potential of epigenetic modulation and immune checkpoint inhibition, the trial failed to meet predetermined endpoints.  

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) of azacitidine in combination with pembrolizumab in relapsed or refractory high-risk neuroblastoma after treatment over a six-month period.  

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) at 12 months from treatment initiation.  
II. To identify the pharmacodynamic effects of azacitidine on the expression of immune checkpoint proteins and the immune cell infiltrate within the tumor microenvironment through immunohistochemistry and flow cytometry analyses.  

**OUTLINE:**  
Patients will receive azacitidine by subcutaneous injection at a dose of 75 mg/m² for five consecutive days, followed by pembrolizumab administered intravenously at a dose of 2 mg/kg every three weeks for a total duration of six months. Treatment will continue in the absence of unacceptable toxicity or disease progression.  

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed relapsed or refractory high-risk neuroblastoma.  
- Patients aged 12 months to 21 years.  
- At least one measurable lesion per RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–2.  
- Adequate organ function as defined by specific laboratory criteria.  
- Signed informed consent from patients or guardians.  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Prior treatment with any immune checkpoint inhibitors.  
- Use of investigational agents or chemotherapy within four weeks prior to study enrollment.  
- Concurrent major systemic illness or uncontrolled infections.  
- Known active central nervous system metastases.  
- History of autoimmune disease or immunodeficiency conditions.  

The trial aimed to recruit a cohort of 120 eligible patients. Unfortunately, the combination therapy failed to demonstrate significant efficacy based on the ORR, with a mere 15% of patients exhibiting measurable tumor response after six cycles of treatment compared to the historical control benchmarks, indicating a lack of sufficient biological activity stemming from azacitidine’s intended epigenetic reprogramming effects.  

The challenges leading to the trial's failure can be attributed to multiple factors, including:  

1. The inherent complexity and heterogeneity of neuroblastoma, a disease characterized by diverse genetic and epigenetic profiles, may have masked the potential benefits of azacitidine in a population previously treated with multiple lines of therapy containing varied mechanisms of action.  

2. Mechanistic limitations of azacitidine in eliciting durable immune activation in a highly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment typical of neuroblastoma may have thwarted expected synergistic responses when combined with pembrolizumab.  

3. Insufficient sample size, resulting from stringent eligibility criteria, may have compromised the robustness of statistical analyses and restricted generalizability to the wider high-risk neuroblastoma population.  

4. The pharmacokinetic interaction profile between azacitidine and pembrolizumab might have led to suboptimal exposure to one or both drugs, impeding the manifestation of the desired clinical outcomes.  

5. The adverse event profiles and tolerability issues associated with the combination regimen could have resulted in significant treatment interruptions, dose adjustments, or early termination, thereby impacting the overall efficacy of the therapeutic approach.  

In conclusion, while azacitidine's demethylating properties offered considerable promise for enhancing the immunogenicity of tumors, the intricate clinical realities of treating high-risk neuroblastoma ultimately precluded the success of this combination therapy trial.
