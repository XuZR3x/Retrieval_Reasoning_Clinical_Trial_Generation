<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Evaluation of Metformin's Role in Cardiovascular Risk Reduction Among Overweight Adults with Prediabetes: A 52-Week Randomized Controlled Trial</Title>
    <Abstract>This study aims to investigate the effect of metformin on cardiovascular risk factors in overweight adults diagnosed with prediabetes. Prior research suggests that metformin not only enhances glycemic control but also exerts beneficial effects by improving systemic inflammation and lipid profiles. This trial is designed to evaluate the implications of these effects by employing a comprehensive approach to alter cardiovascular risk parameters, including blood pressure, lipid levels, and inflammatory markers.</Abstract>
    <StudyDesign>
        <Description>A 52-Week, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial</Description>
        <Rationale>The purpose of this trial is to elucidate the cardiovascular protective potential of metformin in a cohort at increased risk for Type 2 diabetes. Given metformin's demonstrated efficacy in reducing insulin resistance and promoting a favorable metabolic profile, it is hypothesized that these mechanisms may further contribute to cardiovascular risk mitigation.</Rationale>
    </StudyDesign>
    <Population>
        <InclusionCriteria>
            <Criterion>Adults aged 30-65 years</Criterion>
            <Criterion>Body mass index (BMI) ≥ 28 kg/m²</Criterion>
            <Criterion>Diagnosed with prediabetes defined by the American Diabetes Association</Criterion>
            <Criterion>Current stable physical activity level and dietary habits</Criterion>
        </InclusionCriteria>
        <ExclusionCriteria>
            <Criterion>History of cardiovascular disease or major depression</Criterion>
            <Criterion>Chronic kidney disease (eGFR < 60 mL/min)</Criterion>
            <Criterion>History of severe allergic reactions to metformin</Criterion>
            <Criterion>Concurrent use of medications affecting cardiovascular outcome measures</Criterion>
        </ExclusionCriteria>
    </Population>
    <Intervention>
        <Description>Participants will receive metformin at a dose of 1500 mg/day or matched placebo for a duration of 52 weeks.</Description>
        <Mechanism>The drug's complex biochemical interactions—such as the enhancement of peripheral glucose uptake, reduction of hepatic gluconeogenesis, and modulation of inflammatory pathways—will be monitored throughout the trial to evaluate its comprehensive impact on cardiovascular health.</Mechanism>
    </Intervention>
    <Outcomes>
        <PrimaryOutcome>Change in 10-year Framingham cardiovascular risk score from baseline to 52 weeks.</PrimaryOutcome>
        <SecondaryOutcomes>
            <Outcome>Changes in lipid profile (HDL, LDL, total cholesterol, and triglycerides).</Outcome>
            <Outcome>Alterations in blood pressure and body composition (waist circumference and BMI).</Outcome>
            <Outcome>Evaluation of inflammatory markers (hs-CRP, interleukin-6).</Outcome>
        </SecondaryOutcomes>
    </Outcomes>
    <StatisticalAnalysis>
        <Description>Data will be analyzed using intention-to-treat principles. Differences in primary and secondary outcomes will be assessed using appropriate statistical methods, such as ANCOVA for continuous measures.</Description>
    </StatisticalAnalysis>
    <Discussion>The thoughtful consideration of inclusion criteria ensures the appropriate patient cohort responsive to metformin’s effects, fostering high fidelity of results reflective of the drug's real-world efficacy in reducing cardiovascular risk factors. The comprehensive analysis of biochemical and clinical endpoints will help to delineate the multifactorial benefits associated with this therapeutic approach.</Discussion>
</ClinicalTrial>
