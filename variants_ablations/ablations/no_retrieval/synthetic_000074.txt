**Successful clinical trial of aflibercept: The Efficacy of Aflibercept in Combination with Standardized Laser Photocoagulation for Persistent Diabetic Macular Edema**

**A Phase II Randomized, Controlled Study Evaluating Intravitreal Aflibercept in Combination with Standard Photocoagulation Therapy in Persistent Diabetic Macular Edema**

**Objective**  
The purpose of this study is to evaluate the combined efficacy and safety of intravitreal aflibercept and standardized laser photocoagulation in the management of persistent diabetic macular edema (DME) that has not responded adequately to previous therapies alone. The trial is designed to assess improvements in visual acuity and reductions in central retinal thickness as primary outcomes.

**Background**  
Diabetic macular edema remains a leading cause of vision loss among working-age individuals due to diabetes mellitus. Current interventions, specifically anti-VEGF therapies, have shown promise; however, a noteworthy proportion of patients fails to achieve desired therapeutic goals. Laser photocoagulation has historically been utilized as a treatment but may not address the underlying vascular abnormalities sufficiently. The efficacy demonstrated by aflibercept in previous studies supports its investigation in conjunction with laser therapy for patients with persistent DME.

**Inclusion Criteria**  
1. Signed informed consent.
2. Adult men and women aged 18 yeas or older.
3. Presence of persistent DME with foveal involvement as confirmed by optical coherence tomography (OCT).
4. Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) ranging from 20/50 to 20/400 in the study eye.
5. Previous anti-VEGF therapy permissible if administered more than 3 months prior to enrollment.
6. Evidence of central subfield retinal thickness (CST) > 300 microns based on OCT.
7. Ability and willingness to comply with study protocol.

**Exclusion Criteria**  
1. Any prior or concomitant treatment with corticosteroids or other investigational therapies in the study eye.
2. Significant concomitant ocular pathology, including advanced glaucoma or retinal vascular occlusions that compromise the outcome assessment.
3. Uncontrolled systemic diseases, such as hypertension or diabetes, that could interfere with the treatment or assessment.
4. History of hypersensitivity to aflibercept or fluorescein dye.
5. Recent (within 3 months preceding enrollment) ocular surgical intervention including vitrectomy or cataract surgery in the study eye.
6. Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Study Design**  
Participants will be randomly assigned to one of two groups: the experimental group receiving intravitreal aflibercept (2 mg) injections monthly for three months followed by standardized laser photocoagulation, and the control group receiving standard laser treatment alone. Evaluations of visual acuity changes using Snellen chart, as well as CST measurements via OCT, will occur at baseline, 3 months (end of initial anti-VEGF treatment), and at 6 and 12 months post-intervention.

**Statistical Analysis**  
Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline characteristics. Changes in BCVA and CST from baseline to post-treatment time points will be analyzed using mixed-models analysis of variance (ANOVA) for repeated measures, considering a p-value of <0.05 as statistically significant.

**Outcome Measures**  
1. **Primary Outcomes**: 
   - Change in best-corrected visual acuity from baseline to 12 months follow-up.
   - Change in central subfield thickness as measured by OCT from baseline to 12 months.
  
2. **Secondary Outcomes**: 
   - Rate and incidence of adverse events associated with aflibercept administration.
   - Patient-reported outcomes regarding vision-related quality of life measured by the Vision-Related Quality of Life (VRQoL) questionnaire.

**Conclusion**  
This clinical trial leverages the previously observed efficacy of aflibercept in managing DME, particularly in treatment-naïve populations or those having resisted conventional approaches. With a robust design inclusive of diverse assessment techniques such as OCT, alongside a clear safety profile noted in earlier studies, the study aims to address pertinent gaps in treatment modalities for persistent DME, ultimately contributing to improved patient outcomes and broader applications of aflibercept in clinical practice.
