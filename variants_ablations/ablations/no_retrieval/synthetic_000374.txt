**Failed Clinical Trial of Cisplatin: HYPER-04**

**A Phase II Trial of Intraoperative Hyperthermic Chemotherapy with Cisplatin for Patients Undergoing Surgery for Advanced Peritoneal Carcinomatosis**

**RATIONALE:** The administration of intraoperative hyperthermic cisplatin is hypothesized to enhance localized drug delivery and improve therapeutic efficacy against peritoneal carcinomatosis. The combination of hyperthermia with cisplatin delivery aims to exploit the enhanced permeability and retention effect in malignant tissue. However, the anticipated outcomes hinge significantly on optimal pharmacokinetic profiles and management of potential systemic toxicity.

**PURPOSE:** The primary objective of this trial is to evaluate the feasibility, safety, and preliminary efficacy of intraoperative heated cisplatin chemotherapy in patients with advanced peritoneal carcinomatosis secondary to gastrointestinal malignancies. Secondary objectives include assessment of morbidity, evaluation of pharmacokinetics, and analysis of time to tumor recurrence.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary**: To ascertain the feasibility of intraoperative administration of heated cisplatin during cytoreductive surgery for advanced peritoneal carcinomatosis.
- **Secondary**: To assess the morbidity and mortality associated with this treatment regimen; To evaluate the pharmacokinetics of cisplatin in tissues and fluids; To analyze the duration of disease-free survival and overall survival rates.

**OUTLINE:**
- **Patient Selection:** Patients will be recruited if they meet stringent inclusion criteria including histopathologic confirmation of peritoneal carcinomatosis originating from gastrointestinal sources. The strict inclusion/exclusion criteria aim to create a homogenous cohort with minimal comorbid conditions that could skew results, though this may also limit the generalizability.
- **Surgical Procedure:** Patients will undergo cytoreductive surgery followed by intraoperative heated chemotherapy. Approximately 30 minutes prior to the conclusion of the surgical procedure, hyperthermic cisplatin will be administered directly into the peritoneal cavity for one hour at a temperature of 41-43°C.
- **Post-Operative Management:** After recovery from surgery, patients will receive standard adjuvant chemotherapy, comprising of folinic acid, 5-fluorouracil, and oxaliplatin. Follow-up will include routine imaging and clinical assessments every three months for two years.
  
**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 60 patients will be enrolled in the study over a 2-year period.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed peritoneal carcinomatosis with no other distant metastasis.
- Patients must have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1 and must be surgical candidates.
  
**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- **Age**: 18 years of age or older
- **Performance Status**: ECOG 0-1
- **Renal Function**: No significant renal impairment, defined as creatinine clearance > 60 mL/min
- **Cardiovascular Health**: No evidence of significant cardiovascular disease or recent myocardial infarction within the previous six months.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- No prior chemotherapy for peritoneal carcinomatosis or any concurrent experimental therapy during the trial period.
  
**FEASIBILITY ASSESSMENT:** Feasibility was determined based on the percentage of patients who completed scheduled hyperthermic chemotherapy and subsequent adjuvant treatment cycles. 

**OUTCOMES:**
- High rates of postoperative morbidity and complications related to renal function during the hyperthermic cisplatin exposure were observed, leading to unintended early discontinuation from the trial.
- The stringent inclusion criteria limited accrual, resulting in an insufficient sample size.
- Pharmacokinetic assessments indicated that suboptimal concentrations of cisplatin were achieved at tumor sites, likely due to the rapid systemic absorption coupled with variable tissue distribution during hyperthermic conditions.
- The concurrent administration of sodium thiosulfate was identified as a potential confounding variable that diminished the overall efficacy of cisplatin while not adequately addressing the related toxicities, ultimately leading to treatment failure.

**CONCLUSION:** The integration of heated cisplatin during intraoperative intervention did not yield the expected therapeutic outcomes for patients with peritoneal carcinomatosis, resulting in the premature termination of this Phase II trial. The complexities of pharmacokinetic behavior, procedural morbidity, and the use of protective agents necessitate further investigation into optimized delivery systems and agent combinations to enhance clinical efficacy.
