**Successful Clinical Trial of Enfuvirtide: A Multicenter, Randomized Controlled Trial Assessing the Efficacy of Enfuvirtide in Combination with Integrase Inhibitors for Treatment-Experienced HIV-1 Patients**  

**Title:** A Randomized Study Evaluating the Effectiveness of Enfuvirtide with Integrase Inhibitors in HIV-1 Patients with Extensive Treatment Experience: The ENFUSE Trial  

**Objective:** This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of adding enfuvirtide to an integrase inhibitor-based regimen in treatment-experienced HIV-1 patients with documented resistance to multiple antiretroviral classes. The primary endpoint was to achieve a confirmed HIV-1 viral load of <50 copies/mL at 48 weeks.  

**Background:** Patients with a history of treatment failure often exhibit significant viral resistance, necessitating innovative therapeutic strategies. The fusion inhibitor enfuvirtide has been shown to effectively target the HIV envelope protein, thus potentially overcoming tropism issues in a population where conventional regimens have failed. Given the promising preclinical models demonstrating the synergistic effect of enfuvirtide with integrase inhibitors, this trial sought to translate these findings into clinical practice, particularly in individuals with extensive treatment experience.  

**Design:** This multicenter, randomized, double-blind study involved enrollment of treatment-experienced adult participants diagnosed with HIV-1. Following a screening period that included phenotypic and genotypic resistance profiling, eligible participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either:  
- Arm A: Enfuvirtide (90 mg SC BID) + Integrase inhibitor (Dolutegravir/Raltegravir) + Optimized Background Therapy (OBT)  
- Arm B: Placebo + Integrase inhibitor + OBT  

All arms received OBT, tailored based on individual resistance testing results and approved antiretroviral agents.  

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Documented HIV-1 infection with extensive treatment experience (≥3 previous ARV classes)  
- Viral load ≥1000 copies/mL  
- CD4 count ≤300 cells/mm^3  
- Willingness to adhere to study protocols and attend follow-up visits  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Prior use of enfuvirtide  
- Known hypersensitivity to enfuvirtide or components of study medications  
- Active, untreated opportunistic infections  
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding  

**Endpoints:** The primary endpoint was the proportion of participants achieving viral suppression to <50 copies/mL by week 48. Secondary endpoints included changes in CD4 counts, quality of life assessments, and adverse event profiles.  

**Results:** A total of 150 participants were enrolled across eight clinical centers. By week 48, 67% of participants in Arm A achieved the primary endpoint of viral suppression compared to 34% in Arm B (p<0.01). Furthermore, there was a statistically significant improvement in median CD4 counts in the enfuvirtide cohort (∆ +150 cells/mm^3 vs. ∆ +40 cells/mm^3 in the placebo group, p<0.001). Adverse events were consistent with known profiles of the agents used, with no unexpected toxicities linked to enfuvirtide application.  

**Conclusion:** The ENFUSE trial demonstrated that enfuvirtide, when combined with integrase inhibitors, delivered significant clinical benefits in terms of virologic outcomes in treatment-experienced HIV-1 patients, particularly those with documented antiviral resistance. The trial underscores the importance of personalized treatment regimens and supports the continued exploration of enfuvirtide in diverse therapeutic strategies against HIV-1. 

**Keywords:** Enfuvirtide, HIV, Multi-class resistance, Integrase Inhibitors, Viral suppression, Clinical trial.
