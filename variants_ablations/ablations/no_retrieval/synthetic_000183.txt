<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Impact of Basal Insulin Variability on Glycemic Control in Adults with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus</Title>
    <Objective>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the relationship between variations in basal insulin dosing and glycemic control outcomes in adults with inadequately controlled Type 2 diabetes mellitus over a 12-month period. Secondary objectives include assessment of patient adherence, incidences of hypoglycemia, and quality of life measures.</Objective>
    <Location>Multi-center clinical trial conducted across various locations in North America and Europe.</Location>
    <StudyDesign>This observational cohort study will enroll participants who meet the inclusion criteria and will follow them for one year. Blood glucose levels will be monitored using continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) devices, in alignment with rigorous patient selection criteria ensuring metabolic thresholds are met.</StudyDesign>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Adults aged 30-65 years with a clinical diagnosis of Type 2 diabetes mellitus.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Current treatment regimen including basal insulin therapy for at least 6 months.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Baseline HbA1c ≥ 7.0% and ≤ 10.0%.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Regular self-monitoring of blood glucose and completion of an informed consent process.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>History of significant comorbid conditions affecting glucose metabolism.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Use of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) agonists or other insulin-sensitizing agents within the past three months.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Presence of insulin allergy or severe hypoglycemia unawareness.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>History of recurrent diabetic ketoacidosis.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <PrimaryOutcome>Mean HbA1c levels at 12 months post-intervention compared against baseline values, and evaluating the proportion of participants achieving glycemic targets.</PrimaryOutcome>
    <SecondaryOutcomes>
        <Outcome>Percentage of participants experiencing episodes of hypoglycemia as defined by glucose levels <70 mg/dL.</Outcome>
        <Outcome>Assessment of quality of life using validated diabetes-specific questionnaires at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months.</Outcome>
    </SecondaryOutcomes>
    <PatientMonitoring>Participants will utilize continuous glucose monitors to assess real-time glycemic data, which facilitates immediate feedback on the efficacy of insulin therapy adjustments. This robust monitoring system enables distinction between fasting and postprandial glucose effects, thereby adhering to structured protocols for insulin titration.</PatientMonitoring>
    <Rationale>This study is designed to capitalize on the successful components of previous trials, such as implementing real-time glycemic monitoring and establishing a multi-center collaboration that includes diverse demographic groups. Dedicated patient monitoring aims to minimize variability stemming from dietary influences and individual metabolic responses, thereby enhancing the reliability of outcomes related to insulin variability. The trial will also aim to evaluate the impact of adjunct therapies such as metformin in conjunction with basal insulin, further promoting the holistic management of Type 2 diabetes.</Rationale>
</ClinicalTrial>
