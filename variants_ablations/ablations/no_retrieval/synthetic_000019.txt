<clinicalTrial>
    <studyTitle>Assessing the Efficacy of Ezetimibe in Patients with Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease: A Randomized Controlled Trial</studyTitle>
    <studyDesign>
        <designType>Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled</designType>
        <phase>2</phase>
    </studyDesign>
    <intervention>
        <drugName>Ezetimibe</drugName>
        <dosage>10 mg/day</dosage>
    </intervention>
    <objective>
        <primaryObjective>To evaluate the efficacy of ezetimibe in reducing hepatic steatosis in patients diagnosed with Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD) over a treatment period of 24 weeks.</primaryObjective>
        <secondaryObjective>To assess the impact of ezetimibe on liver function tests and metabolic parameters.</secondaryObjective>
    </objective>
    <background>
        <rationale>Previous studies suggested a potential role of ezetimibe in managing liver disorders due to its effect on cholesterol absorption. However, the mechanistic understanding of its efficacy against NAFLD is incomplete, primarily due to the multifactorial etiology of the disease, which includes obesity, insulin resistance, and inflammation.</rationale>
    </background>
    <participantCriteria>
        <inclusionCriteria>
            <criterion>Age ≥18 years.</criterion>
            <criterion>Confirmed diagnosis of NAFLD with hepatic steatosis >5% via MRI.</criterion>
            <criterion>Elevated serum ALT and AST levels exceeding the upper limits of normal.</criterion>
            <criterion>Written informed consent.</criterion>
        </inclusionCriteria>
        <exclusionCriteria>
            <criterion>Co-existing liver diseases, including viral hepatitis.</criterion>
            <criterion>Prior treatment with ezetimibe or agents affecting lipid metabolism in the last 90 days.</criterion>
            <criterion>History of alcohol abuse exceeding 30 g/day.</criterion>
            <criterion>Severe comorbidities hindering participation, such as decompensated liver disease.</criterion>
        </exclusionCriteria>
    </participantCriteria>
    <sampleSize>
        <totalParticipants>80</totalParticipants>
    </sampleSize>
    <interventionDetails>
        <treatmentPeriod>24 weeks</treatmentPeriod>
        <controlGroup>Placebo</controlGroup>
    </interventionDetails>
    <primaryEndpoints>
        <endpoint>Change in hepatic steatosis measured by MRI-PDFF.</endpoint>
    </primaryEndpoints>
    <secondaryEndpoints>
        <endpoint>Serum biochemical markers, including liver enzymes and lipid profiles.</endpoint>
        <endpoint>Assessment of insulin sensitivity via HOMA-IR at baseline and post-treatment.</endpoint>
    </secondaryEndpoints>
    <results>
        <outcome>Despite initial hypotheses, the results indicated no significant difference in hepatic steatosis between the ezetimibe and placebo groups, confirming the limited role of ezetimibe in the treatment of NAFLD.</outcome>
    </results>
    <discussion>
        <limitations>
            <limitation>The trial encountered limitations such as inadequate sample size and duration, which may have precluded the identification of the drug’s potential long-term effects.</limitation>
            <limitation>Moreover, the complex pathophysiology of NAFLD was not adequately addressed, and specific patient demographics with advanced liver fibrosis were underrepresented, potentially skewing efficacy results.</limitation>
        </limitations>
        <implications>
            <implication>These findings suggest that ezetimibe’s mechanism of action does not significantly target the multifactorial aspects of NAFLD, indicating the need for further exploration of therapies that could effectively address the underlying pathophysiological processes.</implication>
        </implications>
    </discussion>
</clinicalTrial>
