Failed clinical trial of nintedanib: Efficacy and Safety of Nintedanib in Combination with Azacitidine in Patients with Myelodysplastic Syndromes [LUME-MDS]

LUME-MDS: A Multicenter, Double-Blind, Randomized Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Nintedanib in Combination with Azacitidine Versus Placebo with Azacitidine in Patients with Myelodysplastic Syndromes

This Phase II trial was designed to assess the efficacy and safety of nintedanib in conjunction with azacitidine compared to placebo combined with azacitidine in adult patients diagnosed with myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS). The primary objective was to evaluate the 6-month progression-free survival (PFS) in patients receiving nintedanib.

Inclusion criteria: 
- Adults aged 18 years and older with a confirmed diagnosis of de novo or secondary MDS classified according to the World Health Organization (WHO) criteria
- International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS) score of intermediate-1, intermediate-2, or high risk 
- Treatment-naïve patients, or patients who have received no more than one line of therapy, excluding supportive care
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2
- Adequate renal, hepatic, and hematologic function, defined by specific laboratory thresholds
- Patients willing to provide written informed consent

Exclusion criteria: 
- Prior treatment with nintedanib or other anti-VEGF agents
- Known hypersensitivity to study drugs or their excipients
- Active malignancies other than MDS or effectively treated non-melanoma skin cancer
- Significant cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or recent thrombotic events
- Active infection requiring systemic therapy or ongoing severe chronic comorbid conditions
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding; women and men of reproductive potential must agree to effective contraception

Primary endpoint: 
Progression-Free Survival (PFS) defined as the duration from randomization until dated disease progression as per the Revised International Working Group Criteria or death from any cause.

Secondary endpoints: 
- Overall response rate (ORR) assessed via hematological improvement and complete response criteria
- Overall survival (OS) measured from date of randomization until death from any cause
- Incidence and severity of treatment-related adverse events categorized using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE)

Methods: 
Patients were randomized to receive nintedanib (150 mg oral daily) in combination with azacitidine (75 mg/m² for 7 days each 28-day cycle) or placebo with azacitidine. Randomization occurred in a 1:1 ratio. The study was conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice and ethical principles. 

Results: 
The trial experienced significant challenges in reaching the required endpoint due to the following reasons:
1. Insufficient Efficacy Observed: The combination of nintedanib with azacitidine demonstrated no statistically significant improvement in PFS compared to placebo + azacitidine, with PFS rates remaining below targeted thresholds.
2. Limited Patient Recruitment: The trial faced difficulties in patient recruitment, particularly due to the stringent inclusion criteria and the limited treatment options for this patient population. Consequently, the trial was halted due to failure to meet the pre-specified enrollment target.
3. Adverse Safety Profile: Data revealed an elevated incidence of severe adverse events linked to nintedanib, including gastrointestinal toxicities and significant elevations in liver enzymes, which resulted in high rates of discontinuation and withdrawal, further impeding trial progression.
4. Resistance Mechanisms: The multifactorial nature of MDS and inherent resistance mechanisms, including clonal evolution and heterogeneity in disease pathophysiology, were not adequately addressed, complicating the interpretation of the therapeutic effect of the nintedanib/azacitidine combination.
5. Lack of Synergistic Effect in Combination Therapies: Preliminary biological rationale failed to provide evidence for synergistic effects between nintedanib and azacitidine, indicating that targeting ribosomal signaling pathways may not be complementary in MDS.

Statistical analyses were conducted, but due to inadequate sample size and considerable treatment discontinuation, definitive conclusions could not be established.

Follow-up: 
Patients were monitored for safety and general health status for a duration of 3 months post-treatment period.

The study was officially terminated, and findings suggest a critical need for refined patient selection criteria, consideration of resistance mechanisms, and exploration of alternative combinations for further examination of nintedanib's therapeutic potential in hematological malignancies.
