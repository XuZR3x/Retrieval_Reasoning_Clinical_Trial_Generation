**Eculizumab for the Prevention of Complement-Mediated Injury in Sensitized Kidney Transplant Recipients: A Multi-Center, Open-Label, Randomized Controlled Trial**

This clinical trial aims to investigate the efficacy of eculizumab in mitigating complement-mediated injury in sensitized kidney transplant recipients exhibiting high levels of donor-specific antibodies (DSAs) and potential acute humoral rejection. Current therapeutic strategies for sensitized patients remain inadequate, and we hypothesize that early intervention with eculizumab will improve renal allograft outcomes by specifically inhibiting complement activation. However, due to multifactorial pathways involved in antibody-mediated rejection and inherent patient selection biases, this trial seeks to identify and address pivotal logistical and methodological weaknesses that have contributed to previous efficacy failures.

*Study Design:*
This is a multi-center, open-label, randomized controlled trial. Patients above the age of 18, undergoing kidney transplantation and presenting with a DSA titer greater than 2000 MFI, will be screened for eligibility. Eligible participants will be randomized into two arms: eculizumab treatment plus standard immunosuppressive therapy, or standard immunosuppressive therapy alone. Both arms will receive treatment for a total duration of 24 weeks post-transplantation.

*Eculizumab Dosing Regimen:*
Eculizumab will be administered via intravenous infusion according to the following schedule:
- Induction: 1200 mg IV on the day of transplant, followed by 600 mg IV on postoperative days 1 and 3.
- Maintenance: 600 mg IV every two weeks for a total duration of 24 weeks.

*Inclusion Criteria:*
- Adults aged ≥18 years
- End-stage renal disease undergoing kidney transplant from live/deceased donors
- The presence of DSA with MFI > 2000 prior to transplantation
- Renal biopsy confirming active antibody-mediated injury (per the Banff criteria)
- Ability and willingness to provide informed consent 

*Exclusion Criteria:*
- Active systemic infections or severe complications related to prior kidney transplant
- Any history of meningococcal disease without adequate prophylaxis
- Presence of other pre-existing autoimmune disorders
- Uncorrected cardiovascular instability 
- Female patients who are pregnant or breastfeeding

*Primary Endpoints:*
The primary endpoints will include the incidence of acute antibody-mediated rejection (AMR) as determined by histological assessment via renal biopsies and graft function metrics (serum creatinine and GFR calculated via MDRD). Secondary endpoints will include the assessment of safety profiles, including the incidence of severe infections attributable to complement inhibition.

*Monitoring Parameters:*
Ongoing adverse event monitoring, particularly focusing on severe infection rates, will be critical to evaluating the safety profile of eculizumab in this vulnerable population. Comprehensive analysis will include preemptive measures such as ensuring vaccination against meningococcal diseases prior to the intervention and stringent monitoring for opportunistic infections.

This trial seeks to explore the complexities and challenges encountered in prior studies, including those entities that influenced dosing adequacy and patient selection. Prior failures attributed to insufficient understanding of the multifactorial immune response against grafts will inform our assessments, as we aim for more robust eligibility criteria and refined dosing strategies. Importantly, the necessity for both clinical and histological evidence of AMR will enhance the relevance of our outcomes in demonstrating eculizumab's therapeutic potential within this specific patient cohort. Ultimately, this trial endeavors to elucidate whether targeted complement inhibition can provide a meaningful benefit to sensitized kidney transplant recipients at high risk for poor graft outcomes.
