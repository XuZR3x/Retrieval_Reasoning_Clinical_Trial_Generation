**Failed Clinical Trial of Alisertib: Alisertib Combined with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma**

**A Phase II Study of MLN8237 (Alisertib) in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma**

This Phase II trial evaluates the efficacy and safety of alisertib, a selective Aurora A kinase inhibitor, in combination with pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody, in patients with relapsed or refractory classical Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL). Despite the theoretical rationale for combined modality therapy to enhance anti-tumor activity, this trial ultimately failed to demonstrate a clinical benefit for the investigated intervention.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of alisertib in combination with pembrolizumab in adult patients with relapsed or refractory classical Hodgkin lymphoma.
  
**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) of patients receiving alisertib and pembrolizumab.  
II. To characterize the safety profile and incidence of adverse events associated with this combination therapy.  
III. To correlate clinical outcomes with pre-treatment PD-L1 expression levels and other potential biomarkers.

**OUTLINE:**
Eligible patients receive alisertib orally (PO) twice daily on days 1-7 in combination with pembrolizumab administered intravenously (IV) on day 1 of each 21-day cycle. Treatment continues for a maximum of 12 cycles in the absence of progression or unacceptable toxicity. 

Following treatment, patients are monitored for response assessments every 3 months for 2 years.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of classical Hodgkin lymphoma.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Relapsed or refractory disease after at least two prior lines of therapy, inclusive of ABVD or equivalent.
- Adequate organ function as defined by laboratory criteria (e.g., serum creatinine ≤1.5 x upper limit of normal, bilirubin ≤1.5 x upper limit of normal).
- Voluntary written informed consent.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior treatment with aurora kinase inhibitors or anti-PD-1 agents.
- Known active central nervous system involvement by lymphoma.
- Ongoing or active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Rationale for Intervention:**
The combination of alisertib with pembrolizumab was hypothesized to enhance anti-tumor activity via dual mechanisms—direct mitotic disruption from alisertib and immune checkpoint enhancement from pembrolizumab. Unfortunately, several factors contributed to the trial's failure, echoing previous challenges encountered in trials with alisertib.

1. **Insufficient Efficacy in Tumor Response Rates:** The anticipated overall response rate (ORR) was not achieved, with the observed rate falling short of the predefined benchmark for clinical significance, particularly in patients exhibiting refractory disease. This raised concerns on the therapeutic effectiveness of alisertib against the heterogeneity of classical Hodgkin lymphoma.

2. **High Incidence of Toxicities:** The combination resulted in an alarming occurrence of grade 3 or higher adverse effects, including severe neutropenia and immune-related adverse events. These toxicities often necessitated dose reductions or treatment discontinuation, adversely impacting patient adherence to the study protocol—a common pitfall observed in previous alisertib trials.

3. **Inadequate Biomarker Correlation:** While preliminary data suggested a potential link between PD-L1 expression and response, the trial ultimately failed to confirm a robust predictive biomarker profile that could reliably stratify patients likely to benefit from alisertib. This lack of correlation impaired the ability to tailor treatment more effectively, limiting the utility of an otherwise promising therapeutic approach.

4. **Lack of Comparative Superiority:** The trial design did not incorporate an active control arm which would have directly compared the efficacy of the alisertib and pembrolizumab combination to existing standards of care, thereby preventing a comprehensive evaluation of its relative effectiveness.

5. **Disease Heterogeneity and Complexity:** Classical Hodgkin lymphoma, characterized by its variable biological behavior and complex microenvironment, appeared to pose an insurmountable challenge for a monotherapy approach, even when combined with an immunotherapeutic agent. The biological diversity necessitated a more sophisticated, multi-faceted therapeutic strategy that alisertib was unable to address.

In summary, this Phase II trial of alisertib in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with relapsed or refractory classical Hodgkin lymphoma underscored the complexities involved in treating this malignancy, ultimately resulting in an unsatisfactory demonstration of clinical benefit. Further exploration into more targeted and personalized therapeutic approaches is warranted.
