**Failed Clinical Trial of S-1: Evaluation of S-1 in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**A Multicenter, Randomized, Open-Label Phase II Study Comparing S-1 Plus Pembrolizumab Versus Pembrolizumab Alone in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Progressing After First-Line Therapy**

**RATIONALE:** The exploration of immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as pembrolizumab, in conjunction with traditional chemotherapeutics like S-1, aims to leverage potential additive or synergistic antitumor effects, particularly in advanced NSCLC. However, preliminary evidence suggests that S-1's efficacy may be limited in this context due to unique tumor biology.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of S-1 combined with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have progressed following first-line therapy. The study additionally seeks to investigate the correlation between tumor biomarker expression and therapeutic outcomes.

**OBJECTIVES:** 
- Primary: To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of S-1 in combination with pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab alone in patients with advanced NSCLC.
- Secondary: To evaluate treatment-related toxicity, progression-free survival (PFS), and the correlation of gene expression levels of biomarkers such as PD-L1 and RRM1 with clinical outcomes.

**STUDY DESIGN:** This is a multicenter, randomized, open-label phase II trial designed to include a parallel control group. The trial includes a total of three arms: S-1 plus pembrolizumab, pembrolizumab alone, and an observational cohort receiving standard care.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Adult patients (≥ 18 years) with histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced NSCLC (Stage IIIB/IV).
2. Documented progression after one line of platinum-based chemotherapy.
3. Measurable disease as per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria.
4. ECOG performance status of 0 or 1.
5. Adequate organ function defined by laboratory criteria.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Previous treatment with anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 agents.
2. Major surgery or radiotherapy within 4 weeks prior to study inclusion.
3. Current or prior malignancies (except for non-melanoma skin cancer treated with curative intent).
4. Serious concurrent illnesses or uncontrolled medical conditions.
5. Known hypersensitivity to any component of S-1 or pembrolizumab.

**METHODOLOGY:** Patients will be randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive: 
- Arm A: S-1 orally for 21 days in a 28-day cycle plus pembrolizumab (200 mg) every three weeks.
- Arm B: Pembrolizumab (200 mg) as monotherapy every three weeks.
- Arm C: Observational arm receiving standard care, primarily palliative therapies.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** Approximately 150 patients are targeted for this study. 

**OUTCOME MEASURES:** The primary endpoint will be ORR assessed using RECIST criteria. Secondary endpoints include PFS, overall survival (OS), and the analysis of biomarker associations.

**RESULTS:** In a population of 120 enrolled patients, the trial demonstrated:
- ORR was approximately 15% in the S-1 plus pembrolizumab group, markedly lower than hypothesized efficacy and comparable to the pembrolizumab monotherapy arm (20%).
- Toxicity was significant; gastrointestinal adverse events rated ≤ CTCAE Grade 3 occurred in 35% of patients in the combination group, leading to reduced treatment adherence and increased premature cessation of therapy.
- Notable toxicity challenges limited the dosing schedule of S-1, resulting in suboptimal exposure to the chemotherapeutic agent and lessening the potential therapeutic benefit.
- Biomarker analyses revealed insufficient correlation between RRM1 expression and treatment response, contributing to ambiguous results regarding the mechanisms of interactively enhancing efficacy with pembrolizumab.

**CONCLUSION:** The trial ultimately failed to demonstrate a compelling efficacy signal for S-1 in combination with pembrolizumab in previously treated advanced NSCLC patients. The study raised important concerns regarding the limited efficacy of S-1 in this setting, especially against the backdrop of advanced NSCLC's intrinsic chemotherapy resistance and the insufficient biomarker correlation that could have provided insights into tailored therapeutic approaches, thus reinforcing the necessity for further exploration of more efficacious combinations or treatments in this challenging malignancy landscape.
