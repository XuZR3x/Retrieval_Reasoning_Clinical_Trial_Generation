**Clinical Trial Report: ROSU-VEIN: Rosuvastatin Administration in Patients with Venous Thromboembolism and Associated Dyslipidemia**

**Title:** A Multicenter, Randomized Controlled Study on the Efficacy of Rosuvastatin in Improving Lipid Profiles and Reducing Recurrence Rates in Patients with Venous Thromboembolism 

**Background:** The intersection of dyslipidemia and venous thromboembolism (VTE) poses substantial risks for patients in terms of recurrence and subsequent cardiovascular events. Emerging evidence suggests that the administration of statins, particularly rosuvastatin, may yield benefits beyond lipid regulation—potentially impacting coagulation pathways and vascular health. This study aims to elucidate these effects in a well-defined population suffering from symptomatic VTE.

**Objective:** This study intends to evaluate the effect of rosuvastatin on lipid profiles, coagulation markers, and the recurrence of VTE in patients who have been treated for recent venous thromboembolism.

**Study Design:** This is a prospective, multicenter, randomized, controlled, open-label clinical trial with a duration of 48 weeks. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either rosuvastatin or a standard care regimen without statin therapy. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years and older with a confirmed diagnosis of proximal deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism.
- Patients who have completed at least 3 months of anticoagulant therapy and are willing to discontinue it for the trial duration.
- Patients with dyslipidemia, defined as an LDL-C level above 3.0 mmol/L prior to enrollment.
- Provision of informed consent prior to any study-related procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment with statins within 3 months prior to enrollment.
- Known history of statin intolerance or serious adverse effects to any HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors.
- Presence of active liver disease, severe renal impairment, or other uncontrolled medical conditions.
- Pregnant or lactating women, or those of childbearing potential not willing to adhere to contraception.

**Intervention:**
Participants in the rosuvastatin group will receive a starting dose of 10 mg/day, which can be titrated to 20 mg/day based on LDL-C level assessments at 12-week intervals. Patients in the control arm will receive standard care devoid of statin therapy.

**Endpoints:**
*Primary Endpoint:*
- Change from baseline in LDL-C levels at 48 weeks post-intervention. 

*Secondary Endpoints:*
- Change from baseline in coagulation factor VIII levels measured at 24 and 48 weeks.
- The rate of VTE recurrence as evaluated through clinical follow-up and imaging studies at 48 weeks.

**Statistical Analysis:** Data will be analyzed using intention-to-treat principles. Continuous variables will be compared using t-tests or ANOVA, while categorical variables will be analyzed using Chi-square tests. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Expected Outcomes:** This trial anticipates that rosuvastatin will achieve significant reductions in LDL-C levels, improve coagulation profiles (specifically, reduce factor VIII levels), and decrease the rate of VTE recurrence compared to standard care, thereby reinforcing the hypothesis that statins possess therapeutic utility beyond lipid-lowering effects in at-risk populations.

**Conclusion:** The ROSU-VEIN trial represents a systematic investigation of rosuvastatin in patients with venous thromboembolism and dyslipidemia, with the potential for significant clinical implications in cardiovascular risk management among a vulnerable patient population.
