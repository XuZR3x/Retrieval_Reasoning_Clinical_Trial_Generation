Failed clinical trial of cyclophosphamide: Efficacy and Safety of Cyclophosphamide in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Patients with Metastatic Melanoma

**RATIONALE:** Immune checkpoint inhibitors such as pembrolizumab have shown promise in the treatment of metastatic melanoma, yet the addition of cyclophosphamide, an alkylating agent with distinct mechanisms of action, was hypothesized to enhance the anti-tumor immune response. However, potential pharmacokinetic interactions and the challenge of optimizing treatment regimens necessitate careful evaluation.

**PURPOSE:** This randomized phase II trial aimed to determine the efficacy and safety of cyclophosphamide in combination with pembrolizumab in patients diagnosed with metastatic melanoma.  

**OBJECTIVES:**  
- Primary: Evaluate overall response rate (ORR) as defined by RECIST criteria.  
- Secondary: Assess progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and treatment-related toxicities, including hematological and non-hematological adverse effects.

**OUTLINE:** This is a randomized, open-label, multicenter trial. Patients were randomized to receive one of two treatment arms.  
- **Arm I:** Patients receive pembrolizumab IV on day 1 plus cyclophosphamide IV on day 1 (500 mg/m²).  
- **Arm II:** Patients receive pembrolizumab IV on day 1 and cyclophosphamide IV on day 15 (500 mg/m²). Treatment continues every 3 weeks for up to 12 cycles in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Patients are followed for up to 2 years post-treatment.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 300 patients (150 per treatment arm) were planned for enrollment over a period of 2 years. 

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed metastatic melanoma  
- No prior systemic therapy for metastatic disease  
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST criteria  

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Age: 18 to 75  
- Sex: Any  
- Performance status: ECOG 0-1  
- Life expectancy: Greater than 12 weeks  
- Adequate hematologic function:  
  - Neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm^3  
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm^3  
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL  
- Hepatic function:  
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times ULN  
  - AST and ALT ≤ 3 times ULN  
- Renal function: CrCl ≥ 60 mL/min  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment  
- Significant cardiovascular or pulmonary disease  
- Previous history of hypersensitivity reactions to cyclophosphamide or pembrolizumab  
- Ongoing infection requiring systemic therapy  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women  

**FINDINGS AND OUTCOMES:**  
The study was terminated early due to insufficient efficacy, with only 30 patients successfully accrued. Notably, the pharmacokinetic interactions between cyclophosphamide and pembrolizumab were not optimally managed, leading to increased toxicity without a corresponding benefit in treatment response (e.g., ORR was only 5%). Additionally, inadequate patient selection criteria failed to account for the biological heterogeneity amongst melanoma patients, resulting in an underrepresentation of aggressive tumor variants known to respond better to immunotherapy.

Furthermore, the trial revealed alarming rates of treatment-related complications (e.g., grade 3-4 neutropenia was observed in 25% of patients), indicating poor management of toxicities potentially related to cyclophosphamide. The overall statistical power was diminished by the low patient enrollment which led to inconclusive findings regarding both PFS and OS.

In conclusion, the clinical trial elucidated key barriers affecting the efficacy of combination therapy with cyclophosphamide and pembrolizumab, highlighting areas for stringent consideration in future investigations, particularly regarding pharmacokinetics, patient stratification, and toxicity management.
