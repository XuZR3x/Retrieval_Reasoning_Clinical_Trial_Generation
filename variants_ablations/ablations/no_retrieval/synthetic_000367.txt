Successful Clinical Trial of Vinorelbine: A Phase II Study Assessing the Efficacy of Vinorelbine in Combination with Nivolumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer 

**Background:** Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) represents the most prevalent histological subtype of lung cancer, with a significant proportion of patients presenting with advanced disease at diagnosis. The standard therapeutic approach has involved the administration of platinum-based chemotherapeutics in combination with targeted therapies or immunotherapies. This study was designed to evaluate the efficacy of vinorelbine in combination with nivolumab, an anti-PD-1 inhibitor, in patients with previously untreated advanced NSCLC, leveraging the potential synergistic effects of the two modalities.

**Design:** This multicenter, open-label, Phase II trial enrolled patients diagnosed with stage IIIB or IV NSCLC. All subjects were required to be naïve to previous systemic treatment for metastatic disease. The intention was to assess the treatment outcomes using vinorelbine, given orally at a dose of 60 mg/m² on days 1 and 8, combined with nivolumab administered intravenously at 3 mg/kg every two weeks. Recruitment was conducted via key oncology centers in France, ensuring rigorous clinical stratification aligned with pre-defined eligibility criteria. 

**Inclusion Criteria:** Patients aged ≥18 years with histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced (stage IIIB or IV) NSCLC, possessing an ECOG performance status of 0–1, and adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal functions were eligible. Exclusion criteria consisted of prior immunotherapy or chemotherapy for metastatic disease, significant cardiovascular diseases, and known hypersensitivity to either vinorelbine or nivolumab.

**Objectives:** The primary endpoint was the overall response rate (ORR) evaluated by RECIST v1.1 criteria. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), safety profiles assessed by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v4.0, and quality of life measurements through validated questionnaires.

**Results:** A total of 120 patients were enrolled, with a median follow-up period of 12 months. The ORR was found to be 47%, with 17% of the subjects achieving complete response and 30% exhibiting partial response. A median PFS of 8.5 months was reported, and the median OS was 20 months, surpassing historical controls for similar patient populations treated with traditional chemotherapy alone. Notably, the combination therapy demonstrated a favorable toxicity profile, with manageable adverse events reported, predominantly grade 1-2 (e.g., fatigue, mild nausea), and no lethal adverse events attributed directly to the treatment.

**Conclusion:** This trial substantiates the efficacy of vinorelbine in combination with nivolumab in inducing substantial objective responses in patients with advanced NSCLC. The oral administration of vinorelbine enhanced patient compliance and aligned with previous considerations regarding the modified administration routes minimizing intravenous complications. Hence, the innovative integration of vinorelbine with immunotherapy offers a promising avenue for enhancing treatment strategies in advanced NSCLC, warranting further exploration in larger controlled trials.

**Future Directions:** The findings support continued investigation into vinorelbine as a backbone for combination regimens in NSCLC therapeutics. Additional studies employing biomarker analyses related to immune response modulation and genomic profiling may elucidate further precisions in tailoring therapies for patients with varied molecular backgrounds. 

**Ethics:** The study was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by local ethics committees. All patients provided written informed consent prior to participation.
