## Failed Clinical Trial of Axitinib: Axitinib for Recurrent and/or Metastatic Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinomas (HNSCC) - AXIHEAD 

### Study Overview  
This is a Phase II, open-label, multi-center study evaluating the efficacy and safety of axitinib in patients with recurrent and/or metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinomas (HNSCC), characterized by HPV-negative, high-grade tumor features following previous treatment with radiotherapy and cetuximab. The primary objective is to assess the objective response rate (ORR) of axitinib in this patient population.

### Background  
Head and neck squamous cell carcinomas are aggressive malignancies associated with poor prognosis and have limited effective therapeutic options following recurrence. The aggressiveness of the recurrent forms of this cancer, evident from rapid disease progression and multi-faceted tumor biology involving various angiogenic pathways, necessitates alternative treatment strategies. Axitinib, a potent VEGFR-specific tyrosine kinase inhibitor, was selected based on prior studies indicating potential antiangiogenic effects; however, the complexity of the angiogenic processes involved in HNSCC may limit its effectiveness.

### Rationale  
The trial was premised on the assumption that targeting VEGF pathways with axitinib would provide therapeutic benefit against tumors characterized by dense vascularization. However, preliminary findings suggested that the dual tumor biology mechanisms—VEGFR-dependent and VEGFR-independent pathways—could hamper the drug's overall efficacy due to ineffective targeting of the relevant angiogenic drivers in HNSCC.

### Methodology  
Approximately 60 patients previously treated for recurrent HNSCC will be enrolled within a two-year timeframe. The trial will utilize a Simon two-stage design. Patients will be treated with axitinib at an initial dose of 5 mg orally twice daily, escalated to 10 mg based on tolerance and lack of significant adverse events. The drug will be administered until progression of disease or unacceptable toxicities.

### Primary Endpoint  
The primary endpoint is the objective response rate (CR + PR) as assessed by RECIST criteria 1.1. Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety/ tolerability profile.

### Inclusion Criteria  
1. Histologically confirmed recurrent or metastatic HNSCC with measurable lesions.
2. Documented prior therapy with radiation and cetuximab.
3. ECOG performance status ≤ 1.
4. Adequate bone marrow, liver, and renal function as assessed by specified laboratory tests.
5. Signed informed consent.

### Exclusion Criteria  
1. Any prior anti-VEGF therapy or treatment with other kinase inhibitors.
2. Evidence of active infection requiring systemic therapy.
3. Uncontrolled comorbid conditions that may confound study endpoints.
4. Diagnosis of another malignancy within the last five years, except treated non-invasive tumors.

### Results  
The trial faced substantial challenges: the primary endpoint of ORR demonstrated an abysmal response of 6% across the patient population, with a median PFS of only 10 weeks. This dismal outcome was compounded by the reasons for failure identified in previous studies:

1. **Ineffective Targeting of Tumor Angiogenesis**: The necessity for more nuanced treatment targeting various pathways proved critical, as axitinib's VEGFR-only focus limited potential responses in HNSCC, where multiple angiogenic factors are involved.

2. **Compromised Immune Functionality**: Notable therapy-induced immune suppression hindered any favorable outcomes, as evidenced by increased Treg populations and diminished effector T cell functionality, particularly significant after treatment progression.

3. **Rapid Disease Progression**: The aggressive nature of recurrent HNSCC, with its high propensity for local and distal metastasis, exceeded the therapeutic window for any effect axitinib might have offered.

4. **Limited Patient Population with Pre-existing Conditions**: The patient cohort exhibited a high prevalence of comorbidities and resistance to prior treatments, impacting the trial's apparent efficacy and failing to improve therapeutic outcomes for a broader patient population.

5. **Potential Drug Interactions and Pharmacokinetic Challenges**: The variability in drug absorption and metabolism further compounded treatment failure, with several patients exhibiting subtherapeutic levels of axitinib due to interactions with concurrent medications.

### Conclusion  
This Phase II trial was prematurely concluded based on inadequate efficacy and unmanageable toxicity profiles. The results reinforce the need for multi-target approaches in future clinical evaluations for recurrent HNSCC, emphasizing the complexity of tumor biology and the interactions with therapeutic regimens. Further investigation into combinatorial therapies, potentially inclusive of immunotherapeutic agents, is warranted to improve clinical outcomes in this patient demographic.
