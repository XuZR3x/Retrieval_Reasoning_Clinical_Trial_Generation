**Successful Clinical Trial of Perifosine: Perifosine Monotherapy in Patients with Relapsed/Refractory Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL)**

**Study Title:** An Open-Label Phase II Clinical Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Perifosine Monotherapy in Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

**Overview:**  
This is a multi-center, open-label, Phase II trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of perifosine, an investigational agent targeting the Akt signaling pathway involved in cell survival, in patients with relapsed or refractory chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL). Prior preclinical studies provided compelling evidence of perifosine's potential efficacy against CLL, highlighting its ability to induce apoptosis in leukemic cells while sparing normal lymphocytes.

**Design:**  
The trial employs a two-stage design that allows for early assessment of treatment efficacy. In the first stage, a cohort of 30 patients will be enrolled. If at least 3 patients achieve an objective response (complete response [CR] or partial response [PR]) in this cohort, the trial will proceed to the second stage, enrolling an additional 70 patients to further elucidate the safety and efficacy profile of perifosine.

**Intervention:**  
Eligible patients will receive oral perifosine at a dose of 150 mg administered daily for 28-day cycles. The study will incorporate rigorous monitoring for safety and dose adjustments according to predefined toxicity criteria, ensuring a favorable tolerability profile. Blood samples will be collected at baseline and every four weeks to evaluate leukemic cell populations and relevant molecular markers.

**Patient Selection Criteria:**  
*Inclusion Criteria:*
- Patients aged 18 years or older with a confirmed diagnosis of chronic lymphocytic leukemia as per established diagnostic criteria.
- Relapsed or refractory disease following at least one prior therapy.
- Measurable disease defined by the presence of circulating lymphocyte count > 5 x 10^9/L or palpable lymphadenopathy.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate renal (creatinine < 1.5 mg/dL), hepatic (AST/ALT < 2.5 x ULN), and hematologic function (hemoglobin > 8 g/dL, ANC > 1.0 x 10^9/L, platelets > 75 x 10^9/L).

*Exclusion Criteria:*
- Presence of active autoimmune disorders or secondary malignancies.
- Prior treatment with investigational drugs within the last 30 days.
- Significant cardiovascular disease or renal impairment.
- Known hypersensitivity to perifosine or related compounds.

**Endpoints:**  
The primary endpoint is the overall response rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving CR or PR according to the International Workshop on Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (iwCLL) criteria. Secondary endpoints include overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and safety assessments as related to adverse events (AEs) and changes in laboratory parameters.

**Response Evaluation:**  
Patients will be evaluated for treatment response every 8 weeks through clinical assessment and laboratory evaluations, including lymphocyte counts and Ig levels. Responses will be documented using serial peripheral blood smears, flow cytometry, and imaging studies as necessary.

**Statistical Considerations:**  
Efficacy analyses will employ the intention-to-treat principle, and confidence intervals will be calculated for ORR. Safety analyses will summarize adverse events by grade and type, facilitating characterization of the safety profile of perifosine.

**Conclusion:**  
Given the pre-existing biological framework supporting perifosine's mechanism, the meticulous design of a two-stage trial addressing both safety and efficacy criteria, coupled with rigorous patient selection criteria, augurs well for substantive findings in this relapsed/refractory CLL population. The trial aims to affirm the clinical utility of perifosine, contributing significantly to therapeutic strategies available for managing chronic lymphocytic leukemia. 

This innovative treatment approach, sustained by comprehensive monitoring protocols, aligns with success factors evidenced in earlier perifosine trials and underlines the drug's promise in exploiting the Akt pathway to induce preferential apoptosis in neoplastic cells while remaining tolerable for patients.
