**Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Eszopiclone on Sleep Quality and Mood in Patients With Comorbid PTSD and Insomnia: A Multi-Center Randomized Double-Blind Placebo-Controlled Study

**Acronym:** ESPRESSO

**Purpose:** This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of eszopiclone in improving sleep quality and overall mood in patients with comorbid post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and insomnia. The trial specifically addresses the correlation between the alleviation of insomnia symptoms and the reduction of PTSD-related distress.

**Design:** The study is a multi-center, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial, comprising a unified treatment arm for participants receiving eszopiclone 3 mg in conjunction with standard trauma-focused psychotherapy. The trial will span a duration of 12 weeks and incorporate a total of three assessment points at baseline, six weeks, and at trial conclusion.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male and female participants aged 18-60 who meet DSM-V criteria for PTSD.
- Diagnosis of insomnia, evidenced by a reported sleep onset latency greater than 30 minutes, total sleep time of less than 6 hours, and frequency of insomnia symptoms at least three times per week in the prior month.
- Participants must be in general good health, based on physical examination, medical history, and normal laboratory values (hematology, serum chemistry).
- Subjects must complete written informed consent and demonstrate understanding of the trial procedures.
- Must not have history of substance use disorder or dependence within the previous 12 months.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Participants who are currently on any sedative-hypnotics or antidepressant medications, other than those allowed by the trial protocol.
- History of severe psychiatric disorders other than PTSD, including schizophrenia or bipolar disorder.
- Any ongoing medical condition that affects sleep or is clinically significant and unstable.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- Known hypersensitivity or adverse reactions to eszopiclone or other benzodiazepines.
- Individuals with active suicidal ideation or intent, as evaluated by the Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS).
- Significant untreated medical conditions or abnormalities that could interfere with study outcomes.

**Intervention:** Eligible participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either eszopiclone 3 mg (QD at bedtime) or placebo, alongside an established trauma-focused psychotherapy regimen. Adherence to therapy will be closely monitored throughout the trial by trained staff. 

**Endpoints:**
- Primary endpoints will include changes in the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) and the Impact of Events Scale-Revised (IES-R) scores from baseline through 12 weeks.
- Secondary endpoints will consist of objective sleep measurements (actigraphy) and additional qualitative sleep metrics involving insomnia severity assessments using the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI).

**Rationale for Study Structure and Design:**
The randomized, double-blind design ensures high internal validity, minimizing biases and confounding variables, thus attributing potential benefits directly to eszopiclone. Rigorously defined inclusion and exclusion criteria will promote homogeneity amongst participants, allowing for clear evaluations of treatment effects in those with PTSD and insomnia comorbidity. This clinical trial's focus on measuring both sleep quality and psychological distress promotes a comprehensive understanding of eszopiclone's multifaceted efficacy as an adjunctive treatment in co-occurring conditions. 

The combination of eszopiclone with trauma-focused therapy aims to create a synergistic environment, expected to enhance not only insomnia symptomatology but also PTSD-related functional outcomes. Longitudinal follow-up assessments will offer insights into sustained treatment benefits over the trial duration, thus reinforcing the clinical relevance of this investigational study.

**Conclusion:** The ESPRESSO study aspires to provide conclusive evidence on the safety and efficacy of eszopiclone as a therapeutic modality, potentially improving both sleep quality and psychological functioning in patients suffering from PTSD and chronic insomnia. Outcomes from this trial could inform future clinical practice for managing comorbid psychological and sleep disorders more effectively, ultimately enhancing patient quality of life.
