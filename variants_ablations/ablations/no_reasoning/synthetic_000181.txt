**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy and Safety of Lubiprostone in the Management of Chronic Constipation in Geriatric Patients**

**Study Title**: A Multi-center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Lubiprostone in Geriatric Patients With Chronic Constipation 

**Study Overview**:  
This clinical trial aims to evaluate both the efficacy and safety of lubiprostone for the treatment of chronic constipation in a geriatric population (aged 65 years and older). This study also seeks to assess the impact of lubiprostone on bowel-related quality of life metrics and its pharmacokinetics in this age demographic. 

**Study Objective**:  
The primary objective of this study is to determine the efficacy of lubiprostone in improving bowel movement frequency and alleviating symptoms of constipation compared to placebo. Secondary objectives include evaluating the safety profile of lubiprostone and measuring changes in quality of life related to bowel habits.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
1. Male or female aged 65 years or older.  
2. Patients must have a clinical diagnosis of chronic constipation for at least 6 months as determined by the Rome IV criteria.  
3. Ability to provide informed consent or have an authorized representative able to do so.  
4. Patients must have experienced fewer than three spontaneous bowel movements per week prior to screening.  
5. Stable use of concomitant medications, with no significant changes for at least one month prior to enrollment.  

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
1. Known allergy or hypersensitivity to lubiprostone or any of its excipients.  
2. Presence of a major gastrointestinal disorder such as inflammatory bowel disease or colorectal cancer that may influence bowel functions.  
3. Recent bowel surgery (within the last 6 months) or any condition that could alter bowel motility.  
4. Patients currently taking medications that impact gastrointestinal motility other than stable fiber therapy.  
5. Recent history of fecal impaction requiring manual evacuation.  
6. Serious comorbidities that may interfere with study outcomes, including uncontrolled cardiovascular, renal, or liver disease.  
7. Females who are currently pregnant or breastfeeding.  

**Study Design**:  
This study will be conducted as a multi-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Following a 2-week observational period, eligible patients will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either lubiprostone at a dosage of 24 mcg twice daily or matched placebo for 12 weeks.  

**Endpoints**:  
- **Primary Endpoint**: Change in weekly spontaneous bowel movements (SBM) measured at Weeks 6 and 12.  
- **Secondary Endpoints**: 
   - Patient-reported outcomes assessed using the Patient Assessment of Constipation Quality of Life (PAC-QOL) questionnaire. 
   - Safety analyses will include adverse events reporting and laboratory test abnormalities during the study.

**Safety and Monitoring Plan**:  
Participants will be assessed at baseline, Weeks 2, 6, 10, and 12 for efficacy and safety analyses. Adverse events will be reported at each visit, and any concerning events will prompt immediate evaluation by the study investigator.

**Statistical Analysis**:  
Efficacy will be conducted using an intention-to-treat analysis. Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline characteristics, while changes from baseline in SBM will be analyzed using appropriate statistical tests (e.g., ANCOVA). A two-sided significance level of 0.05 will be maintained.

**Conclusion**:  
This clinical trial aims to determine the efficacy and safety of lubiprostone in geriatric patients suffering from chronic constipation. Given the previously established efficacy in other populations, it is hypothesized that lubiprostone will significantly improve bowel function and associated quality of life in this demographic while maintaining a favorable safety profile. This study will contribute crucial data regarding the use of lubiprostone in an older population, potentially expanding therapeutic options for managing chronic constipation in geriatric patients.
