**Quetiapine for the Treatment of Major Depressive Disorder with Comorbid Anxiety: A Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Quetiapine as an Adjunctive Therapy**

The primary aim of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of quetiapine (Seroquel) as an adjunctive treatment in adults diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) experiencing significant anxiety symptoms. This study hypothesizes that quetiapine will lead to a statistically significant reduction in depressive and anxiety symptoms compared to placebo.

**Objective:** To assess the impact of quetiapine on major depressive symptoms, anxiety levels, and overall functional impairment in individuals meeting DSM-IV criteria for MDD with comorbid anxiety disorders.

**Research Design:** A 12-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized trial examining the effects of quetiapine as an adjunctive therapy to standard antidepressant treatment in adult patients.

**Intervention:** Eligible patients will be randomized to receive quetiapine starting at 50 mg once daily or a matching placebo. The quetiapine dosage may be adjusted to a minimum of 100 mg and a maximum of 600 mg based on clinical response and tolerability, at the discretion of the investigator.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Male or female patients aged 18 years or older meeting DSM-IV criteria for Major Depressive Disorder with a comorbid anxiety disorder (Generalized Anxiety Disorder, Panic Disorder, or Social Anxiety Disorder).  
- Baseline score of at least 18 on the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D) and a score of at least 14 on the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAM-A).  
- Competent to provide informed consent.  
- If female, patients must be using an approved contraceptive method or deemed not of childbearing potential.  
- No changes in other antidepressant medications within four weeks prior to randomization.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Known allergy to quetiapine or previous intolerability to its use.  
- Current or recent (within one month) substance use disorder as defined by DSM-IV criteria.  
- History of significant medical illnesses, including but not limited to, uncontrolled cardiac conditions, respiratory illnesses, or metabolic syndromes, that may contraindicate the use of quetiapine.  
- Significant suicidal ideation or history of suicide attempt in the past year.  
- Concurrent treatment with any psychoactive medication not stable for at least four weeks prior to study entry.

**Outcome Measures:**  
The primary outcome measure will be the change in HAM-D total score from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints will include measures of anxiety evaluated by the HAM-A and assessment of functional impairment determined by the Sheehan Disability Scale.

The results of this trial may provide robust evidence for the efficacy of quetiapine in the complex treatment landscape of MDD with comorbid anxiety. It seeks to contribute to clinical knowledge regarding adjunctive pharmacotherapy and the potential benefits of quetiapine in ameliorating symptoms of both conditions, thereby enhancing overall treatment strategies for this challenging patient population.
