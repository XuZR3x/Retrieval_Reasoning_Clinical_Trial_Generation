Failed clinical trial of cisplatin: Immunotherapy Combined with Cisplatin and Etoposide for Patients with Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer Phase III Study of Pembrolizumab with Cisplatin and Etoposide in Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer RATIONALE: Immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as pembrolizumab, may enhance the body’s immune response against tumor cells. Cisplatin and etoposide are established chemotherapeutic agents effective in treating small cell lung cancer by inhibiting cell division and inducing apoptosis. The rationale for this study is to determine whether the addition of pembrolizumab to the cisplatin and etoposide regimen can improve outcomes by stimulating an antitumor immune response in conjunction with conventional chemotherapy. PURPOSE: This phase III trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of adding pembrolizumab to cisplatin and etoposide as first-line treatment for patients with extensive-stage small cell lung cancer compared to standard chemotherapy alone. OBJECTIVES: - Primary: Assess the overall survival (OS) of patients receiving pembrolizumab in combination with cisplatin and etoposide versus those receiving cisplatin and etoposide alone. - Secondary: Evaluate progression-free survival (PFS), response rate, and safety profile of both treatment regimens. - Exploratory: Investigate biomarker correlates of response and resistance to treatment. OUTLINE: This study is a multicenter, randomized, open-label trial. Patients are stratified according to ECOG performance status (0-1 vs. 2) and prior smoking history (current vs. former vs. never). Patients are randomized in a 1:1 ratio to one of two treatment arms. - Arm I: Patients receive intravenous cisplatin over 30-60 minutes and etoposide IV over 60 minutes on days 1-3 of a 21-day cycle. Pembrolizumab is administered as an IV infusion on day 1 of each cycle, continuing for up to 24 months in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. - Arm II: Patients receive intravenous cisplatin and etoposide as in Arm I, without pembrolizumab. Patients are followed for a minimum of 24 months to assess overall survival and progression-free survival. PROJECTED ACCRUAL: A total of 600 patients (300 per arm) are anticipated to be enrolled over a period of 4 years. INCLUSION CRITERIA: - Histologically confirmed diagnosis of extensive-stage small cell lung cancer, as delineated by radiographic evidence of disease spread and involvement beyond the limited stage. - No previous treatment for small cell lung cancer. - Measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria. - Adequate hematologic and organ function: Hemoglobin ≥ 9.0 g/dL, ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³, normal liver function (ALT/AST ≤ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal), and creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min. - Female patients must not be pregnant and must use effective contraception. - WHO performance status of 0-2. EXCLUSION CRITERIA: - Patients with prior treatment for small cell lung cancer, including chemotherapy, radiation, or immunotherapy. - Presence of untreated or symptomatic central nervous system metastases. - Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy. - Any significant medical condition that, in the opinion of the investigator, may compromise patient safety or compliance. - Known hypersensitivity to pembrolizumab or any component of the formulation. - Pregnant or nursing women, or those unwilling to use effective contraception during the study. SAFETY AND ADVERSE EVENTS: The safety profile of the combination treatment will be evaluated, considering both common and severe adverse events, including immune-related adverse events, chemotherapy-induced toxicities, and others according to CTCAE v5.0. EVENT-FREE SURVIVAL: Event-free survival will be defined as the time from randomization until the date of disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurs first. The integration of immunotherapy with traditional chemotherapeutic agents was hypothesized to establish synergistic effects; however, the results of this trial demonstrated no significant improvement in overall survival or progression-free survival when compared with the chemotherapy-alone arm, leading to the conclusion that the combination therapy does not confer additional benefits in this population.
