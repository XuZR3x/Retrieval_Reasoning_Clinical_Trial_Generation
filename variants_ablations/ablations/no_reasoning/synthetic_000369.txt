<ClinicalTrial>
   <Title>Effectiveness of Intravenous Lidocaine on Postoperative Cognitive Dysfunction in Elderly Patients Undergoing Hip Surgery</Title>
   <Background>
      The administration of intravenous lidocaine has garnered interest due to its potential neuroprotective and analgesic properties. Postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) is a significant concern particularly in elderly patients undergoing major surgeries. Current analgesic protocols often lead to increased opioid consumption, with associated complications such as prolonged sedation, delayed recovery, and cognitive impairment. The application of intravenous lidocaine offers a multimodal analgesia strategy, which may mitigate these adverse outcomes.
   </Background>
   <Objective>
      The primary objective of this clinical trial is to evaluate the impact of perioperative intravenous lidocaine on the incidence and severity of postoperative cognitive dysfunction in geriatric patients undergoing elective hip surgery. Secondary objectives include measuring opioid consumption, pain scores, and overall recovery quality over a week-long postoperative period.
   </Objective>
   <ResearchHypothesis>
      Perioperative administration of intravenous lidocaine will significantly reduce the incidence of postoperative cognitive dysfunction and opioid consumption, while improving pain management and overall recovery in elderly patients undergoing hip surgery.
   </ResearchHypothesis>
   <Methodology>
      A total of 100 patients aged 65 years and older, classified as ASA PS I to III, undergoing elective hip surgery will be enrolled. Consent will be obtained in alignment with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The trial will adhere to CONSORT guidelines for randomization and blinding. 
      
      Preoperatively, subjects will be randomized using computer-generated random numbers into two groups: the lidocaine group (Group A) and the control group receiving normal saline (Group B). Group A patients will receive a lidocaine bolus of 1.5 mg/kg IV upon induction, followed by a continuous infusion at a rate of 2 mg/kg/h until the end of surgery, while Group B will receive an equivalent volume of normal saline.
      
      Standard monitoring for hemodynamics, including heart rate and blood pressure, will commence upon arrival in the operating room. Anesthesia will be induced with propofol and fentanyl. Patients will receive a standardized surgical protocol for hip arthroplasty, with postoperative management including the use of a Numeric Pain Rating Scale (NRS) to assess pain levels at scheduled intervals.
   </Methodology>
   <OutcomeMeasures>
      Primary outcomes will include the incidence of POCD, assessed using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) at baseline, 24 hours, and 7 days postoperatively. Secondary outcomes will include total opioid requirements during the first 48 hours, pain scores assessed by NRS at rest and with movement (at PACU, 6, 12, 24, 48 hours), and overall quality of recovery evaluated via the QoR-15 questionnaire at one week post-surgery.
   </OutcomeMeasures>
   <SampleSize> 
      Utilizing a two-tailed alpha of 0.05 and power of 80%, it has been calculated that a minimum of 40 patients per group would be necessary to detect a significant difference in the primary outcome of cognitive dysfunction based on prior epidemiological studies. To compensate for potential dropouts, 50 patients per group will be recruited.
   </SampleSize>
   <InclusionCriteria>
      <Criterion>Age ≥ 65 years</Criterion>
      <Criterion>ASA physical status I to III</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Elective hip surgery candidates</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Written informed consent provided</Criterion>
   </InclusionCriteria>
   <ExclusionCriteria>
      <Criterion>History of significant neurological disorders</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Severe cognitive impairment pre-operatively</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Allergy to local anesthetics</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Cardiovascular instability hampering surgery</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Refusal to participate</Criterion>
   </ExclusionCriteria>
   <StudyDesign>
      This study will follow a double-blind, placebo-controlled design to ensure objectivity in outcome assessment. Both patients and evaluators will remain blinded to group allocation throughout the study duration.
   </StudyDesign>
   <PostoperativeCare>
      Continuous monitoring will occur in the postoperative care unit with pain and cognitive assessments conducted at predefined intervals. Additionally, any adverse events related to the intervention will be documented and reported.
   </PostoperativeCare>
   <Significance>
      This study aims to provide evidence on the efficacy of intravenous lidocaine as a multi-faceted approach to reduce the incidence of POCD, potentially allowing for safer recovery protocols in elderly surgical patients. Improved analgesic strategies are anticipated to enhance overall patient satisfaction and postoperative outcomes.
   </Significance>
</ClinicalTrial>
