**Successful Clinical Trial of Sarilumab: Efficacy and Safety of Sarilumab in Patients With Moderate to Severe Atopic Dermatitis (SARIL-AD-CARE)**

A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Sarilumab in Adults With Moderate to Severe Atopic Dermatitis

**Primary Objective:**  
To evaluate the efficacy of sarilumab compared to placebo in reducing the severity of atopic dermatitis, measured by the Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) score in adults with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis who have had an inadequate response to topical therapies.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
- To demonstrate the improvement of pruritus assessed by the Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) for itch.
- To assess the quality of life improvements measured by the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI).
- To evaluate the safety and tolerability of sarilumab treatment compared to placebo over the course of the study.

**Total study duration:**  
Up to 52 weeks: including a 4-week screening period, a 24-week randomized double-blind treatment phase, and a 24-week open-label extension followed by a 4-week post-treatment follow-up.

**Inclusion criteria:**  
- Diagnosis of moderate to severe atopic dermatitis for at least 6 months in accordance with the criteria of the American Academy of Dermatology.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- EASI score of ≥ 16 at screening.
- Participants must have had an inadequate response to at least two categories of topical therapies.

**Exclusion criteria:**  
- Current treatment with systemic immunomodulators or biologics for atopic dermatitis within 12 weeks prior to screening.
- History of hypersensitivity to sarilumab or its components.
- Active infections requiring systemic antibiotics, or a history of recurrent infections.

**Study Design:**  
Participants were randomly assigned in a 2:1 ratio to receive either sarilumab (administered subcutaneously every 2 weeks) or placebo. The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in EASI score at Week 24. Secondary endpoints included change from baseline in pruritus NRS and DLQI scores.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
The primary endpoint was analyzed using last observation carried forward (LOCF) method. Comparisons between treatment groups were conducted using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) with treatment, concurrent medications, and baseline scores as covariates.

**Study Results:**  
At Week 24, sarilumab demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in EASI scores compared to placebo, with a mean difference of 32% (p < 0.001). Improvement in pruritus NRS was also significant with sarilumab showing a 45% reduction versus a 10% decrease in the placebo group at the same time point (p < 0.001). Quality of life, as measured by DLQI, improved significantly, with sarilumab showing a 55% reduction compared to 20% in the placebo cohort (p < 0.01). 

**Safety Profile:**  
Sarilumab was generally well tolerated. The most commonly reported adverse events were injection site reactions (18% vs. 4% in the placebo group) and upper respiratory infections. Serious adverse events occurred in less than 2% of participants in both groups.

**Conclusions:**  
The results indicate that sarilumab is an effective and safe treatment option for adults with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis who have not adequately responded to topical therapies. Further long-term studies are warranted to evaluate its sustained efficacy and safety profile beyond the 52-week period.
