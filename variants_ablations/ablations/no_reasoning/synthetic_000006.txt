**Failed Clinical Trial of Aliskiren: Aliskiren Versus Placebo in Patients With Stage 3 to 4 Chronic Kidney Disease With Elevated Inflammatory Markers**

**Phase 3 Study of the Impact of Aliskiren on Renal Function and Inflammation Biomarkers in Patients With Chronic Kidney Disease**

**Study Objective:**  
To evaluate the efficacy of aliskiren, a direct renin inhibitor, in improving renal function and reducing inflammatory markers in patients with stage 3 to 4 non-diabetic chronic kidney disease (CKD) who present with elevated levels of specific inflammatory biomarkers (such as C-reactive protein and interleukin-6) compared to placebo.

**Methods:**  
This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial will involve patients diagnosed with stage 3 to 4 non-diabetic CKD. Participants will be assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either aliskiren at a daily dose of 150 mg or matching placebo for a duration of 24 months. Throughout the trial, all subjects will adhere to a low-sodium diet, with optional adjunctive antihypertensive medications to maintain blood pressure below 130/80 mmHg.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Male or female individuals aged 18 - 75 years
- Diagnosis of stage 3 or 4 non-diabetic CKD, defined by eGFR parameters:
  - Stage 3: eGFR 30-59 mL/min/1.73 m²
  - Stage 4: eGFR 15-29 mL/min/1.73 m²
- Elevated inflammatory markers (C-reactive protein (CRP) >3 mg/L or interleukin-6 >10 pg/mL)
- Written informed consent obtained

Exclusion Criteria:
- eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m²
- Recent acute kidney injury or unstable kidney function
- Known secondary causes of hypertension (such as renovascular disease)
- Concurrent treatment with RAAS inhibitors (ACE inhibitors or ARBs)
- Serum potassium >5.5 mmol/L
- History of severe allergic reactions to renin inhibitors
- Pregnancy, lactation, or failure to utilize reliable contraception in women of childbearing potential
- Major co-morbidities limiting life expectancy (malignancy, uncontrolled cardiovascular disease, etc.)

**Primary Outcomes:**
- Rate of change in eGFR from baseline at 12 and 24 months
- Change in inflammatory marker levels (CRP and interleukin-6) from baseline to 3, 12, and 24 months

**Secondary Outcomes:**
- Incidence of adverse events, including hyperkalemia and hypotension
- Quality of life assessments using validated questionnaires (e.g., EQ-5D)

**Study Rationale:**  
The rationale for this clinical trial stems from previous failed studies suggesting that while aliskiren has shown potential benefits in specific diabetic populations, its role in slowing renal disease progression in non-diabetic CKD has not been clearly established. The presence of elevated inflammatory markers indicating a heightened inflammatory response in CKD prompts hypotheses that targeting renin could yield additional therapeutic benefits in this population, potentially impacting renal function and related inflammation.

**Final Assessment:**  
Despite the novel hypothesis and comprehensive design, the trial was ultimately deemed unsuccessful as interim analyses demonstrated no significant improvement in renal function or reduction in inflammation markers with aliskiren treatment over placebo. Additionally, safety profiles raised concerns about acceptable levels of serum potassium and the management of adverse events, leading to early termination of the study. 

This outcome reiterates the complexities of managing CKD with direct renin inhibitors and highlights the need for further investigations into both the efficacy and safety of aliskiren in various CKD subpopulations before definitive conclusions can be drawn.
