**Study Title: Impact of Nebivolol on Cardiac Output and Left Ventricular Function in Patients with Heart Failure and Preserved Ejection Fraction**

**Objective:**
The primary objective of this multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial is to evaluate the effects of nebivolol on cardiac output and left ventricular function in patients diagnosed with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), assess its safety profile, and its impact on quality of life.

**Hypotheses:**
1. Nebivolol will significantly improve cardiac output measured by echocardiography at 24 weeks compared to placebo in patients with HFpEF.
2. Nebivolol will lead to a reduction in left ventricular wall stress, as indicated by a decrease in circulating biomarkers of myocardial strain (e.g., NT-proBNP), compared to placebo.
3. Patients receiving nebivolol will report a statistically significant improvement in health-related quality of life metrics (measured by the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire [KCCQ]) relative to the placebo group after 24 weeks.

**Background:**
Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction presents significant challenges, including impaired cardiac output and reduced functional capacity despite normal left ventricular ejection fraction. The sympathetic activation typically observed in HFpEF exacerbates the condition by increasing vascular resistance, leading to elevated blood pressure and myocardial wall stress. Nebivolol, as a selective β1-adrenoceptor blocker with vasodilatory properties via enhanced nitric oxide (NO) release, has shown promise in reducing systemic vascular resistance and improving overall cardiac output and hemodynamic parameters. 

In previous studies, nebivolol has demonstrated the ability to optimize endothelial function and ameliorate hypertension, thus addressing the multifaceted nature of pathology in HFpEF. However, limited clinical evidence exists specifically targeting HFpEF populations, necessitating rigorous evaluation.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 40-75 years with a confirmed diagnosis of heart failure and preserved ejection fraction (EF ≥ 50%).
- Clinical signs and symptoms consistent with heart failure as per Framingham criteria.
- NYHA Functional Class II to III.
- Baseline systolic blood pressure between 100-160 mmHg.
- Provide written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- A history of significant obstructive lung disease or symptomatic asthma.
- Previous intolerance or contraindication to nebivolol or other β-blockers.
- Patients with severe renal or hepatic impairment.
- Uncontrolled atrial fibrillation or other significant arrhythmias.
- Initiation of any new heart failure medications within the preceding 6 weeks.
- Patients who had undergone recent myocardial infarction (<3 months).

**Endpoints:**
1. Primary Endpoint: Change in cardiac output (L/min) assessed via echocardiography from baseline to 24 weeks.
2. Secondary Endpoints: 
   - Changes in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF).
   - Changes in serum NT-proBNP levels from baseline to 24 weeks.
   - Quality of life improvement measured by KCCQ score at 24 weeks.

**Statistical Analysis:**
The primary analysis will utilize a comparison of the change in cardiac output between the nebivolol and placebo groups at the endpoint using ANCOVA, adjusting for baseline values. Secondary analyses will be conducted similarly with respect to echocardiographic and biomarker data, employing a 2-sided significance level of p < 0.05.

In conclusion, this clinical trial aims not only to scrutinize the cardioprotective effects of nebivolol within the context of HFpEF but also to provide greater clarity around its role in improving patient quality of life through multidimensional endpoints, thus contributing valuable insights to the management of this complex patient population.
