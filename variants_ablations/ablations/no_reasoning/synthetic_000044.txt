Failed clinical trial of gemcitabine: A Phase II Randomized Study of Gemcitabine Versus Gemcitabine Combined with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy of combining gemcitabine with pembrolizumab in comparison to gemcitabine alone in patients diagnosed with advanced pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) who have not received prior systemic treatment. Inclusion Criteria: - Confirmed histological diagnosis of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. - Patients must have locally advanced unresectable or metastatic disease. - No prior systemic treatment for pancreatic cancer (including chemotherapy or immunotherapy). - Patients must be at least 18 years of age. - ECOG Performance Status (PS) of 0 to 1. - Adequate organ function demonstrated by laboratory assessments, including bilirubin, liver enzymes, and renal function. - Signed informed consent obtained from the patient. Exclusion Criteria: - Previous treatment with gemcitabine or anti-PD-1 therapy. - Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment within the last two years. - Ongoing or prior use of immunosuppressive therapy. - History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to pembrolizumab or gemcitabine. - Compromised renal, liver, or cardiac function not attributed to malignancy. - Uncontrolled infections or concurrent malignancy. The primary endpoint of the trial will evaluate overall survival, comparing the two groups at 6 months post-treatment initiation. The secondary endpoints will assess progression-free survival, overall response rates, and safety profiles between the two treatment regimens. The study is designed hypothesizing that the addition of pembrolizumab to gemcitabine will result in improved survival outcomes compared to gemcitabine alone; however, previous trials with immune checkpoint inhibitors in the same setting have yielded disappointing efficacy results, suggesting a limitation in the efficacy of immune modulation in the context of pancreatic adenocarcinoma. Adverse events will be monitored continuously throughout the study, and an interim analysis will be executed at the 3-month mark to evaluate safety and response, as it is hypothesized that the high burden of the disease and immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment may impede the anticipated synergistic effects of the combination therapy.
