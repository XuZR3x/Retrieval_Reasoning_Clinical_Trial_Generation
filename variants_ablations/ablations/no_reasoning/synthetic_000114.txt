**Successful clinical trial of atazanavir: Assessment of Atazanavir in Combination with NRTIs and its Impact on Viral Suppression and Immunological Response in HIV-Infected Patients**

**Study Title:** A Multicentre Randomized Controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy of Atazanavir in Combination with NRTIs in Achieving Viral Suppression and Immunological Restoration Among Treatment-Naive HIV-Infected Patients

**Objective:** The primary objective of this trial is to evaluate the efficacy of atazanavir (ATV) when used in combination with nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) in achieving viral suppression (defined as HIV-1 RNA < 50 copies/mL) and improving CD4 cell counts in treatment-naive individuals infected with HIV-1.

**Study Design:** This is a multicentre, open-label, randomized controlled trial. Participants will be recruited from diverse geographical locations, including North America, Europe, Africa, and Asia, representing a broad demographic of HIV-infected individuals. 

**Study Duration:** The duration of the study will be 96 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years and older, diagnosed with HIV-1 infection per laboratory criteria.
- Baseline HIV-1 RNA level ≥ 5,000 copies/mL.
- CD4 count between 200 and 500 cells/mm³.
- No prior exposure to antiretroviral therapy (ART), except for the use of single-dose nevirapine for prevention of mother-to-child transmission.
- Ability to provide written informed consent.
- Willingness to comply with study protocol and attend all follow-up visits.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of allergic reactions to atazanavir or any components of the study regimen.
- Presence of any current or historic acute hepatitis or hepatic insufficiency affecting drug metabolism.
- Women who are pregnant, breastfeeding, or not using adequate contraceptive methods.
- Participation in any clinical trial with investigational products in the past 30 days.
- Active drug or alcohol dependence that could jeopardize adherence to therapy.
- Concurrent severe medical conditions that could interfere with study endpoints.

**Randomization:** Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either:
1. Atazanavir once daily (300 mg) in combination with two NRTIs (tenofovir disoproxil fumarate and emtricitabine).
2. A control group receiving a fixed-dose combination of efavirenz with the same two NRTIs.

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary outcome measure will be the proportion of participants achieving HIV-1 RNA < 50 copies/mL at week 48.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Change in CD4 cell count from baseline to week 48.
- Incidence of adverse events related to treatment.
- Quality of life assessment (using validated questionnaires).
- Adherence to antiretroviral therapy measured by pill counts and self-reported compliance.

**Data Collection:** Blood samples will be collected for virological and immunological assessment at baseline, week 12, week 24, and week 48. Safety assessments will include routine laboratory tests (liver function tests, lipid profiles) and clinical evaluations at each study visit.

**Statistical Analysis:** Efficacy will be analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis, utilizing chi-square tests for categorical variables and t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous variables. A multivariate logistic regression model will assess the predictors of viral suppression.

**Safety Monitoring:** An independent Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will evaluate the safety data throughout the study. Adverse events will be categorized according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 4.03.

**Expected Outcome:** It is hypothesized that participants receiving atazanavir in combination with NRTIs will achieve superior rates of viral suppression and better immunological outcomes compared to the control group, thereby supporting the use of atazanavir as a first-line therapy in treatment-naive populations.

This trial seeks to establish the role of atazanavir as a cornerstone in antiretroviral therapy regimens, ultimately contributing to improved treatment paradigms for HIV-infected individuals globally.
