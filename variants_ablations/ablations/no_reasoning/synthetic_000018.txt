**Failed Clinical Trial of Tivozanib: A Phase II Study of Tivozanib in Combination With Imatinib for Patients With Advanced Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors (GISTs)**

**Study Overview:** This study is a Phase II, open-label, multicenter, randomized trial designed to investigate the efficacy and safety of tivozanib in combination with imatinib in patients with advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs) who have failed previous imatinib therapy. The primary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and objective response rate (ORR) compared to historical data for imatinib monotherapy.

**Background:** Tivozanib, an anti-angiogenic agent targeting vascular endothelial growth factor receptors, was hypothesized to enhance the therapeutic effects of imatinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, by modulating tumor vascularization and potentially improving nutrient supply to neoplastic tissues. Previous studies highlighted the need for alternative treatment regimens in imatinib-resistant GISTs.

**Study Design:** Eligible patients were randomized to receive either tivozanib combined with imatinib or imatinib monotherapy. Treatment cycles were repeated every 28 days. Imaging assessments via computed tomography (CT) scans were performed at baseline and every 8 weeks to evaluate tumor response based on Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v1.1. 

**Interim Analysis:** An interim analysis was conducted to assess the efficacy following the enrollment of 120 patients. The primary endpoint of PFS after 6 months demonstrated no significant difference between the experimental arm and historical controls for imatinib alone. Additionally, rates of ORR were clinically insignificant, with a disparity observed in overall clinical benefit.

**Safety Profile:** Tivozanib was generally well-tolerated; however, an augmented incidence of adverse events was recorded in the combination therapy group, particularly higher-grade hypertension and gastrointestinal toxicities. Serious adverse events leading to discontinuation were documented in 15% of trial participants receiving the combination therapy, versus 5% in the imatinib-only cohort.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST).
- Documented disease progression following imatinib therapy.
- Measurable lesions according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- ECOG performance status of 0 or 1.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with any anti-VEGF agent other than imatinib.
- Significant comorbid conditions predisposing to cardiovascular complications.
- Uncontrolled hypertension or serious concurrent medical illness.
- Major surgery within 28 days prior to randomization.

**Outcome Measures:** 
1. **Progression-Free Survival (PFS):** Time from randomization to disease progression or death from any cause.
2. **Overall Survival (OS):** Duration from randomization until death from any cause.
3. **Objective Response Rates (ORR):** The proportion of participants achieving complete or partial responses as evaluated by imaging.

**Conclusion:** Due to the lack of improvement in PFS, OS, and ORR in the tivozanib plus imatinib arm compared to a historical cohort of imatinib alone, the trial was terminated prematurely. Comprehensive biomarker analysis revealed potential mechanisms of resistance that were not effectively addressed by the combination regimen. The results underscore the complexity of optimizing treatment for advanced GISTs and the necessity for further investigation into alternative combinatory strategies or novel therapeutic agents.

**Follow-Up:** Patients were followed for the assessment of long-term outcomes until 24 months post-treatment cessation. The findings from this trial, although disappointing, contribute to the understanding of GIST therapy and the exploration of combination therapies involving targeted agents. Further studies are warranted to evaluate more successful strategies in this patient population.
