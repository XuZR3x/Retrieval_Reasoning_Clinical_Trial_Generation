<clinical_trial>
  <title>Evaluation of Enzastaurin in Patients with Advanced Melanoma: A Phase II Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study</title>
  <design>A Phase II Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Enzastaurin versus Placebo in Combination with Standard Immunotherapy in Patients with Advanced Melanoma</design>
  <objective>The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy of enzastaurin in improving progression-free survival (PFS) compared to placebo as part of a combined immunotherapy regimen in patients with advanced melanoma. Secondary objectives include the evaluation of overall survival (OS), safety profile, and response rates using RECIST criteria.</objective>
  
  <inclusion_criteria>
    <criteria>Histologically confirmed advanced melanoma (Stage III or IV);</criteria>
    <criteria>Age ≥18 years;</criteria>
    <criteria>Must have received at least one prior line of systemic therapy;</criteria>
    <criteria>Patients must be willing to provide informed consent;</criteria>
    <criteria>Patients must have measurable disease as determined by CT or MRI scans;</criteria>
    <criteria>Must have an ECOG performance status of 0-2.</criteria>
  </inclusion_criteria>
  
  <exclusion_criteria>
    <criteria>Secondary malignancies treated within the last 3 years;</criteria>
    <criteria>History of severe adverse reactions to immunotherapy;</criteria>
    <criteria>Uncontrolled cardiovascular disease or active infection;</criteria>
    <criteria>Pregnant or breastfeeding women;</criteria>
    <criteria>Active autoimmune diseases requiring ongoing systemic treatment;</criteria>
    <criteria>Impairment of the gastrointestinal tract preventing adequate oral intake.</criteria>
  </exclusion_criteria>

  <endpoints>
    <primary_endpoint>Progression-Free Survival (PFS), defined as the duration from randomization to the first instance of disease progression or death.</primary_endpoint>
    <secondary_endpoint>Overall Survival (OS) and Objective Response Rate (ORR) assessed at 12 and 24 weeks post-treatment initiation.</secondary_endpoint>
    <safety_endpoint>Evaluating the frequency and severity of adverse events (AEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs) in accordance with Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.</safety_endpoint>
  </endpoints>
  
  <results>
    <finding>The combination therapy of enzastaurin with standard immunotherapy did not demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in PFS compared to placebo (median PFS of 3.5 months vs. 4.0 months, p=0.75). Furthermore, the ORR was comparable, with 15% in the enzastaurin group versus 14% in the placebo group.</finding>
    <adverse_events>A total of 65 grade 3 or higher AEs were reported, most commonly including fatigue (15%), hypertension (10%), and rash (8%) in the enzastaurin group.</adverse_events>
  </results>
  
  <conclusion>The addition of enzastaurin to standard immunotherapy did not lead to improved therapeutic outcomes in patients with advanced melanoma. Notably, this trial underlines the necessity for further research to understand the mechanisms leading to lack of efficacy of enzastaurin in this patient population, particularly in the context of prior exposure to immunotherapeutic agents.</conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
