**Successful Clinical Trial of Abatacept: Long-Term Efficacy and Safety of Abatacept in Established Rheumatoid Arthritis**

A Phase IV Multi-center, Open-Label Study to Assess Long-Term Efficacy and Safety of Abatacept in Patients With Established Rheumatoid Arthritis Who Have Inadequate Response to Conventional DMARDs

The objective of this study is to evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety profile of abatacept in patients with established rheumatoid arthritis (RA) who exhibit an inadequate response to conventional disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs). The study will assess the improvement in clinical symptoms and joint function over a 24-month treatment period.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male and female patients aged 18 years and older, diagnosed with moderate to severe established RA (≥6 months) per ACR/EULAR classification criteria.
- Documented inadequate response to at least one conventional DMARD (including methotrexate) within the 12 months prior to screening.
- Disease activity score (DAS28) ≥ 3.2 at screening, with at least one tender and swollen joint.
- Patients are required to provide written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with biologic DMARDs or Janus kinase inhibitors (JAKs) within the last 6 months.
- Current or history of serious infections, including but not limited to tuberculosis, invasive fungal infections, and HIV.
- Known malignancies within the past 5 years, except for localized non-melanoma skin cancer.
- Pregnancy or lactation, or women of childbearing potential not using contraceptive measures.
- Systemic lupus erythematosus or other autoimmune connective tissue diseases.

**Primary Endpoint:**
- The primary efficacy endpoint will be improvement in Disease Activity Score 28 (DAS28) from baseline to 24 months assessed by the number of patients achieving a DAS28 score of ≤ 2.6.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Assessment of joint damage progression via radiographic analysis (Sharp-van der Heijde score) at baseline and 24 months.
- Analysis of safety parameters, including adverse events (AEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs).

**Statistical Analysis:**
Descriptive statistics will be used to summarize demographic and baseline characteristics. The proportion of patients achieving DAS28 remission will be compared using a Chi-square test.

**Conclusion:**
This study aims to provide substantial evidence regarding the long-term clinical benefits and safety profile of abatacept in patients with established rheumatoid arthritis inadequately managed by conventional DMARDs, thereby reinforcing its therapeutic potential in advanced RA management strategies. The anticipated outcomes could substantiate the role of abatacept as a critical intervention in achieving sustained remission and preventing joint damage in this patient cohort.
