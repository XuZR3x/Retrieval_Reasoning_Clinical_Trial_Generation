**Failed Clinical Trial of Plitidepsin: A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Plitidepsin in Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Diffuse Large B-cell Lymphoma**

**Overview:**
This is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm Phase II clinical trial designed to assess the efficacy of plitidepsin in patients with relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL). Given the aggressive nature of DLBCL and the limited treatment options available for patients who have failed previous therapies, this study aims to evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) following administration of plitidepsin.

**Objectives:**
The primary endpoint of the study is the overall response rate (ORR) as defined by the revised International Working Group (IWG) criteria. Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and the safety profile of plitidepsin.

**Study Design:**
The trial enrolled a total of 40 participants from approximately 12 investigative sites globally. Slowly accruing patient enrollment led to the postponement of futility and efficacy assessments beyond the planned timeline.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Written informed consent from the patient prior to any study-specific procedure.
2. Age ≥ 18 years.
3. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of relapsed or refractory DLBCL.
4. At least one prior line of systemic therapy, including one immunochemotherapy regimen.
5. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2.
6. Measurable disease as per IWG criteria.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior or concomitant treatment with plitidepsin or agents targeting the JNK pathway.
2. Active uncontrolled infections or significant comorbidities that may impact the patient's safety.
3. Pregnant or breastfeeding women. 
4. Patients with known hypersensitivity to plitidepsin formulation components.

**Methods:**
Treatment consisted of plitidepsin administered intravenously at a predetermined dose on a 21-day cycle. The ORR was evaluated post-treatment based on clinical assessments and imaging studies, adhering strictly to IWG criteria. An Independent Data Monitoring Committee (IDMC) was responsible for oversight of data and safety evaluations throughout the clinical trial.

**Endpoints:**
1. Primary Endpoint: Overall response rate (ORR) per IWG criteria, requiring a minimum of 10 patients to achieve an evaluable response.
2. Secondary Endpoints: Progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) assessed at 3, 6, and 12 months post-treatment initiation.

**Results:**
Despite an initial goal to enroll at least 40 patients and adequately assess the primary endpoint, the trial commenced with only 25 patients enrolled by the midpoint of the evaluation period. Subsequently, only 19 patients received treatment with plitidepsin. Among these, 3 patients demonstrated partial response (PR) while the remainder had stable disease (SD) or progressed post-treatment. The overall response rate did not meet the predetermined threshold of 15% responders required to consider plitidepsin as having a potential efficacy in this patient population. Consequently, the IDMC recommended termination of the study due to insufficient evidence of clinical benefit.

**Safety Profile:**
The combination of plitidepsin with other systemic therapies raised concerns regarding adverse events, particularly myelosuppression and cardiac toxicity including QT prolongation, which were notable in the small treatment cohort.

**Conclusion:**
The clinical trial evaluating plitidepsin in patients with relapsed or refractory DLBCL did not demonstrate sufficient efficacy to warrant further investigation. The low recruitment rate and disappointing response suggest that plitidepsin may not possess the desired therapeutic efficacy within this particular malignancy. Further research to explore alternative therapeutic frameworks or combination therapies involving plitidepsin may be necessary for optimizing patient outcomes in DLBCL. 

The findings underscore the complexities and challenges of conducting trials in relapsed hematologic malignancies, and the need for ongoing adaptation of investigational approaches to effectively target difficult-to-treat cancers.
