<clinical_trial>
   <title>Long-Term Effects of Ivacaftor on Quality of Life and Nutritional Status in Cystic Fibrosis Patients with G551D Mutation</title>
   <objective>This study aims to evaluate the long-term impact of ivacaftor on quality of life, nutritional status, and respiratory function in patients with cystic fibrosis (CF) harboring the G551D mutation. We hypothesize that ivacaftor treatment will lead to significant improvement in quality of life and nutritional biomarkers, in addition to enhancing pulmonary function.</objective>
   <design>A multi-center, randomized, open-label, parallel-group trial to assess the effects of ivacaftor on quality of life and nutritional parameters in CF subjects with the G551D mutation. A total of 60 patients aged 12-40 years will be enrolled and randomly assigned to either the ivacaftor treatment arm or the control arm receiving standard care over a treatment duration of 24 weeks.</design>
   <methodology>Patients undergoing the study will complete screening assessments (Week -4), baseline quality of life assessments using the Cystic Fibrosis Questionnaire-Revised (CFQ-R) and nutritional screenings (Week 0). Following randomization, patients in the ivacaftor arm will commence treatment with 150 mg orally twice daily. Patients in the control group will continue their standard therapy. Follow-up assessments will be conducted at Weeks 8, 16, and 24, during which quality of life, nutritional status (including Body Mass Index [BMI] and fat soluble vitamins A, D, E, K levels), and pulmonary function via FEV1 will be monitored. Additionally, longitudinal evaluation of airway microbiota and inflammatory markers (including IL-6 and TNFα) will be conducted at baseline and at the end of the study period.</methodology>
   <inclusion_criteria>
      <criterion>Confirmed diagnosis of CF based on standard criteria (sweat chloride test, genotyping).</criterion>
      <criterion>Presence of at least one G551D CFTR mutation.</criterion>
      <criterion>Age range between 12 and 40 years.</criterion>
      <criterion>Ability to complete quality of life questionnaires and nutritional assessments.</criterion>
      <criterion>Baseline FEV1 >40% of predicted.</criterion>
   </inclusion_criteria>
   <exclusion_criteria>
      <criterion>Current participation in another investigational drug study.</criterion>
      <criterion>Known hypersensitivity to ivacaftor or similar CF therapeutic agents.</criterion>
      <criterion>Acute exacerbations or respiratory infections within four weeks prior to enrollment.</criterion>
      <criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding females as determined by positive pregnancy test during screening.</criterion>
   </exclusion_criteria>
   <primary_endpoints>
      <endpoint>Absolute change from baseline in CFQ-R total score after 24 weeks.</endpoint>
      <endpoint>Change from baseline in BMI and serum levels of fat soluble vitamins.</endpoint>
   </primary_endpoints>
   <secondary_endpoints>
      <endpoint>Change in FEV1 and inflammatory markers from baseline to Week 24.</endpoint>
      <endpoint>Incidence of respiratory infections and adverse events throughout the study duration.</endpoint>
   </secondary_endpoints>
   <anticipated_outcomes>It is anticipated that patients treated with ivacaftor will exhibit significant improvements in quality of life, nutritional status, and pulmonary function compared to standard care, which may further substantiate the efficacy of ivacaftor in modulating CF-related complications.</anticipated_outcomes>
   <conclusion>This trial seeks to expand the understanding of ivacaftor beyond respiratory outcomes to quality of life and nutritional health aspects among CF patients, thereby reinforcing the multidimensional benefits of CFTR modulators in the management of cystic fibrosis.</conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
