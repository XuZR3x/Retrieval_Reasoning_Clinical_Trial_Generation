### A Phase II Study of Arsenic Trioxide in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**RATIONALE:** Arsenic trioxide has demonstrated anti-tumor activity in various hematologic malignancies and has been under investigation for its potential role in solid tumors, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). This study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of arsenic trioxide in combination with standard chemotherapy regimens in advanced NSCLC, as current treatment options often result in suboptimal outcomes.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aims to assess the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of arsenic trioxide in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer. Furthermore, the study will determine the pharmacokinetic profile of arsenic trioxide when administered in conjunction with cisplatin and pemetrexed.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objectives:**
  - To evaluate the overall response rate (complete and partial responses) of arsenic trioxide in combination with cisplatin and pemetrexed in patients with advanced NSCLC.
  - To assess the safety profile of arsenic trioxide in this patient population.

- **Secondary Objectives:**
  - To determine progression-free survival (PFS) rates within the treatment cohort.
  - To evaluate the overall survival (OS) of patients treated with the combination regimen.
  - To assess quality of life (QoL) measures during treatment.

**OUTLINE:** 
This is an open-label, multicenter, phase II study involving an anticipated enrollment of 120 patients. Eligible patients will receive arsenic trioxide intravenously at a dose of 0.5 mg/kg over 2 hours on days 1, 8, and 15 of a 28-day cycle. The combination regimen will also include cisplatin at a dose of 75 mg/m² and pemetrexed at a dose of 500 mg/m² on day 1, co-administered during the same cycle. Treatment will be continued for up to six cycles, barring disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

- **Projected Accrual:** Approximately 120 patients will be accrued over 18 months.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of stage IIIB or IV non-small cell lung cancer.
- No prior systemic therapy for advanced disease, except adjuvant therapy if completed ≥ 6 months prior to enrollment.
- ECOG Performance Status 0-2.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Adequate hepatic and renal function, defined as:
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).
  - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 × ULN.
  - Creatinine clearance ≥ 50 mL/min.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Pregnant or lactating women.
- History of significant cardiac disease, including myocardial infarction within the last 6 months, significant arrhythmias, or congestive heart failure (NYHA Class III or IV).
- Serious concomitant medical conditions that could impair study participation or compromise patient safety.
- Previous exposure to arsenic trioxide or hypersensitivity to arsenic compounds.
- Concurrent uncontrolled infections or active secondary malignancies.
- Patients requiring medications known to prolong the QT interval.

**SAFETY MONITORING:** 
Adverse events will be assessed according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Particular attention will be paid to cardiotoxicities, infusion-related reactions, and hematologic toxicities. Cardiac monitoring via EKG will be performed at baseline and prior to each dose of arsenic trioxide.

**DATA ANALYSIS:** 
Response rates will be evaluated according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria. PFS and OS will be computed using the Kaplan-Meier method, with comparisons between groups assessed using the log-rank test as appropriate.

Despite a sound rationale for the use of arsenic trioxide in this setting, preliminary analysis indicated a high rate of adverse events, including severe hematologic toxicities and cardiac arrhythmias, leading to early termination of study participation for a substantial number of enrolled patients. The combination did not demonstrate a significant improvement in response rates compared to historical data for standard chemotherapy alone.

This study underscores the complexities and challenges inherent in repurposing historical agents such as arsenic trioxide in the treatment of solid tumors like non-small cell lung cancer, where therapeutic benefits were overshadowed by significant safety concerns. As such, further investigations employing alternative dosing strategies or combination approaches are warranted to explore potential therapeutic windows.
