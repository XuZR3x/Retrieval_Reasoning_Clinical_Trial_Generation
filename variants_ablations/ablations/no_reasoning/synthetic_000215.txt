**Failed Clinical Trial of Regorafenib: Evaluation of Regorafenib in Combination with Immunotherapy in Advanced Melanoma**

**Title:** A Phase II Study of Regorafenib with Anti-PD-1 Therapy in Patients with Advanced Melanoma: A Randomized, Open-Label Trial

**Objective:** The primary objective of the study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of regorafenib in combination with anti-PD-1 therapy (pembrolizumab) compared to pembrolizumab alone in patients with advanced melanoma who have received prior systemic therapy. Given the limited therapeutic options available for this cohort and the increasing exploration of combination regimens, this trial aims to elucidate potential synergies between pathway modulation and immune checkpoint inhibition.

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, randomized, open-label Phase II study. Participants will be enrolled across multiple sites, with a planned enrollment of 150 patients over 18 months. Eligible participants will be randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either regorafenib in conjunction with pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab monotherapy. The treatment regimen will consist of oral regorafenib (160 mg daily for 21 days of a 28-day cycle) in combination with pembrolizumab administered intravenously every 3 weeks until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal.

**Endpoints:** The primary endpoint of this study will be progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months. Secondary endpoints include the overall response rate (ORR), overall survival (OS), and the assessment of treatment-related adverse events as categorized by the NCI Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced melanoma (Stage III or IV).
- Patients must have progressed on or after at least one line of prior systemic therapy for advanced melanoma, inclusive of anti-PD-1 agents.
- Measurable disease per RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status (PS) of 0-1.
- Age ≥ 18 years with a life expectancy of at least 12 weeks.
- Adequate organ function as defined by laboratory parameters: 
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN
  - Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) ≤ 2.5 x ULN 
  - Creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min as per the modified diet in renal disease (MDRD) formula.
- Informed consent obtained from all participants.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with regorafenib or other multi-kinase inhibitors within 6 months of enrollment.
- Concurrent participation in another clinical trial involving investigational drugs or procedures.
- Uncontrolled hypertension (>150/90 mmHg) or clinically significant cardiovascular disease.
- History of autoimmune disease requiring systemic immunosuppressive therapy or active central nervous system metastases.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women, or women of childbearing potential not using adequate contraception. 

**Data Analysis:** The primary analysis will be performed using an intent-to-treat approach. Patients will be evaluated for PFS at 6 months via Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. The safety profile will be outlined based on observed adverse events, categorized by severity.

**Rationale for Study Design:** This study was conceived based on preclinical studies suggesting that regorafenib's multi-targeted action might enhance the anti-tumor immune response generated by PD-1 blockade. Conversely, previous clinical trials demonstrated limited efficacy of regorafenib as a monotherapy, necessitating exploration of its potential benefit when used in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors. 

**Study Termination:** Unfortunately, this trial was terminated due to observed adverse events and a lack of statistically significant improvement in PFS when compared to the pembrolizumab monotherapy group. The combination therapy exhibited a higher incidence of Grade 3 and 4 adverse effects, primarily hyperbilirubinemia and hypertension, leading to concerns regarding the safety profile in this already compromised patient population. Consequently, due to the unfavorable risk-to-benefit ratio, the trial was deemed unsuccessful, and further investigations into regorafenib as a combination therapy in this setting were put on hold.

**Conclusion:** Further exploration of regorafenib in combination with established immunotherapeutic approaches will require a thorough re-evaluation of the dosing regimens and patient selection criteria to enhance the therapeutic index while minimizing associated toxicities.
