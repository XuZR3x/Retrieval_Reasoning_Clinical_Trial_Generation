**Title:** A Phase II Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Vandetanib (ZD6474) in Combination with Standard Chemotherapy in Patients With Stage IV Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

**Overview:**  
This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase II clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vandetanib (ZD6474) administered in conjunction with standard chemotherapy in patients diagnosed with stage IV non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Non-small cell lung cancer is the most common form of lung cancer and is associated with a poor prognosis, necessitating novel therapeutic approaches that can improve survival outcomes. Vandetanib, a potent inhibitor of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR), is hypothesized to enhance the effects of conventional chemotherapy agents through its dual action mechanism.

**Rationale:**  
The primary rationale for this trial stems from the substantial expression of EGFR in NSCLC, which plays a pivotal role in tumor proliferation and survival mechanisms. Furthermore, aberrant VEGF signaling is implicated in tumor angiogenesis, contributing to the aggressive nature of NSCLC. Vandetanib's pharmacological profile, characterized by its oral bioavailability and capability to inhibit EGFR and VEGFR, suggests that it may offer a synergistic effect when combined with standard chemotherapy regimens. Previous studies have indicated that the combination of chemotherapy and ECM inhibitors could provide a means to overcome drug resistance and improve clinical outcomes.

**Study Design:**  
This is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study involving two arms: one receiving vandetanib in conjunction with a standard chemotherapy regimen (cisplatin and pemetrexed) and the other receiving a matched placebo with the same chemotherapy regimen. Participants will be stratified based on previous treatment exposure and performance status.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically confirmed stage IV NSCLC.  
- Patients must be 18 years or older.  
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.  
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
- Adequate hematological function (Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³, Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³).  
- Adequate hepatic function (Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN, AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN).  
- Renal function (Creatinine Clearance ≥ 50 mL/min).  
- Female patients must have a negative pregnancy test and willingness to use contraception.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Prior treatment with EGFR inhibitors or VEGF inhibitors.  
- Active interstitial lung disease or serious uncontrolled comorbidity.  
- History of cardiac events (e.g., myocardial infarction, arrhythmias) within the past six months.  
- QTc interval > 480 msec at screening.  
- Ongoing treatment with medications known to prolong QT interval.  
- Any major surgery within 4 weeks prior to enrollment.  
- Concurrent conditions that, in the opinion of the investigator, would jeopardize compliance with the protocol.

**Objectives:**  
The primary objective of the study is to assess progression-free survival (PFS) in patients receiving vandetanib combined with the chemotherapy regimen compared to those receiving placebo. Secondary objectives include evaluating overall survival (OS), overall response rate (ORR), and the safety profile of the combination therapy as indicated by the incidence of treatment-related adverse events, laboratory abnormalities, and quality of life assessments.

**Endpoints:**  
- Primary endpoint: Progression-free survival as assessed by radiological evaluation using RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
- Secondary endpoints: Overall survival, overall response rate, and treatment-related adverse events as graded by the CTCAEv5 criteria. 

**Statistical Analysis:**  
A sample size of 200 participants will be needed to determine the efficacy of vandetanib against placebo in prolonging PFS with a power of 80% and two-sided alpha of 0.05. The Kaplan-Meier method will be utilized for survival analysis and comparisons will be performed using the log-rank test. Adverse event data will be summarized using descriptive statistics.

**Conclusion:**  
This Phase II trial is significant as it assesses the clinical benefit of combining vandetanib with standard chemotherapy for patients with advanced NSCLC—a cohort often facing limited therapeutic options. Given the established role of EGFR and VEGF in tumor biology, the anticipated results may provide a foundation for future larger scale studies aimed at improving outcomes for patients with this challenging malignancy.
