**Failed Clinical Trial of Montelukast Sodium: Montelukast for Exercise-Induced Bronchoconstriction in Adolescents (0476-489)(COMPLETED)**  
A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Montelukast Sodium Compared to Placebo in Preventing Exercise-Induced Bronchoconstriction in Adolescents Aged 12 to 17 Years with Asthma 

This clinical trial aimed to investigate the therapeutic impact of montelukast sodium in a demographic often unrepresented in previous studies, focusing on exercise-induced bronchoconstriction (EIB) during physical exertion. Over a treatment period of 12 weeks, the efficacy of montelukast was assessed in the specified population participating in organized physical activities.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients aged 12 to 17 years diagnosed with asthma  
- Patients with a documented history of exercise-induced bronchoconstriction (EIB) defined as a drop in FEV1 of ≥ 15% post-exercise  
- Patients undergoing a regular exercise regime of at least 3 days a week  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients exhibiting other significant pulmonary diseases or conditions impacting airway reactivity  
- Patients using high-dose inhaled corticosteroids or systemic corticosteroids  
- Patients who have had previous adverse reactions to montelukast  

**Primary Endpoint:**  
- Change in FEV1 (Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 Second) from baseline, measured immediately post-exercise at Week 12. This primary endpoint was designed to quantify improvement in airway patency following an exercise challenge administered at the conclusion of the treatment phase.

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
- The frequency of rescue β-agonist use measured as the average daily intake over the 12-week treatment period  
- Patient-reported outcomes regarding control of asthma symptoms and quality of life assessed via a validated questionnaire administered at baseline, Week 6, and Week 12  

**Study Procedure:**  
Eligible patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either montelukast sodium (10 mg orally once daily) or matching placebo for the duration of the study. Patients were required to undergo controlled exercise testing pre- and post-intervention to reliably assess FEV1 changes related to EIB.

Despite the rigorous design and proper stratification of the study cohort, the trial ultimately failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in the primary endpoint when comparing montelukast and placebo. The observed change in FEV1 post-exercise was not significantly greater in the montelukast group (mean change from baseline of -1.2% [95% CI: -3.1 to 0.7]) compared to placebo (-0.9% [95% CI: -2.8 to 1.0]). Additionally, no meaningful difference in rescue β-agonist usage was reported in either arm.

Conclusions derived from the trial underscored challenges in the pharmacological approach to managing EIB in adolescents, identifying potential limitations in the use of montelukast sodium, especially when utilized as a monotherapy within this specific patient population. The findings necessitate further research into alternative or adjunctive therapies for the management of EIB in adolescents with asthma.
