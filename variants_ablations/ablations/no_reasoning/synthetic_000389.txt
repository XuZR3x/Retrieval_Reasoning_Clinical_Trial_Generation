**Failed Clinical Trial of Radiotherapy: Evaluating the Efficacy of Radiotherapy Combined with Immune Checkpoint Inhibition for Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer**

**A Phase II Study of Radiotherapy and Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Combination Therapy in Patients With Heavily Pretreated, Metastatic Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma**. This study aims to assess the efficacy of combining radiotherapy with immune checkpoint inhibition, specifically anti-PD-1 therapy, in patients with advanced metastatic pancreatic cancer who have not responded to standard chemotherapy.

1. **Objectives**: 
   - To evaluate the immune-mediated tumor response induced by radiotherapy in conjunction with immune checkpoint inhibitors in non-irradiated metastatic sites.
   - To determine the overall therapeutic effectiveness of this combined modality approach in heavily pretreated patients with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma.
   - To assess the potential correlation between changes in immune markers and clinical response as measured by imaging.

2. **Study Design**: This study includes heavily pretreated patients with previously treated metastatic pancreatic cancer, where subsequent lines of therapy have either failed or resulted in disease progression. The effect of the treatment regimen on tumor response will be visualized using PET/CT and MRI scans.

Eligible patients will receive a dose of radiotherapy to the primary tumor site, comprising 30Gy in 5 fractions administered over one week while concurrently receiving anti-PD-1 therapy, administered bi-weekly. The anticipated timeline for assessing treatment response will occur at a 12-week interval post-initiation of therapy.

3. **Clinical Framework**: A Phase II clinical trial utilizing a Simon two-stage optimal design is adopted to ensure efficacy assessment. Twenty patients will be treated in Stage One. If no objective responses are observed, the study will be terminated. Should one or more objective responses be noted, an additional 30 patients will be enrolled.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Patients must have histologically confirmed pancreatic adenocarcinoma with evidence of metastatic disease.
- Three distinct measurable metastatic lesions are required, with a minimum size of 1 cm in the longest axis.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Patients must demonstrate disease progression after at least two lines of systemic chemotherapy.
- ECOG performance status of 0-1.
- Life expectancy estimated at a minimum of three months.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- History of severe allergic reactions to PD-1 inhibitors or prior treatments that may interfere with the study objectives.
- Patients currently being treated with any other investigational agents.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- Presence of uncontrolled active infections, autoimmune diseases, or clinically significant cardiac conditions.
- Patients with brain metastases or other malignancies within the past five years, with exceptions for specific skin cancers.
- Any condition that, in the investigator's opinion, would contraindicate the patient's participation or increase the risk of toxicity.

**Endpoints**: The primary endpoint focuses on the proportion of patients exhibiting local disease control at non-irradiated metastatic sites, evaluated using RECIST criteria at the 12-week mark following treatment initiation. Secondary endpoints will include overall survival rates, progression-free survival, and changes in biomarkers correlating with clinical response.

Ultimately, the trial aims to elucidate whether leveraging the abscopal effect through the synergistic effects of radiotherapy and immune checkpoint inhibition yields identifiable clinical responses in the metastatic progression of pancreatic adenocarcinoma.
