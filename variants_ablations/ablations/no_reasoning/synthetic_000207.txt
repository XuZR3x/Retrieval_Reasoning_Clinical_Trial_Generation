**Failed Clinical Trial of Efalizumab: Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Efalizumab in Patients with Chronic Active Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)**

**A Phase II Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Assess the Safety and Efficacy of Efalizumab in Adult Patients with Moderate to Severe Chronic Active Inflammatory Bowel Disease**

**RATIONALE:** Efalizumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting CD11a, shows potential in modulating immune responses via inhibition of T cell activation. Chronic active inflammatory bowel disease, encompassing Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, is characterized by dysregulated immune responses leading to intestinal inflammation. The overarching goal of this clinical trial is to evaluate whether early intervention with efalizumab could mitigate inflammatory responses and potentially improve clinical outcomes.

**OBJECTIVES:**

**Primary:** 

- Evaluate the proportion of patients achieving a clinical response defined by a decrease of at least 50% in the Harvey-Bradshaw Index (HBI) for Crohn’s disease or a decrease of at least 3 points in the Ulcerative Colitis Symptom Score (UCSS) at week 12.

**Secondary:** 

- Assess changes in the mucosal healing rates as determined by endoscopic findings compared to baseline.
- Monitor adverse events and long-term safety, particularly concerning serious infections and lymphoproliferative disorders.
- Investigate pharmacokinetic parameters of efalizumab in this specific patient demographic.

**OUTLINE:** Participants will receive subcutaneous efalizumab or placebo once weekly for 12 weeks. The study consists of a pre-treatment screening period, a double-blind treatment phase, and a follow-up observation period. Patients will undergo clinical assessment, endoscopy, and laboratory evaluations to monitor disease activity and potential adverse effects.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**

1. Adult patients (ages 18-75) diagnosed with moderate to severe Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis based on established clinical and endoscopic criteria.
2. Patients must exhibit active disease with a minimum HBI of 7 or UCSS of 4.
3. No History of anti-TNF therapy or biologic agents within the last 3 months prior to enrollment.
4. Written informed consent must be acquired from all participants.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**

1. Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to efalizumab or its components.
2. Patients exhibiting active infections, particularly opportunistic infections or serious infections requiring hospitalization within the last 60 days.
3. Pregnant or breastfeeding women, or those not using effective contraception.
4. History of malignant disease within the past 5 years (excluding non-melanoma skin cancer).
5. Any comorbidities that, per investigator discretion, may complicate treatment or increase the risk of severe adverse events.

**EFFICACY:** The primary outcome will be assessed through measurement of clinical response rates at week 12, while secondary outcomes include analysis of endoscopic healing rates and safety profiles over the 12-week treatment duration. A comprehensive assessment of adverse events will be performed, emphasizing the incidence of infections and the emergence of new anti-donor antibodies as potential markers of aberrant immune responses.

**RESULTS:** The study was terminated prematurely due to a lack of significant efficacy; only 20% of patients receiving efalizumab reached the primary endpoint of clinical response compared to 25% in the placebo group. There was a notable incidence of serious infections in the efalizumab group, prompting concerns regarding the safety profile of the drug in this patient population. Additionally, several participants experienced elevated levels of liver enzymes, raising alarms about potential hepatotoxicity.

**CONCLUSION:** This clinical trial illustrates the challenges associated with the use of efalizumab in managing chronic active inflammatory bowel disease. Despite its theoretical immunological benefits, the observed clinical outcomes and safety profile did not meet the expectations for efficacy, warranting further investigation into alternative therapeutic strategies in this patient population.
