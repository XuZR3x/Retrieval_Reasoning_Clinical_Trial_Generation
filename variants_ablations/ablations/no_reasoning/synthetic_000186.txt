**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy of Dexmedetomidine in Reducing Postoperative Delirium in Elderly Patients Undergoing Hip Fracture Surgery**

**Title**: Evaluation of Dexmedetomidine Infusion for the Reduction of Postoperative Delirium Incidence in Elderly Patients Following Hip Fracture Surgery: A Randomized Controlled Trial.

**Objective**: This study aimed to investigate the efficacy and safety of dexmedetomidine infusion in preventing postoperative delirium in geriatric patients undergoing surgical repair of hip fractures. 

**Study Design**: A single-center, double-blinded, randomized controlled trial was conducted from January 2018 to December 2019 at the Department of Anesthesiology and Surgery, Academic Medical Center. The use of dexmedetomidine, an α2-adrenergic agonist exhibiting sedative, analgesic, and neuroprotective properties, could theoretically mitigate the incidence of postoperative delirium commonly observed in elderly surgical patients.

**Ethical Considerations**: The study protocol received approval from the Institutional Review Board at Academic Medical Center (approval code 34). 

**Participants**: 120 elderly patients, aged 65 and above, who were scheduled for surgical intervention due to hip fractures, were enrolled in the study. 

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Age 65 years and older
- American Society of Anesthesiology (ASA) physical status I to III
- Consent capacity at the time of assessment

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Patients with severe cognitive impairment (pre-existing dementia)
- History of hypersensitivity to dexmedetomidine
- Neurological diseases
- Patients receiving chronic sedative therapy 

**Methods**: Subjects were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either dexmedetomidine (Group D) or placebo saline (Group C) starting preoperatively and continuing throughout the surgical procedure until extubation. The dexmedetomidine infusion was initiated at a rate of 0.5 mcg/kg/hr. Standard anesthesia protocols were maintained, including induction with propofol and maintenance with sevoflurane.

**Primary Outcome**: The primary outcome measured was the incidence of postoperative delirium, evaluated using the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) tool during the first 72 hours postoperatively.

**Secondary Outcomes**:
- Duration of delirium if present
- Total postoperative opioid consumption within the first 48 hours
- Length of postoperative hospital stay
- Incidence of adverse events related to dexmedetomidine infusion including bradycardia and hypotension

**Monitoring**: Continuous intraoperative monitoring of vital signs (heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation) was conducted, along with assessment of sedation levels using the Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS) at 0, 30, 60, and 120 minutes postoperatively.

**Statistical Analysis**: Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25.0. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were represented as means with standard deviations. Comparisons between the two groups were made using the Chi-square test for categorical data and the Student's t-test for continuous data, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05.

**Results**: A total of 120 patients were analyzed, with the incidence of postoperative delirium significantly lower in the dexmedetomidine group (15%) compared to the control group (40%) (p < 0.001). The duration of delirium was significantly shorter in the dexmedetomidine group, with a mean of 1.5 days versus 3 days in the control group (p = 0.002). Mean postoperative opioid consumption was reduced in the dexmedetomidine group (20 mg) compared to the control group (40 mg) (p < 0.001). Adverse effects were minimal, with only three participants experiencing transient bradycardia, which resolved without intervention.

**Conclusion**: The infusion of dexmedetomidine during hip fracture surgery in elderly patients significantly reduces the incidence and duration of postoperative delirium while decreasing opioid requirements. These findings suggest that dexmedetomidine can be considered a valuable adjunct in the perioperative management of elderly patients to improve outcomes and enhance recovery.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Age 65 years and older
- ASA physical status I to III
- Consent capacity at the time of assessment

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Patients with severe cognitive impairment (pre-existing dementia)
- History of hypersensitivity to dexmedetomidine
- Neurological diseases
- Patients receiving chronic sedative therapy 

**Clinical Trial Registration**: The trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03456879).
