**Failed Clinical Trial of Galantamine: IMPACT Study on Galantamine's Effectiveness in Managing Pain in Patients with Fibromyalgia**

**Title:** An Open-Label Trial of Galantamine for the Management of Pain and Functional Impairments in Patients with Fibromyalgia

**Study Design:** This phase IV, open-label, interventional clinical trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of galantamine in adults diagnosed with fibromyalgia. Participants received galantamine to determine its impact on pain perception, functionality, and associated psychological distress. 

**Objectives:** The primary objective was to assess changes in pain severity as measured by the Brief Pain Inventory (BPI) after 12 weeks of treatment with galantamine. Secondary objectives included evaluating improvements in quality of life using the Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire (FIQ) and examining changes in anxiety and depression through the Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale (GAD-7) and the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) respectively.

**Study Population:** A total of 100 patients aged 18-65 years with a physician-confirmed diagnosis of fibromyalgia, based on the American College of Rheumatology criteria, were enrolled. Eligible patients had a mean pain score of ≥4 on the BPI scale over the past week, indicating moderate to severe pain.

**Treatment Protocol:** Participants commenced treatment with galantamine at a starting dose of 4 mg once daily, with a scheduled increase to 8 mg/day after two weeks, and a possible further increase to 12 mg/day depending on tolerability and clinical benefit by week 6. The maximum intervention period was set at 12 weeks, followed by a follow-up phase of 4 weeks post-treatment.

**Outcome Measures:** The primary outcome was the mean change in pain severity measured by the BPI from baseline to week 12. Secondary outcomes assessed overall functioning and quality of life, with changes measured through the FIQ, alongside mental health metrics examined via GAD-7 and PHQ-9 at the same time points.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults 18-65 years of age.
- Diagnosis of fibromyalgia per ACR criteria.
- Baseline BPI score of ≥4.
- Ability to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of significant psychiatric disorders (e.g., severe depression or bipolar disorder).
- Ongoing treatment with opioids or other strong analgesics, which could confound treatment results.
- History of hypersensitivity to galantamine or other cholinesterase inhibitors.
- Clinically significant cardiovascular or gastrointestinal disorders.
- Pregnancy or lactation.

**Results:** The trial commenced in March 2021 and terminated prematurely in June 2022 due to failure to meet primary endpoint goals. While some participants noted subjective improvement in cognitive aspects and daily functionality during informal assessments, the primary analysis of BPI scores demonstrated no significant reductions in pain severity from baseline to endpoint (p=0.87), failing to reach statistical significance after 12 weeks. Furthermore, secondary outcomes regarding quality of life and psychological distress did not show any substantial advancements, leading to the conclusion of insufficient efficacy.

**Safety Monitoring:** Throughout the trial duration, participant safety was continuously monitored, revealing a higher incidence of adverse events, including gastrointestinal distress and bradycardia, particularly at higher dosages of galantamine. This raised concerns about the tolerability of the medication in this patient population.

**Conclusion:** The IMPACT Study aimed to investigate the non-opioid role of galantamine in managing fibromyalgia-related pain but ultimately failed to demonstrate sufficient effectiveness compared to baseline assessments. Consequently, the results indicate that galantamine may not be a viable treatment option for pain management in fibromyalgia and advise careful appraisal for future studies pursuing similar intervention pathways. Potential modifications in trial designs, including stratified analyses based on baseline characteristics or dose adjustments, are suggested for subsequent evaluations of galantamine in diverse patient cohorts.
