<clinical_trial>
    <title>Evaluating the Efficacy of Ferric Carboxymaltose in Patients with Functional Iron Deficiency and Heart Failure</title>
    <objective>
        A Multi-center, Randomized Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Intravenous Ferric Carboxymaltose for the Correction of Functional Iron Deficiency in Patients with Heart Failure and Comorbid Anemia.
    </objective>
    <background>
        Heart failure (HF) is often complicated by anemia, which adversely affects exercise tolerance, quality of life, and overall clinical outcomes. The presence of anemia in HF patients is frequently associated with functional iron deficiency (FID). Traditional treatment approaches involving red blood cell transfusions and erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs) have been subject to scrutiny due to potential negative impacts on mortality and morbidity. Given the positive outcomes related to intravenous iron administration, this study aimed to investigate the use of ferric carboxymaltose (FCM) in ameliorating anemia in this vulnerable population.
    </background>
    <design>
        A multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized trial involving adults aged 18 to 85 years with diagnosed HF and concurrent functional iron deficiency anemia. Patients will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either FCM (1000 mg IV) or placebo on day one, followed by standard heart failure medications and symptomatic management. Assessment will be performed at baseline, 4 weeks, and at 8 weeks post-intervention.
    </design>
    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Male or female subjects aged ≥18 years.</criterion>
        <criterion>Diagnosed with symptomatic heart failure (NYHA Class II-IV).</criterion>
        <criterion>Signed informed consent obtained.</criterion>
        <criterion>Hemoglobin levels <12 g/dL for women and <13 g/dL for men at screening.</criterion>
        <criterion>Transferrin saturation ≤20% and serum ferritin <100 ng/mL.</criterion>
        <criterion>Stable medication regimen for HF for at least 4 weeks prior to enrollment.</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Recent blood transfusion (within 60 days prior to screening).</criterion>
        <criterion>Hypersensitivity to iron formulations.</criterion>
        <criterion>Active or significant bleeding disorders.</criterion>
        <criterion>Malignancies or ongoing infections.</criterion>
        <criterion>Severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min).</criterion>
        <criterion>Documented history of chronic inflammatory disease.</criterion>
        <criterion>Pregnant or nursing women, or non-compliance with contraception requirements.</criterion>
        <criterion>Participation in other clinical trials involving investigational products within 30 days of screening.</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <outcome_measures>
        <primary_outcome>Change in hemoglobin levels from baseline to 8 weeks post-intervention.</primary_outcome>
        <secondary_outcome>Incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events.</secondary_outcome>
        <secondary_outcome>Change in quality of life assessed by the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ).</secondary_outcome>
    </outcome_measures>
    <statistical_analysis>
        A sample size of 300 participants will provide adequate power to detect differences in hemoglobin levels with a two-tailed significance level set at 0.05. Analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) will compare the hemoglobin changes between groups, controlling for baseline values. Safety analyses will be conducted on the intention-to-treat population.
    </statistical_analysis>
</clinical_trial>
