**Failed clinical trial of natalizumab: Efficacy and Safety of Natalizumab in Preventing Progression of Neurodegeneration in Patients with Alzheimer's Disease**

A Phase II, Multicenter, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Assess the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of Intravenous Natalizumab (BG00002) in Patients with Mild to Moderate Alzheimer's Disease

The primary objective of this study is to evaluate whether intravenous administration of natalizumab can effectively slow cognitive decline as measured through standard neuropsychological assessments over a 12-month period in patients diagnosed with mild to moderate Alzheimer’s disease. The secondary objectives include the assessment of natalizumab’s safety profile, its effects on daily living activities and quality of life as reported by caregivers, and the exploration of the biomarker response related to tau and amyloid pathology.

**Key Inclusion Criteria:**
- Clinical diagnosis of mild to moderate Alzheimer’s disease based on the DSM-5 and confirmed by clinical and neuroimaging measures.
- Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score of 10 to 24 points inclusive at Screening.
- Age between 50 and 85 years, with both male and female subjects eligible for participation.
- Must have a caregiver or study partner who is knowledgeable about the patient’s clinical status and is able to provide reliable information.
- Participants must be on a stable dose of acetylcholinesterase inhibitors or memantine for at least 3 months prior to randomization.

**Key Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of hypersensitivity to natalizumab or any component of the formulation.
- Current diagnosis of significant psychiatric disorder that may interfere with the interpretation of the study results.
- History of seizures or comorbid neurological diseases such as Multiple Sclerosis, Parkinson's disease, or other forms of dementia.
- Active or recent history (within 1 year) of inflammatory or autoimmune diseases requiring systemic therapy.
- Presence of any contraindications for MRI scanning.
  
**Primary Endpoint:**
Change in cognitive function from baseline to Month 12 as assessed by the Alzheimer’s Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive Subscale (ADAS-Cog).

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Change in functionality as measured by the Activities of Daily Living (ADL) scale.
- Change in quality of life scores as reported by caregivers using the Quality of Life in Alzheimer’s Disease scale (QoL-AD).
- Safety assessment based on the incidence of adverse events and serious adverse events throughout the study duration.

**Outcome Measures:**
The efficacy of natalizumab will be primarily determined by the difference in ADAS-Cog scores between participants receiving natalizumab and those receiving placebo. Safety will be assessed by monitoring adverse events, laboratory abnormalities, and physical examination findings.

**Notes:**
Recent analyses indicate an unanticipated prevalence of adverse events such as hypersensitivity reactions and mild infusion-related reactions among participants receiving natalizumab. The anticipated mechanistic hypothesis, which suggested a decrease in neuroinflammation through the inhibition of lymphocyte migration into the central nervous system, did not yield clinically meaningful improvements, resulting in the overarching conclusion of ineffectiveness in neuroprotection compared to placebo.
