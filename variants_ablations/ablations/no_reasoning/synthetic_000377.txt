**Failed Clinical Trial of Cytarabine: Evaluation of Cytarabine in Combination with Nivolumab for Treatment of Adults with Relapsed Acute Myeloid Leukemia**  
**A Randomized Phase II Study of Cytarabine with Nivolumab Versus Cytarabine Alone in Patients with Relapsed Acute Myeloid Leukemia** 

RATIONALE: In the treatment of relapsed acute myeloid leukemia (AML), cytarabine, a key chemotherapeutic agent, is often employed for its capacity to inhibit DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing cells. Nivolumab, a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitor, is hypothesized to further enhance antitumor immunity by blocking inhibitory signals that dampen T-cell activation and proliferation. Previous studies have indicated potential synergistic effects when combining traditional cytotoxic agents with immunotherapies. However, the efficacy and safety profiles of cytarabine in conjunction with nivolumab remain under investigation. 

PURPOSE: This phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of cytarabine when administered in combination with nivolumab compared to cytarabine monotherapy in adults diagnosed with relapsed AML. 

OBJECTIVES:
- PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:
  I. To compare the rate of complete remission (CR) plus complete remission with incomplete recovery (CRi) achieved with cytarabine in combination with nivolumab versus cytarabine alone in adults (ages 18-75) with relapsed AML.
  
- SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:
  I. To assess and compare the toxicity profiles of cytarabine plus nivolumab versus cytarabine alone.
  II. To evaluate overall survival and disease-free survival rates in both treatment arms.
  III. To analyze immune response correlates, including changes in T-cell populations and cytokine profiles following treatment.

OUTLINE: Patients are randomized into one of two treatment arms. 

- ARM A: Patients receive cytarabine intravenously (IV) continuously over 72 hours on days 1-3 and nivolumab IV on day 4 of a 28-day cycle.

- ARM B: Patients receive cytarabine as specified in Arm A.

Patients will be followed for response assessment and safety monitoring at predetermined intervals.

INCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Adults with pathologically confirmed diagnosis of relapsed AML.
- Patients must have received at least one prior line of therapy for AML.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0-2.
- Adequate hematological function (absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,000/μL, platelet count ≥ 50,000/μL).
- End-organ function: serum creatinine ≤ 2.0 mg/dL, AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN, total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL.
- Female patients of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and agree to use effective contraception.

EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Documented central nervous system involvement or active infection.
- Known hypersensitivity to nivolumab or cytarabine.
- Participation in another investigational drug study within 30 days prior to enrollment.
- Active autoimmune disease that necessitates systemic treatment.
- Prior treatment with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors.

RESPONSE RATE:  
The CR/CRi rate will be assessed at the end of the study per standardized response criteria for AML, with CR defined as bone marrow showing less than 5% myeloblasts, recovery of peripheral blood counts, and absence of leukemic cells in peripheral blood and bone marrow.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION: Preliminary data indicated a concerning trend in both efficacy and safety in subjects treated with the combination regimen, leading to early termination of the trial. CR/CRi rates were significantly lower than expected, with unexpected toxicities including increased rates of immune-mediated adverse events. Additionally, the overall survival rates showed no substantial improvement when compared to historical controls receiving cytarabine alone. These results underscore the complexity of combining immune checkpoint inhibitors with conventional chemotherapeutics in this patient population and highlight the need for further investigation into optimal synergistic strategies. 

This study illustrates the challenges faced in developing effective treatments in relapsed AML and serves as a cautionary tale for future clinical endeavors involving cytarabine and novel immunotherapeutic agents.
