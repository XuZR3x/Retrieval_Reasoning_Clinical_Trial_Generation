**Failed Clinical Trial of Enzastaurin: Evaluation of Enzastaurin in Combination with Anti-PD-1 Therapy for Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

A Randomized, Phase 2, Double-Blind Study of Enzastaurin Plus Pembrolizumab Versus Pembrolizumab Plus Placebo in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC).

The primary objective of this clinical trial is to evaluate the efficacy of the addition of enzastaurin to pembrolizumab compared to pembrolizumab in combination with a placebo in improving overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with advanced NSCLC who have not received prior systemic therapy. While pembrolizumab has demonstrated significant efficacy in NSCLC treatment, a novel agent like enzastaurin, which modulates the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, is hypothesized to enhance the anti-tumor response when combined with immunotherapy.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Adults aged 18 years and older with histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced or metastatic NSCLC
- Presence of PD-L1 expression, corresponding to ≥1% by immunohistochemistry
- No prior systemic therapy for advanced or metastatic NSCLC
- Adequate organ function as defined by prespecified laboratory criteria
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0, 1, or 2
- Written informed consent obtained.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Previous treatment with any anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 therapy
- Concurrent use of any investigational agent or anticancer therapy
- Known central nervous system metastasis requiring treatment
- Major cardiovascular disorder or significant comorbidity that could interfere with treatment
- Previous malignancy (except for non-melanoma skin cancer or in situ cancers) diagnosed within the last 5 years
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding, or failure to use adequate contraceptive methods.

**Primary Endpoint: Overall Survival (OS)**
OS was defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause. The analysis was performed using Kaplan-Meier methods, and comparisons between treatment arms were conducted using the log-rank test to evaluate differences in survival distributions.

**Secondary Endpoint: Progression-Free Survival (PFS)**
PFS was assessed as the time from randomization to the first documentation of disease progression (PD) or death from any cause. PD was determined according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST v1.1) guidelines, characterized by an increase of at least 20% in the sum of the longest diameters of target lesions, or the emergence of new lesions, or unequivocal progression of existing non-target lesions.

**Outcomes of the Study**
Despite an initial hypothesis that the addition of enzastaurin would augment the clinical efficacy of pembrolizumab in reducing tumor burden and prolonging survival, the trial did not meet its primary endpoint. The combination treatment failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in OS and PFS when compared to the pembrolizumab plus placebo cohort. Adverse events noted were consistent with those expected from pembrolizumab monotherapy, however, the addition of enzastaurin did not enhance therapeutic benefits as anticipated.

This failure highlights the importance of further investigation into the mechanisms of resistance against immunotherapies when combined with targeted agents like enzastaurin, as understanding such interactions could inform more effective treatment strategies for patients with advanced NSCLC.
