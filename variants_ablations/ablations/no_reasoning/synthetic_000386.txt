<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Evaluation of Bupivacaine Efficacy in Managing Postoperative Pain After Total Knee Arthroplasty: A Randomized Controlled Trial</Title>
    <Abstract>
        The aim of this clinical trial is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of bupivacaine administered through a single-shot lumbar plexus block compared to placebo in patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty (TKA). Despite the established role of regional anesthesia in providing postoperative analgesia, the efficacy of bupivacaine specifically in the context of TKA requires further investigation, given the variability in pain control and potential risk of neurologic complications.
    </Abstract>
    <Background>
        Total knee arthroplasty is associated with significant postoperative pain, necessitating effective analgesic regimens to enhance recovery and minimize opioid consumption. Although bupivacaine has been widely utilized for regional blockade, evidence pertaining to its safety profile and the incidence of transient neurological symptoms remains inconclusive. This study seeks to address this gap.
    </Background>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        - Adults aged 18 to 80 years scheduled for primary unilateral TKA.
        - Patients consenting to receive a lumbar plexus block as part of their anesthesia and analgesia plan.
        - ASA classification of 1-3.
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        - Patients with a history of neurological disorders or previous lower limb surgeries.
        - Coagulation disorders or anticoagulant therapy.
        - Pregnancy or planned pregnancy in the near term.
        - Infections at the injection site.
        - Patients with language barriers that preclude informed consent.
        - Patient refusal to participate in the study.
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <Methods>
        This prospective, randomized, double-blind controlled trial will enroll 120 patients. Following the inclusion and exclusion criteria screening, participants will be randomized into two groups: Group A will receive a single-shot lumbar plexus block with bupivacaine 0.5% (20 mL), while Group B will receive saline as a placebo. The primary endpoint will be the total opioid consumption (in mg of morphine equivalents) during the first 48 hours post-surgery. Secondary endpoints will include numerical rating scale (NRS) scores for pain at rest and during movement at specified time intervals, as well as the incidence of transient neurological symptoms.
    </Methods>
    <Endpoints>
        <PrimaryEndpoint>
            Total postoperative opioid consumption (mg morphine equivalents) within the first 48 hours.
        </PrimaryEndpoint>
        <SecondaryEndpoints>
            - Incidence of transient neurological symptoms within the first 30 days postoperatively.
            - NRS pain scores at 24, 48, and 72 hours postoperatively.
        </SecondaryEndpoints>
    </Endpoints>
    <Results>
        The trial was terminated prematurely before reaching the target enrollment due to an unexpectedly high incidence of transient neurological symptoms reported among participants receiving bupivacaine. These symptoms included lower extremity weakness and paresthesias persisting beyond the expected duration of the anesthetic effect. No significant differences were observed in opioid consumption between the two groups, as both were statistically similar.
    </Results>
    <Conclusions>
        The findings suggest that while bupivacaine is commonly employed in perioperative pain management, its use as a single-shot lumbar plexus block in total knee arthroplasty is associated with an increased risk of postoperative neurologic complications. Consequently, this trial did not support the routine use of bupivacaine for this application, pointing to the necessity of exploring alternative strategies to maximize analgesia with a favorable safety profile.
    </Conclusions>
</ClinicalTrial>
