**Successful Clinical Trial of Bosentan: Efficacy of Bosentan in Patients With Early-Stage Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (EPAT)**

**A Phase 3, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Multicenter Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Bosentan in Patients With Early-Stage Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension**

This Phase 3 clinical trial aims to assess the efficacy and safety of bosentan in patients diagnosed with early-stage pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), specifically those classified under World Health Organization (WHO) Pulmonary Hypertension Classification Group 1, Stage I. The primary objective of the trial is to evaluate the improvement in exercise capacity, quantified by the change in 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) after 24 weeks of intervention.

**Design Overview:**
The study employs a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design to ensure robust results regarding the efficacy of bosentan compared to placebo. Patients will be randomly assigned to receive either bosentan (administered at a dose of 62.5 mg twice daily) or a matching placebo, with continuation of treatment for a total of 24 weeks. The study will also explore secondary endpoints, including quality of life assessments through validated questionnaires and safety evaluations including adverse events monitoring.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Signed informed consent obtained from the patient or their legal representative prior to enrollment.
2. Adult males and females aged 18 years and older; females of childbearing potential must either be surgically sterilized or utilize an effective method of contraception. 
3. Patients diagnosed with early-stage PAH (WHO PAH Classification Group I, Stage I) exhibiting:
   - Mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) ≥ 20 mm Hg at rest.
   - Pulmonary vascular resistance ≥ 2 Wood units.
   - 6MWD of ≥ 80 meters and < 300 meters at baseline. 
4. Patients naive to treatment with advanced PAH therapies (i.e., ERAs, PDE-5 inhibitors, or prostacyclins).

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Pregnant or nursing women.
2. Patients with systemic diseases known to potentially affect pulmonary pressure, including but not limited to significant valvular heart disease or chronic pulmonary disease.
3. Patients with significant liver impairment (Child-Pugh Class B or C) or liver enzymes exceeding three times the upper limit of normal.
4. History of hypersensitivity to bosentan or any components of the formulation.
5. Participation in another investigational study within 30 days prior to the screening visit.
6. Patients with a history of significant cardiovascular events such as myocardial infarction or stroke within the preceding 6 months.

**Outcome Measures:**
The primary outcome measure will be the change in 6MWD from baseline to week 24, with analyses considering a clinically significant improvement defined as an increase of ≥ 30 meters. Secondary measures will include:
- Change in quality of life as measured by the EQ-5D scale.
- Assessment of safety through the frequency and severity of treatment-emergent adverse events.
- Evaluation of left ventricular function via echocardiography at baseline and follow-up.

Outcome measures will be statistically analyzed using intention-to-treat principles, with significance set at p<0.05 considered statistically significant.

**Number of Patients Required:**
It is estimated that 170 patients will be required to provide adequate power to detect a significant difference in the primary endpoint, with an expected drop-out rate of 10%.

This study aims to further elucidate the therapeutic benefits of bosentan in early-stage pulmonary arterial hypertension by demonstrating significant improvements in functional capacity and overall patient quality of life. 

By following a structured and methodologically rigorous approach, the EPAT trial anticipates contributing valuable insights into the effective management of PAH and supporting the viability of bosentan as a first-line therapeutic option in patients with early-stage PAH.
