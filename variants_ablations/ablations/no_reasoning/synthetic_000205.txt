**Failed Clinical Trial of Dovitinib: A Phase II Study of Dovitinib in Combination with Pembrolizumab for the Treatment of Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) After PD-1 Inhibitor Failure**

**Abstract:**
This Phase II, open-label, multicenter study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Dovitinib, an oral multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor, in combination with Pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody, in patients with advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer who have experienced disease progression after prior treatment with PD-1 inhibitors. The study hypothesized that Dovitinib would provide a synergistic anti-tumor effect when combined with Pembrolizumab, enhancing overall survival and progression-free survival compared to historical controls.

**Study Design:**
The trial was designed as a single-arm study involving patients with advanced, histologically confirmed NSCLC who had demonstrated disease progression following PD-1 inhibitor therapy. The primary endpoint was overall response rate (ORR), with secondary endpoints including progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety profile assessed via Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults (≥ 18 years) diagnosed with advanced NSCLC with confirmed progression post-PD-1 inhibitor therapy.
- Measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- ECOG performance status 0-2.
- Adequate organ and marrow function, defined as:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm3
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm3
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 9.0 g/dL
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 x ULN
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN
  - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN (5.0 x ULN for those with hepatic metastases).

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of hypersensitivity to dovitinib or pembrolizumab.
- Prior treatment with other tyrosine kinase inhibitors, immunotherapy, or chemotherapy within 4 weeks of starting study drug.
- Presence of brain metastases or uncontrolled interstitial lung disease.
- Concurrent severe uncontrolled medical conditions that may jeopardize participation.
- Clinically significant cardiovascular disease (e.g., New York Heart Association Class III or IV).

**Treatment Plan:**
Patients received Dovitinib at a dose of 500 mg orally once daily in combination with Pembrolizumab at 200 mg intravenously every three weeks. Treatment continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent.

**Results:**
Despite an initial optimistic outlook, the trial was ultimately unsuccessful. The anticipated synergistic effect of Dovitinib and Pembrolizumab did not manifest, yielding an ORR of 6%, significantly lower than the expected threshold of 20% for investigational agents in this population. Additionally, PFS and OS data were disappointing, with a median PFS of only 2.4 months and a median OS of 6.3 months. The safety profile indicated a greater incidence of grade 3 and above adverse events compared to historical data, particularly hypertension, elevated liver enzymes, and immune-related adverse events.

**Conclusion:**
This study of Dovitinib in combination with Pembrolizumab did not meet its primary endpoint of ORR and faced significant challenges in terms of patient safety and tolerability. It highlights the complexities of treating advanced NSCLC, particularly in a post-PD-1 inhibitor setting. Future investigations should consider alternative combinations or sequential therapies that might yield improved outcomes in this difficult-to-treat population. This trial exemplifies the unpredictability of targeted therapies and the necessity for rigorous safety and efficacy assessments in early-phase clinical trials.
