Failed clinical trial of doxorubicin: Doxorubicin versus Gemcitabine and Doxorubicin in Patients with Advanced Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma

Phase III Randomized, Open-Label, Multicenter Clinical Trial of Doxorubicin vs. Gemcitabine and Doxorubicin Combination Therapy in Chemotherapy-Naive Patients with Advanced Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma

RATIONALE: Pancreatic adenocarcinoma presents significant therapeutic challenges, with a notable proclivity for metastatic spread and a notoriously poor prognosis. The hypothesized combination of gemcitabine and doxorubicin posits a mechanistic synergy, whereby the unique pharmacodynamics of gemcitabine, an antimetabolite that inhibits DNA synthesis, could enhance the cytotoxic effects of doxorubicin, an anthracycline that intercalates DNA and disrupts replication. This study investigates if the combination therapy yields improved overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) outcomes compared to doxorubicin alone in patients with advanced disease states.

OBJECTIVES:
Primary:
- To ascertain the overall survival of patients receiving gemcitabine and doxorubicin compared to those on doxorubicin monotherapy.

Secondary:
- To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) rates in both treatment arms.
- To assess the incidence of adverse events and overall tolerability of the therapeutic regimens.
- To examine quality of life metrics and symptom burden pre- and post-treatment via validated scales.

OUTLINE: This investigation is designed as a randomized clinical trial enrolling chemotherapy-naïve patients presenting with advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma.

Study Arms:
- Arm A: Patients receive doxorubicin (60 mg/m² IV every 21 days).
- Arm B: Patients receive gemcitabine (1,000 mg/m² IV weekly for the first three weeks, followed by doxorubicin as above).

In both arms, treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or the decision by the attending physician to discontinue for any reason. 

Inclusion Criteria:
- Adults aged 18 to 75 years.
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma (unresectable stage III or metastatic).
- No prior chemotherapy or radiation treatment for pancreatic cancer.
- Measurable disease per RECIST criteria.
- ECOG performance status of 0 to 2.
- Adequate hematological parameters: hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL, absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/µL, platelet count ≥ 100,000/µL.
- Liver function tests within normal limits, with ALT and AST ≤ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Concurrent malignancies (except for non-melanoma skin cancers).
- History of severe cardiac disease—defined as a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) < 50% via echocardiogram.
- Significant renal impairment characterized by creatinine clearance < 50 mL/min.
- Active infection requiring antibiotic treatment.
- Pregnant or lactating women.

OUTCOME MEASURES:
Overall survival will be defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause. Progression-free survival is defined as the time from randomization until disease progression or death. The incidence of adverse events will be monitored continuously throughout the study duration, with specific attention to cardiotoxicity and hematological toxicities.

The hypothesis will test whether the combination of gemcitabine and doxorubicin can significantly prolong survival in this patient population, based on previous studies showing marginal improvements in OS with dual-agent regimens compared to single-agent therapies. 

Despite the theoretical underpinning of synergistic effects, results indicated no significant improvement in either overall survival (median OS 6.5 months for Arm A vs. 6.2 months for Arm B) or progression-free survival (median PFS 4.2 months for Arm A vs. 3.8 months for Arm B). The observed adverse event profile was consistent with previous literature on doxorubicin, which led to trial discontinuation based on the lack of clinical benefit relative to added toxicity. 

CONCLUSION: The investigation concludes that the addition of gemcitabine to doxorubicin in the treatment of advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma does not enhance survival outcomes and may pose increased risks of adverse effects in this patient cohort, necessitating a reevaluation of treatment paradigms in this therapeutic area.
