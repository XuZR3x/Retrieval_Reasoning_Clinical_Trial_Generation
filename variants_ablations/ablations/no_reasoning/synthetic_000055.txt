**Success Clinical Trial of Avastin: Avastin Combined with Dolastatin and Dexamethasone as a Treatment for Advanced Alveolar Soft Part Sarcoma**

**A Phase II, Open-Label, Multicenter Study of Avastin in Combination with Dolastatin and Dexamethasone for Patients with Advanced Alveolar Soft Part Sarcoma**

The objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the combination of avastin, dolastatin, and dexamethasone in patients diagnosed with advanced alveolar soft part sarcoma (ASPS), a rare malignancy characterized by slow growth but aggressive behavior and metastatic potential. Previous research has highlighted the role of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) in ASPS and highlighted that avastin, as an anti-VEGF therapy, can mitigate tumor progression by inhibiting angiogenesis, thereby improving patient outcomes.

Dolastatin, a potent microtubule inhibitor derived from the sea hare, has demonstrated antitumor activity by disrupting mitotic spindle formation and has previously shown promise in soft tissue sarcomas. Dexamethasone is utilized in this regimen for its potential to reduce inflammation and its possible synergistic effects in combination with chemotherapy to enhance therapeutic efficacy. Given the unique characteristics of ASPS and the challenges posed by its treatment, this study aims to provide insight into a novel therapeutic approach.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced alveolar soft part sarcoma (third line or higher);
- Age: 18-75 years;
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2;
- Measurable disease according to RECIST criteria;
- Adequate hematological function defined by: WBC count ≥ 4 × 10^9/L, Hemoglobin ≥ 100 g/L, platelet count ≥ 100 × 10^9/L;
- Adequate renal function (serum creatinine < 1.5 × ULN);
- Adequate liver function: ALT/AST ≤ 2.5 × ULN, total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 × ULN;
- No history of other malignancies within the past five years;
- Voluntary participation and signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Clinically significant cardiovascular disease, defined as myocardial infarction or cerebrovascular accident within the last six months;
- Active bleeding disorders or significant coagulopathy;
- Previous treatment with dolastatin or avastin within the last 30 days;
- Concurrent malignancies or malignancies requiring extensive surgical intervention;
- Uncontrolled hypertension (systolic BP ≥ 160 mmHg or diastolic BP ≥ 100 mmHg);
- Known hypersensitivity to dolastatin or avastin;
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding, and fertile women not using effective contraception.

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint of the study is the overall response rate (ORR), defined as the percentage of patients demonstrating a complete or partial response after 4 cycles of treatment. Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety profiles determined by the incidence of adverse events graded by the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI CTCAE v5.0).

**Statistical Analysis:**
Progression-free and overall survival will be analyzed using Kaplan-Meier methodologies. A sample size of 60 patients is targeted, with a planned interim analysis at the one-third mark to evaluate safety and efficacy.

**Treatment Protocol:**
Patients will receive the combination treatment of avastin (15 mg/kg intravenously every three weeks), dolastatin (30 mg/m² on days 1 and 15 of each 28-day cycle), and dexamethasone (4 mg orally daily for the first 14 days of each cycle). Response evaluations will be conducted every two cycles utilizing imaging techniques such as CT or MRI.

This study represents an innovative approach in the treatment of advanced ASPS, potentially setting a new standard for future therapeutic strategies leveraging avastin's mechanism alongside targeted chemotherapeutics.
