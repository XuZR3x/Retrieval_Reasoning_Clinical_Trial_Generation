**Failed Clinical Trial of Paclitaxel: Study of TGR-1202 in Combination with Paclitaxel for Patients with Relapsed/Refractory Aggressive B-Cell Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma**

**Phase 3 Trial, Randomized, Open-Label, of TGR-1202 in Combination with Paclitaxel Versus Paclitaxel Alone in Patients with Relapsed/Refractory Aggressive B-Cell Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma** 

**RATIONALE:** Aggressive B-cell Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) represents a significant clinical challenge, characterized by rapid disease progression and high mortality rates. The prevailing treatment protocols often utilize chemotherapy regimens that include paclitaxel; however, the need for improvements in overall survival and reduced treatment-related morbidity remains paramount. TGR-1202, a selective inhibitor of PI3K delta, has demonstrated potential in preliminary studies for enhancing anti-tumor efficacy when combined with standard chemotherapeutics. Thus, this trial aims to evaluate the effectiveness of TGR-1202 in combination with paclitaxel versus paclitaxel alone in terms of overall survival in this patient cohort.

**PURPOSE:** The purpose of this clinical trial is to establish whether the addition of TGR-1202 to paclitaxel significantly improves overall survival time compared to paclitaxel monotherapy in patients with relapsed or refractory aggressive B-cell NHL. Preliminary data suggest potential synergistic effects between TGR-1202 and paclitaxel; however, clinical validation is necessary through a large-scale trial.

**OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the effect of the combination of TGR-1202 and paclitaxel on overall survival compared to paclitaxel alone in patients with relapsed or refractory aggressive B-cell NHL. 
II. To evaluate secondary endpoints including progression-free survival, objective response rate, and treatment-related adverse events.

**OUTLINE:** This is a phase 3, multicenter, randomized, open-label trial with two treatment arms. 

- **Arm I:** Patients receive TGR-1202 (300 mg orally daily) in combination with paclitaxel (175 mg/m² IV on day 1 of a 21-day cycle) for a total of 6 cycles unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.

- **Arm II:** Patients receive paclitaxel alone (175 mg/m² IV on day 1 of a 21-day cycle) for a total of 6 cycles unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.

After completion of the treatment, patients will be followed for outcomes every 3 months for the first 2 years, subsequently transitioning to follow-up every 6 months for a further 2 years, and then annually thereafter.

**PRIMARY ENDPOINT:** The primary endpoint of this study is to determine the overall survival at 2 years post-randomization.

**SECONDARY ENDPOINTS:** Secondary endpoints will include progression-free survival, objective response rate, duration of response, and treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) as assessed by the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of relapsed or refractory aggressive B-cell NHL.
- Patients must have received at least one prior line of therapy including at least one chemotherapy regimen.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function as defined by specific laboratory criteria.
- Age ≥ 18 years, capable of providing informed consent.
  
**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior treatment with TGR-1202 or any other PI3K inhibitors.
- Patients with CNS involvement or other significant co-morbidities prohibiting chemotherapy.
- Pregnant or nursing women.
- Active infection not controlled with antibiotics or other conditions imparting risk for severe toxicity.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** It is anticipated that a total of 600 patients (300 per arm) will be accrued for this study over 4 years.

Overall survival within the scope of this trial is anticipated to be closely monitored, with a follow-up period of approximately 3 years post-randomization for the assessment of efficacy and safety.

**TRIAL STATUS:** As of the last update, this trial has been prematurely halted due to an inability to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in overall survival when comparing the intervention with paclitaxel alone, as well as increased rates of adverse events exceeding threshold levels in the experimental arm. This result prompts further investigation into the use of TGR-1202 in different therapeutic settings or in a stratified patient population.
