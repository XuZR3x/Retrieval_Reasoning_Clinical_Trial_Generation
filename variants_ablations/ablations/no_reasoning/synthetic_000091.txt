**Title:** A Multicenter, Phase II Study of Tivozanib in Patients With Advanced Hepatocellular Carcinoma

**Abstract:**
This Phase II clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of tivozanib in patients diagnosed with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who have previously received standard systemic therapies. Hepatocellular carcinoma is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, and novel therapeutic options are urgently needed. Tivozanib, a potent and selective vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR) inhibitor, has shown promising antitumor activity in various malignancies. The mechanism of action mediates tumor growth inhibition through anti-angiogenic pathways. Our study focuses on assessing the clinical benefit of tivozanib in a targeted patient population with advanced HCC.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To evaluate the overall response rate (defined as the proportion of patients exhibiting either a complete response [CR] or partial response [PR] assessed at 24 weeks following treatment with tivozanib).

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months.  
II. To determine overall survival (up to 2 years post-treatment).  
III. To characterize the safety profile and tolerability of tivozanib treatment in this cohort.  
IV. To analyze biomarkers correlating clinical outcomes with progression of disease using circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) and liver function tests.

**OUTLINE:**
Eligible patients will receive tivozanib hydrochloride orally (PO) once daily on a 3-week-on, 1-week-off schedule. Each treatment cycle will last 28 days, and treatment will continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Patients will undergo imaging studies (CT or MRI) at baseline, and then every 8 weeks during treatment to monitor response, followed by a follow-up period for up to 24 months post-treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed hepatocellular carcinoma.
- Patients must have advanced HCC with a Child-Pugh score of A or B.
- Patients must have measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Patients must have experienced disease progression on or after treatment with standard systemic therapies (e.g., sorafenib, regorafenib, or atezolizumab).
- Patients must provide written informed consent and have the ability to comply with study protocols including follow-up visits.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of allergic reactions to compounds similar to tivozanib.
- Concurrent participation in another clinical trial or receipt of other investigational products within 30 days prior to registration.
- Presence of significant cardiovascular conditions such as symptomatic congestive heart failure, myocardial infarction, or uncontrolled hypertension.
- Evidence of active CNS metastasis or other significant organ dysfunction.
- Patients with active infection requiring systemic antibiotics or documented autoimmune disease requiring treatment.

**Primary Endpoint:**
Overall response rate (ORR) will be assessed by imaging studies at 24 weeks, defined based on RECIST 1.1 criteria. CR denotes complete resolution of all target lesions, while PR denotes at least a 30% reduction in the sum of the diameters of target lesions from baseline.

**Statistical Analysis:**
The ORR and PFS will be summarized using descriptive statistics. The corresponding 95% confidence intervals will also be calculated. Safety analyses will be conducted according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) Version 5.0 to categorize and report adverse events throughout treatment.

**Expected Outcome:**
We hypothesize that tivozanib will demonstrate a significant improvement in overall response rates and PFS compared to historical controls, establishing its potential as a novel treatment option for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma. This study aims to provide critical insights into the therapeutic role of tivozanib in a challenging cohort of patients with limited treatment options. 

**Conclusion:**
This multicenter Phase II study aims to expand the possibilities for patients with advanced HCC by investigating the efficacy and safety of tivozanib, moving towards innovative therapies that can significantly impact patient outcomes in this malignancy. The findings from this study may contribute to enhancing clinical practice for managing hepatocellular carcinoma and provide a framework for future combination studies with immune checkpoint inhibitors.
