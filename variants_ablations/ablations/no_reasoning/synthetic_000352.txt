**Successful Clinical Trial of Vorinostat: Vorinostat in Combination With Radiotherapy for Patients With High-Grade Gliomas**

**Phase I Trial of Vorinostat (Suberoylanilide Hydroxamic Acid; SAHA) in Combination With Radiotherapy in Patients With High-Grade Gliomas**

**RATIONALE**: Vorinostat may enhance the efficacy of radiotherapy by inducing apoptosis in cancer cells through the modulation of key signaling pathways and promoting DNA repair inhibition in high-grade gliomas. The combination of vorinostat and radiotherapy may lead to improved tumor response and patient survival.

**PURPOSE**: This phase I trial investigates the safety, tolerability, and optimal dosing of vorinostat in combination with standard radiotherapy for patients diagnosed with high-grade gliomas, namely glioblastoma multiforme.

**OBJECTIVES**:
- **Primary**: To assess dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) and non-hematologic adverse effects of vorinostat when administered in conjunction with radiotherapy.
- **Secondary**: To evaluate preliminary antitumor activity based on progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS); to monitor changes in tumor markers post-treatment and assess quality of life (QoL) metrics through patient-reported outcome measures.

**OUTLINE**: This is a dose-escalation study of vorinostat. Patients will receive concurrent treatment starting on the first day of radiotherapy with vorinostat administered orally. The treatment regimen is as follows:

- Patients will receive vorinostat at escalating dose levels of 100mg, 200mg, and 300mg orally once daily (OD) starting from the initiation of radiotherapy (60Gy in 30 fractions).
- Radiotherapy will be administered concurrently over 6 weeks with 5 fractions per week.
- Treatment cycles will continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity arises, for up to 12 weeks. 

Clinical evaluations, including MRI scans, will be conducted every 8 weeks following the completion of radiotherapy to assess tumor response. Blood samples will be collected for pharmacokinetic analysis and to evaluate immune system parameters and inflammatory cytokine production, including IL-6 and TNFα. 

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of high-grade glioma, specifically glioblastoma multiforme.
- Patients must have undergone surgical resection or biopsy and exhibit residual or recurrent disease post-operatively.
- Patients with a Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) score of 70 or above.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Age ≥ 18 years and ≤ 75 years.
- First-line treatment setting; no prior systemic therapy for glioblastoma (exception: steroids).
- Adequate organ function, defined as:
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/μL
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/μL
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL

- Patients must be consenting (with appropriate decision-making capacity) and agree to use contraception during the study period.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY**:
- No prior treatment with HDAC inhibitors or other investigational agents for gliomas.
- No other concurrent malignancies or uncontrolled co-morbidities that could interfere with treatment response or assessment.

**SAFETY ASSESSMENT**:
Adverse events will be monitored according to National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

**PRIMARY OUTCOME MEASURES**:
Determination of maximum tolerated dose (MTD) in combination with standard radiotherapy, delineated as the highest dose at which 0 out of 3 or ≤ 1 out of 6 patients experience DLT.

**SECONDARY OUTCOME MEASURES**:
- Evaluation of overall survival and progression-free survival as assessed by clinical and radiological examinations.
- Assessment of patient-reported outcomes regarding quality of life during and after treatment.

**FOLLOW-UP**:
After completion of study treatment, patients will be monitored for at least 30 days for evaluation of adverse effects and clinical response, followed by routine follow-ups every 3 months for up to 2 years. 

This study anticipates contributing critical insights into the synergistic potential of vorinostat and radiotherapy in the management of high-grade gliomas and aims to enhance standard treatment protocols with novel therapeutic approaches.
