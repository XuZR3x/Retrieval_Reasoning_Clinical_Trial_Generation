**Failed Clinical Trial of Nilotinib**: A Phase II Study of Nilotinib in Combination With Low-Dose Chemotherapy in Patients With Philadelphia Chromosome-Positive Chronic Myeloid Leukemia in Chronic Phase Previously Treated With Imatinib or Dasatinib

**Study Overview**: This multicenter, single-arm study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of nilotinib in conjunction with low-dose chemotherapy in adult patients diagnosed with Philadelphia chromosome-positive (Ph+) chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) in chronic phase, specifically those who had previously received therapy with imatinib or dasatinib. The study investigated whether adding nilotinib to a regimen of low-dose cytarabine and anthracycline could enhance response rates while maintaining manageable toxicity levels, particularly in patients who demonstrated minimal response to standard care.

**Objectives**: 
- **Primary Objective**: To assess the overall response rate (ORR) as defined by Complete Cytogenetic Response (CCyR) at 6 months post-treatment initiation.
- **Secondary Objectives**: To evaluate the safety profile of the treatment, determine the duration of response (DOR), assess the time to progression (TTP), and evaluate patient-reported outcomes via validated quality of life questionnaires.

**Study Design**: 
This study planned to enroll approximately 150 patients over 12 months. Participants underwent treatment with nilotinib administered at a dose of 400 mg orally twice daily in conjunction with low-dose chemotherapy consisting of strict regulation doses of cytarabine and daunorubicin. Follow-ups were scheduled every four weeks after treatment initiation to monitor treatment efficacy and safety parameters.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Adult patients (≥18 years) with histologically confirmed Ph+ CML-CP.
2. Patients who demonstrated intolerance or suboptimal response to imatinib or dasatinib as assessed by the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (0, 1, or 2).
3. No existing treatment-related morbidities that could complicate the assessment of study drug effects.
4. Documented availability of BCR-ABL transcript levels to confirm eligibility.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
1. Previous treatment with nilotinib or any additional TKIs.
2. Presence of accelerated or blast phase CML.
3. Major cardiovascular disease or history of significant myocardial infarction.
4. Uncontrolled metabolic parameters, particularly electrolytes affecting cardiac function like potassium and magnesium.
5. Known significant interactions with nullifying CYP3A4 medications.
6. Inability to comply with study requirements due to psychosocial or other health issues.
7. Pregnant or breastfeeding women, or individuals of childbearing potential without adequate contraception.

**Intervention**: Patients received nilotinib orally at 400 mg BID, combined with a bespoke low-dose chemotherapy regimen administered intravenously (IV) on a cyclic basis. Treatment continued for 6 months, followed by an evaluation period.

**Study Outcome Measures**: 
- **Efficacy Endpoint**: Percentage of patients achieving CCyR by 6 months.
- **Safety Endpoint**: Incidence of adverse events (AEs) graded according to NCI Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0.
- **Secondary Clinical Outcomes**: TTP, DOR, and overall survival (OS).

**Results**: The study faced significant recruitment challenges, achieving a total enrollment of 45 patients over the predefined timeline. Interim analyses indicated an extremely low CCyR rate of 10% after six months of treatment, with a higher-than-expected incidence of severe AEs leading to discontinuation of therapy in 30% of the participants. The trial was ultimately terminated prematurely due to lack of efficacy and recruitment difficulties, rendering the results statistically insignificant.

**Conclusion**: The trial reaffirmed the challenges associated with utilizing nilotinib in patients with suboptimal responses to previously established therapies. The findings suggest that nilotinib, in combination with low-dose chemotherapy, may not provide clinical advantage in this specific patient population, prompting re-evaluation of treatment protocols for CML-CP patients who remain intolerant to standard TKI treatments. Future studies should seek revised methodologies to enhance patient recruitment and explore combination therapies that may synergize better with the mechanisms of nilotinib.

**Trial Status**: Terminated due to insufficient recruitment and lack of meaningful efficacy.
