**A Phase II Study of Sunitinib Malate in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**RATIONALE:** Sunitinib malate is a multitargeted receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor that may inhibit tumor growth by blocking angiogenesis and tumor cell proliferation. Pembrolizumab is a PD-1 inhibitor that enhances T-cell responses against tumors. Combining sunitinib with pembrolizumab may exhibit synergistic effects through dual mechanisms of action, potentially improving outcomes in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of sunitinib malate when administered concurrently with pembrolizumab among patients diagnosed with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC who have not received prior systemic therapy.

**OBJECTIVES:**

- **Primary Objective:**
  - To determine the objective response rate (ORR) of patients with advanced NSCLC receiving sunitinib malate in combination with pembrolizumab.

- **Secondary Objectives:**
  - To evaluate the safety profile of the combination therapy.
  - To assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).
  - To evaluate biomarkers associated with treatment response.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, open-label study. Eligible patients will receive sunitinib malate orally at a dose of 50 mg daily for the first 28 days, in conjunction with pembrolizumab administered intravenously at a dose of 200 mg every 3 weeks. Treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent.

Patients will undergo imaging studies via CT scans to assess tumor response according to RECIST 1.1 criteria at baseline and every 8 weeks thereafter. Laboratory evaluations, including complete blood counts and liver function tests, will be conducted before each administration of pembrolizumab.

**ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA:**

- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  - Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of NSCLC, stage IIIB or IV.
  - No prior systemic therapy for metastatic disease.
  - Measurable disease defined as at least one lesion ≥1 cm in longest diameter by imaging techniques.
  - Karnofsky performance status (KPS) ≥ 70%.
  - Adequate organ function as defined by:
    - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³
    - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
    - Hemoglobin ≥ 9.0 g/dL
    - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN)
    - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 times ULN
    - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 times ULN
  - Age ≥ 18 years old.
  - Signed informed consent.

- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  - History of severe allergic reactions to drugs containing similar components.
  - Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment within the past 2 years.
  - History of untreated CNS metastases or leptomeningeal disease.
  - Concurrent malignancy other than adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancer or cervical carcinoma in situ.
  - Any serious medical conditions or psychiatric illness that would preclude participation.
  - Pregnant or breastfeeding females.

**EFFICACY AND SAFETY ASSESSMENTS:** 

The primary endpoint will be the ORR assessed via imaging studies at predetermined intervals. Responses will be classified as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD).

Safety will be evaluated throughout the study using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0, with particular attention to potential combinations of adverse events from the two treatment modalities.

**FOLLOW-UP:** Patients will be followed up for 30 days post-treatment discontinuation for safety monitoring. Subsequent follow-ups will occur every 12 weeks for OS until disease progression or patient withdrawal.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES:** This study's findings could establish the efficacy and safety of the combination of sunitinib malate with pembrolizumab in patients with NSCLC, thereby providing a potential new treatment option that enhances tumor response rates and extends survival. 

The overall success and contribution of this study to the broader landscape of NSCLC treatment remain to be determined as we pursue important health outcomes for affected patients.
