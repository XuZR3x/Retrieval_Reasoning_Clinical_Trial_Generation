Efficacy of Estradiol in Treating Cognitive Dysfunction in Postmenopausal Women

This clinical trial aims to evaluate the effectiveness of estradiol therapy in alleviating cognitive dysfunction associated with menopause in postmenopausal women. The primary objective is to assess whether estradiol treatment can enhance cognitive function as measured by standardized neuropsychological tests.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Healthy postmenopausal women aged 45-65 years.
- A minimum of 12 months without menstrual bleeding, indicating natural menopause.
- A score of 24-30 on the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) to indicate mild cognitive dysfunction.
- Self-reported moderate to severe cognitive complaints on the Cognitive Failures Questionnaire (CFQ).
- Normal thyroid function as indicated by baseline thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) levels.
- Negative pregnancy test within 14 days prior to enrollment.

Exclusion Criteria:
- History of hormone replacement therapy or any form of exogenous estrogens within 3 months prior to enrollment.
- Significant psychiatric diagnoses including major depressive disorder or schizophrenia.
- Neurological disorders, including but not limited to significant dementia, Alzheimer’s disease, or history of stroke.
- Any malignant condition, particularly hormone-sensitive cancers such as breast or endometrial cancer.
- Current use of psychoactive medications, including antidepressants and antipsychotics.
- History of thromboembolic disorders or cardiovascular disease.
- Active liver disease or significant renal impairment.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding at any point during the study.

Primary Outcome Measures:
- Change in cognitive function assessed by the Wechsler Memory Scale (WMS) from baseline to the end of the study period (12 weeks).
- Secondary outcome measures to include improvements in cognitive complaints assessed by the CFQ post-treatment.

This study aims to address the ongoing debate regarding the role of estradiol in cognitive function and to assess whether it presents a viable therapeutic option. Prior investigations have demonstrated mixed results regarding estrogen therapy’s efficacy on cognitive health, with some showing no significant improvement in global cognitive function or specific cognitive domains. The current study will yield critical data regarding the association between estradiol treatment and cognitive outcomes, potentially informing future research and clinical practices.
