**Failed Clinical Trial of Tivozanib: Tivozanib in Combination With Immunotherapy for Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**A Phase II Study Evaluating Tivozanib Plus PD-1 Inhibitor in Patients With Previously Treated Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

This research study is a Phase II clinical trial designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of tivozanib, an investigational anti-angiogenesis agent, in combination with a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitor for the treatment of patients with metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have received prior systemic therapy. Prior research has suggested that combining anti-angiogenic therapies with immunotherapy may enhance anti-tumor responses; however, this trial seeks to assess the clinical outcomes and biomarker correlations to determine the effectiveness of this combination.

Participants will undergo standard screening tests to confirm eligibility prior to enrollment. Screening will include a thorough medical history review, physical examination, imaging assessments (CT and/or PET scans), laboratory evaluations (including electrolyte levels, liver function tests, and blood counts), and an assessment of performance status using the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) criteria.

If you consent to participate in this study, you will be assigned to receive tivozanib orally once daily for 21 consecutive days in a 28-day cycle, alongside the PD-1 inhibitor administered as per its established regimen. The treatment duration is expected to last up to 12 months for those who remain on study. Regular follow-up assessments will occur every 8 weeks, including imaging studies to evaluate tumor response, and laboratory checks to monitor safety parameters. 

The primary endpoint of the trial is progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months, with secondary endpoints including overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), duration of response (DoR), safety profile, and biomarker analysis to investigate potential predictive and prognostic factors. 

Participants will provide informed consent and should meet the following inclusion criteria: 
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed metastatic NSCLC.
- Previously treated with at least one systemic therapy without disease progression.
- At least one measurable lesion according to RECIST criteria.
- ECOG performance status of 0 to 1.
- Adequate hematologic and organ function.

Exclusion criteria include, but are not limited to:
- Any prior treatment with PD-1 inhibitors or other immunotherapies.
- Active central nervous system metastases or untreated brain lesions.
- Significant cardiovascular abnormalities, including myocardial infarction within the past 6 months or uncontrolled hypertension.
- Current or recent use of any agents that significantly affect the CYP450 enzymatic system.
- History of gastrointestinal disorders affecting drug absorption or requiring surgical interventions.

The study will be conducted at multiple centers and aims to enroll approximately 150 participants. Long-term follow-up will assess survival outcomes for up to 2 years after treatment initiation, and participants who derive clinical benefit may be eligible for continued treatment in a rollover study.

**Primary Objective:**
To evaluate the proportion of patients with metastatic NSCLC who remain progression-free at 6 months following treatment with tivozanib in combination with a PD-1 inhibitor.

**Results Overview:**
Upon review of interim data, the trial encountered insufficient evidence of efficacy in terms of PFS and overall survival metrics when compared to historical controls. The independent data monitoring committee recommended discontinuation of enrollment, resulting in a premature termination of the study due to lack of clinical benefit.

**Conclusion:**
The trial's early termination underscores significant challenges in enhancing the efficacy of tivozanib through combination therapy with immunotherapeutic agents in the setting of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, warranting further investigation into the mechanisms of action and potential resistance pathways involved in anti-angiogenic treatment strategies.
