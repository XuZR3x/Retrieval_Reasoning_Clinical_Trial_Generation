<clinical_trial>
  <title>Randomized, Double-blind, Active Comparator Study of the Efficacy and Safety of Telmisartan in Patients with Resistant Hypertension</title>
  <design>Prospective, Randomized, Double-blind, Active Comparator</design>
  <objective>To evaluate the efficacy and safety of telmisartan in patients diagnosed with resistant hypertension, specifically assessing reduction in systolic blood pressure (SBP) over a treatment period of 12 weeks compared to amlodipine.</objective>
  <intervention>
    <name>Telmisartan</name>
    <dose>80 mg once daily</dose>
    <duration>12 weeks</duration>
  </intervention>
  <comparator>
    <name>Amlodipine</name>
    <dose>10 mg once daily</dose>
  </comparator>
  <inclusion_criteria>
    <criteria>1. Adult patients aged 18 to 75 years with a diagnosis of resistant hypertension, defined as SBP ≥ 140 mmHg despite treatment with at least three antihypertensive medications.</criteria>
    <criteria>2. Ability to provide written informed consent in accordance with Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and local Institutional Review Boards (IRBs).</criteria>
    <criteria>3. Patients must not have switched regimen in the last 30 days nor be on any long-acting diuretics.</criteria>
    <criteria>4. Weight ≥50 kg and ≤150 kg.</criteria>
    <criteria>5. Relevant laboratory tests showing normal renal and hepatic function, SBP ≥ 140 mmHg at screening.</criteria>
  </inclusion_criteria>
  <exclusion_criteria>
    <criteria>1. Secondary hypertension or conditions such as pheochromocytoma, primary hyperaldosteronism, or sleep apnea.</criteria>
    <criteria>2. History of severe cardiopulmonary disease, myocardial infarction, or stroke within the last 6 months.</criteria>
    <criteria>3. Pregnancy, lactation, or females of childbearing potential without effective contraception.</criteria>
    <criteria>4. Previous hypersensitivity reaction to angiotensin II receptor antagonists.</criteria>
    <criteria>5. Significant electrolyte imbalances, including hyperkalemia or hyponatremia.</criteria>
    <criteria>6. Concomitant use of NSAIDs or other medications with potential renal impairment risks.</criteria>
  </exclusion_criteria>
  <endpoints>
    <primary_endpoint>Change from baseline in seated SBP after 12 weeks of treatment.</primary_endpoint>
    <secondary_endpoint>Assessment of safety through the incidence of adverse events, lab test abnormalities, and quality of life assessments via the EQ-5D scale.</secondary_endpoint>
  </endpoints>
  <results>
    <findings>Results demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in SBP in the telmisartan group compared to the amlodipine group (p < 0.05) along with an acceptable safety profile, supporting the use of telmisartan as an effective therapy in resistant hypertension.</findings>
  </results>
  <conclusion>This study provides robust evidence for the efficacy and safety of telmisartan in managing resistant hypertension, potentially allowing for a change in treatment paradigms in the management of this challenging population.</conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
