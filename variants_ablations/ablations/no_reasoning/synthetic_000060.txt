**Clinical Trial Title: Hydroxyurea in the Management of Complications Associated with Sickle Cell Disease in Pediatric Patients: The Sickle Cell Outcomes Assessment in Children (SCOPe) Trial**

**Study Design:** 
The SCOPe trial is a multi-center, phase II randomized controlled trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of hydroxyurea in managing variable clinical complications in pediatric patients with sickle cell disease (SCD). This study emphasizes a broad assessment of both clinical and psychosocial outcomes to enhance the quality of life in children afflicted with severe forms of SCD. 

**Study Objectives:**
1. To determine the efficacy of hydroxyurea in reducing the frequency of vaso-occlusive crises (VOC) and hospitalizations in children with SCD.
2. To assess the improvement in hematological parameters, specifically fetal hemoglobin (HbF) levels and white blood cell counts, following hydroxyurea therapy.
3. To evaluate the psychological wellbeing and quality of life (QoL) improvements in children receiving hydroxyurea, as evidenced by validated psychometric scales.
4. To investigate the association of hydroxyurea therapy with measured pain levels using an established pediatric pain scale.
5. To analyze the economic impact of hydroxyurea treatment on hospital resource utilization and parent-reported expenses.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Pediatric patients aged 2-16 years with a confirmed diagnosis of SCD (HbSS or HbSβ0 thalassemia).
2. Documented history of at least two hospitalizations due to VOC within the preceding 12 months.
3. Parent or guardian must provide informed consent, with verbal assent from the child as per local IRB/Ethics Board guidelines.
4. Patients must demonstrate compliance willingness to adhere to the study protocol including medication and follow-up evaluations.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Known hypersensitivity to hydroxyurea.
2. Coexisting severe chronic illness that may confound results (e.g., malignancies, significant cardiac or hepatic disorders).
3. Acute infection requiring immediate antibiotic treatment.
4. Recent history of blood transfusions within the last 60 days prior to enrollment.
5. Participation in another investigational drug study during the trial duration.

**Primary Considerations:**
The primary endpoint involves the rate of VOC, defined as unintentional acute pain requiring medical intervention, measured at six months post-initiation of hydroxyurea therapy. The expected rate of reduction compares to a control group receiving standard supportive care alone. 

**Outcome Measurements:**
1. Hospitalization frequency due to VOC over a 12-month follow-up period.
2. Changes in laboratory values inclusive of HbF, hemoglobin concentration, and leukocyte counts.
3. Analyses utilizing validated tools such as the PedsQL™ for QoL and the numeric rating scale for pain assessments.
4. Economic evaluations focusing on caregiver burden and healthcare costs associated with therapy.

**Statistical Analysis:**
A sample size of 150 patients, 75 per treatment group, was determined necessary to achieve 80% power to detect a statistically significant difference in hospitalizations. Analysis will be conducted based on intention-to-treat principles, employing mixed-effects models for outcome measurements over time.

**Ethical Considerations:**
The SCOPe trial received approval from the relevant institutional review boards, and every participant's family will receive thorough counseling regarding the potential benefits and risks associated with hydroxyurea therapy, highlighting the empowerment in managing SCD.

**Expected Outcomes:**
It is anticipated that the SCOPe trial will provide compelling evidence supporting the utilization of hydroxyurea as a standard therapeutic modality for enhancing the clinical management of children with sickle cell disease, while simultaneously improving their quality of life and reducing healthcare costs associated with recurrent hospitalizations. The results will potentially contribute to changing treatment paradigms and improving access to care, particularly in resource-limited settings.
