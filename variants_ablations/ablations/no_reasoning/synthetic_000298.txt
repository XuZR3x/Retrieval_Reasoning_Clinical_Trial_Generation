**FAILURE CLINICAL TRIAL OF EXPAREL: Evaluation of Liposomal Bupivacaine in Patients Undergoing Total Abdominal Hysterectomy for Postsurgical Analgesia: A Randomized, Double-Blind Controlled Trial**

**TITLE OF THE RESEARCH PROJECT**  
A randomized, double-blind controlled trial of liposomal bupivacaine for postsurgical analgesia in patients undergoing total abdominal hysterectomy.

**PROJECT SUMMARY**  
For patients undergoing total abdominal hysterectomy, administration of 80 mg of liposomal bupivacaine at the surgical site may not result in a significant reduction in postoperative pain compared to standard multimodal analgesia, leading to high utilization of opioid pain medications.

**STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM**  
Patients undergoing total abdominal hysterectomy frequently experience severe postoperative pain, which traditionally necessitates the use of opioid-based analgesics for management. The escalating opioid crisis emphasizes the need for effective, non-opioid alternatives that minimize reliance on narcotics and potential long-term dependence.

**THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK**  
Prior studies have indicated that liposomal formulations of bupivacaine can provide extended pain relief in various surgical settings. The hypothesis posits that a single administration of liposomal bupivacaine will provide adequate analgesia and thereby reduce opioid consumption post-hysterectomy. However, evidence from analogous trials reveals variances in efficacy across different surgical types and patient populations.

**RESEARCH OBJECTIVE**  
To assess the effectiveness of liposomal bupivacaine for postoperative analgesia in patients undergoing total abdominal hysterectomy by comparing its effects on opioid consumption and pain scores against a control group receiving standard multimodal analgesia.

**METHODOLOGY**  
Patients admitted to XYZ Hospital who are scheduled for a total abdominal hysterectomy will be screened for eligibility. Exclusion criteria include:  
1) Patients with a history of intolerance or allergy to bupivacaine or other local anesthetics.  
2) Patients with significant contraindications for surgery, including coagulopathy or active infection.  
3) Women who are pregnant or breastfeeding.  
4) Patients with chronic pain syndromes requiring long-term opioid therapy.

Eligible patients will be randomized into two groups using a computer-generated randomization scheme. Group A will receive 80 mg of liposomal bupivacaine mixed with 20 mL of saline administered at the surgical site. Group B will receive 20 mL of placebo solution (saline) administered similarly. Both groups will also receive a standardized postoperative analgesic regimen consisting of acetaminophen and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).

Postoperative pain will be assessed at 24, 48, and 72 hours using a Numeric Rating Scale (NRS). The total consumption of opioids in morphine milligram equivalents (MME) will be recorded during the hospital stay. Additional evaluations will include patient-reported satisfaction and adverse effects, particularly focusing on any instances of respiratory depression or sedation.

**PLAN FOR ANALYSIS OF RESULTS**  
It is anticipated that a 25% reduction in opioid consumption in the liposomal bupivacaine group compared to the control group will be a meaningful endpoint. The trial will utilize intention-to-treat analysis, leveraging ANOVA for continuous variables and chi-squared tests for categorical variables to compare outcomes between groups.

**TIMETABLE**  
The study will initiate upon Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval and is projected to span 18 months.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA**  
- Scheduled for total abdominal hysterectomy  
- Age 18 years or older  
- Capable of providing informed consent  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA**  
- Current chronic opioid use (>30 mg/day oral morphine equivalent for >2 weeks)  
- Prior pelvic surgery  
- Significant comorbidities impacting surgical recovery  

**OUTCOMES**  
The primary outcome will be the total opioid consumption during the first 72 hours postoperatively. Secondary outcomes will encompass pain scores at specified intervals, overall patient satisfaction, and the frequency of adverse events.

In summary, this investigation seeks to provide clarity on the efficacy and safety of liposomal bupivacaine as an adjunct to pain management in abdominal gynecological surgeries, addressing the urgent need for multimodal approaches to postoperative pain control.
