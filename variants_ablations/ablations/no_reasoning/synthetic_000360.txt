    <studyName>Laboratory Biomarker Analysis of Circulating MicroRNAs in Patients with Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) Receiving Nivolumab</studyName>
    <RATIONALE>Investigating circulating microRNAs in plasma may provide insights into potential biomarkers that predict response to nivolumab therapy in patients with NSCLC. Identification of such biomarkers could allow for more tailored therapeutic approaches in this patient population.</RATIONALE>
    
    <PURPOSE>This research trial aims to evaluate the relationship between circulating microRNA levels and clinical outcomes in patients with NSCLC who are treated with nivolumab, focusing on overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS).</PURPOSE>
    
    <OBJECTIVES>
      <objective>To determine whether baseline levels of circulating miR-21, miR-126, and miR-155 can predict overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in patients treated with nivolumab.</objective>
      <objective>To assess changes in circulating microRNA profiles pre- and post-treatment in association with clinical response markers.</objective>
      <objective>To explore the correlation between microRNA levels and immune checkpoint activity, assessed through tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density in surgical specimens.</objective>
    </OBJECTIVES>
    
    <OUTLINE>
      <design>Plasma samples from patients enrolled in the clinical trial of nivolumab will be analyzed for circulating microRNAs using quantitative reverse transcription Polymerase Chain Reaction (qRT-PCR).</design>
      <sampleRequirements>Stored plasma specimens will be utilized without additional patient involvement, adhering to the ethical standards of patient consent.</sampleRequirements>
      <analysis>MicroRNA expression levels will be correlated with clinical outcomes, specifically PFS and OS, alongside immune activity markers derived from tumor tissue.</analysis>
    </OUTLINE>
    
    <INCLUSION_CRITERIA>
      <criterion>Histologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with metastatic disease.</criterion>
      <criterion>Patients must have received at least one prior chemotherapy regimen.</criterion>
      <criterion>Patients must be receiving nivolumab treatment as part of standard clinical practice.</criterion>
      <criterion>Age ≥ 18 years with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.</criterion>
      <criterion>Ability to provide written informed consent.</criterion>
    </INCLUSION_CRITERIA>
    
    <EXCLUSION_CRITERIA>
      <criterion>Patients with known hypersensitivity to nivolumab or any of its components.</criterion>
      <criterion>Patients who underwent major surgery within 28 days prior to enrollment.</criterion>
      <criterion>Patients with active autoimmune disorders or concurrent malignancies.</criterion>
      <criterion>Known infections requiring systemic treatment.</criterion>
      <criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding females are also excluded.</criterion>
    </EXCLUSION_CRITERIA>

    <FOLLOW_UP>Patients are to be monitored for progression-free survival and overall survival data at 6, 12, and 18 months post-treatment conclusion.</FOLLOW_UP>

    <RESULTS>
      <finding>The trial was unsuccessful in establishing a predictive biomarker profile for response to nivolumab therapy in NSCLC.</finding>
      <finding>No significant correlation was observed between baseline microRNA levels and clinical outcomes, leading to a lack of actionable biomarkers for clinical implementation.</finding>
      <finding>Further investigations revealed significant variability in microRNA expression, which complicates their utility as consistent prognostic indicators in this population.</finding>
    </RESULTS>
