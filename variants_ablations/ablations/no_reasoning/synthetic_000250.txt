<clinical_trial>
    <title>A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study of Nebivolol on Cardiovascular Outcomes in Geriatric Patients with Resistant Hypertension</title>
    <objective>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of nebivolol in reducing cardiovascular events among geriatric patients aged 65 years and older who have resistant hypertension. Secondary objectives include the assessment of safety, tolerability, and effects on quality of life.</objective>
    
    <background>Resistant hypertension remains a significant challenge in the geriatric population, where its prevalence often exceeds 20%. Due to the complex nature of hypertension in older adults, including altered pharmacokinetics and comorbid conditions, effective management is crucial. Nebivolol, a third-generation beta-blocker, is distinguished by its ability to enhance nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability, thereby promoting vasodilation and potentially ameliorating vascular stiffness seen in this cohort.</background>

    <design>
        <type>Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial</type>
        <duration>12 months</duration>
        <methodology>Four hundred elderly patients with resistant hypertension will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either nebivolol (5 mg daily, titrated to a maximum of 10 mg) or placebo. Randomization will be achieved through a computer-generated random sequence. Blinding will be maintained for both participants and investigators throughout the trial.</methodology>
    </design>

    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Men and women aged 65 years or older</criterion>
        <criterion>Diagnosis of resistant hypertension defined as office blood pressure ≥140/90 mmHg despite treatment with three antihypertensive agents</criterion>
        <criterion>Willingness to comply with study procedures and provide informed consent</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>

    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Secondary hypertension</criterion>
        <criterion>History of myocardial infarction or stroke within the past 12 months</criterion>
        <criterion>Severe renal or hepatic impairment</criterion>
        <criterion>History of inappropriate response to beta-blockers</criterion>
        <criterion>Participation in other clinical trials within the last 30 days</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>

    <outcomes>
        <primary_outcome>Reduction in the composite rate of cardiovascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular-related mortality) over 12 months.</primary_outcome>
        <secondary_outcomes> 
            <outcome>Change in systolic and diastolic blood pressure from baseline to 12 months</outcome>
            <outcome>Assessment of quality of life via the EQ-5D scale</outcome>
            <outcome>Incidence of adverse effects as recorded on the Adverse Event Reporting Form.</outcome>
        </secondary_outcomes>
    </outcomes>

    <analysis>
        <method>The primary analysis will be an Intent-to-Treat (ITT) approach, utilizing the Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF) methodology for missing data. Categorical outcomes will be assessed using chi-square tests, while continuous variables will be analyzed via ANCOVA.</method>
    </analysis>

    <anticipated_results>We hypothesize that nebivolol will yield a statistically significant reduction in the composite cardiovascular events compared to placebo, along with improved blood pressure control and enhanced quality of life metrics.</anticipated_results>
    
    <conclusion>This trial aims to provide crucial data on the role of nebivolol in managing resistant hypertension in elderly patients, addressing an urgent need for effective therapeutic strategies in this vulnerable population.</conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
