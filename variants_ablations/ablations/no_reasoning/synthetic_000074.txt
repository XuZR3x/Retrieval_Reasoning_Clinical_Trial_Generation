**Failed Clinical Trial of Selinexor: SELNET (Selinexor for Neoplasm Treated with Targeted Therapy)**

**A Phase II Multicenter Study of Oral Selinexor (KPT-330) in Patients With Various Solid Tumors Progressing after Targeted Therapy**

This is a phase II multicenter, open-label clinical trial designed to assess the efficacy and safety of selinexor (KPT-330) in patients diagnosed with advanced solid tumors that have shown progression following targeted therapies. The primary objective is to evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) of selinexor in a cohort of patients who are resistant to therapy benefiting from inhibitors of driver mutations, such as BRAF and EGFR inhibitors.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To determine the ORR defined as the percentage of patients achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 after 12 weeks of selinexor treatment.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess progression-free survival (PFS) from the start of treatment until disease progression or death.
II. To evaluate changes in circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) levels before and after treatment with selinexor.
III. To analyze quality of life outcomes using the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) QLQ-C30 core questionnaire.
IV. To characterize the safety profile, including the incidence of adverse events graded per the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

**OUTLINE:**
Eligible patients will receive oral selinexor at a dose of 60 mg three times weekly for a 28-day cycle, continuing until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of solid tumor with measurable disease.
- Evidence of disease progression following at least one line of targeted therapy (e.g., TKI, BRAF, or EGFR inhibitors).
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function as defined by laboratory values.
- Ability to provide a pre-treatment and on-treatment tumor biopsy for biomarker analysis.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Patients with prior treatment using selinexor.
- Concurrent use of other systemic anticancer therapies or investigational agents.
- Active malignancy other than the study-specific solid tumor.
- Second malignancy (unless in remission for at least 2 years).
- History of uncontrolled cardiovascular disease, severe psychiatric disorders, or active autoimmune disorders requiring treatment.

**PROGRESSIVE DISEASE DEFINED AS:**
Any increase in the sum of diameters of target lesions by >20% compared to the smallest sum observed or the appearance of one or more new lesions.

**FAILURE AND OUTCOMES:**
Despite the promising mechanism targeting the nuclear export protein XPO1, this trial was terminated after interim analysis revealed a significant lack of efficacy. The ORR at the primary endpoint was recorded at 10%, which failed to meet the predetermined threshold of 25% for clinical significance. 

Adverse events predominantly included severe gastrointestinal toxicities such as grade 3 nausea and diarrhea, resulting in a high discontinuation rate (30%). Biomarker analyses indicated minimal changes in ctDNA levels, suggesting no substantial tumor engagement by selinexor and a consistent pattern of resistance in previously targeted tumors.

**CONCLUSION:**
The SELNET trial highlighted the limitations of selinexor-induced modulation of tumor biology in the context of previously resistant solid tumors after targeted therapies. Further studies will need to refine patient selection criteria and explore combination therapies with more robust synergies for overcoming resistance mechanisms.
