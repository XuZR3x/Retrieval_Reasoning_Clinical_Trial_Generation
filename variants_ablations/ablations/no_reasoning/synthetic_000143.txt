**Clinical Trial Report**

**Title:** A Phase II Clinical Trial of Alisertib in Combination with Everolimus in Treating Patients with Relapsed/Refractory Ewing Sarcoma

**Background:** Ewing Sarcoma is a rare, aggressive malignancy that predominantly affects children and adolescents. Current treatment protocols for Ewing Sarcoma, including chemotherapy and radiation, demonstrate limited efficacy in relapsed or refractory cases. Alisertib, a potent Aurora A kinase inhibitor, has shown promising results in preclinical studies by inducing cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in tumor cells. Everolimus, a selective inhibitor of the mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), may further potentiate the antitumor effects of alisertib by inhibiting cell proliferation. The combination of these two agents may provide a synergistic effect for the treatment of relapsed or refractory Ewing Sarcoma.

**Primary Objectives:**
1. To evaluate the safety and tolerability of alisertib in combination with everolimus in patients with relapsed or refractory Ewing Sarcoma.
2. To determine the overall response rate (ORR) in terms of complete response (CR) and partial response (PR) among treated patients.

**Secondary Objectives:**
1. To assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates at 6 and 12 months following treatment.
2. To evaluate pharmacokinetic parameters of alisertib and everolimus, including peak plasma concentration (Cmax), time to peak concentration (Tmax), and area under the curve (AUC).
3. To investigate the impact of aurora A kinase expression levels on treatment outcomes by analyzing pre-treatment tissue samples.

**Outline:** This is a multicenter, open-label, dose-escalation study. Patients will receive alisertib orally (PO) twice daily (BID) on days 1-7 and everolimus orally (PO) once daily (QD) on days 1-28. Treatment will be administered in 28-day cycles in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. After treatment, participants will be followed up for 30 days.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of Ewing Sarcoma, relapsed or refractory after at least one prior line of chemotherapy.
- Patients must be ≥ 12 years of age with measurable disease as per RECIST criteria.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0-2.
- Adequate bone marrow function, defined as: ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³, and hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL.
- Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times upper limit of normal (ULN), aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) ≤ 2.5 times ULN.
- Ability to provide written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with known central nervous system (CNS) metastases.
- Prior treatment with any aurora kinase inhibitor or everolimus.
- Significant cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled medical conditions.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- Concomitant use of strong CYP3A4 inducers or inhibitors.

**Desired Outcomes:**
The primary endpoint will focus on evaluating the safety profile and tolerability, utilizing the National Cancer Institute's Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 for the grading of adverse effects. The overall response rate (ORR) will be monitored every two cycles through imaging assessments.

**Statistical Analysis:** Response rates will be calculated with 95% confidence intervals. PFS and OS will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, with comparisons made via the log-rank test.

**Conclusion:** The investigational combination of alisertib and everolimus aims to potentiate therapeutic efficacy through simultaneous targeting of oncogenic signaling pathways in Ewing Sarcoma. It is hypothesized that the dual mechanism of action may improve clinical outcomes in patients where conventional therapies have failed, providing a necessary avenue for effective treatment options in this challenging population. Following completion of this study, further exploration of biomarkers related to treatment response will be conducted to refine patient selection for future clinical investigations.
