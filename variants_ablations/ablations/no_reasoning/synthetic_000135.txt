<ClinicalTrial>
  <TrialName>Evaluation of Peginterferon Alfa-2a and Ribavirin for Non-responders in Chronic Hepatitis C With Genotype 1 and 4: A Multicenter, Randomized Trial</TrialName>
  <StudyDesign>Randomized, open-label, multicenter clinical trial</StudyDesign>
  <Background>
    This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of peginterferon alfa-2a (Pegasys) combined with ribavirin in patients with chronic hepatitis C (CHC) genotypes 1 or 4 who did not achieve a sustained virologic response (SVR) from previous antiviral therapies. Chronic Hepatitis C is often associated with high rates of non-response in patients with genotype 1 and 4, necessitating advanced treatment strategies to improve health outcomes.
  </Background>
  <Objectives>
    <Objective>To assess the virologic response rates of peginterferon alfa-2a and ribavirin in patients previously unresponsive to standard of care.</Objective>
    <Objective>To evaluate the safety and tolerability of the combination therapy in this demographic.</Objective>
  </Objectives>
  <Intervention>
    Patients will be randomized to receive either peginterferon alfa-2a (180 mcg subcutaneously weekly) in combination with ribavirin (800-1200 mg orally daily) for a total treatment duration of 48 weeks, with follow-up for an additional 24 weeks to assess sustained virologic response.
  </Intervention>
  <SampleSize>Target enrollment is 600 patients.</SampleSize>
  <InclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>Adult patients aged 18 to 75 years.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Patients diagnosed with chronic hepatitis C, genotype 1 or 4.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Documented previous treatment with peginterferon and ribavirin or other HCV therapies lacking SVR.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Serological evidence of chronic hepatitis C infection.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Compensated liver disease.</Criterion>
  </InclusionCriteria>
  <ExclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>Previous treatment with direct-acting antiviral agents (DAAs).</Criterion>
    <Criterion>History of decompensated liver disease or hepatic failure.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Active comorbidities that may complicate treatment (e.g., severe chronic lung disease, renal insufficiency).</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding women.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Coinfection with HIV or hepatitis B.</Criterion>
  </ExclusionCriteria>
  <Endpoints>
    <Endpoint>
      <Type>Primary</Type>
      <Description>Proportion of patients achieving undetectable HCV RNA at week 24 post-treatment.</Description>
    </Endpoint>
    <Endpoint>
      <Type>Secondary</Type>
      <Description>Assessment of liver function tests and adverse event profile throughout treatment.</Description>
    </Endpoint>
  </Endpoints>
  <PreliminaryFindings>
    The interim analysis suggested that the combination therapy was associated with an unacceptable profile of severe side effects, including hematologic abnormalities and increased liver enzymes. Moreover, the achieved SVR rates did not exceed 20%, which is significantly lower than the pre-defined threshold for success. Based on these findings, the study was halted prior to completion due to evident lack of efficacy and safety concerns.
  </PreliminaryFindings>
  <Conclusion>
    This trial highlights critical challenges in the management of patients with chronic hepatitis C genotype 1 and 4. The exceedingly low sustained virologic response rates paired with adverse effects necessitate a reevaluation of treatment strategies and further exploration into alternative therapeutic agents or regimens. 
  </Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
