**Failed Clinical Trial of Fulvestrant: Evaluation of Fulvestrant in Combination with Novel Immunotherapy in Advanced Hormone Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer**

**A Phase II Study of Fulvestrant in Combination with PD-1 Inhibitor (Pembrolizumab) in Patients with Advanced Hormone Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer Unresponsive to Endocrine Therapy**

This Phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of fulvestrant administered in conjunction with the PD-1 inhibitor pembrolizumab in patients with advanced hormone receptor-positive breast cancer who have demonstrated disease progression following at least one line of endocrine therapy. The rationale for this study originates from preclinical observations which suggest an immunomodulatory effect of fulvestrant that could potentially enhance the response to immune checkpoint inhibitors, thereby stimulating antitumor immunity in a treatment-resistant environment.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**

I. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of fulvestrant combined with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced hormone receptor-positive breast cancer who exhibit progression after previous endocrine treatments.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**

I. To evaluate the median progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in the study population receiving the combination therapy compared to historical controls.

II. To characterize the safety profile of the combination therapy by recording treatment-related adverse events in accordance with CTCAE v5.0 (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events).

III. To analyze immune biomarker changes pre- and post-treatment to ascertain potential predictors of treatment response, including tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and PD-L1 expression levels.

**OUTLINE:**

Patients will receive fulvestrant via intramuscular injection at a dose of 500 mg on day 1, followed by pembrolizumab administered intravenously at a dose of 200 mg on day 1 of a 21-day cycle. Treatment cycles will continue until disease progression is observed or there are unacceptable toxicities. Patients will be monitored biweekly for treatment response and safety. 

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**

- Patients must have histologically confirmed adenocarcinoma of the breast with positive estrogen (ER) and/or progesterone (PR) receptors.
- Patients must be postmenopausal, with a documented estrogen deprivation status.
- Patients must have stage IV or inoperable locally advanced disease.
- Patients must have measurable disease, defined as at least one lesion that can be measured in at least one dimension.
- Prior exposure to endocrine therapies is required, with documented disease progression on such treatment within 6 months.
- Patients must have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
- Patients must have a life expectancy of at least 3 months.
- Adequate organ function as evidenced by laboratory tests, including total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x the institutional upper limit and adequate renal function.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**

- Patients who have received chemotherapy or other systemic treatments within 4 weeks prior to enrollment.
- Presence of symptomatic visceral metastases, including significant hepatic impairment or active brain metastases.
- Prior treatment with any PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors.
- History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to previous immunotherapy agents.
- Ongoing autoimmune disorders or requiring systemic steroids.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**

The primary outcome will be assessed by the overall response rate according to RECIST v1.1 criteria. Secondary measures will include median progression-free survival and overall survival analyzed from date of enrollment to the date of progression or death.

**RESULTS:**

The trial ultimately failed to demonstrate meaningful efficacy of the combination therapy in terms of overall response rate, with less than 10% of enrolled participants achieving partial or complete responses. The median progression-free survival was markedly lower than anticipated. Toxicity profiles were consistent with previously reported adverse events associated with these agents; however, the combination did not yield a significant clinical benefit. Poor biomarker response rates further suggested inadequate immune activation in the study population. 

In conclusion, the anticipated synergy between fulvestrant and pembrolizumab was not observed, marking this intervention as a failure in terms of clinical efficacy for the treatment of hormone receptor-positive advanced breast cancer. Further investigation into combination immunotherapeutic strategies remains warranted despite these unfavorable findings.
