**Failed Clinical Trial of Dapagliflozin: Efficacy of Dapagliflozin in Reducing Cardiovascular Risk Factors in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes and Hypertension**

**A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy of Dapagliflozin on Cardiovascular Risk Factors in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and Concurrent Hypertension**

The present study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of dapagliflozin in reducing cardiovascular risk factors among patients diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and concurrent hypertension over a study duration of 12 weeks. The primary hypothesis was that dapagliflozin would yield significant improvements in blood pressure control, lipid profile, and other relevant cardiovascular risk factors compared to placebo.

**Rationale:** Patients with T2DM are at an increased risk of developing cardiovascular diseases (CVD), and concurrent hypertension further exacerbates this risk. Dapagliflozin, a sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT-2) inhibitor, has demonstrated potential benefits in glycemic control and weight reduction. However, its specific impact on cardiovascular risk markers in this patient population remains inadequately characterized.

**Objective:** To evaluate the change in systolic and diastolic blood pressure, as well as alterations in lipid profile and other biochemical parameters, between the dapagliflozin and placebo groups.

**Study Design:** This was a double-blind, placebo-controlled, multi-center trial designed to enroll eligible participants across multiple sites. 

**Study Population:** Adults aged 40 to 75 with a diagnosis of T2DM (HbA1c between 7.0% and 10.0%) and hypertension (systolic blood pressure ≥130 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure ≥80 mmHg) were eligible for enrollment.

**Intervention:** Eligible participants were randomly assigned to receive either dapagliflozin 10 mg orally once daily or matching placebo for 12 weeks. All subjects continued their current antihypertensive therapy throughout the study period.

**Main Study Parameters/Endpoints:** The primary endpoint was the change in systolic blood pressure from baseline at the end of the treatment phase. Secondary endpoints included changes in diastolic blood pressure, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), triglycerides, and glucose levels.

**Nature and Extent of the Burden and Risks Associated with Participation:** Participants were required to attend regular follow-up visits, undergo routine blood pressure measurements, blood sampling for lipid profiles, and glycemic control assessments at baseline, and weeks 4, 8, and 12. Potential risks associated with dapagliflozin included dehydration and urinary tract infections, particularly in patients with additional medication affecting blood pressure regulation.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Provision of signed informed consent prior to study involvement.
2. Male or female aged 40 to 75 years, with documented diagnosis of T2DM.
3. Concurrent hypertension treatment with stable antihypertensive therapy for at least 3 months prior to study entry.
4. Body Mass Index (BMI) between 25 to 40 kg/m².

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Type 1 diabetes mellitus or a history of diabetic ketoacidosis.
2. Patients currently receiving treatment with glucocorticoids or other agents impacting glucose metabolism not permitted in the study.
3. Patients with serious cardiovascular disease, defined as recent myocardial infarction or coronary revascularization within the last 6 months.
4. Significant renal impairment, defined as estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <60 mL/min.

**Results:** The primary endpoints demonstrated that dapagliflozin failed to achieve any statistically significant reduction in systolic (mean difference = -1.2 mmHg; 95% CI: -4.5 to 2.1; p=0.49) and diastolic blood pressure (mean difference = -0.5 mmHg; 95% CI: -3.1 to 2.1; p=0.67) compared to placebo. Furthermore, lipid profile analyses did not reveal notable alterations in LDL-C, HDL-C, or triglyceride levels across groups. The lack of observed impact on these cardiovascular risk factors indicates that dapagliflozin does not confer expected cardiovascular benefits in this particular patient cohort.

In conclusion, this trial was unsuccessful in demonstrating the efficacy of dapagliflozin in managing key cardiovascular risk factors in patients with T2DM and hypertension, highlighting the complexity of treatment responses in this population and necessitating future research into alternative therapeutic strategies.
