**Failed clinical trial of ribavirin: Efficacy of Ribavirin Monotherapy in Patients With Ebola Virus Disease (EVD)**  
**A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Ribavirin Monotherapy in Patients Diagnosed With Ebola Virus Disease**

Ebola virus disease (EVD) represents a significant public health threat, characterized by high mortality rates during outbreaks primarily affecting Sub-Saharan Africa. Currently, therapeutic options remain limited, and clinical management focuses largely on supportive care. Ribavirin, an antiviral medication previously investigated for other viral infections, had shown potential in vitro activity against Ebola virus. This study aimed to assess the efficacy of ribavirin monotherapy in terms of reducing mortality, length of hospitalization, time to symptom resolution, and the incidence of long-term complications in patients diagnosed with EVD compared to a placebo.

The study hypothesized that ribavirin treatment would yield a statistically significant improvement over placebo in the primary endpoints of mortality at 28 days post-treatment and duration of hospitalization.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adult patients aged 18 years and older  
- Confirmed diagnosis of EVD through RT-PCR  
- Presentation to healthcare facility within 3 days of symptom onset  
- Provision of written informed consent  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients with known hypersensitivity to ribavirin  
- Previous antiviral therapy within 4 weeks of enrollment  
- Significant co-morbid conditions such as renal failure (creatinine clearance < 50 mL/min)  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding individuals  
- Patients with severe cardiovascular abnormalities or any condition limiting adherence to the study protocol  

The study enrolled 430 participants across multiple healthcare facilities in West Africa. The treatment group received ribavirin (200 mg three times daily), while the control group received a matched placebo. Outcomes were evaluated at 28 days for mortality and secondary outcomes including length of hospital stay, time to symptom resolution, and occurrence of post-viral sequelae at 12 weeks.

**Results:**  
By the conclusion of the study, the ribavirin group demonstrated no significant reduction in mortality compared to placebo (52% vs. 54%, p=0.73). Secondary outcomes such as days to recovery and hospital length of stay showed no statistically significant differences between the two groups. Adverse events reported included hemolytic anemia and gastrointestinal disturbances in the ribavirin cohort, with no discernible benefit for patients receiving the drug.

The findings indicated that ribavirin monotherapy did not improve clinical outcomes for patients with EVD, thus leading to the early termination of the study. This trial reinforces the complexities of antiviral treatment in severe viral infections and highlights the necessity for ongoing research to identify effective therapeutic strategies for EVD, as well as the need for caution when extrapolating results from other viral infections to Ebola virus.

In conclusion, ribavirin's lack of effectiveness against EVD will necessitate the continued search for alternative therapeutic agents or novel treatment combinations to address this global health emergency.
