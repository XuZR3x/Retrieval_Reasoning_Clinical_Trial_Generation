**Failed Clinical Trial of Ticagrelor: Efficacy of Ticagrelor in Patients Undergoing Elective Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) for Stable Angina**

**Objective:** The primary aim of this prospective, randomized, multicenter trial is to evaluate the effectiveness of ticagrelor compared to clopidogrel in reducing adverse cardiovascular events in patients with stable angina undergoing elective PCI. The hypothesis is that ticagrelor may offer superior clinical outcomes due to its pharmacodynamic profile and rapid platelet inhibition compared to clopidogrel.

**Major Inclusion Criteria:** 
1. Adults aged 30 to 75 years diagnosed with stable angina, confirmed via non-invasive stress testing.
2. Patients scheduled to undergo elective PCI without the need for emergency revascularization.
3. Written informed consent obtained prior to randomization.

**Major Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Known hypersensitivity or adverse reaction to ticagrelor or clopidogrel.
2. History of major bleeding events within the past 6 months.
3. Presence of active bleeding or conditions associated with an increased bleeding risk (e.g., coagulation disorders).
4. History of stroke (ischemic or hemorrhagic) within the previous year.
5. Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min).
6. Current use of anticoagulants that cannot be discontinued (e.g., warfarin, direct oral anticoagulants).
7. Patients with significant liver dysfunction (Child-Pugh Class B or C).
8. Concurrent participation in any investigational drug study.

**Design:** In this open-label, randomized controlled trial, participants are assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either ticagrelor (90 mg BID) or clopidogrel (75 mg QD) for a duration of 12 months following PCI. Routine aspirin therapy (81 mg daily) will be administered to all participants, consistent with clinical practice guidelines.

**Endpoints:** 
- **Primary Efficacy Endpoint:** The composite of all-cause mortality, myocardial infarction (MI), stroke, and unplanned revascularization within 12 months post-PCI. 
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Individual components of the primary endpoint, as well as major bleeding events and quality of life assessed using the Seattle Angina Questionnaire at baseline, 6, and 12 months.

**Monitoring:** Participants will undergo scheduled follow-ups at 1, 6, and 12 months post-PCI. Clinical events and any adverse effects related to the study medications will be meticulously recorded and analyzed.

**Results and Conclusion:** The trial ultimately demonstrated that ticagrelor did not yield a statistically significant improvement in the composite primary endpoint compared to clopidogrel. While ticagrelor was associated with a trend toward a lower incidence of MI, this did not translate into a significant overall clinical benefit. Notably, the study also reported an increased incidence of major bleeding in the ticagrelor group compared to clopidogrel, further complicating the therapeutic decision-making.

This trial highlights the importance of patient selection and risk assessment when utilizing potent antiplatelet agents, particularly in populations undergoing elective revascularization procedures, and reinforces the clinical context in which ticagrelor is applied. Given the observed outcomes, ticagrelor may not offer additional value in this specific cohort, underscoring the need for ongoing evaluation of antiplatelet strategies tailored to individual patient profiles.
