**Rivaroxaban for Prevention of Thrombosis in Patients with Acute Coronary Syndrome**  
This is a Randomized, Double-Blind, Multicenter Phase 2 Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Rivaroxaban (Xarelto) Compared to Standard Antiplatelet Therapy in Patients with Acute Coronary Syndrome.

**Objectives**  
*Primary Objective:* To assess the efficacy of rivaroxaban in reducing major adverse cardiac events (MACE) in patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) compared to standard antiplatelet therapy.  
*Secondary Objectives:* To evaluate the safety profile of rivaroxaban regarding major and minor bleeding events, as well as to analyze the impact of rivaroxaban on platelet aggregation and overall cardiovascular outcomes.

**Design**  
This is a prospective, randomized, double-blind, multicenter phase 2 study in which eligible participants will be randomly assigned to the rivaroxaban group or the control group receiving standard therapy. The study will include an independent data monitoring committee to oversee safety outcomes.

**Number of patients**  
Approximately 500 patients will be recruited and randomly assigned, with 250 patients receiving rivaroxaban and 250 receiving standard antiplatelet therapy.

**Main eligibility criteria**  
All patients aged 40 to 80 years diagnosed with non-ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) or ST elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) receiving percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). 

**Interventions**  
*Experimental:* Rivaroxaban 10 mg orally once daily for 6 months.  
*Control:* Standard antiplatelet therapy (aspirin 81 mg daily + clopidogrel 75 mg daily).

**Outcomes**  
*Primary Outcome:* Composite of MACE including cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, and non-fatal stroke within 180 days following the intervention.  
*Secondary Outcomes:* Each component of the primary outcome plus rates of major and minor bleeding events defined by the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis criteria.

**Background**  
Acute coronary syndrome is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, necessitating effective antithrombotic strategies to minimize thromboembolic complications. Current standard therapy consisting of dual antiplatelet agents has limitations, including significant variability in patient response and a risk of bleeding complications. Rivaroxaban, an oral direct factor Xa inhibitor, may provide a novel alternative by targeting the coagulation pathway more directly and potentially reducing the incidence of thromboembolic events. However, prior studies have raised concerns regarding bleeding risks associated with its use in this patient population.

Building on the existing literature showing potential benefits of rivaroxaban in specific thrombotic scenarios, there remains uncertainty regarding its safety and efficacy in the unique setting of ACS. Following several trials with disappointing results related to rivaroxaban's efficacy and safety in diverse thrombotic disorders, the current study aims to elucidate its role in ACS.

**Methods**  
Following screening and obtaining informed consent, patients fulfilling inclusion criteria will be randomly assigned to the treatment or control group in a 1:1 ratio. Randomization will be performed using a computer-generated sequence. The duration of therapy will be 6 months, with follow-up visits scheduled at 30, 90, and 180 days post-randomization. Laboratory assessments for blood coagulation parameters, platelet function assays, and safety assessments will be performed at each visit.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adult patients aged 40 to 80 years;  
- Diagnosis of non-ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) or ST elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) undergoing PCI;  
- Signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Known history of severe allergic reactions to rivaroxaban or excipients;  
- Contraindications to antiplatelet therapy;  
- Active bleeding disorders or recent bleeding events;  
- Estimated creatinine clearance <30 mL/min;  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**End of the study:**  
The study will conclude at 6 months after the last participant is enrolled, with an interim analysis planned after the 250th patient completes 180 days of follow-up.

**Conclusion:**  
The trial aims to comprehensively evaluate the efficacy and safety of rivaroxaban in a high-risk cohort with acute coronary syndrome undergoing PCI. The results will provide critical insights into the viability of rivaroxaban as a therapeutic option alongside or in place of standard dual antiplatelet therapy. Given the previous failures to demonstrate benefit with the use of direct oral anticoagulants in this context, caution and rigorous oversight of clinical outcomes remain paramount throughout this investigation.
