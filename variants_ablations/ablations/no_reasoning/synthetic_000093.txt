Failed clinical trial of gabapentin: The Efficacy of Gabapentin in the Management of Pain in Post-Herpetic Neuralgia 

The Efficacy of Gabapentin in the Management of Pain in Post-Herpetic Neuralgia

The primary objective of this clinical investigation was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of gabapentin in the management of pain in patients diagnosed with post-herpetic neuralgia (PHN). Furthermore, this study aimed to compare the analgesic outcomes observed in a cohort of Japanese elders with previously established findings in Western populations, thereby assessing any possible need for adaptive dosing adjustments tailored for the Asian demographic.

The study was initiated on January 15, 2010, and included a total of 75 eligible participants. However, due to the emergence of significant adverse events during the treatment phase, including an unexpectedly high incidence of sedation and dizziness, the study was terminated prematurely on June 10, 2011. Despite the known side effects of gabapentin, the severity and frequency observed in this patient cohort prompted the ethical decision to halt further enrollment and intervention in this trial.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Men and women aged 50 years and older.
- Clinical diagnosis of post-herpetic neuralgia documented for at least 3 months.
- Capable of providing written informed consent.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Treatment with gabapentin or other anticonvulsants within the previous 12 weeks.
- History of hypersensitivity reactions to gabapentin or its analogs.
- Significant renal impairment (creatinine clearance <30 mL/min).
- Active psychiatric disorders that are uncontrolled or treated with medications causing potential interactions.
- Coexisting chronic pain conditions that would confound the evaluation of PHN.

Outcome Measures:
The primary outcome measure was the reduction of average pain intensity, quantified via the Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) over the 8-week treatment period. Pain relief was defined as a decrease of at least 30% from baseline values in average daily NRS scores. Secondary outcomes included the recording of tolerability measures, incidence of adverse effects, and quality of life assessments using the Short Form Health Survey (SF-36).

Despite the intention to establish gabapentin as an effective option for alleviating chronic pain associated with post-herpetic neuralgia, the trial results demonstrated insufficient efficacy to justify its use in this specific population. As noted, the adverse events observed were consistently reported in the context of nerve pain syndromes; however, their occurrence at a higher frequency relative to clinical expectations necessitated a reevaluation of the therapeutic protocol. 

Future investigations will be warranted to delineate the risk-benefit ratio of gabapentin in similar cohorts through more robust designs that incorporate strategies for monitoring and mitigating adverse effects.
