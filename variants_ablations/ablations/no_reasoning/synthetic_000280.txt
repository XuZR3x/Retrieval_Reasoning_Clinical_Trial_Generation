Successful clinical trial of sitagliptin: Evaluation of Sitagliptin in Combination With Individualized Diet and Exercise Regimen in Patients With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (MK-0431-512) A Phase III, Multicenter, Randomized, Open-label Clinical Trial to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of Sitagliptin When Combined With Tailored Nutritional and Physical Activity Interventions in Subjects With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of sitagliptin as an adjunct to an individualized diet and exercise intervention for glycemic control in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) who are inadequately managed by lifestyle modifications alone. The primary hypothesis is that participants receiving sitagliptin combined with individualized dietary guidance and a structured exercise program will exhibit a significantly reduced hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) at 24 weeks compared to those receiving dietary and exercise guidance alone. Inclusion Criteria: - Diagnosis of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) - Age ≥ 18 years and ≤ 75 years - HbA1c levels between 7.0% and 10.5% at screening - Participants must have followed a consistent diet/exercise regimen for at least 8 weeks prior to enrollment - Must not have received any pharmacologic treatment for diabetes for at least 12 weeks prior to screening Exclusion Criteria: - History of Type 1 diabetes mellitus - Secondary causes of diabetes (e.g., endocrine disorders) - Ongoing treatment with any oral antihyperglycemic agents other than metformin - Any history of severe hypoglycemia or diabetic ketoacidosis - Significant comorbidities that may interfere with study assessments (e.g., active infections, autoimmune disorders) - Pregnancy, breastfeeding, or planned pregnancy during the study period - Known hypersensitivity to sitagliptin or any of its excipients Change From Baseline in Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) This outcome will be evaluated as the change from baseline to week 24, defined as the difference in HbA1c percentage from baseline and after 24 weeks of intervention. This change will be modeled using an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) with terms for treatment, baseline HbA1c, and other relevant covariates. Continuous glucose monitoring data will also be collected to corroborate the findings regarding glycemic fluctuations. Secondary outcomes include the assessment of fasting plasma glucose (FPG), weight change, incidence of hypoglycemia, and overall treatment satisfaction as measured by participant-reported outcome questionnaires. This rigorous approach is expected to provide critical insights into the role of pharmacological intervention coupled with lifestyle modifications in optimizing glycemic control in patients with T2DM.
