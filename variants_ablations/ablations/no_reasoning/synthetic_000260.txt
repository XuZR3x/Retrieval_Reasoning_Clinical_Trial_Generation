**Title:** Efficacy of Aprepitant in Combination with Ondansetron for the Prevention of Nausea and Vomiting in Adult Patients Undergoing Chemotherapy: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Abstract:** This study evaluates the efficacy and safety of aprepitant, when combined with ondansetron, in preventing chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV) in adult patients receiving moderately to highly emetogenic chemotherapy regimens. Despite the availability of several anti-emetic agents, CINV remains a significant challenge, affecting patient quality of life and adherence to treatment protocols. The current clinical trial addresses this gap by adding the neurokinin-1 receptor antagonist aprepitant to the standard prophylactic regimen. 

**Objectives:** 
- Primary: To determine the efficacy of aprepitant in reducing the incidence of both acute and delayed nausea and vomiting compared to standard ondansetron therapy alone.
- Secondary: To assess the overall impact of the aprepitant-ondansetron regimen on patients' quality of life and the safety profile of combined anti-emetic therapy.

**Study Design:** 
A multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial will be conducted across various institutions to ensure a heterogeneous population. Patients will be randomly assigned to receive either aprepitant combined with ondansetron (experimental group) or ondansetron plus placebo (control group).

**Population:** 
Adult patients aged 18-65 years, diagnosed with malignancies requiring chemotherapy regimens categorized as moderately or highly emetogenic, are eligible for enrollment. Specific chemotherapy protocols will include (e.g., Cisplatin, Doxorubicin, Cyclophosphamide, and 5-Fluorouracil).

**Sampling Technique:** 
Patients fulfilling the inclusion criteria will be identified, and informed consent will be secured. Using computer-generated randomization, eligible participants will be assigned, with the investigator and subjects unaware of the assigned groups until study completion.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults (18-65 years) with a confirmed diagnosis of malignancy.
- Scheduled to receive chemotherapy classified as moderately to highly emetogenic.
- Karnofsky Performance Status of ≥60.
- Adequate bone marrow function defined by hemoglobin ≥10 g/dL, ANC >1500/mm3, and platelets >100,000/mm3.
- Written informed consent obtained from participants.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of hypersensitivity to aprepitant or ondansetron.
- Concurrent use of prophylactic anti-emetics other than those studied (ondansetron).
- Significant organ dysfunction or uncontrolled medical conditions.
- Pregnant or nursing women, or women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.
- Participation in another clinical trial involving investigational agents within 30 days prior to the study.

**Data Collection Procedures:**
- Patients will receive ondansetron intravenously or orally before chemotherapy on Day 1 and then aprepitant orally for three consecutive days. 
- Nausea and vomiting will be assessed using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) scale, and adjunctive anti-emetics usage will be documented.
- Quality of life will be evaluated using the Functional Living Index - Emesis (FLIE) scale before chemotherapy and at follow-up visits on Days 7 and 14.

**Outcome Measures:**
- Primary outcomes will focus on the proportion of patients experiencing control of acute vomiting (24 hours post-chemotherapy) and delayed vomiting (Days 2-5 post-chemotherapy) with no need for rescue anti-emetics.
- Secondary endpoints will evaluate the overall satisfaction with anti-emetic management and any adverse effects experienced during the treatment period.

**Projected Accrual:**
A total of 150 participants will be recruited over a 12-month period. Enrollment will conclude once the target number is reached, or upon achieving statistical significance in primary outcomes.

**Data Analysis:**
Fisher’s exact test and Chi-square tests will be employed to compare categorical variables. Continuous variables will be analyzed using t-tests, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

In conclusion, this clinical trial will provide critical insights into the role of aprepitant as a cornerstone in anti-emetic therapy for adult chemotherapy patients, with the aim to enhance therapeutic efficacy and improve patient quality of life during chemotherapy interventions.
