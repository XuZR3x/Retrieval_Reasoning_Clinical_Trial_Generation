**A Phase III Study of Cytarabine and Venetoclax in Combination for Treatment of Older Adults with Newly Diagnosed Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

**RATIONALE:** Chemotherapeutic agents like cytarabine exert their antineoplastic effects through mechanisms that inhibit DNA synthesis and cell proliferation, effectively targeting rapidly dividing cancer cells. The BCL-2 inhibitor venetoclax has demonstrated its capacity to synergize with cytarabine, potentially enhancing the apoptotic response in acute myeloid leukemia (AML) cells that are often reliant on this anti-apoptotic pathway for survival. This study evaluates the efficacy and tolerability of the combination of cytarabine and venetoclax in an elderly population afflicted by de novo AML.

**PURPOSE:** The primary aim of this randomized phase III trial is to compare the complete response rate between older adults with newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukemia treated with cytarabine alone versus cytarabine in conjunction with venetoclax. Secondary endpoints include assessment of overall survival, event-free survival, toxicity profiles, and correlation of baseline patient factors with treatment outcomes.

**OBJECTIVES:**

- **Primary:** To determine and compare the complete remission rate in elderly patients with newly diagnosed AML receiving cytarabine monotherapy versus those receiving a combination of cytarabine and venetoclax.

- **Secondary:**
  - To compare overall survival between both treatment arms.
  - To assess event-free survival in patients receiving the two regimens.
  - To evaluate the toxicity associated with cytarabine alone versus cytarabine plus venetoclax.
  - To analyze potential prognostic factors (e.g., genetic mutations, expression of BCL-2 family proteins) affecting the treatment response.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, randomized study will enroll patients aged 65 years and older with a confirmed diagnosis of de novo AML. Participants will be randomly assigned to one of two treatment arms:

- **Arm I:** Patients will receive cytarabine administered intravenously (IV) over 7 days.
  
- **Arm II:** Patients will receive cytarabine as in Arm I plus daily oral venetoclax for the duration of the cytarabine administration, followed by maintenance therapy with venetoclax alone contingent upon remission status.

Following induction therapy, patients will be monitored for remission status and will receive follow-up assessments at regular intervals over a 5-year period to record relevant clinical data and long-term outcomes.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 750 patients will be recruited for the study over a period of 5 years.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**

- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of acute myeloid leukemia (not defined by the presence of acute promyelocytic leukemia).
- No preceding therapies for AML are permitted.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**

- Age ≥ 65 years.
- Performance status: WHO score of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function:
  - Hematologic: A ≥ 5,000/μl white blood cell count, without severe cytopenias.
  - Hepatic: Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) and AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 times ULN.
  - Renal: Creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**

- No prior cytotoxic chemotherapy or targeted therapy for AML.
- No use of myelosuppressive agents within 4 weeks prior to enrollment is permitted.
  
This study is expected to provide critical insight into the efficacy of combination therapy with cytarabine and venetoclax, potentially redefining the therapeutic landscape for older patients suffering from AML.
