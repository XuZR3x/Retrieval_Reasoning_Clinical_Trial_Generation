**Failed Clinical Trial of Laboratory Biomarker Analysis: Lacosamide in Treating Patients With Refractory Epilepsy**  
**Phase II Study of Lacosamide in Patients With Refractory Epilepsy**  

This phase II trial examines the efficacy and safety of lacosamide in treating patients with refractory epilepsy. Lacosamide is believed to work by selectively enhancing slow inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels, potentially leading to a reduction in neuronal excitability. 

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To estimate the proportion of patients who achieve seizure freedom for at least 6 months while receiving lacosamide, compared to a control group receiving placebo.  
II. To evaluate the clinical toxicities associated with lacosamide treatment in this patient population.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To assess the reduction in seizure frequency in patients receiving lacosamide versus placebo.  
II. To determine overall quality of life improvements in patients undergoing lacosamide treatment.  
III. To explore the association between biomarker profiles (as assessed by genomic and proteomic analyses) and therapeutic response to lacosamide.  
IV. To collect pharmacokinetic data related to lacosamide concentrations and assess their impact on clinical outcomes.  
V. To investigate any correlation between baseline clinical characteristics and treatment response, including age, sex, and genetic markers.

**OUTLINE:**  
This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study. Patients are administered either lacosamide (starting dose 100 mg/day, titrated up to 600 mg/day) or matching placebo for 24 weeks. Following randomization, patients are monitored for seizure reduction and adverse effects with follow-up evaluations every 8 weeks. After completion of the treatment period, patients remain in long-term follow-up for additional data collection on seizure status and adverse effects.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adults aged 18-65 years with a diagnosis of refractory epilepsy, defined as failure of at least two tolerated antiepileptic drugs.  
- Documented history of recurrent seizure episodes within the past year.  
- Patients must demonstrate a stable regimen of antiepileptic medications for at least 4 weeks prior to enrollment.  
- Baseline seizure frequency must be documented with at least 4 seizures in the previous month.  
- Patients must have a signed informed consent prior to participation.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Any history of hypersensitivity to lacosamide or its components.  
- Patients with a history of significant cardiac arrhythmias or untreated hypertension.  
- Concomitant use of strong CYP450 enzyme inducers or inhibitors, as they may interfere with lacosamide metabolism.  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women, or those not utilizing effective contraception.  
- Patients with significant comorbid conditions, such as active malignancy or severe psychiatric disorders, that might impede compliance with study requirements.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
The primary endpoint is the proportion of patients who attain seizure freedom (defined as 12 consecutive weeks without seizures) at the conclusion of the 24-week treatment period, with further evaluations based on responsiveness in biomarker analyses.

**INTERIM ANALYSIS:**  
An interim analysis was scheduled at 50% enrollment to assess safety signals and early efficacy. 

**RESULTS:**  
The trial was terminated early due to a lack of statistical significance in the primary endpoint, with only 10% of patients achieving seizure freedom compared to 8% in the placebo group. Notably, the expected correlation between various laboratory biomarkers and therapeutic outcomes was not observed. Adverse events were reported in a similar proportion across both lacosamide and placebo cohorts, primarily consisting of headaches and dizziness.

Considered collectively, these findings underscore the limited potential of lacosamide as a monotherapy in this refractory population and highlight significant challenges in the identification of predictive biomarkers for treatment response in epilepsy. Further studies assessing drug interactions and biomarker utility for patient stratification are warranted to enhance therapeutic precision.
