**Successful Clinical Trial of Insulin: Evaluation of Alternative Dosing Regimens for Optimizing Glycemic Control in Individuals with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus**

**Title:** Comparative Study on Pre-meal Insulin Regimen Versus Basal Insulin Therapy for Reducing Glycemic Variability in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy of an alternative pre-meal insulin regimen compared to a standard basal insulin alone in reducing postprandial glycemic variability in patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM). The secondary objective is to evaluate the impact of these regimens on the levels of fasting glucose.

Following institutional review board approval at the University of Diabetes Research Center, 50 subjects (25 males, 25 females) diagnosed with T2DM were recruited for this open-label, randomized, parallel-group study. Inclusion criteria mandated participants to be aged between 30 and 70 years, with a documented diagnosis of T2DM for a minimum of two years, and an HbA1c level between 7.0% and 10.0%. 

**Methods:** Participants were stratified into two treatment groups: Group A received a premixed insulin analog (insulin aspart 70/30) administered 30 minutes before meals, while Group B continued their baseline insulin regimen comprising exclusively of long-acting insulin (insulin glargine). All participants maintained a stable diet and exercise routine throughout the trial period, which lasted for three months. 

At baseline and the study's conclusion, the glycemic variability was quantified using continuous glucose monitoring (CGM). Participants were instructed to maintain a food diary to standardize carbohydrate intake throughout the trial duration. Fasting plasma glucose and 2-hour postprandial glucose excursions were measured at specified intervals using standardized laboratory methods.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Clinical diagnosis of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus
- Age between 30 and 70 years
- Treated with either basal insulin or oral hypoglycemic agents
- HbA1c between 7.0% and 10.0%
- No history of severe hypoglycemia in the past year
- No significant comorbidities affecting glycemic control

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previously participated in another interventional study within the last three months
- Current treatment with GLP-1 receptor agonists or SGLT2 inhibitors
- Significant renal impairment (eGFR < 45 mL/min)
- Pregnant or breastfeeding females

**Outcomes:** The primary endpoint was the mean glycemic variability, assessed through flat data from the CGM sensors deployed during the study period. The secondary endpoints included comparisons of fasting glucose levels and the proportion of patients achieving target glycemic goals (defined as HbA1c < 7.0% by study conclusion).

**Statistical Analysis:** Continuous variables were analyzed using ANOVA while categorical data were assessed with Chi-square tests. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

**Results:** Data analysis revealed that participants in Group A exhibited a statistically significant reduction in glycemic variability as reflected by a 25% decrease in the calculated coefficient of variation of glucose readings when compared to Group B. Furthermore, there was notable improvement in mean fasting glucose levels in Group A by the end of the study.

**Conclusion:** The intervention demonstrated that pre-meal administration of rapid-acting insulin alongside dietary regulation effectively decreased glycemic variability and enhanced overall glycemic control in patients suffering from Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus. This study provides a compelling argument for considering alternative insulin dosing regimens in clinical practice, validating the hypothesis that timely insulin administration can result in significant metabolic benefits.

**Ethics:** The study was conducted in full compliance with ethical standards and all participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment. 

**Keywords:** Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, Insulin, Glycemic Variability, Premeal Insulin Administration, Randomized Clinical Trial.
