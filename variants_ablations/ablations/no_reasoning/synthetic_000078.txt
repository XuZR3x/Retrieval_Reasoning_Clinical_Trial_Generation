**Successful clinical trial of atomoxetine: Atomoxetine for the Treatment of Behavioral Symptoms in Patients with Autistic Spectrum Disorder (ASD)**  

A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Atomoxetine in Treating Behavioral Symptoms of Autism Spectrum Disorder in Adolescents  

The objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of atomoxetine in alleviating behavioral symptoms associated with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) in adolescent patients aged 12 to 18 years. This trial assesses the impact of atomoxetine on the core symptoms of ASD, including inattentiveness, hyperactivity, and impulsivity, as well as on associated behavioral problems such as irritability, aggression, and anxiety.  

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Male or female adolescents aged 12 to 18 years  
- Diagnosis of Autism Spectrum Disorder as per DSM-5 criteria  
- Having a Clinical Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I) score of ≥ 4 (moderate symptoms) at baseline  
- Body weight ≥ 30 kg and ≤ 85 kg  
- Written informed consent from a parent or guardian, and assent from the patient  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Current or historical diagnosis of bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, or any psychotic disorder  
- Major depressive episode as per DSM-5, including history of significant suicidal ideation or attempt within the past year  
- Current use of medications that may interact with atomoxetine, such as MAO inhibitors or certain antidepressants  
- A history of severe allergies to atomoxetine or any of its constituents  
- Known cardiovascular conditions or significant cardiac abnormalities  
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding in female participants, or failure to use medically acceptable contraception  

The study consists of a treatment phase of 12 weeks, during which subjects will be randomly assigned to receive either atomoxetine at flexible doses up to a maximum of 100 mg/day or placebo. The primary endpoint is the change in the Aberrant Behavior Checklist (ABC) scores, which will be assessed at baseline and after 12 weeks of treatment. Secondary endpoints include changes in CGI-I scores, as well as assessments of sleep quality, anxiety, and family impact through validated scales.  

Participants will undergo comprehensive psychiatric evaluations at baseline, Week 6, and Week 12. Furthermore, safety monitoring will be conducted throughout the trial to identify any adverse events leading to the discontinuation of therapy. The impact of atomoxetine on family dynamics and caregiver burden will also be assessed via standardized questionnaires.  

This clinical trial aims to determine the effectiveness of atomoxetine in managing behavioral symptoms amongst adolescents with ASD, thereby contributing to the optimization of therapeutic strategies and improving overall quality of life for affected individuals and their families.
