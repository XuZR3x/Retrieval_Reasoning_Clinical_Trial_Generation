**Successful Clinical Trial of SR58611A: An Eight-Week Study Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of SR58611A in Patients With Social Anxiety Disorder**

**A Multicenter, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of SR58611A (350 mg q12) Compared to Placebo in Outpatients With Social Anxiety Disorder**

The primary objective of this study is to investigate the efficacy and safety of SR58611A in patients diagnosed with Social Anxiety Disorder (SAD). The design of this trial is structured to ensure robust data collection and analysis, wherein both clinical efficacy and tolerability will be closely monitored.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Outpatients aged between 18 and 65 years.
- Patients diagnosed with Social Anxiety Disorder according to DSM-IV-TR criteria, confirmed utilizing the Mini International Neuropsychiatric Interview (MINI) with Social Anxiety Disorder module.
- A baseline total score of 20 or higher on the Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale (LSAS).
- Subjects must have voluntarily provided their written informed consent and be capable of adhering to the study protocol.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with a concurrent diagnosis of Major Depressive Disorder or any other anxiety disorder, as verified by the MINI.
- A total score of 18 or higher on the Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) at screening or baseline.
- Individuals at moderate to high risk for suicide, as deemed by the Investigator.
- Any history of bipolar disorder, psychotic disorders, or personality disorders as per the criteria of the MINI.
- Severe or unstable comorbid medical conditions judged by the Investigator may contraindicate trial participation.
- Pregnant or lactating females, and females of childbearing potential not employing effective contraception throughout the study duration.
- Individuals with a positive urine drug screen for illicit substances.
- Participation in a clinical trial involving an experimental therapy within the last three months prior to screening.

The primary outcome measure for this study is the change in the LSAS total score from baseline to Day 56 post-treatment initiation. Key secondary endpoints include the evaluation of changes in the Clinical Global Impression-Severity (CGI-S) score, the percentage of patients achieving substantial clinical improvement on the LSAS, and the assessment of safety through recorded adverse events and tolerability.

**Study Design:**
The trial adopts a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel-group design across multiple clinical centers. Eligible participants will be randomly assigned to receive either SR58611A (350 mg q12) or a placebo for a total duration of eight weeks. The screening phase includes a one-week lead-in period where baseline assessments will be conducted.

Regular follow-ups will be implemented to monitor adverse events and treatment adherence throughout the study. Data will be compiled to ensure a comprehensive understanding of the impact of SR58611A on social anxiety, and findings will be subject to relevant statistical analysis to ascertain significance.

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint focuses on assessing change in LSAS total score, with the principal analysis evaluating the differences between SR58611A and placebo groups at Week 8. Secondary endpoints will also include variations in anxiety-related quality of life measures and overall safety evaluations.

This investigation aims to transcend traditional treatment modalities currently employed for Social Anxiety Disorder and provide a well-documented efficacy profile for SR58611A, thereby contributing significant clinical insights and potential therapeutic pathways for patients experiencing this debilitating condition. 

Ultimately, this study is anticipated to reinforce the clinical utility of SR58611A, advancing not only pharmacological treatment strategies but potentially enhancing the quality of life for individuals afflicted by Social Anxiety Disorder.
