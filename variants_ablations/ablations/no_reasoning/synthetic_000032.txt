**Successful Clinical Trial of Vildagliptin: Efficacy of Vildagliptin in Reducing Postprandial Hyperglycemia in Patients With Type 2 Diabetes: A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind, Parallel-group Study**

**Study Objectives:**
This clinical trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy of vildagliptin (50 mg once daily) compared to placebo in reducing postprandial hyperglycemia in patients with inadequately controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). The study focuses on patients exhibiting substantial postprandial glucose excursions, which may contribute to overall glycemic control deterioration and complicate diabetes management.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients (aged 18-75 years) with a confirmed diagnosis of T2DM.
- Participants exhibiting an HbA1c level between 7.0% and 9.0% (inclusive) at screening.
- Documented history of inadequate glycemic control, defined by fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL and significant postprandial glucose (PPG) ≥ 180 mg/dL following a standard meal.
- Individuals who have been on a stable dose of metformin (≥ 1500 mg/day) for at least three months prior to enrollment.
- Willingness to maintain dietary and exercise habits during the trial period.
- Informed consent obtained prior to any study-related procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of type 1 diabetes, secondary diabetes, or any diabetes-related acute complications within the past year.
- Significant renal impairment (eGFR < 60 mL/min) or any acute metabolic diabetic complications, including diabetic ketoacidosis.
- Previous use of any oral anti-diabetic agents other than metformin within the last three months before randomization.
- Pregnancy, lactation, or chance of becoming pregnant during the study.
- History of clinically significant hepatic or cardiovascular conditions.

**Methods:**
The study was conducted over a 24-week period in multiple centers across diverse locations. Following the screening phase, participants were randomized to receive either vildagliptin (50 mg once daily) or matching placebo. Primary efficacy endpoints included changes in postprandial glucose levels from baseline at 2 hours following a standardized meal. Secondary endpoints encompassed the change in HbA1c levels, incidence of adverse events, and overall safety assessments.

**Outcome Measures:**
The primary outcome of the study was the absolute change in postprandial glucose from baseline to 24 weeks. Secondary outcome measures included changes in HbA1c from baseline, the frequency and severity of adverse events, and laboratory evaluations monitoring liver and kidney function.

**Results:**
The clinical trial successfully demonstrated that vildagliptin significantly reduced postprandial glucose levels compared to placebo (p < 0.001). Concurrently, a notable reduction in HbA1c levels was observed, with a greater percentage of participants achieving the HbA1c target of <7.0%. Adverse event profiles were consistent with previous findings, indicating that vildagliptin was well-tolerated among participants, with no substantial safety concerns reported over the duration of the study.

**Conclusion:**
This trial substantiates the efficacy and safety of vildagliptin as an adjunct therapy in patients with T2DM, particularly those struggling with postprandial hyperglycemia. The findings support its implementation in early therapeutic intervention strategies aimed at improving glycemic control.

**Keywords:** Vildagliptin, type 2 diabetes, postprandial hyperglycemia, clinical trial.
