**Failed Clinical Trial of Cytarabine: Combination of Cytarabine, Venetoclax and Low-Dose Dexamethasone in Patients With Relapsed Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL)**

**A Phase II Study of Cytarabine Combined With Venetoclax and Low-Dose Dexamethasone in Patients With Relapsed Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia** 

This is a single-arm, open-label Phase II clinical trial investigating the efficacy and safety of cytarabine in combination with venetoclax and low-dose dexamethasone in patients diagnosed with relapsed acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL). Given the pivotal involvement of anti-apoptotic BCL-2 proteins in the pathogenesis of ALL, the combination aims to enhance cytotoxicity of cytarabine by concurrently inhibiting BCL-2, potentially resulting in improved clinical outcomes. Previous studies have demonstrated the role of venetoclax in augmenting the effectiveness of standard chemotherapeutic agents; however, this combination's evaluation in ALL, particularly with cytarabine, remains inadequately explored. 

**Treatment Regimen:** Cytarabine will be administered intravenously at a dose of 100 mg/m² daily for 5 consecutive days during each treatment cycle, combined with oral venetoclax starting at 400 mg/day for 28 consecutive days, alongside low-dose dexamethasone (10 mg orally daily) for the duration of the 28-day cycle. The treatment will continue for a maximum of 6 cycles unless there is evidence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years and older with a confirmed diagnosis of relapsed or refractory ALL, defined by the presence of ≥5% blasts in peripheral blood or bone marrow.
- Participants must have received at least one prior line of therapy for ALL and have experienced relapse or progression post-treatment.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of ≤ 2 with adequate organ function, specifically:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,000/mm³
  - Platelet count ≥ 50,000/mm³
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 x upper limit of normal (ULN)
  - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with venetoclax or any agents targeting BCL-2 family proteins within 30 days prior to enrollment.
- Active central nervous system involvement by leukemia.
- Patients with secondary malignancies, other than localized skin cancer, will be excluded.
- Significant cardiovascular disease, including congestive heart failure (New York Heart Association Class III or IV).
- Pregnant or lactating women or women of childbearing potential not using adequate contraception.

**Primary Objective:**
- To evaluate the overall response rate (complete remission [CR] + complete remission with incomplete count recovery [CRi]) of cytarabine in combination with venetoclax and low-dose dexamethasone in relapsed ALL.

**Secondary Objectives:**
- To assess overall survival, duration of response, and disease-free survival in patients receiving the combination therapy.

**Outcome Measures:**
- The primary endpoint will be overall response rate, defined as the percentage of patients achieving CR/CRi after 2 cycles of treatment.
- Secondary outcome measures include survival rates at 6 and 12 months post-therapy initiation.

**Statistical Analysis:**
- Descriptive statistics will be used to summarize patient characteristics and responses. The percentage of patients achieving remission will be presented with 95% confidence intervals. Overall survival will be evaluated using the Kaplan-Meier method.

**Clinical Trial Failure:**
Despite the initial hypothesis that targeting BCL-2 together with cytotoxic chemotherapy could yield synergistic effects in relapsed ALL, the study was terminated early due to suboptimal overall response rates, defined as less than 20% complete response after 2 cycles, along with an unexpectedly high frequency of severe adverse events, notably pneumonia and neutropenic fever, leading to increased mortality within the treatment cohort. Comprehensive evaluations revealed the limited benefit of this combination in a heavily pre-treated population and highlighted the inherent chemosensitivity challenges associated with relapsed ALL. 

**Conclusion:**
The trial underscored the complexity of managing relapsed ALL and the necessity for novel, targeted approaches in combination with traditional chemotherapy agents, necessitating further exploration of alternative treatment regimens that could effectively address the unique biological etiology of this disease.
