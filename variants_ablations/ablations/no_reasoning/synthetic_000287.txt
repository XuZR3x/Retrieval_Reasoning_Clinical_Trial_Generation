**Failed Clinical Trial of Abemaciclib: A Study of Abemaciclib (LY2835219) in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Urothelial Carcinoma**

**JUNIPER-UC: A Randomized Phase 3 Study of Abemaciclib Plus Pembrolizumab Versus Pembrolizumab Alone in Participants with Advanced Urothelial Carcinoma Who Have Progressed After Prior Chemotherapy**

The primary objective of this trial is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of abemaciclib in combination with pembrolizumab compared to pembrolizumab monotherapy in patients diagnosed with advanced urothelial carcinoma who have experienced disease progression following one or two lines of platinum-based chemotherapy.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced urothelial carcinoma.
- Documented progression of disease following one or two lines of platinum-based chemotherapy.
- Prior treatment with either immune checkpoint inhibitors or other targeted therapies is permitted, provided progression is documented.
- Measurable disease per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST 1.1).
- An Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 0 to 1.
- Availability of adequate formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue for biomarker analysis.
- Adequate hematological, renal, and liver function as defined by standard laboratory criteria.
- Females of child-bearing potential must have a negative serum pregnancy test ≤ 7 days prior to initiation of treatment and agree to use effective contraception.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment with any cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) 4 and 6 inhibitors.
- Evidence of central nervous system metastases that are symptomatic or unstable.
- Active autoimmune disease that requires systemic treatment, or history of severe adverse reaction to pembrolizumab or any monoclonal antibody.
- Significant cardiovascular disease or history of complications associated with previous chemotherapy.
- Concurrent treatment with other investigational agents or anticancer therapies.

**Progression Free Survival (PFS)**
PFS is defined as the time from randomization to the date of investigator-assessed disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurs first. Progressive disease is characterized by at least a 20% increase in the sum of the longest diameters of target lesions, based on the smallest sum observed during the study, or the emergence of new lesions.

The study did not meet its primary endpoint, demonstrating inadequate prolongation of PFS when abemaciclib was added to pembrolizumab compared to pembrolizumab alone. Despite the initial rationale based on preclinical data suggesting potential synergistic effects between abemaciclib and immunotherapy, no statistically significant improvement in PFS (median PFS of 4.5 months vs 4.2 months) was observed.

Concomitantly, adverse events related to the combined treatment regimen rendered the study unsustainable, leading to early termination. A higher incidence of gastrointestinal toxicities, hematological abnormalities, and immune-related adverse events was reported among the abemaciclib combination group, which further corroborated the challenges of combining CDK4/6 inhibitors with immune checkpoint blockade in this cohort.

Considering these findings, the application of abemaciclib in tandem with pembrolizumab does not warrant further investigation for the treatment of advanced urothelial carcinoma under these clinical parameters. Future studies may consider alternative regimens or dosing schedules to elucidate any remaining potential of abemaciclib in combination with other agents in the urothelial carcinoma treatment landscape.
