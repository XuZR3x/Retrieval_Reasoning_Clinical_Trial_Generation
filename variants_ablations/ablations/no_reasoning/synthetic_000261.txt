**Successful Clinical Trial of Lidocaine: Evaluation of Lidocaine Nasal Gel for Post-Surgical Pain Management in Patients Undergoing Septoplasty**

**Evaluation of Lidocaine Nasal Gel for Post-Surgical Pain Management in Patients Undergoing Septoplasty** 

This study aimed to investigate the effectiveness of lidocaine nasal gel in alleviating post-operative pain in patients who underwent septoplasty. Previous research indicated that local anesthetic agents exhibit significant analgesic effects in various surgical settings, which prompted this exploration into topical lidocaine formulations.

The clinical trial was designed as a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. A total of 120 subjects, ages 18-65, scheduled for elective septoplasty were enrolled and randomized into two groups: one receiving lidocaine nasal gel (5% concentration) and a placebo group receiving a similar gel without active ingredients. The primary outcome was the pain intensity measured by the Numerical Pain Rating Scale (NRS) at 24 hours post-surgery.

Prior to the surgical intervention, subjects underwent a thorough preoperative assessment, ensuring they met the inclusion criteria, which included: being male or female, over the age of 18, requiring septoplasty, and providing informed consent. Exclusion criteria were in place to rule out patients with contraindications to lidocaine, severe renal impairment, or a history of allergic reactions to anesthetic agents.

Lidocaine nasal gel was administered immediately before the surgical incision and re-applied in the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) at the 30-minute mark by the attending clinician, ensuring a consistent delivery of the active compound. The control group received a matching placebo formulation that lacked lidocaine but was otherwise identical in appearance and method of application.

Post-operative pain levels were systematically evaluated at baseline, 1, 6, and 24 hours after surgery, alongside secondary outcomes including the incidence of nausea, vomiting, and patient satisfaction based on a tailored postoperative questionnaire focused on recovery quality.

Statistical analyses revealed that patients in the lidocaine group exhibited a statistically significant reduction in pain scores at the 24-hour mark compared to the placebo group (mean NRS score decrease of 40% from baseline versus a mere 10% in the control group), with clinical relevance confirmed through a greater than 30% reduction in pain intensity perceived by individuals in the active treatment group.

Furthermore, secondary outcomes associated with lidocaine use demonstrated promising results, with fewer instances of postoperative nausea and vomiting noted in the lidocaine group, thereby contributing to a more favorable recovery experience. Patient satisfaction scores were notably higher, correlating with lower pain intensity experienced in the initial postoperative period.

In conclusion, the clinical evidence generated from this trial supports the hypothesis that lidocaine nasal gel is effective in reducing postoperative pain following septoplasty, representing a viable non-opioid analgesic option in clinical practice. The implementation of this intervention could potentially enhance patient comfort and accelerate recovery, minimizing the reliance on systemic opioids and their associated side effects. Further research may expand on these findings across various surgical contexts and evaluate long-term outcomes associated with this innovative analgesic approach. 

### Inclusion Criteria:
1. Male or Female, aged 18-65 years.
2. Scheduled for elective septoplasty.
3. Informed consent obtained.

### Exclusion Criteria:
1. History of allergic reactions to local anesthetics.
2. Severe renal impairment.
3. Active nasal infection or severe nasal obstruction.
4. Pregnant or lactating females.
5. Current use of anticoagulants or antiplatelet therapy.

### Pain Intensity Measurement:
The primary endpoint involved measuring patient-reported pain intensity using the NRS, where 0 indicated "no pain" and 10 indicated "the worst pain imaginable". A reduction in NRS scores of greater than 30% at 24 hours was considered clinically significant.

The findings from this trial provide a rigorous assessment of lidocaine nasal gel's role in postoperative pain management, paving the way for its broader application in surgical analgesia. Further studies could focus on optimizing dosing regimens and expanding the scope of its application in varied clinical populations.
