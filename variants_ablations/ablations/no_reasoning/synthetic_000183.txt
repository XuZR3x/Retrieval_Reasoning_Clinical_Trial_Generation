<clinical_trial>
    <title> RISE Study: Rosuvastatin Intervention for Reduction of Inflammatory Biomarkers in Chronic Kidney Disease Patients </title>
    
    <objective>
        This Phase 2 clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of rosuvastatin in reducing inflammatory biomarkers and improving cardiovascular risk profile in patients with stage 3 to 4 chronic kidney disease (CKD). Given the established link between CKD, inflammation, and increased cardiovascular risk, this study seeks to determine whether rosuvastatin can confer additional benefits beyond lipid-lowering in this particular patient population.
    </objective>
    
    <background>
        Chronic kidney disease is associated with heightened systemic inflammation and an increased incidence of cardiovascular events, attributed in part to dyslipidemia. Recent findings indicate that statins, including rosuvastatin, may exert anti-inflammatory effects independent of their lipid-lowering capacity, thereby potentially mitigating cardiovascular risk in CKD patients. The RISE study hypothesizes that rosuvastatin treatment will lead to a statistically significant reduction in inflammatory biomarkers such as C-reactive protein (CRP) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), while concurrently improving lipid profiles.
    </background>
    
    <methodology>
        The study will enroll patients aged 50-80 years with stage 3 or 4 CKD, characterized by an eGFR between 15 and 59 mL/min/1.73m². Participants will be randomized in a double-blind fashion to receive either rosuvastatin 20 mg once daily or placebo for a period of 24 weeks. Primary endpoints will include changes in high-sensitivity CRP and IL-6 levels from baseline to 24 weeks. Secondary endpoints will assess changes in lipid profiles, including low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), as well as adverse effects related to treatment.
    </methodology>
    
    <inclusion_criteria>
        - Males and females aged 50 to 80 years old.
        - Diagnosed with stage 3 or 4 chronic kidney disease.
        - Serum LDL-C > 130 mg/dL.
        - Providing written informed consent.
    </inclusion_criteria>
    
    <exclusion_criteria>
        - Individuals with acute coronary syndrome within the past 6 months.
        - History of statin intolerance or severe myopathy.
        - Active liver disease, defined as transaminases greater than three times the upper limit of normal.
        - Individuals currently undergoing dialysis or having a history of kidney transplant.
        - Patients participating in other investigational studies.
    </exclusion_criteria>
    
    <primary_outcome>
        - Percent change in high-sensitivity CRP levels from baseline to 24 weeks.
    </primary_outcome>
    
    <secondary_outcome>
        - Percent change in IL-6 levels from baseline to 24 weeks.
        - Change in LDL-C and HDL-C levels from baseline to 24 weeks.
        - Incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events during and post-treatment.
    </secondary_outcome>

    <results>
        The RISE study successfully demonstrated that rosuvastatin significantly reduced high-sensitivity CRP levels by an average of 30.4% from baseline to 24 weeks compared to the placebo group (p < 0.001). Additionally, IL-6 levels decreased by 25.2% in the treatment group versus 2.5% in the placebo group (p < 0.001). Improvements in lipid parameters were observed, including a mean reduction of 36% in LDL-C levels. The treatment was found to be well-tolerated with a low incidence of adverse events, consistent with prior studies of rosuvastatin.
    </results>
    
    <conclusion>
        The RISE study provides robust evidence supporting the potential of rosuvastatin to reduce inflammatory biomarkers in patients with chronic kidney disease. This finding underscores the drug’s additional cardiovascular protective effects beyond its lipid-lowering capabilities, indicating its potential role in the comprehensive management of CKD patients.
    </conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
