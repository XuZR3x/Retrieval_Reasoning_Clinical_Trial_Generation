**Failed clinical trial of telmisartan: Telmisartan in Managing Inflammatory Markers in Obese Patients at Risk of Diabetes (TOMORROW Study)**

**Background:** The increasing prevalence of obesity and its association with chronic low-grade inflammation and insulin resistance constitutes a significant challenge in the management of concomitant metabolic disorders, particularly those leading to type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). Telmisartan, an angiotensin II type 1 receptor antagonist, has exhibited potential benefits in modulating metabolic profiles, including reduction of blood pressure and improvement in certain inflammatory markers. This study aimed to investigate the effects of telmisartan on inflammatory markers and insulin sensitivity in obese patients at risk for T2DM, in conjunction with a controlled diet and lifestyle modification plan.

**Aims:** The primary objective was to assess the impact of 12 weeks of telmisartan treatment on inflammatory markers (C-reactive protein, interleukin-6) and insulin sensitivity as measured by the Homeostasis Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR) in obese individuals. Secondary objectives included evaluating changes in body weight, waist circumference, and quality of life metrics over the study period.

**Design:** This was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted across two centers in the United States. A total of 220 participants aged 18-65 years, with a body mass index (BMI) of 30-40 kg/m² and having at least one additional cardiovascular risk factor, were recruited. Participants were randomized (1:1) to receive either telmisartan 80 mg daily or matching placebo for a duration of 12 weeks, alongside a structured intervention comprising dietary guidance and physical activity encouragement.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Provision of informed consent.
- Age ≥ 18 years and < 65 years.
- BMI between 30-40 kg/m².
- At least one cardiovascular risk factor (e.g., hypertension, dyslipidemia, family history of diabetes).
- No prior use of angiotensin receptor blockers or any intervention affecting insulin sensitivity within the last three months.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous diagnosis of diabetes mellitus or undergoing treatment for T2DM.
- Presence of chronic inflammatory diseases (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis).
- Significant liver or renal dysfunction (serum creatinine >1.5 mg/dL).
- History of myocardial infarction or stroke in the last 6 months.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding status.
- Known sensitivity or contraindication to telmisartan.

**Treatment Protocol:** Following the screening and baseline visit, patients undergo randomization into the telmisartan or placebo group. Treatment was administered in a controlled environment, with bi-weekly follow-up to monitor adherence, side effects, and lifestyle modifications. Blood samples were collected at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks for the quantitative assessment of inflammatory markers and glucose metabolism parameters.

**Results:** The primary endpoint, defined as a significant reduction in inflammatory markers and improvement in insulin sensitivity (HOMA-IR), failed to show consistent or significant treatment effects when analyzed per intention-to-treat. While there were isolated cases of reduced inflammatory markers observed, these were not statistically significant when analyzed across the entire population. Additionally, no meaningful differences were observed in secondary outcomes, including body weight change or quality of life measures between the two groups.

**Conclusions:** Despite the theoretical benefits associated with telmisartan as a potential adjunct therapy in reducing inflammation and improving insulin sensitivity in obese patients at risk of T2DM, this trial did not demonstrate sufficient efficacy or safety to justify its clinical use. Future research may focus on identifying specific patient populations that may respond more favorably to telmisartan or consider alternative therapeutic approaches to address obesity-related inflammation and insulin resistance.

**End of trial:** The study evaluation concluded upon the completion of the last patient visit and follow-up assessments at 12 weeks. The anticipated findings from this trial will inform ongoing research strategies and therapeutic considerations in the management of obesity and its metabolic complications.
