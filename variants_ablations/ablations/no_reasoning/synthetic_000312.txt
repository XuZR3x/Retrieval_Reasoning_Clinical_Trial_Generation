**Failed Clinical Trial of Mifepristone: Efficacy and Safety of Mifepristone in the Treatment of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)**

**A Phase II, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study of Mifepristone for the Treatment of Symptoms of PTSD**

The purpose of this study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of mifepristone in individuals diagnosed with Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD). Mifepristone has previously demonstrated benefits in other psychiatric disorders, yet its application in PTSD remains unexplored, highlighting the need for rigorous investigation. This study aimed to assess the therapeutic potential of mifepristone in alleviating core PTSD symptoms, including intrusive memories, hyperarousal, and avoidance behavior.

This clinical trial was designed as a double-blind, placebo-controlled investigation, enrolling subjects over a 12-week period. Participants were randomized into two groups: one receiving 600 mg mifepristone daily and the other receiving matching placebo. Initial assessments involved evaluating the severity of PTSD symptoms utilizing the Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale (CAPS), and baseline physical and psychiatric health was confirmed through comprehensive evaluations.

Upon consent and screening, baseline measures were established to monitor participants for compliance and symptom severity. Throughout the 12-week treatment phase, participants underwent bi-weekly assessments for adherence to the prescribed regimen and safety monitoring, including laboratory tests, vital sign measurements, and psychiatric evaluations. 

The primary endpoint was the change in CAPS scores from baseline to the end of the treatment phase, while secondary outcomes included assessments of functional impairment, quality of life metrics, and adverse event reporting. A final post-treatment follow-up was conducted 4 weeks post-intervention to evaluate the durability of any treatment-related effects and assess for potential residual symptoms.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 to 65 with a confirmed diagnosis of PTSD as per DSM-5 criteria.
- History of traumatic exposure with significant distress displayed through CAPS screening.
- Capability to provide informed consent and follow study protocols.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Current treatment with antidepressants, antipsychotics, or any other psychotropic medications within four weeks prior to enrollment.
- History of severe endocrine disorders or active substance use disorders.
- Pregnant or lactating females, or those intending to become pregnant during the study.
- Significant uncontrolled medical conditions that could confound results (e.g., renal or hepatic impairment).

Adverse events were meticulously documented, with particular attention on potential glucocorticoid-related effects, given the pharmacological profile of mifepristone. Despite initial hypotheses suggesting symptom reduction, the primary analysis revealed that mifepristone did not confer any statistically significant advantage over placebo in reducing PTSD symptoms, evidenced by unchanged CAPS scores throughout the study duration.

Moreover, the incidence of adverse effects such as endocrine dysregulation and psychological disturbances were noted at higher rates within the mifepristone group, contributing to concerns regarding tolerability and safety. Following comprehensive analysis and review of the data set, the clinical trial was concluded prematurely due to lack of efficacy and observed safety concerns, ultimately leading to its classification as a failed trial.

The results of this study underscore the necessity for further exploration into alternative therapeutic options for PTSD, reinforcing the complexity of the disorder and the need for targeted pharmacologic strategies.
