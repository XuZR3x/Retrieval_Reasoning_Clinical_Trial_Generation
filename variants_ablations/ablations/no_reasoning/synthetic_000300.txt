**Failed Clinical Trial of Olanzapine: A Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Olanzapine in Treating Insomnia in Patients with Major Depressive Disorder**

**Title**: A Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Trial Assessing the Efficacy of Olanzapine for Insomnia in Patients with Major Depressive Disorder

**Objective**: The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of olanzapine in reducing insomnia symptoms in patients diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder (MDD). Secondary objectives include assessing improvements in overall depressive symptoms as measured by standardized depression scales.

**Design**: This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving adult patients diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder who also report persistent insomnia symptoms.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Adult males and females aged 18 to 65 years.
- Diagnosis of Major Depressive Disorder according to DSM-5 criteria, confirmed through structured clinical interview.
- Patients must report insomnia, defined as difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep, occurring at least three times per week over the past month.
- A Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS) score of 15 or higher at baseline.
- Written informed consent obtained prior to any study-related procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Presence of primary sleep disorders other than insomnia (e.g., sleep apnea, restless legs syndrome).
- Recent history (within the last 6 months) of substance abuse or central nervous system disorders (excluding MDD).
- Current treatment with antidepressants or other psychoactive drugs that could interfere with olanzapine or the study objectives.
- Patients who have previously received olanzapine treatment.
- Significant medical illnesses (e.g., renal or hepatic impairment) that would contraindicate the use of olanzapine.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Intervention**: Participants will be randomized to receive either olanzapine or a placebo. The olanzapine group will receive a starting dosage of 5 mg at bedtime, titrated to a maximum dose of 15 mg based on clinical response and tolerability.

**Outcome Measures**:
- Primary outcome: Change in insomnia severity as measured by the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) from baseline to week 8.
- Secondary outcomes: Changes in depressive symptoms assessed by HDRS and quality of life measures using the WHOQOL-BREF.

**Statistical Analysis**: The analysis of variance (ANOVA) will be utilized to compare the primary and secondary endpoints between the olanzapine and placebo groups. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Expected Results**: Given the neuroleptic activity of olanzapine, it is hypothesized that olanzapine would lead to significant reductions in insomnia severity and improvements in overall depressive symptoms compared to placebo.

**Conclusion**: This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of olanzapine as a potential treatment for insomnia in the context of Major Depressive Disorder. Despite the rationale behind targeting insomnia due to the sedative properties of olanzapine, previous studies have indicated mixed results regarding its efficacy in psychological conditions, suggesting that an alternative mechanism or treatment may be necessary for effectively managing concomitant symptoms of insomnia and depression.

**Outcome**: The study was terminated early due to a lack of significant efficacy in the primary and secondary outcomes in the olanzapine group compared to the placebo cohort, indicating a failure of olanzapine in addressing insomnia symptoms within this specific patient population. Adverse events noted during the trial led to further concerns regarding safety and outlined the necessity for cautious prescription practices concerning olanzapine in treating insomnia.
