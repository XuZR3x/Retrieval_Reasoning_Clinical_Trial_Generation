**Failed Clinical Trial of Azacitidine: Phase II Study of Azacitidine in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Participants with Advanced Triple Negative Breast Cancer**

**A Randomized Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy of Azacitidine in Conjunction with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Triple Negative Breast Cancer Unresponsive to Standard Therapies**

This multicenter, open-label, phase II trial investigates the effectiveness of the combination of azacitidine, a hypomethylating agent, and pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody, in patients with advanced triple negative breast cancer (TNBC) that has progressed after at least one line of standard therapy. While previous studies suggested a potential synergistic effect of epigenetic modulation by azacitidine and immune checkpoint blockade by pembrolizumab, the results of this trial indicate limited clinical benefit.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of patients receiving azacitidine in combination with pembrolizumab, as compared to historical controls receiving pembrolizumab monotherapy in similar patient populations.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in patients treated with azacitidine and pembrolizumab.  
II. To investigate the immunological correlates of treatment by assessing changes in T-cell activation and tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes pre- and post-treatment.

**OUTLINE:**  
Patients are randomized to receive azacitidine administered subcutaneously at a dose of 75 mg/m² on days 1, 3, and 5 of a 28-day cycle, in conjunction with pembrolizumab at a standard dose (200 mg IV) on day 1 of each cycle for up to 24 weeks, unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Age ≥ 18 years with histologically confirmed advanced TNBC, characterized by negative expression of estrogen receptors (ER) and progesterone receptors (PR), and no HER2/neu amplification.  
- Patients must have progressed on at least one systemic therapy for advanced disease.  
- Performance Status: Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score of 0 to 2.  
- Adequate organ function as determined by standard laboratory tests.  
- Written informed consent must be obtained prior to study entry.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Prior exposure to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors or hypomethylating agents.  
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment within the past two years.  
- Known hypersensitivity to azacitidine or pembrolizumab.  
- Concurrent systemic steroid usage or any other immunosuppressive agents.  
- Significant cardiovascular or pulmonary disease that would place the patient at risk.  
- Investigational therapy within 30 days prior to randomization.  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women, or women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.

**OUTCOME METRICS:**
The primary endpoint was the ORR, defined as the percentage of participants achieving complete or partial response assessed by RECIST 1.1 criteria. Secondary endpoints included PFS and OS, evaluated at 6 months and 12 months, respectively.

**RESULTS:**
The trial enrolled 120 patients; however, a preliminary analysis indicated an ORR of only 10%, which was significantly lower than expected when compared to historical controls (response rates of 25-30% with pembrolizumab alone in similar cohorts). Both PFS and OS did not demonstrate meaningful improvement compared to the benchmark data.

**CONCLUSION:**
The combination of azacitidine and pembrolizumab did not result in a clinically significant response in patients with advanced triple negative breast cancer after failing prior therapies, leading to the conclusion that this dual treatment approach does not provide additional benefit in this population. Further investigations are warranted to understand the underlying mechanisms of resistance and to identify biomarkers predictive of response to combination therapies in TNBC. The trial has been terminated due to lack of efficacy, and patients will be referred for alternative treatment options.
