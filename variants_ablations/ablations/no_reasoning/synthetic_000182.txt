**Failed Clinical Trial of S-1: Efficacy of S-1 Versus Best Supportive Care in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Study Title:** A Phase 2 Non-Randomized, Open-Label Study of S-1 Versus Best Supportive Care in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) Who Have Progressed After Platinum-Based Chemotherapy

**Study Overview:**  
The primary goal of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of S-1 in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have failed prior platinum-based chemotherapy regimens. NSCLC remains a challenging malignancy, with limited treatment options following first-line chemotherapy, necessitating the exploration of novel therapeutic agents like S-1. Given the suboptimal response rates associated with standard therapies in this setting, S-1, an oral fluoropyrimidine prodrug, presents a potential alternative.

**Rationale:**  
S-1 combines Tegafur (a prodrug of 5-FU) with two modulators, Gimeracil and Oteracil, which are intended to optimize the pharmacokinetic profile of 5-FU for enhanced antitumor activity while minimizing gastrointestinal toxicity. However, existing literature indicates that the efficacy of S-1 in heavily pre-treated cancer populations, particularly in NSCLC, is uncertain and has not been sufficiently capitalized on in a clinical setting.

**Methodology:**  
- **Design:** This is a multicenter, two-stage, open-label, Phase 2 clinical trial.
- **Treatment:**
  - Intervention group will receive S-1 at a dose of 25 mg/m² orally twice daily for 14 days of a 21-day cycle.
  - Control group will receive best supportive care (BSC) without any specific anticancer treatment.
- **Duration:** Participants will continue treatment until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity, with a minimum follow-up period of 6 months post-treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced NSCLC not amenable to curative surgery or radiotherapy.  
2. Documented progression of disease after one prior platinum-based chemotherapy regimen.  
3. Measurable disease as defined by RECIST criteria (≥ 20 mm in at least one dimension).  
4. Aged 18 years or older.  
5. Adequate organ function as assessed by laboratory tests and performance status (ECOG 0-2).  
6. Written informed consent provided.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Previous treatment with S-1 or other fluoropyrimidines.  
2. Concurrent active malignancies requiring therapy.  
3. Known central nervous system metastases not adequately controlled.  
4. Serious uncontrolled medical conditions that would affect study participation, including cardiac disease, severe infections, or significant pulmonary disease.  
5. Pregnant or lactating women.  
6. Known hypersensitivity to components of S-1.

**Outcomes:**  
- **Primary Endpoint:** Overall survival (OS) measured from the date of randomization until death due to any cause. 
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Progression-free survival (PFS), safety profile (adverse event rate), quality of life assessments via validated questionnaires. 

**Statistical Analysis:**  
The study was designed to enroll approximately 120 patients with a 1:1 allocation to evaluate the efficacy of S-1 versus BSC. The primary analysis will involve a comparison of OS between the two groups using Kaplan-Meier survival curves and log-rank tests.

**Results:**  
The trial was terminated early due to a lack of significant efficacy observed in the intervention group compared to BSC. The overall survival rates did not demonstrate a clinically meaningful difference, with no substantial improvement over best supportive care. Additionally, the safety profile indicated increased adverse events particularly gastrointestinal-related toxicity without a corresponding increase in therapeutic benefit.

**Conclusion:**  
This trial underscores the limitations of S-1 as a therapeutic option in the heavily pre-treated NSCLC population. The findings suggest that while S-1 has theoretical benefits due to its unique composition, it does not translate into improved survival or quality of life in the context of advanced disease following chemotherapy. Further investigation into potential biomarkers predictive of responsiveness and the patient selection process may be essential for future trials involving S-1 in NSCLC.
