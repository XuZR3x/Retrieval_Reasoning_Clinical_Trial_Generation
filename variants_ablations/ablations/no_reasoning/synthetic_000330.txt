**Successful Clinical Trial of Cixutumumab: A Study of Cixutumumab in Combination with Pembrolizumab and Chemotherapy in Patients with Advanced Melanoma**

**A Phase 1/2 Study Evaluating the Safety, Efficacy, and Pharmacodynamics of IMC-A12 Combined with Pembrolizumab in Patients With Advanced Melanoma**

This clinical trial investigates the administration of cixutumumab (IMC-A12), an anti-IGF-IR monoclonal antibody, in conjunction with pembrolizumab (a PD-1 inhibitor) and standard chemotherapy in patients diagnosed with advanced melanoma. The study aims to evaluate the safety profile, pharmacokinetics, and preliminary efficacy of this combination therapy in a cohort of participants exhibiting disease progression despite standard treatment options. 

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**

1. To characterize the safety and tolerability of cixutumumab in combination with pembrolizumab and chemotherapy in patients with advanced melanoma.
2. To determine the pharmacokinetic (PK) profile of cixutumumab and pembrolizumab during the treatment regimen.
3. To evaluate preliminary efficacy through overall response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) based on RECIST criteria.

**OUTLINE:**

This trial is designed as a single-arm, open-label study comprising dose-escalation and dose-expansion phases. Following informed consent, participants will start therapy in Cohort 1, receiving intravenous (IV) cixutumumab at a dose of 10 mg/kg and pembrolizumab at a dose of 200 mg every 21 days, alongside standard chemotherapy which includes carboplatin and paclitaxel. The treatment duration consists of two cycles, each lasting 42 days. 

Upon completion of two cycles, patients who exhibit a best overall response of CR, PR, or stable disease (SD) will proceed to extended treatment for up to an additional six cycles until disease progression (PD) or unacceptable toxicity occurs. 

**DOSE ESCALATION:**

The dose-escalation scheme will follow a traditional 3+3 design for cixutumumab to identify the maximum tolerated dose (MTD). In the presence of dose-limiting toxicities (DLT), including but not limited to Grade 4 neutropenia lasting > 7 days, Grade ≥ 3 non-hematologic toxicities, or severe immunological responses, additional participants will be enrolled to further assess safety at the observed dose.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**

- Histologically confirmed metastatic or unresectable melanoma.
- Patients previously treated with at least one line of systemic therapy, excluding pembrolizumab or other PD-1 inhibitors.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
- Adequate hematologic function: 
  - ANC ≥ 1500/µL 
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/µL 
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL
- Satisfactory renal and hepatic function, defined by serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL and total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the institutional normal.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**

- Active central nervous system metastases or carcinomatous meningitis.
- Uncontrolled hypertension or serious cardiovascular diseases including recent myocardial infarction.
- History of severe allergic reactions to monoclonal antibodies.
- Concurrent participation in other clinical trials involving investigational agents within 30 days prior to study enrollment.

**EFFICACY ASSESSMENT:**

The primary efficacy evaluation will be based on the ORR through imaging assessments performed at baseline and every 8 weeks thereafter, utilizing the RECIST criteria. Secondary endpoints will include progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates documented through patient follow-up at 3-month intervals.

**STUDY LOCATION:**

Participants will be enrolled at the leading oncology center, the Berman Cancer Institute, Baltimore, Maryland, in cooperation with multiple referral clinics throughout the region. A total of approximately 50 participants are anticipated to ensure robust data collection and analysis. 

**DATA MONITORING:**

Safety monitoring will occur continuously, with regular assessments by the Independent Data Monitoring Committee (IDMC) to ensure participant welfare. Should significant adverse events arise, the treatment protocol will be subject to immediate review and necessary adjustments by the Principal Investigator in conjunction with institutional guidelines.

This clinical trial endeavors to provide critical knowledge regarding the innovative combination of cixutumumab, pembrolizumab, and standard chemotherapy, potentially leading to enhanced therapeutic avenues for patients with advanced melanoma.
