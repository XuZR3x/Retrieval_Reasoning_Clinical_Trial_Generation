**A Phase II Study of Arsenic Trioxide and Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** Arsenic trioxide (ATO) possesses multifaceted mechanisms of action, including the induction of apoptosis in tumor cells and modulation of immune responses. Immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as PD-1 and CTLA-4 inhibitors, have revolutionized the treatment landscape for advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) by reactivating the immune system against tumor cells. The combination of arsenic trioxide with immune checkpoint inhibitors may enhance antitumor activity through synergistic effects by both directly inducing tumor cell death and enhancing immune-mediated responses.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial investigates the safety, tolerability, and antitumor activity of arsenic trioxide administered in conjunction with immune checkpoint inhibitors in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** Assess the overall response rate (ORR) of patients treated with arsenic trioxide in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors.
- **Secondary:** Evaluate progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and the safety profile of the combination therapy.

**OUTLINE:** This is a two-arm multicenter phase II study. 

- **Arm A:** Patients will receive arsenic trioxide IV over 1 hour on days 1-5, followed by an immune checkpoint inhibitor (either anti-PD-1 or anti-CTLA-4) administered on day 8 and every 21 days thereafter.
  
- **Arm B:** Patients will receive immune checkpoint inhibitors as monotherapy (control group).

Following 12 weeks of treatment, clinical and radiological evaluations will be performed to determine tumor response. Patients will continue treatment until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. 

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** Approximately 100 patients will be enrolled, with 50 patients assigned to each arm.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer
- Stage IIIB/IV disease, not amenable to surgical resection 
- Measurable disease as per RECIST criteria
- No active brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease
- No history of autoimmune disease requiring immunosuppressive therapy within past 5 years

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age ≥ 18 years
- Performance status: ECOG 0-2
- Life expectancy ≥ 3 months
- Hematologic parameters: WBC > 3,000/mm^3; Absolute neutrophil count > 1,500/mm^3; Platelet count > 100,000/mm^3; Hemoglobin > 10 g/dL
- Biochemical parameters: Creatinine < 1.5 mg/dL; Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL; Transaminases ≤ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal
- Not pregnant or breastfeeding; negative pregnancy test required

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior treatment with arsenic trioxide or immune checkpoint inhibitors
- Active second malignancy (except non-melanoma skin cancer or other curatively treated malignancies)
- Hypersensitivity to ATO or any component of the study drugs
- Uncontrolled infections, severe comorbidities, or significant cardiovascular or neurological conditions
- Concurrent treatment with other investigational agents or therapies

**FOLLOW-UP:** Patients will be followed every 8 weeks for clinical assessment, radiological evaluation, and adverse event monitoring for 2 years post-treatment.

**ENDPOINTS:** The study will evaluate the primary endpoint of ORR at 12 weeks, while secondary endpoints include PFS, OS, and adverse event profiles categorized according to CTCAE v5.0.

This phase II clinical trial aims to provide valuable insights into the therapeutic potential of combining arsenic trioxide with immune checkpoint inhibition in advanced non-small cell lung cancer, potentially paving the way for new treatment paradigms in this challenging malignancy.
