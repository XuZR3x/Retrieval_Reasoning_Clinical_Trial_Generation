**Failed Clinical Trial of Darunavir: IMPACT-HIV (Investigating Management of Therapeutic Adherence among PAtients under Combination Therapy for HIV)**

The objective of this study was to investigate the impact of a comprehensive adherence strategy on the efficacy of darunavir combined with other antiretroviral drugs in treatment-experienced HIV-infected individuals. The primary hypothesis posited that an enhanced adherence intervention, comprising both electronic pill caps and personalized counseling, would result in higher rates of virologic suppression at 48 weeks compared to standard care.

IMPACT-HIV was conducted as a multicenter, randomized, open-label, phase IV interventional trial enrolling HIV-1 infected adults who had documented adherence challenges due to prior treatment failures or complex treatment regimens. Participants were randomized into two arms: one receiving a modified adherence support intervention (main intervention group) and the other following standard care protocols without augmented adherence support (control group).

In the intervention group, electronic tracking devices were provided to assess pill-taking behavior, coupled with real-time text messages reminding participants to take their medication, while control group participants were given standard educational materials about adherence. Both arms were administered a regimen comprising darunavir (800 mg) and cobicistat (150 mg) once daily along with a background regimen of two NRTIs.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Documented HIV-1 infection with prior evidence of immunologic or virologic treatment failure.
- Age 18-65 years.
- Participants must have been on any ART regimen for at least 6 months before randomization.
- Ability to provide informed consent and comply with mobile health monitoring requirements.
- Confirmation of persistent HIV-1 RNA levels ≥ 1000 copies/mL within 30 days prior to screening.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
- Known hypersensitivity to darunavir or components of the study regimen.
- Utilization of concomitant medications contraindicated with darunavir and cobicistat.
- Active substance abuse, psychiatric conditions, or other comorbid disorders affecting adherence.
- Recent history of opportunistic infections necessitating hospitalization.

The primary endpoint was defined as the proportion of participants with HIV-1 RNA levels ≤50 copies/mL at 48 weeks post-randomization, assessed using a validated RT-PCR assay. Secondary endpoints included the adherence rates assessed by electronic pill caps, quality of life measurements, and incidence of adverse events related to therapy.

Despite the rigorous protocol, the trial did not meet its primary endpoint of virologic suppression. The analysis indicated that only 42% of participants in the intervention arm achieved the targeted suppression compared to 38% in the control group (p = 0.432). These findings revealed that the combination of technological adherence interventions did not translate into statistically significant improvements in clinical virologic outcomes, raising concerns about the interplay of multifactorial barriers to medication adherence in the cohort assessed.

Subgroup analyses suggested a trend towards better outcomes in individuals without significant prior treatment failures or adverse psychosocial determinants, while participants in both arms exhibited decreased adherence over time, suggesting that adherence strategies may require more comprehensive integration with other supportive services.

The trial highlighted challenges linked to the optimization of adherence among populations with complex needs and the possibility that interventions alone may be insufficient without addressing broader contextual factors influencing treatment engagement. Further research is warranted to explore a more holistic approach to management involving multidisciplinary teams tailored to individual patient concerns.

The primary analysis estimated the proportion of participants with HIV-1 RNA ≤50 copies/mL, employing a statistical analysis plan that treated all non-evaluable participants as virologic failures. Secondary outcomes were insufficiently powered and thus could not contribute meaningfully to the interpretability of clinical data.

**Percent of Participants with HIV-1 RNA Levels ≤50 Copies/mL at Week 48**
The measurement obtained at week 48 determined the effectiveness of both the intervention and control arms, wherein both cohorts fell below expected efficacy benchmarks set by preceding trials. Further modifications of adherence models within future trials are recommended to dissect and target underlying variables contributing to non-adherence and treatment failures in this population.
