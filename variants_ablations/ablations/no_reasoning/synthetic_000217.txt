**Failed Clinical Trial of Insulin Glulisine: Evaluation of Insulin Glulisine Versus Insulin Glargine on Glycemic Control and Quality of Life in Adult Patients With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus**

*Title*: A Randomized, Open-Label, Multicenter Trial Evaluating the Efficacy of Insulin Glulisine Compared to Insulin Glargine in Attaining Glycemic Targets and Assessing Quality of Life in Adult Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

*Primary Objective*: To determine the differences in HbA1c levels at 24 weeks between patients receiving insulin glulisine and those receiving insulin glargine as prandial insulin in a setting where treatment adherence varies.

*Secondary Objectives*:
- To analyze patient-reported quality of life metrics utilizing the Diabetes Quality of Life Scale (DQOL).
- To evaluate the incidence and frequency of hypoglycemic episodes.
- To collect data on weight change from baseline to week 24.
- To assess the total daily insulin requirements.

*Inclusion Criteria*:
- Adult patients (aged 18-75 years) with a confirmed diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus for a minimum duration of one year.
- Patients currently on a regimen involving insulin glargine with an HbA1c >7.5% and ≤10% at screening.
- Ability to perform self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) at least four times per day.
- Written informed consent to participate in the study.

*Exclusion Criteria*:
- Patients treated with insulin other than glargine or with insulin analogs not included in the study.
- Documented history of recurrent severe hypoglycemia or hypoglycemic unawareness.
- Significant psychiatric illnesses or cognitive impairments that may interfere with participation.
- Creatinine levels >1.5 mg/dL (133 µmol/L) in males or >1.4 mg/dL (124 µmol/L) in females.
- Patients exhibiting a non-compliant behavior history during prior treatment regimens.

*Study Design*: This clinical trial employed a randomized, open-label design, conducted at multiple sites. The study population was randomized in a 1:1 fashion to receive either insulin glulisine or insulin glargine administered before meals for a period of 24 weeks, with a follow-up visit at 28 weeks.

*Statistical Analysis*: Statistical comparisons were performed on a per-protocol and intention-to-treat basis. The primary endpoint, HbA1c levels, was analyzed using an ANCOVA model while adjustments were made for baseline values. Secondary outcomes were evaluated descriptively.

*Results*: The study did not meet its primary endpoint, with the mean change in HbA1c not significantly differing between the insulin glulisine group and the insulin glargine group at week 24. Additionally, the secondary outcomes indicated no substantial differences in the quality of life scores nor in the incidence of hypoglycemia between treatment groups.

*Conclusions*: The trial findings suggest that insulin glulisine does not confer a superior glycemic control compared to insulin glargine when both are administered in a real-world context with variable adherence. These results highlight the necessity for further research to explore patient-specific factors impacting intervention efficacy, as well as the importance of maintaining adherence to prescribed treatment protocols in diabetes management. Future studies may benefit from a revised design that incorporates more stringent adherence monitoring and patient engagement strategies.
