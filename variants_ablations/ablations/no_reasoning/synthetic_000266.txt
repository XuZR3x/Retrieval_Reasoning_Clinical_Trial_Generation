**Failed Clinical Trial of Apixaban: Investigation of the Efficacy of Apixaban in the Prevention of Thromboembolic Events Following Surgical Hip Replacement (SAPHIRE) A Phase III Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind Controlled Trial to Assess the Role of Apixaban in Reducing Thromboembolic Events in Patients Undergoing Elective Total Hip Replacement**

The purpose of this study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of apixaban compared with standard care, specifically low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH), in preventing thromboembolic complications, such as deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE), in patients undergoing elective total hip replacement surgery. Patients undergoing hip replacement are at increased risk for venous thromboembolism due to stasis and surgical trauma. Apixaban, an oral direct Factor Xa inhibitor, offers a novel approach by minimizing the need for monitoring and allowing convenience in administration.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 40 years and older scheduled for elective total hip replacement surgery.
- Written informed consent provided by the subject or their legally authorized representative.
- Absence of current anticoagulation therapy or blood anti-platelet therapies that could confound results.
- Ability to comply with all protocol requirements and follow-up visits.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of active bleeding or significant bleeding disorders.
- Renal impairment defined as CrCl <30 mL/min.
- Previous thromboembolic events within the past three months or current DVT/PE.
- Patients requiring concomitant anticoagulation for any other medical condition.
- Major trauma or orthopedic surgeries within the last three months.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women or women of childbearing potential who are not capable of using effective contraception during the study period.

The primary endpoint of this study was the incidence of DVT and PE evaluated by Doppler ultrasound and computed tomography (CT) pulmonary angiography at 30 days postoperatively. Secondary endpoints included major and minor bleeding events, hospital readmission rates, and all-cause mortality.

The trial concluded without demonstrating a statistically significant reduction in the incidence of thromboembolic events when administering apixaban compared with LMWH. Furthermore, an increase in adverse bleeding events was noted in the apixaban cohort, leading to a substantial concern regarding its use in this surgical population.

The findings from this trial highlight the complexity and multifactorial nature of thromboembolism prevention in surgical settings and underscore the necessity of robust overlapping protocols and clearer selection criteria for anticoagulant trials. Future investigations should explore comparative effectiveness in more heterogeneous populations and consider tailoring anticoagulant strategies based on individual risk profiles.
