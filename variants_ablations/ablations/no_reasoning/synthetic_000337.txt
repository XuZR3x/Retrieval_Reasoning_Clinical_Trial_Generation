<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Evaluation of Enoxaparin Versus Standard Care in Patients With Non-ST-Elevation Acute Coronary Syndrome (NSTE-ACS)</Title>
    <Objective>The primary objective of the study is to assess the efficacy of enoxaparin compared to standard care in reducing adverse cardiac events in patients presenting with Non-ST-Elevation Acute Coronary Syndrome (NSTE-ACS).</Objective>
    <Design>A prospective, multicenter, randomized, controlled trial designed to compare the effectiveness and safety of enoxaparin (1.0 mg/kg subcutaneously every 12 hours) versus standard care (aspirin and clopidogrel without enoxaparin) in the management of NSTE-ACS. Eligible patients will be randomized 1:1 using an interactive web response system (IWRS) upon presentation to the emergency department.</Design>
    <StudyPopulation>The study will enroll approximately 900 subjects across 60 medical centers in North America and Europe. Participants must be adults aged ≥18 years with a diagnosis of NSTE-ACS, evidenced by chest discomfort and significant cardiac biomarker elevations, but no ST-segment elevation on ECG. 
    </StudyPopulation>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>1. Adults aged 18 years or older.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>2. Clinical presentation indicative of NSTE-ACS for at least 20 minutes within the prior 24 hours.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>3. Positive troponin or other cardiac biomarker consistent with myocardial injury.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>4. Willingness to provide informed consent.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>5. Agreement to comply with study provisions and follow-ups.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>1. Known contraindications to enoxaparin, including active bleeding or severe thrombocytopenia.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>2. History of stroke or transient ischemic attack within the past 30 days.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>3. Thrombolytic therapy within 72 hours before randomization.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>4. Active malignancy with metastatic disease.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>5. Major surgery or trauma within the previous two months.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>6. Enrollment in another interventional clinical trial.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <PrimaryOutcome>The primary outcome measure will be the composite incidence of all-cause mortality, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or urgent revascularization occurring within 30 days from study enrollment.</PrimaryOutcome>
    <SecondaryOutcomes>Secondary outcomes will include: 1) Major bleeding events as defined by the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) criteria; 2) Length of hospital stay; 3) Quality of life assessments using the EQ-5D scale at baseline and 30 days post-enrollment.</SecondaryOutcomes>
    <Rationale>Previous studies evaluating enoxaparin in similar indications have indicated potential advantages in reducing ischemic events. However, the lack of clear benefit in NSTE-ACS management, particularly in the context of potential bleeding risks, motivated this investigation. The expectation of superior outcomes in NSTE-ACS due to enoxaparin administration, especially when compared to standard care, was hypothesized but not supported based on real-world clinical outcomes encountered during the trial.</Rationale>
    <ResultsAsPlanned>The study will report results at the conclusion of the 30-day follow-up period. An analysis of data will determine the safety and efficacy of enoxaparin in achieving improved clinical outcomes in comparison to conventional therapy.</ResultsAsPlanned>
    <Conclusion>Due to the higher incidence of major bleeding complications observed in the enoxaparin group compared to the control group, alongside no significant improvement in clinical outcomes, the trial was terminated early, concluding that enoxaparin did not confer a net benefit in this patient population.</Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
