Failed clinical trial of levetiracetam: Assessing the Impact of Levetiracetam on Gait Stability in Patients with Parkinson's Disease A Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Levetiracetam on Gait Stability in Parkinson's Disease Patients Levetiracetam, an antiepileptic medication, has shown promise in modulating excitatory neurotransmitter release and has potential implications for symptom management in neurodegenerative disorders such as Parkinson's Disease (PD). Given that gait instability is a prominent symptom of PD, this study aimed to determine if Levetiracetam could improve gait stability and overall mobility among affected patients. Inclusion Criteria: 1. Adults aged 40-80 years diagnosed with idiopathic Parkinson’s Disease per the UK Parkinson's Disease Society Brain Bank Criteria. 2. Participants exhibiting gait instability as defined by a Functional Gait Assessment score of less than 22. 3. Individuals on a stable dose of Parkinson's disease medication (levodopa, dopamine agonists, monoamine oxidase B inhibitors) for at least 3 months prior to study entry. 4. Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study procedures. 5. No neuroleptic medication usage for at least 3 months. Exclusion Criteria: 1. Significant cognitive impairment defined by a Mini-Mental State Examination score of less than 24. 2. History of seizures or concurrent use of antiepileptic medications. 3. Uncontrolled medical conditions (e.g., severe hypertension, active malignancy). 4. Individuals with a known hypersensitivity to Levetiracetam or any related compounds. 5. Participation in another clinical trial overlapping with the study period. Primary Outcome: The primary outcome measure is the change in the Functional Gait Assessment (FGA) score from baseline to the end of the 12-week intervention period. Secondary outcomes include fall incidence, tremor severity (measured by the Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale), and quality of life (assessed through the Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire-39). The study aimed to elucidate whether Levetiracetam could enhance gait stability, yet results indicated no significant improvement compared to placebo, highlighting the complexity of symptom management in Parkinson’s Disease.
