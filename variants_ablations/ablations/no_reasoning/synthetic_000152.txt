**Successful Clinical Trial of Pegfilgrastim: Evaluation of Dose-Dense Chemotherapy Regimens with Pegfilgrastim in Early Stage Triple-Negative Breast Cancer**

**RATIONALE**: Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is associated with aggressive behavior and poor prognosis. Standard chemotherapy regimens may not yield optimal outcomes for patients with resected TNBC. Pegfilgrastim, a pegylated form of filgrastim, is a colony-stimulating factor that enhances the recovery of neutrophils following chemotherapy, potentially ameliorating adverse effects and permitting dose-dense therapy. Assessing the effectiveness of pegfilgrastim in combination with intensified chemotherapy regimens may improve the overall outcomes in TNBC patients after surgical intervention.

**PURPOSE**: This randomized phase III trial aims to determine the efficacy of a dose-dense chemotherapy regimen, employing either an anthracycline-based regimen or a taxane-based regimen, with pegfilgrastim in patients with early stage TNBC who have undergone surgical resection.

**OBJECTIVES**:
- **Primary**: To compare disease-free survival (DFS) between patients receiving dose-dense chemotherapy with pegfilgrastim versus those receiving standard chemotherapy without pegfilgrastim.
- **Secondary**: To evaluate overall survival (OS), safety profiles, and quality of life among treated patients, as well as the rate of pathological complete response (pCR) in the neoadjuvant setting.

**OUTLINE**: This multicenter, randomized study stratifies patients based on nodal involvement and performance status, allocating them into two primary treatment arms:
- **Arm I**: Patients receive doxorubicin IV and cyclophosphamide IV on day 1, followed by pegfilgrastim SC on day 2. Treatment repeats every 14 days for 4 courses. Patients then receive paclitaxel IV on day 1 with pegfilgrastim administered on day 2, continuing every 14 days for an additional 4 courses.
- **Arm II**: Patients receive standard chemotherapy of doxorubicin IV and cyclophosphamide on day 1 repeated every 21 days for 4 courses, followed by paclitaxel IV every 21 days for 4 courses, without pegfilgrastim.

In both arms, treatment proceeds in the absence of unacceptable toxicity. All patients with complete response may be candidates for radiation therapy based on clinical criteria.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL**: A total of 1,500 patients will be accrued for this study.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of invasive breast cancer
- Triple-negative breast cancer (ER-, PR-, HER2-)
- Clinical stage I-III with no evidence of distant metastasis
- Previously resected disease with clear surgical margins typical of conservative surgery or modified radical mastectomy

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Gender: Female
- Age: 18 years or older
- ECOG performance status 0-1
- Sufficient hematological profiles (ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³, WBC ≥ 4,000/mm³, platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³, hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL)
- No active systemic infection, unresolved coagulopathy, or hypersensitivity to study medications

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY**: Patients are excluded if they have received prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy for breast cancer, and no other malignancies within the past 5 years, with the exception of adequately treated non-invasive cancers.

**DISEASE-FREE SURVIVAL (DFS)** at 5 years and overall survival rates are to be assessed during the follow-up period which continues for 10 years post-treatment completion. Quality of life assessments are integrated at multiple points during and post-therapy.

The trial is designed to provide robust data on the efficacy of pegfilgrastim in enabling dose-dense chemotherapy regimens in a patient population historically burdened with a high risk of treatment failure and associated morbidity.
