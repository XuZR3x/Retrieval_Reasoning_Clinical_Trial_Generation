**Failed clinical trial of aflibercept: Aflibercept in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Title:** A Phase 2, Open-Label, Multicenter Study of Aflibercept in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer that Exhibited Disease Progression Following Platinum-based Chemotherapy

**Objective:** This clinical trial aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of aflibercept in combination with pembrolizumab in subjects with advanced NSCLC that has progressed on prior platinum-based chemotherapy. The primary objective is to assess the overall response rate (ORR) as per RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**Design:** The study is designed as a Phase 2, open-label, multicenter study. Patients who meet the eligibility criteria will receive aflibercept intravenously (IV) at a dose of 4 mg/kg followed by pembrolizumab at the dose of 200 mg IV every 3 weeks. Treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal from the study.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced NSCLC, with documented disease progression after at least one line of platinum-based chemotherapy.
2. Aged 18 years or older.
3. Measurable disease as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.
4. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
5. Life expectancy of at least 12 weeks.
6. Adequate organ function as defined by laboratory parameters.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior treatment with anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 therapies.
2. Concurrent malignancies or history of malignancy within the last 5 years, with certain exceptions.
3. Active autoimmune disease or immunodeficiency.
4. Known hypersensitivity to aflibercept, pembrolizumab, or excipients.
5. Significant cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled intercurrent illness.

**Treatment Regimen:** Patients will receive treatment with intravenous aflibercept (4 mg/kg) on day 1 of each 21-day cycle, followed by intravenous pembrolizumab (200 mg) on day 1. Treatment will continue for a maximum of 24 cycles, with response evaluations conducted every 6 weeks, followed by toxicity assessments.

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary endpoint is the overall response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of subjects with a complete or partial response, assessed using RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Progression-Free Survival (PFS) defined as the duration from the date of treatment initiation to the date of disease progression or death.
- Overall Survival (OS) defined as the time from randomization to the date of death due to any cause.
- Safety and tolerability assessed by recording adverse events (AEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs).

**Sample Size:** Approximately 100 patients will be enrolled for this study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the combination therapy.

**Statistical Analysis:** The ORR, PFS, and OS will be calculated with 95% confidence intervals. Efficacy will be analyzed using Kaplan-Meier estimates for survival endpoints.

**Results:** Despite the promising preclinical data supporting the use of aflibercept in combination with pembrolizumab, the study failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in ORR or PFS compared to historical controls, leading to the cessation of the trial. Adverse events associated with the treatment regimen were also prominently reported, including increased rates of immune-related adverse effects that were consistent with findings from previous studies of pembrolizumab.

**Conclusion:** The combination of aflibercept and pembrolizumab did not exhibit the intended therapeutic benefit in improving outcomes for patients with advanced NSCLC after progression on prior treatments. The failure of this trial highlights the complexities associated with targeting angiogenesis and immune modulation in NSCLC and underscores the need for further investigation into alternative therapeutic combinations and regimens.
