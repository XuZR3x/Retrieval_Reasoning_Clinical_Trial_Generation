**Failed Clinical Trial of Perifosine: Perifosine for Treatment of Relapsed Multiple Myeloma**

**Phase II Trial of Perifosine in Patients with Relapsed Multiple Myeloma After Failure of Prior Therapies**  

**RATIONALE:** Perifosine is an oral alkylphospholipid that has been hypothesized to inhibit tumor cell proliferation through multiple mechanisms, including modulation of cell survival pathways. This trial aimed to evaluate its effectiveness in patients with relapsed multiple myeloma who have undergone prior therapy, including proteasome inhibitors and immunomodulatory drugs.  

**PURPOSE:** This Phase II clinical trial was designed to investigate the efficacy and safety of perifosine in patients with multiple myeloma who have demonstrated disease progression following at least two lines of prior therapy.  

**OBJECTIVES:**  
- **Primary Objective:** To assess the overall response rate (ORR) as defined by the International Myeloma Working Group criteria in patients receiving perifosine.  
- **Secondary Objectives:** 
  - To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in treated patients.  
  - To characterize the safety profile of perifosine, including the incidence and severity of adverse events.  
  - To examine changes in laboratory parameters and immunological markers.   

**OUTLINE:**  
Patients enrolled in this study received oral perifosine 100 mg once daily, with treatment continuing until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Patients were monitored for response every 8 weeks, and adverse events were recorded per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v4.0.  

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 50 patients were planned to be accrued within 2 years.  

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:** 
- Histologically confirmed multiple myeloma, meeting the following criteria: 
  - Relapsed or refractory disease after at least two prior treatment regimens. 
  - Prior therapy must include at least one proteasome inhibitor (e.g., bortezomib) and one immunomodulatory drug (e.g., lenalidomide).  
  - Documented progression defined by criteria including an increase in monoclonal protein levels or development of new lesions on imaging studies.  
- No active extramedullary disease.  
- No prior history of hypersensitivity to similar compounds (e.g., alkylphospholipids).  

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:** 
- Age ≥ 18 years.  
- Performance status: ECOG 0-2 or Karnofsky 60-100%.  
- Life expectancy > 3 months.  
- Adequate hematologic function: ANC ≥ 1,000/mm³, platelet count ≥ 75,000/mm³, and hemoglobin ≥ 8 g/dL.  
- Normal renal and hepatic function as defined by creatinine ≤ 2.0 mg/dL and bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal.  
- Informed consent obtained according to institutional guidelines.  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:** 
- Prior treatment with perifosine or any investigational agent within 30 days of study entry.  
- Active infections requiring systemic antibiotics.  
- Concurrent malignancies except adequately treated basal cell carcinoma or in situ carcinoma of the cervix.  
- Severe cardiovascular conditions, including myocardial infarction within the past 6 months.  
- Pregnant or lactating women, or those of childbearing potential without effective contraception.  

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES:** 
Evaluations consisted of clinical response assessed through serum protein electrophoresis and immunofixation, and imaging studies (CT/MRI) to assess bone lesions. Primary endpoint analysis was to occur after 6 months of treatment, with disease assessment every 8 weeks thereafter.  

**RESULTS AND CONCLUSION:** 
Ultimately, this Phase II trial did not meet its primary endpoint of achieving a statistically significant ORR, demonstrating a modest response rate of 12% among the patient population treated with perifosine compared to historical controls. The safety profile suggested a significant incidence of gastrointestinal toxicities, including severe nausea and diarrhea, leading to a premature discontinuation of treatment in a subset of participants. The insufficient clinical efficacy coupled with the observed adverse event profile suggests that perifosine did not provide adequate therapeutic benefit for relapsed multiple myeloma, thereby closing this clinical trial. Further exploration of this agent in other therapeutic contexts is warranted, but its role in this indication is not supported by the current data.
