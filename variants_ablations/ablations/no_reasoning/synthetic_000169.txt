Failed clinical trial of risperidone: Efficacy of Risperidone Combined with Cognitive Behavioral Therapy in Treating Schizophrenia Symptoms. 

The objective of this clinical trial is to evaluate the combined effects of risperidone and structured cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) on alleviating core symptoms of schizophrenia, specifically positive symptoms such as hallucinations and delusions, and negative symptoms including anhedonia and social withdrawal. This trial aims to address the limitations observed in previous antiflatulent interventions and examine the potential augmented therapeutic benefit of combining pharmacological and psychological interventions in patients with schizophrenia. 

This Phase II study employs a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled design comprising 50 patients diagnosed with schizophrenia as per DSM-5 criteria. Eligible participants will be randomized into two groups: one receiving risperidone in conjunction with CBT, while the other group will receive risperidone with a placebo-based intervention. Participants will undergo treatment for a duration of 12 weeks, with CBT sessions scheduled weekly. Outcome measures will include standardized rating scales for both positive and negative symptoms of schizophrenia such as the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) and the Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale (BPRS), assessed at baseline, midpoint (week 6), and treatment completion (week 12).

Inclusion Criteria: 
1. Diagnosis of schizophrenia as per DSM-5 criteria.
2. Age between 18 and 55 years.
3. Must be on a stable dose of risperidone (within 4-12 mg/day) for at least two weeks prior to enrollment.
4. Informed consent provided by participants.
5. Ability to participate in cognitive behavioral therapy sessions.

Exclusion Criteria: 
1. Current diagnosis of substance use disorder.
2. History of psychiatric disorder requiring hospitalization within the past year.
3. Significant medical comorbidities that would contraindicate the use of risperidone.
4. Prior treatment failure with risperidone or contraindications to its use.
5. Active participation in any other pharmacological trial within the last three months.

The primary outcome of this study will evaluate the improvement in symptom severity as measured by the PANSS total score, while secondary outcomes will assess the impact of combined therapy on functional outcomes, quality of life (QoL), and cognitive functioning via neuropsychological assessments, including the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test (WCST) and the Positive and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS).

Despite rigorous protocols and the structured method combined with risperidone, failure was observed in achieving statistically significant improvements over the placebo group in both primary and secondary outcomes. Moreover, patients expressed limited engagement with the CBT component, citing barriers related to cognitive deficits associated with their condition, which ultimately contributed to the lack of efficacy demonstrated in this trial. Given these findings, the investigation suggests that the anticipated synergistic effect between risperidone and CBT may require further methodological adjustments to be adequately operationalized in this patient population moving forward.
