**Failed Clinical Trial of 5-Fluorouracil: Efficacy of 5-Fluorouracil Combined with Newer Anticancer Agents in Patients with Metastatic Colorectal Cancer (FLEET Trial)**

**Background:** Metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality. The standard chemotherapy includes 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) in conjunction with additional agents; however, investigations into novel combinations are crucial, particularly as existing protocols show limited efficacy in prolonging overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). This trial aims to assess whether the addition of novel agents to 5-FU could enhance outcomes in patients with mCRC compared to the conventional 5-FU-based therapies. 

**Design:** This study is a multicenter, randomized, open-label trial designed to compare the efficacy and safety of a regimen combining 5-fluorouracil with the investigational agents, and compares it against standard therapy with 5-fluorouracil and leucovorin (LV5FU2). 

**Aim:** To evaluate the efficacy of a novel treatment regimen of 5-fluorouracil in combination with other anticancer agents against the conventional 5-FU regimen for first-line treatment in mCRC. Primary endpoints include OS and PFS.

**Treatment Comparison:**
- **Control Arm:** Standardization with the 5-FU and leucovorin regimen, administered bi-weekly for an initial duration of 12 weeks, followed by a 6-week evaluation period.
- **Experimental Arm:** Combination therapy including 5-fluorouracil (400 mg/m²) alongside two experimental agents (Agent A and Agent B) both administered bi-weekly for the same duration as the control group.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed metastatic colorectal adenocarcinoma.
- Unresectable metastatic disease with measurable lesions based on RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1.
- Sufficient hematological parameters, renal and liver function test results.
- Male and female participants of childbearing age must agree to use effective contraception during the treatment period.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment for metastatic colorectal cancer (prior adjuvant therapies are permissible if completed >12 months before).
- Any symptomatic central nervous system metastases, as indicated by neurologic symptoms.
- History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to 5-fluorouracil or other component drugs.
- Significant cardiovascular infirmities or active coronary artery disease.
- Pregnant or lactating women, or those who are of reproductive age and unwilling to use contraception.
- Concurrent use of other investigational agents or previous treatments involving the agents in the experimental arm.

**Randomization:** Patients will be randomized through a centralized, secure process, employing stratification based on site, performance status, and number of metastatic sites.

**Outcomes:** 
- Primary outcome: Median OS and PFS at 12 months post-treatment initiation.
- Secondary outcomes: Response rates, time to progression after therapy, quality of life assessments through validated questionnaires, and toxicity profiles as per the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) guidelines.

**Follow-Up:** Participants will undergo assessments every 8 weeks for disease progression until the end of the study or death. The quality of life will be assessed using standardized questionnaires at baseline and subsequently every two months until either progression or the cut-off date of the trial.

**Sample Size and Statistical Analysis:** A total of 600 patients (300 per arm) will be determined necessary to identify a significant difference in median OS from 12 months in the control arm to 15 months in the experimental arm. Statistical analyses will employ an intention-to-treat principle, assessing both unadjusted and adjusted hazard ratios for OS and PFS.

**Estimated Duration of the Trial:** The estimated duration for patient enrollment is 4 years, with a minimum follow-up of 12 months post-treatment initiation.

**Expected Results:** The hypothesis predicts enhanced clinical efficacy of the 5-FU combination therapy compared to standard therapy, seeking to improve overall outcomes. However, this trial ultimately failed, yielding no statistically significant difference in OS and PFS, thereby reaffirming the complexities in the management of mCRC and underlining the necessity for ongoing research and development in treatment paradigms.
