**Title:** A Phase II Study of Regorafenib Versus Standard Supportive Care in Patients With Refractory Metastatic Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma Following First-Line Treatment

**Study Synopsis:** This prospective, multicenter, open-label, randomized Phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of regorafenib compared to standard supportive care in patients with refractory metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who have experienced progression following first-line therapy. Conclusively, the hypothesis asserts that regorafenib may afford a clinically significant benefit in overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS), despite the compound's previous negative results in other metastatic solid tumors.

**Primary Objective:**  
To assess the overall survival (OS) of patients with refractory metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma treated with regorafenib compared to standard supportive care.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
1. To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS).  
2. To assess quality of life (QoL) using the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire during study duration.  
3. To characterize the safety and tolerability of regorafenib in this cohort.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma with disease progression post-first-line chemotherapy (e.g., FOLFIRINOX or gemcitabine-based regimen) within 6 months.  
- Age ≥ 18 years.  
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.  
- Adequate organ function as assessed by laboratory tests conducted ≤ 14 days prior to randomization:  
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x upper limits of normal (ULN)  
  - Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) ≤ 2.5 x ULN  
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 x ULN  
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm3, hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL, absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm3  
- Measurable disease defined via RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
- Signed informed consent obtained prior to any study-related procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Previous treatment with regorafenib or any other investigational antiangiogenic agents.  
- Presence of symptomatic primary malignant disease outside pancreatic adenocarcinoma.  
- Patients with active or clinically significant cardiovascular disease, including congestive heart failure (NYHA Class III-IV), uncontrolled hypertension (systolic > 150 mmHg or diastolic > 90 mmHg), or prior myocardial infarction within 6 months.  
- Known history of bleeding diathesis or active coagulopathy.  
- Active infection requiring systemic therapy.  
- Significant ongoing hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B or C).  
- Any unresolved toxicities from prior therapies classified as NCI-CTCAE Grade ≥ 2 (excluding alopecia).  
- Women who are pregnant or breastfeeding or with a positive pregnancy test.  
- Patients unable to take oral medication due to any condition.

**Endpoints:**  
- **Primary Endpoint:** Overall survival (OS) measured from the date of randomization to the date of death from any cause.  
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Progression-free survival (PFS) assessed via imaging every 8 weeks, quality of life assessments, and adverse events graded per NCI-CTCAE version 5.0.

**Statistical Considerations:**  
This trial was planned to enroll 200 participants, randomized in a 1:1 ratio to either regorafenib or supportive care. Based on historical context and anticipated effect sizes, a one-sided alpha of 0.05 was chosen for all hypotheses. The primary analysis was conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method for survival curves, with comparisons via the log-rank test.

**Study Results:**  
Due to the high rate of adverse events observed and a lack of significant differences in OS and PFS between regorafenib and supportive care, the trial was terminated prematurely after enrolling 125 participants. The incidence of clinically significant grade 3 and 4 toxicities outweighed the overall survival benefit when compared to the prior standard of care. Specifically, 62% of participants on regorafenib experienced adverse events classified as grade 3 or higher, predominantly hypertension, hand-foot skin reaction, and fatigue.

**Conclusion:**  
This trial did not demonstrate a clinically meaningful improvement in overall survival or progression-free survival for regorafenib in the designated cohort of patients with refractory metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma. These findings further corroborate the complexities in successfully integrating regorafenib into therapeutic regimens for specific malignancies. Future studies should consider alternative approaches or combinations, potentially addressing the underlying resistance pathways pertinent to pancreatic cancer.
