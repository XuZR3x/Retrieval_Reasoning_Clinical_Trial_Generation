**Failed Clinical Trial of Sunitinib Malate: Sunitinib in Treating Patients with Refractory Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma**

**A Phase II Study of Sunitinib Malate (Sutent®, SU11248, NCI-Supplied Agent, NSC # 736511) in Patients with Refractory Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma** 

This phase II trial aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of sunitinib malate in patients with refractory pancreatic adenocarcinoma following failure of standard chemotherapeutic regimens. Sunitinib may inhibit tumor growth by blocking receptor tyrosine kinases involved in angiogenesis and cellular proliferation.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. Evaluate the rate of progression-free survival for at least 6 months and overall tumor response in patients with refractory pancreatic adenocarcinoma treated with sunitinib malate.
II. Identify the frequency and severity of adverse events associated with sunitinib malate administration.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. Examine overall survival rates in the study cohort.
II. Determine changes in biomarkers associated with tumor growth and angiogenesis.

**OUTLINE:** 
This is a multicenter, open-label study. Eligible patients will receive oral sunitinib malate at a dose of 50 mg daily for 28 days, followed by a 14-day rest period. Treatment will continue in cycles, repeating every 42 days in the absence of disease progression or intolerable toxicity. Patients will be followed for survival status and long-term outcomes for up to 3 years.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** 
A total of 60 patients will be enrolled in this study.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically confirmed pancreatic adenocarcinoma with metastatic disease.
- Recurrent or refractory to prior therapies including at least one line of systemic chemotherapy such as FOLFIRINOX or gemcitabine-based therapy.
- Performance status: ECOG 0-2.
- Adequate organ function as defined by clinical laboratory parameters.
- At least one measurable lesion as defined by RECIST 1.1.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- History of allergic reactions to sunitinib malate or other similar compounds.
- Presence of significant comorbid conditions that may impede treatment compliance or confound results.
- Active, uncontrolled infection requiring systemic treatment.
- Prior treatment with other antiangiogenic agents (e.g., bevacizumab, pazopanib) or investigational therapies within the last 30 days.
- Secondary malignancies within 5 years prior to study entry, except for nonmelanoma skin cancer.
- Major surgery, including open biopsy, within 28 days of treatment initiation.

**ENDPOINTS:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** The primary endpoint will be the rate of patients achieving progression-free survival at 6 months and overall tumor response measured by RECIST.
- **Secondary Endpoint:** Overall survival at 1 year, quality of life assessments using validated questionnaires, and safety profiles assessed through monitoring adverse events and laboratory values.

**STATISTICAL CONSIDERATIONS:**
The sample size calculation will be based on achieving 80% power to detect a significant improvement in progression-free survival compared to a historical control, with a one-sided alpha level of 0.05. Descriptive statistics will summarize demographic and clinical baseline characteristics. Kaplan-Meier survival curves will be used to assess survival outcomes.

**CONCLUSION:**
This clinical trial aims to elucidate the role of sunitinib malate in managing refractory pancreatic adenocarcinoma. However, the history of sunitinib's inconsistent efficacy in prior studies, particularly in tumors with complex molecular biology such as pancreatic cancer, raises concerns for its success in this cohort. Potential limitations include high rates of treatment-related adverse events and the intrinsic aggressive nature of the disease, which may limit the overall response rates expected. Thus, the study is designed with a comprehensive safety monitoring plan to ensure patient safety throughout the trial duration. 

**RESULTS**:
Despite targeting a crucial pathway in tumor angiogenesis, previous failures in similar settings signal the need for further investigation into biomarkers of response to inform the clinical use of sunitinib in pancreatic cancer treatment paradigms. The anticipated outcome is a thorough assessment of efficacy and tolerability, contributing to the growing body of literature surrounding sunitinib malate in oncology.
