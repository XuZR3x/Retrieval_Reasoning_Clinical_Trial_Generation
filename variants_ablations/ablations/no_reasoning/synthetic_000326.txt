**Failed Clinical Trial of EXPAREL: Evaluation of Liposomal Bupivacaine in Patients Undergoing Lower Limb Amputation**

**TITLE OF THE RESEARCH PROJECT**  
A Randomized Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy of Bupivacaine Extended-release Liposome Injection for Post-Amputation Pain Management in Adults

**PROJECT SUMMARY**  
This study aims to assess the safety and efficacy of 266 mg of liposomal bupivacaine (EX-PAREL) administered at the surgical site in patients undergoing major lower limb amputation. The hypothesis posits that EXPAREL can significantly reduce postoperative opioid consumption and enhance pain control compared to placebo.

**STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM**  
Patients undergoing major lower limb amputations often experience considerable post-operative pain, typically controlled through opioid analgesics. This reliance on opioids raises concerns regarding potential abuse and dependence, especially in patients with chronic pain histories or those at risk for substance use disorders. There is a critical need for effective analgesic alternatives to minimize opioid usage and improve postoperative recovery.

**THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK**  
Previous studies have suggested the potential of liposomal bupivacaine as a long-acting local anesthetic for various surgical settings. The investigators speculate that administering 266 mg of liposomal bupivacaine at the site of amputation will lower opioid consumption and provide adequate analgesia, thereby reducing the risk of dependency and facilitating quicker rehabilitation.

**RESEARCH OBJECTIVE**  
To evaluate the analgesic efficacy of single-dose liposomal bupivacaine versus placebo in reducing postoperative narcotic consumption and assessing overall pain management in patients undergoing major lower limb amputation.

**METHODOLOGY**  
Patients scheduled for elective lower limb amputation at the [Institution Name] will be eligible for inclusion. The exclusion criteria include:  
1) History of opioid use disorder or significant renal/hepatic impairment  
2) Patients with a previous amputated limb on the same side  
3) Patients with systemic infections requiring antibiotic therapy  
4) Known allergies to local anesthetics  
5) Concurrent participation in other clinical trials.

Randomization will be executed using a computerized random number generator, allocating subjects to receive either the EXPAREL injection (20 mL containing 266 mg of liposomal bupivacaine) or a matched placebo (20 mL of saline). The surgical team will remain blinded to the treatment allocation. Pain levels will be monitored using a numeric rating scale (NRS) at specified intervals postoperatively (1, 6, 12, and 24 hours), alongside total opioid consumption quantified in morphine equivalents.

Follow-up will incorporate 28-day assessments for pain levels and overall satisfaction via telephonic interviews. The primary outcome measure will focus on total opioid consumption within the first 72 hours post-surgery. Secondary measures include pain scores and patient satisfaction ratings at all postoperative time points.

**PLAN FOR ANALYSIS OF RESULTS**  
To detect a 40% reduction in opioid consumption with liposomal bupivacaine treatment vs. placebo, and accounting for a significance level of 0.05, a total sample size of 120 patients (60 per group) is required for adequate power. Descriptive and inferential statistics will be applied to evaluate differences in primary and secondary outcome measures.

**TIMETABLE**  
The study will initiate upon receiving Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval and will extend for a duration of 18 months. 

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Scheduled for elective major lower limb amputation  
- Age 18 years or older  
- Ability to provide informed consent.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Known allergy to bupivacaine, liposomes, or other local anesthetics  
- Active infections at the surgical site  
- Significant psychiatric disorders impacting informed consent or compliance.  

**RESULTS AND DISCUSSION**  
Despite thorough preparations and adherence to the methodology, the clinical trial failed to demonstrate a significant difference between the treatment and placebo groups in terms of postoperative narcotic consumption and pain management. The results indicated no statistically significant reduction in total opioid usage, nor did the patients express a higher level of contentment with analgesic management when administered EXPAREL compared to saline placebo. Subsequent analyses revealed potential contributing factors including the psychological state of the patients, variability in pain threshold, and underlying chronic pain conditions that were not adequately controlled for in the selection process.

Patients’ reliance on opioids persisted, emphasizing the need for further exploration into effective multimodal pain management approaches. Additionally, the trial highlighted the challenges of developing successful analgesic interventions within this particular surgical population, demonstrating the complex interplay among individual pain experiences, psychological factors, and the effectiveness of pharmacotherapy.

All subjects were treated ethically, with informed consent obtained prior to participation. Patient data were maintained with strict confidentiality per applicable regulations, ensuring safety and ethical standards throughout the research process.
