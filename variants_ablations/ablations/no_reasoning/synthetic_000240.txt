**Title:** A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Temozolomide in Combination with Decitabine for Patients with Refractory Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) - TEMO-DEC Study

**RATIONALE:** Temozolomide is a well-established alkylating agent that has demonstrated efficacy in various forms of malignancies, particularly in gliomas and melanoma. Decitabine is a cytidine analog that exerts its antineoplastic effects through DNA hypomethylation, potentially restoring normal gene function in tumor cells. This combined approach may enhance the anti-tumor effects in patients with refractory acute myeloid leukemia by altering the epigenetic landscape and enhancing the efficacy of temozolomide.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aims to evaluate the safety, efficacy, and pharmacodynamics of temozolomide in combination with decitabine in patients with refractory AML, hypothesizing that the synergy of both agents may overcome inherent chemoresistance and improve clinical outcomes.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objective:** To determine the overall response rate (complete remission [CR] + complete remission with incomplete blood count recovery [CRi]) of the combination of temozolomide and decitabine in patients with refractory AML.
- **Secondary Objectives:**
  - To assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates.
  - To investigate the safety profile and tolerability of the combined therapy, focusing on adverse event frequency.
  - To explore the pharmacokinetic interactions between temozolomide and decitabine.
  - To evaluate the changes in DNA methylation patterns correlated to treatment response.

**OUTLINE:** This open-label, multicenter Phase II study will enroll patients aged 60 years and older with histopathologically confirmed refractory AML. Participants will receive decitabine intravenously at 20 mg/m² over 1 hour on days 1-5 and temozolomide orally at a daily dose of 150 mg/m² on days 8-20, in a 28-day treatment cycle. Treatment will continue for up to six cycles unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Safety assessments and pharmacokinetics will be conducted during the first cycle of treatment.

**ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA:**
- Adults ≥ 60 years of age.
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of refractory AML, defined as failure to achieve a CR or CRi after two cycles of standard induction therapy.
- Adequate organ function: 
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) > 1,000 cells/mm³
  - Platelets > 50,000 cells/mm³
  - Total bilirubin < 2 times the upper limit of normal
  - AST/ALT < 2.5 times the upper limit of normal
- Performance status of Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) ≤ 2.
- Ability to provide written informed consent.

**INELIGIBILITY CRITERIA:**
- Currently receiving investigational agents or other chemotherapeutics.
- History of allergic reactions to temozolomide or decitabine.
- Patients with active infection, including HIV or hepatitis.
- History of significant gastrointestinal disease affecting drug absorption.
- Patients with hematologic malignancies other than AML.
- Concurrent malignancies, except for surgically treated basal cell carcinoma or cervical carcinoma in situ.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES:** The primary endpoint will be evaluated based on the overall response rate after six cycles of therapy, analyzed per the International Working Group criteria for AML. Secondary endpoints will include PFS and OS, measured from the initiation of treatment until disease progression or death. Safety will be assessed through monitoring of treatment-related adverse events and laboratory evaluations.

**FOLLOW-UP:** Patients will be monitored every 3 months post-treatment for up to 2 years to evaluate long-term outcomes and potential late effects of therapy. Regular imaging and bone marrow biopsies will be utilized to assess remission status and disease recurrence.
