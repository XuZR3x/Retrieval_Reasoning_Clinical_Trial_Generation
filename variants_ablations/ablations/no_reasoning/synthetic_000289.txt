Successful clinical trial of hydrocortisone: Hydrocortisone for the Management of Chronic Fatigue Syndrome Clinical Efficacy of Hydrocortisone in Patients with Chronic Fatigue Syndrome: A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial 

Objective: The purpose of this trial is to evaluate the effects of low-dose hydrocortisone therapy on symptom management and quality of life in patients diagnosed with Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (CFS). Given the multifactorial nature of CFS, characterized by persistent fatigue not alleviated by rest and accompanied by various systemic symptoms, this trial aims to assess whether hydrocortisone can address potential underlying adrenal insufficiency and enhance overall functional capacity.

Study Design: This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial structured to assess the therapeutic outcomes following an intervention with hydrocortisone compared to placebo in adult patients diagnosed with CFS. The hypothesis is predicated on the notion that patients with CFS may exhibit relative adrenal insufficiency, thus leading to dysregulated energy metabolism and heightened fatigue. 

Methods: Upon obtaining informed consent, eligible participants will be randomly assigned to receive either oral hydrocortisone (15 mg/day) or placebo for a duration of 12 weeks. The trial will include baseline evaluations and follow-ups at 4, 8, and 12 weeks post-intervention to assess changes in fatigue severity, quality of life, and cognitive function. The primary outcome will be measured using the Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS), while secondary outcomes will encompass the Short Form Health Survey (SF-36) and cognitive assessments via standardized neuropsychological tests.

Eligibility Criteria:
Inclusion Criteria:
- Adults aged 18 to 65 years.
- Diagnosis of Chronic Fatigue Syndrome as per the revised Fukuda criteria.
- Baseline fatigue score of ≥ 4 on the FSS.
- Ability to provide informed consent.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Presence of secondary causes for fatigue (e.g., hypothyroidism, sleep apnea).
- Chronic inflammatory diseases or autoimmune disorders.
- History of significant psychiatric disorders.
- Current use of systemic corticosteroids or other adrenal-modulating therapies prior to study participation.
- Pregnancy or lactation.

Endpoints: The primary endpoint will be the change in fatigue severity from baseline to week 12, assessed using the FSS. Secondary endpoints include the change in quality of life scores, cognitive function, and patient-reported outcomes regarding daily activities and overall well-being.

Ethical Considerations: The study protocol will conform to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and has received approval from the Institutional Review Board at each participating center. Written informed consent will be procured from all participants prior to enrollment.

Statistical Analysis: Data will be analyzed using intention-to-treat principles. Between-group differences in primary and secondary outcomes will be assessed utilizing ANOVA for repeated measures, with a significance level set at p < 0.05.

Conclusion: If successful, this trial will elucidate the potential of hydrocortisone as a therapeutic modality for the management of Chronic Fatigue Syndrome, thereby contributing to improved clinical strategies aimed at enhancing the quality of life for affected individuals. The findings could spur further investigation into endocrine dysregulation in this patient population, forging paths to more effective management protocols.
