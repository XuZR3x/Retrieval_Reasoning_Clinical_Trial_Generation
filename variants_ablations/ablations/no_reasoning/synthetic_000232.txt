**Failed Clinical Trial of Plerixafor: Plerixafor in Combination with Nivolumab for Refractory Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma**

**Phase I/Ib Study of Plerixafor and Nivolumab in Patients with Refractory Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma**  
This study evaluates the safety, tolerability, and preliminary efficacy of plerixafor in conjunction with nivolumab in patients with classical Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL) that is refractory to standard therapies. Classical Hodgkin lymphoma represents a distinctive subgroup of lymphoid malignancies characterized by the presence of Reed-Sternberg cells. While the advent of checkpoint inhibitors, such as nivolumab, has shown promise in relapsed/refractory cases, there remains an unmet need for effective treatment options. Plerixafor, known for its effect in mobilizing hematopoietic stem cells, is hypothesized to augment the response of nivolumab by enhancing immune cell infiltration into the tumor microenvironment.

**Study Objectives:**  
- **Part 1:** Determine the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of plerixafor when co-administered with nivolumab, with a focus on identifying dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs).
- **Part 2:** Assess the clinical efficacy of the combination therapy in terms of overall response rate (ORR) and progression-free survival (PFS).

**Study Drugs:**  
- **Plerixafor:** A small molecule inhibitor of the CXCR4 chemokine receptor, primarily employed for its utility in mobilizing progenitor cells in the setting of stem cell transplantation.
- **Nivolumab:** A human monoclonal antibody targeting the programmed cell death-1 (PD-1) receptor, enhancing T-cell mediated immune responses against tumor cells.

**Study Design:**  
The study consists of a planned enrollment of 60 subjects. Participants will be divided into cohorts receiving escalating doses of plerixafor for the first 5 days in combination with fixed-dose nivolumab administered every 14 days. Treatment regimens will occur until disease progression or intolerable toxicity, with follow-up assessments performed at defined intervals.

**Drug Administration:**  
Plerixafor is administered subcutaneously at the MTD established during the previous cohort. Nivolumab is infused intravenously at a dose of 3 mg/kg on Days 1 and 15 of each cycle. Cycles are to be repeated every 28 days.

**Monitoring & Assessments:**  
- Laboratory evaluations, including complete blood counts and serum chemistries, will be conducted bi-weekly.
- Tumor assessments via imaging (CT/MRI) will be performed at baseline and every two cycles.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Histologically confirmed classical Hodgkin lymphoma, relapsed post-autologous stem cell transplant or refractory to at least two lines of therapy.
2. Age ≥ 18 years.
3. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
4. Adequate organ function as per the study protocol.
5. Signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Prior treatment with PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors.
2. Presence of primary central nervous system lymphoma (PCNSL) or uncontrolled CNS metastases.
3. Active autoimmune diseases or requiring systemic immunosuppressive therapy.
4. History of severe allergic reactions to monoclonal antibodies.
5. Concurrent malignancies, except for non-melanoma skin cancer and in situ cervical cancer.

**Feasibility and Safety Monitoring:**  
The study will employ close monitoring protocols to assess for DLTs within the initial treatment cycles, specifically concerning myelosuppression, gastrointestinal toxicities, or hypersensitivity reactions. 

**Outcome Measures:**  
- **Primary Endpoint:** MTD of plerixafor in combination with nivolumab as determined by DLT incidence.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** ORR, PFS, and quality of life assessments using validated questionnaires.

**Trial Status:**  
The trial was terminated prematurely after the first cohort due to insufficient safety data and observed DLTs, including severe neutropenia and infusion reactions. Additionally, efficacy outcomes demonstrated no significant improvement in ORR compared to historical benchmarks of nivolumab monotherapy in this patient population. Investigators concluded that while the mechanistic rationale for this combination was plausible, the therapy's overall risk-benefit ratio did not support further clinical development in this context.

**Sponsor:**  
This study was conducted under the auspices of an academic medical institution specializing in oncology research and was supported by collaboration with pharmaceutical partners.
