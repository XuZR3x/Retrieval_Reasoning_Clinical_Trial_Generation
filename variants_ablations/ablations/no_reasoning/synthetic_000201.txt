Failed clinical trial of etoposide: Etoposide, Doxorubicin, and Cyclosporin A in Relapsed Acute Myeloid Leukemia A Phase II Study of Etoposide, Doxorubicin, and Cyclosporin A in Patients With Relapsed Acute Myeloid Leukemia Despite advancements in treatment for acute myeloid leukemia (AML), relapses remain challenging, and response rates to currently available therapies have shown minimal improvement. This Phase II trial investigated the efficacy of a novel regimen involving etoposide, doxorubicin, and cyclosporin A, with the intent to enhance the cytotoxic effects of chemotherapy through the modulation of drug resistance mechanisms. The rationale for this combination was based on the premise that cyclosporin A may potentiate the cytotoxic effects of etoposide and doxorubicin, particularly in relapsed patients displaying multidrug resistance. Design: Open-label, Phase II clinical trial. Primary Objective: To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) of etoposide, doxorubicin, and cyclosporin A in patients with relapsed AML. Secondary endpoints included safety profile, time to treatment failure, and overall survival (OS). Treatment: Etoposide was administered intravenously at a dose of 150 mg/m² on days 1-3, doxorubicin was delivered intravenously at a dose of 60 mg/m² on day 1, while cyclosporin A was given orally at a dosage of 5 mg/kg/day from days 1 to 14. Each treatment cycle spanned 21 days. Inclusion Criteria: - Histologically confirmed acute myeloid leukemia with relapsed disease - Age 18-75 years - Performance status ECOG 0-2 - Adequate organ function (renal function CrCl > 50 ml/min, hepatic function bilirubin < 1.5 x UNL) - Previous treatment including at least one regimen of standard chemotherapy or consolidation therapy - Informed consent signed by all patients Exclusion Criteria: - Patients with central nervous system involvement - Prior treatment with cyclosporin A - History of congestive heart failure or arrhythmias - Evidence of uncontrolled infection - Concurrent malignancies - Fertile men and women not using contraception - Allergy to any component of the study drugs or excipients Primary endpoint was the ORR defined as complete remission (CR) plus partial remission (PR), assessed by standard criteria. Safety was evaluated by monitoring adverse events in accordance with Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Unfortunately, the study was terminated early due to inadequate efficacy observed in the response rate (primary endpoint) and a higher than anticipated incidence of severe non-hematological toxicities, including cardiotoxicity and hepatotoxicity. Despite rigorous patient selection and prior positive preclinical data supporting the combination of etoposide and doxorubicin with cyclosporin A, the trial did not yield the anticipated clinical benefits for this patient population, warranting the conclusion of the study.
