**Failed Clinical Trial of 5-fluorouracil: A Phase II Study of 5-fluorouracil and Gemcitabine for Patients with Advanced Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma**

**Objective:**  
To evaluate the efficacy of the combination of 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) and gemcitabine in patients with advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma, specifically focusing on progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates.

**Primary Objective:**  
To determine the PFS of patients treated with 5-fluorouracil combined with gemcitabine compared to historical controls receiving gemcitabine alone.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
- To assess the overall response rate (ORR) including complete response, partial response, and stable disease.
- To evaluate OS at 1 year and 2 years post-treatment initiation.
- To describe the safety profile and incidence of adverse events related to the combination therapy.
- To analyze quality of life (QoL) metrics before, during, and after the treatment period using validated questionnaires.
- To investigate pharmacogenomic markers potentially associated with treatment efficacy.

**Background:**  
Advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma is a malignancy characterized by poor prognostic outcomes and limited treatment options. While gemcitabine has been a standard treatment modality, the incorporation of 5-fluorouracil, which functions as an antimetabolite hindering DNA synthesis, was hypothesized to offer enhanced therapeutic benefit through synergistic action. Existing literature provides inconclusive data on the effectiveness of this combination, warranting further investigation.

**Study Design:**  
This is a multi-center, randomized, open-label Phase II clinical trial. A total of 100 patients will be enrolled across three participating institutions.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma not amenable to surgical resection.
2. Age ≥ 18 years and ≤ 75 years.
3. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–2.
4. Sufficient organ function as defined by laboratory parameters: 
   - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
   - Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³
   - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL
   - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL.
5. Patients must be aware of the neoplastic condition and provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Concurrent malignancies or previous treatments for pancreatic cancer.
2. Known hypersensitivity to gemcitabine or 5-fluorouracil.
3. Significant liver disease (Child-Pugh class B or C).
4. Pregnant or lactating women.
5. Patients with active and uncontrolled infections.

**Intervention:**  
Participants will receive 5-fluorouracil via continuous infusion at a dosage of 300 mg/m²/day for 5 days, administered concurrently with gemcitabine at a dose of 1,000 mg/m² on days 1, 8, and 15, every 28 days. Each participant may undergo up to 6 cycles of treatment based on tolerance and clinical response.

**Follow-Up and Assessment:**  
Patients will undergo radiological assessment using computed tomography scans after every 2 cycles of treatment, and clinical evaluations will occur bi-weekly to monitor response and side effects. Efficacy will be assessed using the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) guidelines.

**Outcome Measures:**  
- The primary efficacy endpoint will be measured by the PFS rate at 6 months, defined as the time from the start of treatment to disease progression or death from any cause.
- The secondary endpoints will include ORR, OS at 1 and 2 years, reported adverse events according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), and QoL indices.

**Results and Conclusion:**  
Despite stringent eligibility criteria and a robust study design, the trial failed to demonstrate significant improvements in PFS and OS when compared to historical controls receiving gemcitabine alone. Adverse effects were frequent, with instances of severe gastrointestinal toxicity and neutropenia directly linked to the 5-fluorouracil regimen. Consequently, the utility of this combination for advancing treatment options in pancreatic adenocarcinoma is not supported by the evidence from this study.

This trial conclusively highlights the challenges of identifying effective combinations in the treatment of pancreatic cancer, where the complexities of tumor biology may limit the efficacy of traditional chemotherapeutic approaches. Further explorations into alternative therapeutic frameworks or targeted therapies are warranted in this aggressive malignancy.
