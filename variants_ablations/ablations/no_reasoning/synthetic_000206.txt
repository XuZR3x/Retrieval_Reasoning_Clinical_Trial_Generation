**Failed clinical trial of mm-121: A Study of MM-121 in Combination With Cisplatin Versus Cisplatin Alone in Patients With Advanced Esophageal Carcinoma**

**A Phase 2 Randomized Open-Label Study of MM-121 in Combination With Cisplatin Versus Cisplatin Alone in Patients With Advanced Esophageal Carcinoma**

The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the efficacy of the combination of MM-121 and cisplatin compared to cisplatin monotherapy in extending progression-free survival (PFS) in patients diagnosed with advanced esophageal carcinoma.

This was a multicenter, open-label, randomized, phase 2 clinical trial conducted among patients with histologically confirmed unresectable metastatic or locally advanced esophageal cancer. A total of 180 patients were enrolled and subsequently randomized (1:1) to receive either MM-121 in combination with cisplatin or cisplatin alone.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged ≥ 18 years with a diagnosis of unresectable or metastatic esophageal carcinoma.
- Must have received no more than one prior systemic therapy for advanced disease.
- Performance status of ECOG 0 or 1.
- Tissue submitted for heregulin biomarker testing.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Evidence of inflammatory esophageal disease or other active malignancies.
- Previous treatment with any ErbB3-targeting therapies.
- Clinically significant cardiac or renal diseases.
- Symptomatic central nervous system (CNS) metastases requiring treatment.

**Progression-Free Survival (PFS) Definition:**
PFS was defined as the duration from randomization until the first occurrence of disease progression or death from any cause, as assessed by the investigator in accordance with RECIST version 1.1. Patients without documented progression or death at the time of analysis were censored at their last valid tumor assessment.

**Study Outcomes:**
The anticipated primary endpoint was improvement in PFS in the MM-121 plus cisplatin group compared to the control group receiving cisplatin monotherapy. Secondary endpoints included overall response rate (ORR), overall survival (OS), and safety profile of the combination therapy.

**Statistical Analysis:**
The PFS distributions for both treatment arms were to be analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method, along with comparisons through the log-rank test. The OS was evaluated through time-to-event analysis from treatment initiation to death.

**Results:**
Despite the initial hypothesis that the addition of MM-121 would enhance the efficacy of cisplatin in this patient population, the trial ultimately did not meet its primary endpoint. PFS as assessed after a median follow-up of 12 months demonstrated no statistically significant difference between the combination therapy and cisplatin alone (median PFS of 4.6 months vs. 4.3 months, respectively, p=0.85). Additionally, the safety profile of MM-121 combined with cisplatin mirrored that of cisplatin monotherapy, with no unexpected toxicities noted.

**Conclusions:**
The failure of this clinical trial highlights the need for further investigations into the mechanisms underlying the resistance to ErbB3 inhibition in esophageal carcinoma. Interestingly, heregulin status did not yield predictive value for response in the subgroup analysis. Further exploration into combination therapies utilizing alternative agents may be warranted in subsequent research endeavors. Overall, this study did not support the hypothesis that adding MM-121 to cisplatin enhances clinical outcomes in patients with advanced esophageal carcinoma.
