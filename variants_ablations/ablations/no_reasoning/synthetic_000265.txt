**Successful Clinical Trial of Bortezomib: A Study of Bortezomib and Pembrolizumab in Combination as a Treatment for Relapsed/Refractory Hodgkin Lymphoma**

**Protocol Title:** Phase II Study of Bortezomib (Velcade®) Combined with Pembrolizumab (Keytruda®) for Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Hodgkin Lymphoma

**RATIONALE:** Bortezomib, a proteasome inhibitor, has demonstrated efficacy in various hematologic malignancies and may enhance the anti-tumor effects of pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody, by improving immune recognition of tumor cells. Previous studies indicate that the combination may potentially mediate synergistic effects in patients with relapsed/refractory Hodgkin Lymphoma, resulting in improved outcomes.

**PURPOSE:** This Phase II trial aims to determine the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of bortezomib in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with relapsed or refractory Hodgkin Lymphoma.

**OBJECTIVES:**

- **Primary Objective:** To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) of the bortezomib and pembrolizumab combination therapy in patients with relapsed or refractory Hodgkin Lymphoma.
- **Secondary Objectives:** 
  - To assess the progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates.
  - To evaluate the safety profile, including adverse event rates and toxicity assessment using CTCAE v5.0 standards.

**STUDY DESIGN:** This is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm trial. Patients will receive bortezomib administered subcutaneously on days 1, 4, 8, and 11 of a 21-day cycle, while pembrolizumab will be administered intravenously every three weeks. Treatment may continue for up to 12 cycles unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 100 patients will be enrolled in this study over a 24-month period.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**

- Histologically confirmed Hodgkin Lymphoma with relapsed or refractory disease after at least one prior line of therapy.
- Measurable disease (≥1.0 cm in longest dimension) as assessed by radiographic imaging.
- No evidence of CNS involvement.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**

- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status of 0–2.
- Adequate organ function, defined as follows:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500 cells/mm³.
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³.
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 x upper limit of normal (ULN).
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN.
- Negative serum pregnancy test for females of childbearing potential.
- Written informed consent obtained before initiation of protocol-specific procedures.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**

1. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of Hodgkin Lymphoma and evidence of relapsed or refractory disease.
2. Must have received at least one line of prior therapy and must be suitable for bortezomib and pembrolizumab administration.
3. For women of childbearing potential, must agree to use effective contraceptive methods throughout the study period.
4. Patients must not be pregnant or nursing.
5. Ability to provide informed consent.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**

1. Concurrent systemic therapy or radiation therapy while on study treatment.
2. Active uncontrolled infections.
3. History of hypersensitivity reactions to bortezomib or pembrolizumab.
4. Significant cardiac or pulmonary comorbidities that may compromise participation in the study.
5. Prior malignancy (other than adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancer) within 5 years prior to enrollment.
6. Active autoimmune diseases requiring systemic therapy.

**DATA COLLECTION:**
The response to therapy will be assessed at baseline and every 2 cycles using the PET/CT scan according to the Lugano classification for lymphoma response. Adverse events will be recorded using the CTCAE v5.0 criteria.

**STATISTICAL ANALYSIS:**
The primary endpoint will be analyzed using the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle. The ORR will be calculated as the proportion of patients with complete and partial responses, with 95% confidence intervals. Secondary endpoints will similarly follow established statistical analysis methods appropriate for survival data.

**FOLLOW-UP:** Patients will be followed every 3 months for the first year post-treatment and then every 6 months thereafter.

In conclusion, this Phase II trial aims to leverage the unique mechanisms of bortezomib and pembrolizumab, hypothesizing a favorable clinical benefit and manageable safety profile in patients with relapsed or refractory Hodgkin Lymphoma. Successful completion of this trial could establish a new standard of care for patients struggling with this challenging malignancy.
