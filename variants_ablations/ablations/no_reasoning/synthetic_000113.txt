**Candesartan in Non-Ischemic Dilated Cardiomyopathy: A Randomized Control Trial (CANDID) Study**  
A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy of Candesartan in Patients With Non-Ischemic Dilated Cardiomyopathy  

**Objective:**  
The primary objective of this study is to determine the effect of candesartan on the improvement of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) in patients diagnosed with non-ischemic dilated cardiomyopathy (NIDCM) over a 12-month treatment period.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Male or female aged 18 years or older  
- Diagnosis of non-ischemic dilated cardiomyopathy confirmed by echocardiogram  
- Baseline LVEF ≤ 40%  
- Provision of informed consent  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- History of significant renal impairment (creatinine level > 2.0 mg/dL)  
- Uncontrolled hypertension defined as systolic BP > 180 mm Hg or diastolic BP > 110 mm Hg  
- Pregnancy or lactation  
- Previous myocardial infarction or coronary artery bypass graft surgery  

**Endpoints:**  
- The primary endpoint will assess the change in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) from baseline to 12 months, measured via transthoracic echocardiography.  
- Secondary endpoints will include evaluation of New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class, quality of life assessed by EuroQol-5D, and frequency of all-cause hospitalizations related to heart failure during the study period.  

**Statistical Analysis:**  
The statistical analysis will be conducted using intention-to-treat principles. Continuous variables will be analyzed using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) to compare LVEF between the candesartan group and the placebo group, adjusting for baseline values. Secondary endpoints will be assessed using appropriate parametric or non-parametric tests, as required.

**Conclusion:**  
The CANDID study is designed to rigorously assess the efficacy of candesartan in enhancing cardiac function in patients suffering from non-ischemic dilated cardiomyopathy. The expected outcomes derived from this trial may provide insights into the optimal management strategies for patients with heart failure and reduced ejection fraction, potentially substantiating the role of angiotensin receptor blockers in this population. Results from this trial may contribute significantly to establishing guidelines for the treatment of NIDCM moving forward.
