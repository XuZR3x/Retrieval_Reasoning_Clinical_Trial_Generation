Failed clinical trial of reslizumab: Effect of Translational Reslizumab Therapy in Severe Eosinophilic Asthma Patients with Chronic Rhinosinusitis and Nasal Polyps A Phase 2, 12-Week, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Parallel-Group Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Reslizumab in Patients with Severe Eosinophilic Asthma and Comorbid Chronic Rhinosinusitis with Nasal Polyps The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of reslizumab in reducing exacerbation rates and improving asthma control in patients suffering from severe eosinophilic asthma who concurrently have chronic rhinosinusitis and nasal polyps. Inclusion Criteria: 1. Male or female aged 18 to 65 years with a previous diagnosis of asthma per GINA guidelines. 2. Provision of written informed consent prior to participation. 3. Patients must demonstrate a history of poorly controlled asthma, requiring high-dose inhaled corticosteroids in combination with at least one additional controller medication for a minimum of 6 months preceding enrollment. 4. Presence of blood eosinophil levels greater than or equal to 300 cells/microliter at screening. 5. Documented history of chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps confirmed via nasal endoscopy within the last year. 6. Patients must exhibit significant impairments in asthma control, defined as an Asthma Control Questionnaire (ACQ-5) score greater than 1.5 at screening. 7. Participants must be willing and able to understand and comply with study requirements and attend scheduled visits. - Additional criteria apply; please contact the investigator for more information. Exclusion Criteria: 1. Patients requiring systemic corticosteroids for asthma management within the 4 weeks prior to baseline visit. 2. Presence of any other significant comorbid condition that may confound study outcomes or compromise patient safety. 3. Individuals with a diagnosis of hypereosinophilic syndrome or other eosinophil-associated disorders. 4. Patients with a history of malignancy within 5 years prior to screening, except for adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancers. 5. Pregnant or lactating women, or those planning to become pregnant during the study duration. 6. Current smokers or history of smoking equivalent to 10 pack years or more. 7. Any history of severe allergies or hypersensitivity reactions to monoclonal antibodies. 8. Patients with ongoing infections or chronic systemic inflammatory conditions which may interfere with study assessments. - Additional criteria apply; please contact the investigator for more information. The primary endpoint of the trial is the rate of clinically significant asthma exacerbations, defined as the need for systemic corticosteroids, emergency visits, or hospitalizations related to asthma during the 12-week treatment period. Secondary endpoints include change in ACQ-5 score from baseline and improvements in forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1) and peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR) measured at weeks 0, 6, and 12. The analysis of the primary and secondary endpoints will be performed using an intention-to-treat approach, incorporating all randomized participants. Results will be presented as adjusted means with appropriate confidence intervals derived from generalized linear models accounting for baseline covariates. The sample size is predicated on achieving adequate statistical power to detect clinically meaningful differences between treatment groups, with a projected dropout rate considered in the final analysis.
