Failed clinical trial of duloxetine hydrochloride: Evaluation of Duloxetine in Patients With Treatment-Resistant Major Depressive Disorder A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Duloxetine in Treatment-Resistant Major Depressive Disorder The objective of this study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of duloxetine hydrochloride in patients diagnosed with Treatment-Resistant Major Depressive Disorder (TR-MDD), defined as insufficient response to at least two adequate trials of different antidepressants. This trial utilized a multicenter design and was conducted over a 12-week period. Inclusion Criteria: - Males and females aged 18-65 years, diagnosed with TR-MDD as defined by the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5) - Total score on the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD-17) ≥ 20 at both screening and baseline - Patients must have failed to achieve remission after treatment with at least two different classes of antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, or atypical antidepressants) for a minimum of 8 weeks each - Must provide written informed consent in accordance with Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and local regulatory requirements Exclusion Criteria: - Current diagnosis of bipolar disorder, schizophrenia or other psychotic disorders - Presence of any serious or unstable medical condition that could interfere with study participation or the assessment of outcomes, including severe cardiovascular, hepatic, or renal dysfunction - Any prior treatment with investigational drugs within 30 days prior to the screening visit, or any implantable device within that timeframe - History of substance abuse or dependence within the past year - Patients exhibiting suicidal ideation or behavior according to the Clinical Interview Schedule (CIS) - Pregnant or lactating women, or women of childbearing potential not using effective contraceptive methods The primary endpoint was the change from baseline to week 12 in the HAMD-17 total score, measuring the overall severity of depressive symptoms. Secondary endpoints included the response rate defined as a ≥50% reduction in HAMD-17 score and safety assessments through adverse event reporting. 

Despite the proposed significance of the intervention, this clinical trial concluded with a failure to demonstrate a statistically significant difference in efficacy compared to placebo. Notably, adverse effects were prevalent, with an increase in reports of gastrointestinal and nervous system disorders, leading to a high discontinuation rate amongst participants across both active and placebo arms. Thus, duloxetine hydrochloride's role in managing TR-MDD requires further exploration through alternative therapeutic avenues, given these outcomes indicate insufficient benefit-risk superiority.
