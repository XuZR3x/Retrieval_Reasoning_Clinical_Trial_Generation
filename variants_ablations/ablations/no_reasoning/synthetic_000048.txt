**Failed Clinical Trial of Mirabegron: A Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Mirabegron in Patients With Chronic Pelvic Pain Syndrome**

**Study Title:** A Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Mirabegron in Patients With Chronic Pelvic Pain Syndrome

**Introduction:** Chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CPPS) represents a significant challenge in urology. As a complex and multifactorial condition, it encompasses various etiologies, including inflammation, neuropathic pain, and bladder dysfunction. Previous studies have shown that patients suffering from CPPS often exhibit symptoms such as urinary urgency, frequency, and chronic pain, which contribute to significant morbidity. Mirabegron, a selective β3-adrenoceptor agonist, has demonstrated efficacy in treating overactive bladder by promoting bladder relaxation. Therefore, it is hypothesized that mirabegron may ameliorate symptoms in patients with CPPS.

**Study Design:** This is a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled phase II trial aimed at evaluating the effects of mirabegron on symptom improvement as well as safety in CPPS patients over a duration of 12 weeks. A total of 150 participants will be recruited from multiple centers. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either mirabegron 50 mg once daily or matching placebo.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adult patients aged 18-75 years diagnosed with CPPS according to NIH criteria, presenting with chronic pelvic pain for at least 6 months.
2. Patients must have demonstrated moderate to severe symptoms as measured by the National Institutes of Health Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Index (NIH-CPSI) total score of ≥ 15.
3. Patients must be stable on their current medication regimen for at least 4 weeks prior to enrollment without significant changes to their management plan.
4. Patients must provide written informed consent and possess the capacity to understand study protocols.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients with a history of bladder or prostate surgery within the past 6 months.
2. Patients with ongoing urinary tract infections or any condition likely to exacerbate pelvic pain.
3. Previous treatment with mirabegron or any other β3-adrenergic receptor agonists.
4. Patients with significant cardiovascular disease including uncontrolled hypertension.
5. Patients with prior diagnosis of malignancy or chronic pelvic inflammatory disease that may confound results.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Outcome:** Reduction in NIH-CPSI total scores by ≥ 30% from baseline to week 12.
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Changes in additional urinary symptom measures (e.g., International Consultation on Incontinence Questionnaire) and quality of life indices, as well as assessments of safety including adverse events.

**Statistical Analysis:** A total of 150 participants will provide 80% power to detect differences in primary outcomes between the mirabegron and placebo groups, using an alpha level of 0.05. Outcomes will be compared using analysis of variance (ANOVA) and chi-square tests for categorical variables.

**Results:** Recruitment reached a total of 150 participants; however, after six weeks of treatment, interim analysis indicated that the vast majority of patients did not achieve the pre-defined primary endpoint of ≥30% improvement in NIH-CPSI scores. Secondary outcomes, including quality of life measures and urinary symptom indices, did not demonstrate statistically significant differences between treatment and control groups. Moreover, the incidence of reported adverse events, although consistent with prior studies, led to patient discontinuation due to concerns over tolerability, including increased hypertension and tachycardia in 10% of participants taking mirabegron.

**Conclusion:** The trial was prematurely halted due to lack of efficacy and tolerability concerns, which collectively led to the conclusion that mirabegron did not provide substantial symptomatic relief in patients with chronic pelvic pain syndrome. Further research is warranted to explore alternative therapeutic approaches.
