Failed Clinical Trial of Anti-Thymocyte Globulin: Evaluation of Anti-Thymocyte Globulin with Concurrent High-Dose Melphalan for Advanced Multiple Myeloma Patients Undergoing Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation

RATIONALE: The use of anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG) in the pre-transplant conditioning regimen for hematopoietic stem cell transplantation aims to reduce the incidence of graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) while promoting engraftment of donor hematopoietic cells. High-dose melphalan serves as a myeloablative agent to eliminate malignant cells prior to transplantation. The hypothesis posits that this regimen will facilitate improved immunological tolerance of donor cells, enhanced graft-versus-tumor (GVT) effect, and reduced post-transplant complications among patients with advanced multiple myeloma.

PURPOSE: This phase II clinical trial aims to assess the efficacy and safety of ATG combined with high-dose melphalan conditioning in patients with refractory or advanced multiple myeloma receiving allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation.

OBJECTIVES:  
Primary  
- To evaluate the incidence of early graft failure within 100 days post-transplantation among patients receiving ATG as part of the pre-transplant conditioning regimen.  
- To assess the overall response rate to treatment over a 12-month period in patients with measurable disease post-transplant.  

Secondary  
- To monitor the safety profile, including the incidence and severity of both acute and chronic GVHD.  
- To analyze overall survival and progression-free survival rates at 1, 2, and 5 years.  
- To determine the relationship between ATG dosing and chimerism levels in peripheral blood post-transplant.    

OUTLINE: This is a single-arm, open-label clinical trial.  
- Preparative regimen: Patients receive fludarabine IV over 30 minutes on days -10 to -6 and high-dose melphalan IV over 30 minutes on days -4 to -2, followed by ATG IV infusion over 4 hours on days -5 to -2.  
- Graft-versus-host disease prophylaxis: Patients receive cyclosporine IV for 100 days post-transplant, starting on day -1, with addition of methotrexate on days 1, 3, and 6.  
- Allogeneic stem cell transplantation: Patients receive donor hematopoietic stem cells on day 0, followed by supportive care including filgrastim (G-CSF) as needed until white cell recovery.  
- Follow-up: Patients will be assessed weekly for the first three months and quarterly thereafter for a total of 24 months.

PROJECTED ACCRUAL: A total of 30 patients diagnosed with advanced multiple myeloma and suitable for allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation will be enrolled in this study. 

DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:  
- Histologically confirmed multiple myeloma (International Staging System Stage II or III).  
- Evidence of disease progression after at least two lines of therapy including bortezomib and lenalidomide.  
- No active central nervous system involvement.  
- No prior allogeneic transplant.

PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:  
- Age: 18 to 70 years.   
- Performance status: Karnofsky ≥70 or ECOG 0-1 required.   
- Life expectancy: greater than three months.  
- Hematopoietic: sufficient pre-transplant cell counts (WBC > 3,000/mm^3, Platelets > 50,000/mm^3).  
- Renal: Creatinine < 2 mg/dL.  
- Cardiac: Ejection fraction > 40% by echocardiogram.  
More stringent exclusion criteria apply including recent vaccination history and infection status.

PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY: Patients must not have received investigational agents or significant immunosuppressive therapy within 30 days prior to study entry, nor should they be on systemic glucocorticoids.

OUTCOMES:  
- The primary outcome is to assess the rate of graft failure within the first 100 days and overall response rate post-transplantation.  
- Secondary outcomes include the cumulative incidence of acute and chronic GVHD and survival analyses. Additional metrics include assessment of donor chimerism using peripheral blood samples and correlation with ATG dosing.

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS: Data analysis will employ Kaplan-Meier survival estimates for overall survival and progression-free survival, with Cox regression models to assess the influence of variables on clinical outcomes. Safety assessments will be evaluated descriptively.

This clinical trial signifies a meticulous attempt to explore the applicability of ATG in the context of advanced multiple myeloma; nevertheless, it faced a regrettable cessation due to insufficient clinical efficacy and high early post-transplant morbidity, ultimately affirming the challenges associated with immunosuppressive regimens in allogeneic transplantation settings in this patient population.
