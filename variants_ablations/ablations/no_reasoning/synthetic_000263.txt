Failed clinical trial of telbivudine: Efficacy and Safety of Telbivudine in Patients With Chronic Hepatitis B at Risk for Hepatocellular Carcinoma Multicenter, Randomized, Open-label Study of Telbivudine Versus Placebo in Patients With Chronic Hepatitis B Virus Infection and Elevated Alpha-fetoprotein This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of telbivudine in patients with chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection who have elevated alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels, suggesting an increased risk for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). This trial will test whether telbivudine, when administered over a 48-week period, can effectively decrease HBV levels and manage elevated AFP in this high-risk population compared to placebo. Inclusion Criteria: - Documented diagnosis of chronic hepatitis B defined by serological markers, consistent clinical history, and HBV DNA > 5 log10 copies/mL. - Adults aged 18 to 75 years with elevated AFP (> 10 ng/mL). - Liver function tests within normal limits prior to study enrollment. - Patients willing and able to provide informed consent and participate in follow-up assessments. - No prior treatment for HBV or other antiviral therapy within the 12 months preceding the study. Other protocol-defined inclusion criteria may apply. Exclusion Criteria: - Co-infection with hepatitis C virus (HCV), hepatitis D virus (HDV), or human immunodeficiency virus (HIV). - History of hepatic decompensation or liver cirrhosis. - Current or prior malignancy (other than non-melanoma skin cancer) within the past 5 years. - Pregnant or breastfeeding females. - Known hypersensitivity to telbivudine or any of its components. - Use of immunosuppressive therapy or systemic corticosteroids within 3 months prior to study entry. Other protocol-defined exclusion criteria may apply. The Primary Endpoint: The primary endpoint is the rate of sustained virologic response (SVR), defined as an undetectable HBV DNA level at Week 48 and maintenance of this response at the 24-week follow-up visit after cessation of intervention, alongside a significant decrease in AFP levels compared to the baseline. Secondary endpoints include the incidence of adverse events, changes in liver function parameters, and cumulative incidence of hepatocellular carcinoma during the study period.
