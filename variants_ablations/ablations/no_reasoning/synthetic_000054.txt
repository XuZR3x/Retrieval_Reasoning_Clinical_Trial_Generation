Failed clinical trial of hydrocortisone: Efficacy of Hydrocortisone in Acute Exacerbations of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Hydrocortisone in Patients with Acute Exacerbations of COPD Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a prevalent condition that leads to significant morbidity and mortality worldwide. Acute exacerbations of COPD are associated with heightened inflammation, airflow limitation, and further deterioration of lung function. Several studies have posited the potential benefit of corticosteroids, such as hydrocortisone, in managing these exacerbations due to their anti-inflammatory effects. However, previous meta-analyses have shown mixed outcomes regarding the efficacy and safety of corticosteroid treatment, thereby necessitating further investigation. This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of hydrocortisone in alleviating the severity and duration of acute exacerbations in COPD patients. A multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial will be conducted to determine whether hydrocortisone administration can improve clinical outcomes compared to a placebo. The primary outcome will assess the change in forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) from baseline to 14 days post-treatment. Secondary outcomes will include rates of hospitalization, duration of exacerbations, and quality of life measurements assessed using the COPD Assessment Test (CAT) scale. The hypothesis is that patients with acute exacerbations of COPD who receive hydrocortisone will demonstrate significantly improved lung function as measured by FEV1 compared to those receiving placebo. Inclusion Criteria: - Adults aged 40 years or older - Documented diagnosis of COPD (GOLD stage II-IV) - Acute exacerbation requiring physician-initiated treatment - FEV1 < 50% of predicted value - Written informed consent provided by the patient or legally authorized representative Exclusion Criteria: - Concurrent autoimmune disorders requiring steroid therapy - Pregnancy or lactation - History of adverse reactions to corticosteroids - Recent (within the past 30 days) treatment with systemic corticosteroids or receipt of oral corticosteroids exceeding 30 mg/day equivalent - Diagnosis of pneumonia or other significant lung infection at the time of randomization - Previous enrollment in a related hydrocortisone trial This study will enroll 1,200 patients across multiple sites, with randomization to receive either hydrocortisone 50 mg intravenous daily for 5 days versus placebo. Patients will be monitored for clinical efficacy and adverse effects throughout the treatment period and thereafter at 14 days, 30 days, and 90 days for follow-up. The primary endpoints will examine the improvement in lung function, while secondary endpoints will evaluate hospitalization rates and overall health-related quality of life. The findings from this trial will contribute to the understanding of hydrocortisone's role in treating acute exacerbations of COPD and guide future management protocols.
