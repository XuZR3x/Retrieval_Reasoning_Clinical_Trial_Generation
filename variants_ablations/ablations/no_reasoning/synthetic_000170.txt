**Failed Clinical Trial of Sarilumab: Efficacy and Safety Study of Sarilumab in Patients with Psoriatic Arthritis**

**A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Sarilumab in Participants with Active Psoriatic Arthritis Not Adequately Controlled by Conventional DMARD Therapy**

**Primary Objective:**  
- To assess the efficacy of Sarilumab in participants with active Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA) using the ACR response criteria (ACR20) at Week 24.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
- To evaluate the efficacy of Sarilumab based on higher levels of response: ACR 50% and ACR 70% response criteria.  
- To assess the impact on quality of life through the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI).  
- To evaluate joint function improvement as measured by the Health Assessment Questionnaire (HAQ).  
- To determine the safety profile and tolerability of Sarilumab in PsA patients.  
- To analyze the pharmacokinetics of Sarilumab in participants with PsA.

**Study Duration:**  
The total duration for participation in the study for each participant was approximately 32 weeks; this included up to a 4-week screening period, a 24-week double-blind treatment period, and a 4-week safety follow-up.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Diagnosis of PsA based on the Classification Criteria for Psoriatic Arthritis (CASPAR).  
- Participants must have had a minimum of 6 tender and swollen joints, and active skin lesions for at least 6 months prior to screening.  
- Prior treatment with at least one non-biologic Disease Modifying Anti-Rheumatic Drug (DMARD) for a minimum of 12 weeks, with no response or inadequate response, or intolerance to such treatment.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Age < 18 years or > 75 years.  
- History of severe or uncontrolled comorbid conditions including but not limited to cardiovascular events within the last 12 months.  
- Previous treatment with biologic DMARDs or JAK inhibitors within 6 months before screening.  
- Current treatment with systemic corticosteroids exceeding 10 mg/day of prednisone or equivalent within 4 weeks prior to screening.  
- Known hypersensitivity to Sarilumab or any of its components.

**Study Design Overview:**  
A total of 450 participants were enrolled and randomized to receive either Sarilumab or placebo in a 2:1 ratio. The randomization was stratified based on prior DMARD use and severity of PsA. The primary outcome measure was the percentage of participants achieving the ACR20 response at Week 24.

**Results:**  
- Primary Endpoint: At Week 24, the percentage of participants achieving ACR20 in the Sarilumab group was 46%, compared to 48% in the placebo group, failing to meet statistical significance (p=0.675).  
- Secondary Endpoints: ACR50 and ACR70 response rates were similarly unimpressive, with 18% and 6% of participants respectively achieving ACR50 and ACR70.  
- Quality of Life improvement as measured by DLQI showed no significant changes between treatment groups.  
- HAQ scores demonstrated negligible differences with a median reduction of only 0.1 in the Sarilumab group versus 0.2 in the placebo group.

**Safety Profile:**  
The safety analysis revealed comparable adverse events between both groups, with no new safety signals identified. The incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) was reported at 30% in the Sarilumab group and 28% in the placebo group. Notably, there were instances of serious adverse events (SAEs), including infections, reported in both groups but did not substantiate a withdrawable safety profile.

**Conclusion:**  
The trial did not meet its primary efficacy endpoint nor sufficiently demonstrate significant improvements in secondary outcomes, leading to the conclusion that Sarilumab does not provide adequate therapeutic benefit in participants with active Psoriatic Arthritis inadequately controlled by conventional DMARD therapy. Future studies may be warranted to further elucidate the role of Sarilumab in alternative patient populations or in different dosing regimens.
