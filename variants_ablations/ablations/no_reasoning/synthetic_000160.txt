**Failed Clinical Trial of Infliximab: Efficacy and Safety of Infliximab in the Treatment of Severe Psoriatic Arthritis With Joint Involvement (P09876)**

**Severe Psoriatic Arthritis**

**Efficacy and Safety of Infliximab in Severe Psoriatic Arthritis Patients With Peripheral Joint Involvement**

This clinical trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of infliximab (Remicade®) in patients with severe psoriatic arthritis (PsA) with joint involvement, over a treatment period of 24 weeks. Participants will receive an induction regimen of infliximab 5 mg/kg administered via intravenous infusion over a period of 2 hours at Weeks 0, 2, 6, and subsequently every 8 weeks until Week 24. The primary endpoint will focus on the change from baseline in the ACR20 (American College of Rheumatology) response criteria, assessed at Week 24. Secondary endpoints will include evaluation of joint tenderness, inflammation assessed by MRI, and Health Assessment Questionnaire (HAQ) scores.

**Inclusion Criteria:**

- Adults aged 18 years or older.
- Diagnosis of active severe psoriatic arthritis according to the Classification Criteria for Psoriatic Arthritis (CASPAR).
- Presence of at least 5 swollen and tender joints.
- Documented inadequate response to at least one prior conventional disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) or biologic therapy.
- Active skin psoriasis (Psoriasis Area Severity Index [PASI] score ≥ 10).

**Exclusion Criteria:**

- Current or prior history of serious infections (e.g., tuberculosis, sepsis).
- History of malignancy within the last 5 years, except superficial basal cell skin carcinoma.
- Pregnant or nursing women or those planning to become pregnant.
- Known hypersensitivity to infliximab or other components of the formulation.
- Recent history of significant cardiovascular events (e.g., myocardial infarction, stroke) within the past year.

**Primary Outcome Measure:**

- Change from Baseline in ACR20 response at Week 24.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**

- Change from baseline in joint tenderness assessed by the tender joint count.
- Change in joint inflammation evaluated by MRI at Week 24 compared to baseline.
- Change in HAQ scores at Week 24.

Despite its design to harness the potential anti-inflammatory effects of infliximab in mitigating the symptoms of severe psoriatic arthritis, this trial ultimately failed to demonstrate significant clinical efficacy. The expected improvements in primary and secondary endpoints were not achieved, and an unexpected safety profile emerged, including higher rates of infections and adverse events compared to the control group receiving conventional DMARDs.

**Conclusion:**

The disappointing outcomes from this clinical trial highlight the complexities associated with treating severe psoriatic arthritis using TNF-alpha inhibitors such as infliximab. Future studies may need to explore alternative therapeutic strategies, potential combination therapies, or patient subgroups that may benefit from this biologic intervention while addressing concerns related to safety and efficacy in the psoriatic arthritis population.
