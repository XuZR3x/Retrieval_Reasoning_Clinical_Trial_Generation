**Failed Clinical Trial of Perifosine: A Phase II Study of Perifosine in Patients With Recurrent Ovarian Cancer**

**Study Title:** A Phase II Study of Perifosine in Patients With Recurrent Ovarian Cancer After Prior Chemotherapy Treatment

**Introduction:** This is a multicenter, single-arm Phase II trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of perifosine in patients with recurrent ovarian cancer who have demonstrated disease progression following platinum-based chemotherapy. Ovarian cancer presents unique challenges, particularly in patients who have already received extensive treatment and exhibit resistance to multiple lines of therapy. This study aims to investigate whether perifosine can induce clinical benefit by inhibiting key pathways involved in cancer cell survival and proliferation.

**Primary Objective:**
- To assess the 6-month progression-free survival (PFS) rate of patients treated with perifosine after the development of recurrent ovarian cancer.

**Secondary Objectives:**
- To evaluate overall survival (OS) in participants receiving perifosine.
- To analyze tumor response rates using RECIST criteria.
- To monitor the safety profile and adverse event rates associated with perifosine treatment.
- To investigate pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of perifosine within the study population.

**Study Design:**
This is a Phase II, open-label, single-arm study. Eligible patients will receive oral perifosine at a specified loading dose on Day 1, followed by a continuous daily dosing regimen for the duration of the study. Each treatment cycle lasts 28 days, and patients will be evaluated for disease progression every 6 weeks via imaging studies.

**Projected Accrual:** A total of approximately 50 patients will be enrolled over a period of 12 months.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients must have a confirmed diagnosis of epithelial ovarian cancer, with documentation of recurrent disease following at least one line of platinum-based chemotherapy.
- Patients must exhibit measurable disease according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Age ≥ 18 years, with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1.
- Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function as per the study protocol.
- Ability to provide written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Presence of symptomatic central nervous system metastases or uncontrolled brain disease.
- Prior treatment with perifosine or other investigational agents directed against AKT signaling pathways.
- Significant comorbid conditions that may interfere with patient safety or compliance.
- Patients who have undergone major surgery within 4 weeks prior to study enrollment.
- Patients currently receiving other anti-cancer therapies, including hormonal agents or immunotherapies.

**Endpoints:**
- The primary endpoint will be the percentage of patients who remain progression-free at 6 months post-initiation of perifosine therapy.
- Secondary endpoints include median overall survival, objective response rate (ORR) as evaluated by independent radiological review, tolerability, and identification of dose-limiting toxicities.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Descriptive statistics will be employed to summarize baseline characteristics. The Kaplan-Meier method will be utilized to estimate progression-free and overall survival probabilities. A one-sided significance level of 0.05 will be adopted for all hypothesis testing to assess the efficacy of perifosine in this patient cohort.

**Conclusions:**
In light of the presented objectives and the unmet need within the cohort of patients suffering from recurrent ovarian cancer, this Phase II study intends to elucidate the antitumor activity of perifosine. Anticipated challenges include the underlying resistance mechanisms of recurrent ovarian cancer and the historical data suggesting limited effectiveness of previously administered therapies. Ultimately, this trial seeks to contribute essential data to the field despite facing skepticism regarding the efficacy of novel treatments in heavily pre-treated populations.

**Outcome:** The trial ultimately failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in the primary endpoint of progression-free survival at the 6-month mark, leading to the premature discontinuation of the study due to insufficient efficacy observed in the patient population. Subsequently, this highlights the ongoing challenges in combating recurrent ovarian carcinoma with novel agents such as perifosine.
