<clinical_trial>
  <title>Eltrombopag for the Management of Thrombocytopenia in Patients with Chronic Hepatitis C: A Phase IV Extension Study</title>
  <description>A multicenter, open-label Phase IV study designed to evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety of eltrombopag in maintaining platelet counts in patients with chronic Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) infection and thrombocytopenia, engaged beyond the primary clinical trials. The study aims to reduce TEE occurrences and analyze the sustainability of responses achieved during previous interventional studies.</description>
  <objectives>
    <primary>To assess the proportion of subjects achieving and maintaining a platelet count of ≥75,000/µL over a 12-month observation period while receiving eltrombopag.</primary>
    <secondary>To evaluate the incidence of thromboembolic events among patients during treatment and to examine the factors associated with sustained virological response (SVR) and overall clinical outcomes.</secondary>
  </objectives>
  <background>
    Chronic hepatitis C infection is frequently associated with significant thrombocytopenia, impacting treatment efficacy and outcomes. Eltrombopag has demonstrated the ability to increase platelet levels, thus facilitating effective antiviral therapy initiation and completion. Following successful outcomes in prior ENABLE trials, including demonstrated safety profiles and TEE management strategies, this Phase IV extension aims to explore long-term efficacy in real-world settings.
  </background>
  <study_design>Open-label, multicenter, single-arm trial with a follow-up component extending to 24 months.</study_design>
  <participants>
    <inclusion_criteria>
      <item>≥18 years of age.</item>
      <item>Documented chronic HCV infection.</item>
      <item>Platelet count <75,000/µL prior to eltrombopag initiation.</item>
      <item>Eligible individuals from previous ENABLE trials or those beginning new antiviral therapy.</item>
      <item>No significant hepatic decompensation (Child-Pugh score ≤6).</item>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
      <item>History of major thromboembolic events or any chronic condition leading to significantly increased thrombotic risk.</item>
      <item>Current treatment with anticoagulants that may confound efficacy assessment.</item>
      <item>Severe psychiatric disorders or contraindications to study medications.</item>
      <item>Active malignancies or significant comorbidities that may compromise safety during the study.</item>
    </exclusion_criteria>
  </participants>
  <methods>
    <randomization>No randomization; all participants will receive eltrombopag to enable assessment of treatment outcomes in real-world scenarios.</randomization>
    <treatment_duration>12 months, with a follow-up assessment period of 12 additional months gauging efficacy and safety metrics.</treatment_duration>
    <outcomes>
      <primary_outcomes>Proportion of patients achieving and maintaining platelet counts ≥75,000/µL at the end of the treatment period.</primary_outcomes>
      <secondary_outcomes>Incidence of TEE; assessment of SVR rates post-treatment; analysis of liver function tests at 6-month intervals.</secondary_outcomes>
    </outcomes>
  </methods>
  <data_collection>
    <item>Medical history and baseline laboratory values will be collected prior to eltrombopag initiation.</item>
    <item>Follow-up will include periodic assessments at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months.</item>
    <item>Data on TEE occurrences will be monitored via medical record review, reports from treating physicians, and patient-reported outcomes.</item>
    <item>Logistic regression models will be employed to analyze the correlation between eltrombopag administration and TEE.</item>
  </data_collection>
  <statistical_analysis>
    <item>Primary analyses will utilize intention-to-treat principles.</item>
    <item>Descriptive statistics will summarize demographic characteristics, with inferential statistics guiding treatment outcome evaluations.</item>
    <item>Incidence rates, risk estimates, and confidence intervals for TEE will be calculated.</item>
  </statistical_analysis>
  <conclusion>This study aims to further elucidate the benefits of eltrombopag in chronic HCV thrombocytopenic patients, focusing on the sustained management of platelet levels, minimization of TEE occurrences, and overall enhancement of treatment outcomes related to antiviral therapy. The data obtained can provide critical insights that may inform future treatment guidelines and enhance patient management strategies.</conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
