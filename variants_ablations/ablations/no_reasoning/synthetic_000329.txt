**Failed clinical trial of alfuzosin: Alfuzosin in Adolescent Patients with Non-neurogenic Overactive Bladder Symptoms**

**Title:** A Multicenter, Double-blind, Randomized, Placebo-controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Alfuzosin in Adolescent Patients Aged 12-17 Years with Non-neurogenic Overactive Bladder Symptoms

**Primary Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to determine the efficacy of alfuzosin in reducing overactive bladder symptoms as measured by the validated OAB-q questionnaire, comparing alfuzosin to placebo over a treatment period of 12 weeks.

**Secondary Objectives:**
- To evaluate the safety and tolerability of alfuzosin in adolescent patients.
- To assess changes in urinary frequency and urgency episodes compared to baseline.
- To monitor quality of life improvements associated with treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male and female patients aged 12 to 17 years with a clinical diagnosis of non-neurogenic overactive bladder symptoms.
- Patients experiencing at least 8 voids per day and/or 2 or more episodes of urgency in the past week.
- Provision of informed consent from patients and guardians.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Documented neurogenic bladder disorders or previous treatments targeting bladder dysfunction.
- Known urological conditions, structural abnormalities, or urinary tract infections within 30 days prior to enrollment.
- Prior treatment with α1-blockers or other medications addressed to manage overactive bladder within the previous 4 weeks.
- Significant cardiovascular, renal, hepatic, or psychiatric conditions as determined by the investigator.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding adolescents or patients with significant allergies to alfuzosin or any of its components.

**Methodology:** Patients who meet inclusion criteria will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either alfuzosin 0.25 mg twice daily or matching placebo for a duration of 12 weeks. Efficacy assessments will be conducted at baseline, weeks 4, 8, and 12, utilizing the OAB-q and patient-reported urinary diaries. Safety evaluations will include monitoring adverse events throughout the trial period.

**Endpoints:**
- The primary endpoint will be the change from baseline in OAB-q Total score at week 12.
- Secondary endpoints will comprise the frequency of daily urinary episodes, severity of urgency episodes, and the occurrence of adverse reactions.

**Sample Size:** A total of 200 participants will be enrolled, assuming a drop-out rate of approximately 20%.

**Anticipated Analysis:** Data will be analyzed following the intention-to-treat principle utilizing ANOVA and Chi-squared tests for efficacy and safety outcomes respectively.

**Study Duration:** The total study duration is anticipated to be 16 weeks, including 12 weeks of treatment and a 4-week follow-up period.

**Rationale for Failure:** The study experienced a high dropout rate primarily due to adverse side effects which included postural hypotension and dizziness, common side effects associated with alfuzosin. Data collected revealed no statistically significant improvement in OAB symptoms as compared to baseline assessments. Thus, extensive evaluation demonstrated that alfuzosin does not meaningfully alleviate non-neurogenic overactive bladder symptoms in the adolescent population, leading to the termination of the trial due to lack of efficacy. 

**Note:** This clinical trial is registered under the Clinical Trials Registry and complies with ethical standards regarding clinical research involving minors.
