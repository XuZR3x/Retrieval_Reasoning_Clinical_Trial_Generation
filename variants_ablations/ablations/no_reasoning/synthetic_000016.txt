**Clinical Trial Report: Long-term Efficacy and Safety Study of Tipranavir in HIV-1 Infected Patients with Prior Treatment Experience**

**Study Title:** A Multicenter, Open-Label, Phase IV Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Tipranavir in Combination with Optimized Background Therapy in HIV-1 Infected Patients with Documented Treatment Failure and Viral Resistance

**Background:** Tipranavir (TPV) is a non-peptidic protease inhibitor indicated for the treatment of HIV-1 infection. Previous studies have demonstrated its efficacy in both treatment-naïve and heavily pre-treated populations. This trial aims to provide further data on the long-term efficacy and safety of TPV in patients with documented treatment failure, characterized by viral resistance and suboptimal immune response.

**Objectives:** The principal objective of this study is to evaluate the long-term viral load suppression and safety profile of TPV in conjunction with an optimized background therapy in HIV-1 infected adults presenting with significant treatment history.

**Design:** This is a multicenter, open-label, phase IV study. Participants will receive TPV (500 mg twice daily) alongside therapy tailored based on genotypic resistance testing.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adult males and females aged 18-65 years with documented HIV-1 infection and treatment failure defined as persistent viremia (HIV-1 RNA > 1,000 copies/mL).
2. Evidence of genotypic resistance to at least one other protease inhibitor.
3. CD4+ T-cell count < 350 cells/mm³ at screening.
4. Signed informed consent in accordance with GCP and local regulatory guidelines.
5. Ability to adhere to the protocol, which includes a robust follow-up regime.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Pregnant or breastfeeding females.
2. Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B or C).
3. Active substance or alcohol abuse that may interfere with treatment adherence or evaluation.
4. Recent history of serious opportunistic infections (within the past 90 days).
5. Participation in another investigational drug trial within 30 days prior to study entry.
6. Significant cardiovascular, pulmonary, renal, or metabolic disease, as determined by the investigator.

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
1. Change in viral load from baseline to week 48 using the last observation carried forward (LOCF) approach.
2. Incidence and severity of adverse events assessed through patient reports and laboratory evaluations throughout the duration of the study.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
1. Change in CD4+ T-cell count from baseline at week 48.
2. Quality of life assessed using the HIV/AIDS-Targeted Quality of Life (AQoL-6D) scale at baseline, week 24, and week 48.

**Statistical Analysis:** Statistical comparisons will be made using paired t-tests for intra-group analysis and ANOVA for between-group comparisons, with significance defined at p < 0.05.

**Safety Monitoring:** A Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will be established to oversee participant safety and efficacy during the trial and will meet regularly to evaluate ongoing results and make recommendations.

**Anticipated Enrollment:** The study aims to enroll approximately 150 patients across multiple sites, with the duration of participation set to 48 weeks, followed by a 24-week follow-up period to assess sustained outcomes.
