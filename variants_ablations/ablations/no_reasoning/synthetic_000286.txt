**Clinical Trial Report: Effectiveness of Dexmedetomidine in Reducing Postoperative Delirium in Elderly Patients Undergoing Hip Fracture Surgery**

**Title:** Dexmedetomidine Infusion for Preventing Postoperative Delirium in Elderly Patients Undergoing Hip Fracture Surgery: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Abstract:** The present study seeks to evaluate the effectiveness of dexmedetomidine as a preventative intervention for postoperative delirium (POD) in elderly patients undergoing surgical repair for hip fractures. Postoperative delirium is a common and serious complication impacting the elderly population, leading to long-term cognitive impairment and increased hospital stays. This trial aims to establish dexmedetomidine's role in mitigating the incidence of POD by employing its sedative and analgesic properties while avoiding respiratory depression.

**Background:** Elderly patients undergoing hip fracture surgery are at a significant risk for POD due to factors such as age, pre-existing cognitive impairment, and polypharmacy. Traditional narcotics can exacerbate delirium; hence, an alternative approach incorporating dexmedetomidine is warranted. Previous literature suggests that dexmedetomidine might confer neuroprotective effects while facilitating sedation, potentially reducing the occurrence of delirious states.

**Study Design:** This was a multi-center, double-blinded, randomized controlled trial conducted from January 2021 to December 2022 across affluent academic medical institutions. The trial protocol was approved by the institutional review board (IRB) at each participating site.

**Participants:** A total of 120 patients aged 65 years and older, scheduled for elective hip fracture surgery, were recruited. 

**Inclusion Criteria:** 
- Age ≥ 65 years
- ASA Status II-III
- Consent provided

**Exclusion Criteria:** 
- Pre-existing severe cognitive impairment (MMSE < 10)
- Allergy to dexmedetomidine
- Recent use of psychoactive substances or sedatives within 72 hours
- Active severe systemic illness and significant respiratory compromise
- Patient refusal

**Intervention:** Participants were randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to receive either dexmedetomidine (Group D) or normal saline (Group C) as a placebo. Group D received continuous intravenous infusion of dexmedetomidine (0.3 mcg/kg/hr) starting at induction and continued for 24 hours postsurgery, while Group C received an equivalent volume of saline.

**Outcome Measures:** The primary outcome was the incidence of postoperative delirium assessed using the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) within the first 7 postoperative days. Secondary outcomes included length of hospital stay, opioid consumption (measured in oral morphine equivalents), and postoperative pain scores measured by the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) at 12, 24, and 48 hours post-surgery.

**Monitoring:** All patients were closely monitored for vital signs and adverse effects throughout the trial duration. Safety assessments included the frequency of bradycardia, hypotension, and significant changes in oxygen saturation.

**Statistical Analysis:** Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS software, with p-values <0.05 deemed significant. Chi-square tests or Fisher's exact tests were used for categorical data, while Mann-Whitney U tests were used for continuous variables.

**Results:** The trial successfully enrolled 120 participants, with demographic characteristics comparable across both groups. The results indicated a significant reduction in the incidence of postoperative delirium in the dexmedetomidine group (15%) compared to the control group (35%) (p = 0.014). Furthermore, patients receiving dexmedetomidine reported lower VAS scores throughout the postoperative period and required significantly less opioid analgesia (median morphine equivalents: Group D 25 mg vs. Group C 60 mg, p = 0.02). Length of hospital stay was also reduced in Group D (mean stay of 5.2 days vs. 7.1 days in Group C, p < 0.001).

**Conclusion:** The findings of this clinical trial support the effectiveness of dexmedetomidine infusion in reducing the incidence of postoperative delirium and opioid requirements in elderly patients undergoing hip fracture surgery. Given its favorable safety profile and potential for improving postoperative outcomes, dexmedetomidine may be a valuable adjunct in the anesthetic management of this vulnerable patient population. Further studies are warranted to consolidate these findings and evaluate long-term cognitive outcomes.
