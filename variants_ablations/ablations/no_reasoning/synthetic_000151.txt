**Failed Clinical Trial of Raltegravir: Raltegravir Efficacy in Patients With HIV and Cognitive Dysfunction**  
**A Phase II Study of Raltegravir in Patients With HIV and Neurocognitive Impairment**

The persistence of neurocognitive impairment, or HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder (HAND), despite effective combination antiretroviral therapy (cART) underscores a significant issue in the management of HIV. The exact mechanisms remain largely undefined, with hypotheses suggesting residual viral replication and chronic inflammatory processes that provoke neuronal damage. This study aimed to evaluate whether coping with cognitive dysfunction in HIV-infected patients could be improved via the administration of raltegravir, an integrase inhibitor renowned for its antiviral efficacy, hypothesizing that its use may mitigate cerebral inflammation indicative of neurotoxic effects associated with persistent CNS immune activation.

**Study Objectives**  
The primary objective was to assess the impact of raltegravir on cognitive function, measured through a standardized battery of neuropsychological tests over the course of 24 weeks. The secondary objective included monitoring changes in inflammatory biomarkers (including neopterin and CD8+ T cell activation) within both plasma and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).

**Study Design**  
This was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving approximately 100 HIV-positive participants exhibiting mild to moderate cognitive impairment and maintained on stable cART for a minimum of 12 months, inclusive of two nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) and one NNRTI or protease inhibitor (PI) regimen. Subjects were randomized to receive either raltegravir or placebo, in conjunction with their existing therapy for a duration of 24 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Documented HIV-1 infection confirmed by ELISA and Western blot.  
- Participants aged 18 years or older.  
- Cognitive impairment defined as having scored 1 standard deviation below the mean on at least two cognitive tests from the neuropsychological battery at screening.  
- Evidence of ongoing antiretroviral therapy for at least 12 months without change.  
- Plasma HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL and stable CD4+ counts over the past year.  
- Ability to provide informed consent.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Prior neurological disorders or any acute CNS events.  
- Pregnancy or lactation.  
- Active substance abuse that would interfere with compliance.  
- Use of any investigational antiretroviral medications within 30 days prior to study entry.  
- Hematological and biochemical parameters outside established safety limits (e.g., hemoglobin <10 g/dL, creatinine clearance <60 mL/min).  

**Primary Endpoint:**  
- Change from baseline in composite neurocognitive function score after 24 weeks, assessed using a battery including the Repeatable Battery for the Assessment of Neuropsychological Status (RBANS).  

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
- Changes in inflammatory markers in both plasma and CSF, specifically neopterin concentration.  
- Evaluation of CD8+ T cell activation through flow cytometry, assessing co-expression of CD38 and HLA-DR.  

**Outcome Results:**  
The study's interim analysis revealed there was no statistically significant improvement in cognitive scores in the raltegravir group compared to placebo (p=0.57). Additionally, changes in neopterin levels and CD8+ T cell activation showed no significant differences between treatment arms, indicating that stabilization of cognitive function was not accomplished through the intervention of raltegravir as hypothesized. Additionally, adverse events related specifically to the central nervous system were noted in both groups, emphasizing the persistence of CNS complications related to HIV independent of the treatment received.

Conclusively, despite the anticipated benefits of replacing standard therapies with raltegravir in improving cognitive outcomes, this clinical trial substantiated the failure of raltegravir to elicit significant neurocognitive improvements or alterations in immune activation biomarkers among individuals suffering from HIV-associated cognitive dysfunction. Further investigations into more effective therapeutic strategies targeting CNS infiltration and inflammation in the context of HIV infection remain imperative.
