**Title:** A Phase 2, Randomized, Multicenter Study to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of Eribulin Mesylate Plus Pembrolizumab in Patients With Locally Advanced or Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) After First-Line Chemotherapy  

**Study Identifier:** Study E7389-K081-418  

**Background:** Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. The addition of immunotherapy to conventional chemotherapy regimens has demonstrated improved outcomes in various malignancies. This trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of eribulin mesylate in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC following first-line chemotherapy. Eribulin mesylate's unique mechanism of action, along with pembrolizumab's immunomodulatory properties, might enhance therapeutic responses.

**Primary Objective:**  
I. To assess overall survival (OS) at 12 months post-treatment.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
I. To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months.  
II. To determine the overall response rate (ORR) based on RECIST v1.1 criteria.  
III. To analyze the incidence of adverse events (AEs) and quality of life (QoL) metrics using the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire.  
IV. To evaluate the expression of PD-L1 in tumor samples prior to treatment to correlate with therapeutic outcomes.

**Study Design:**  
This is a phase 2, randomized, multicenter trial that will enroll approximately 200 patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC who have progressed after first-line chemotherapy. Participants will be stratified by PD-L1 expression status and randomized 1:1 to receive either eribulin mesylate (1.4 mg/m² intravenously on Days 1 and 8) combined with pembrolizumab (200 mg intravenously on Day 1 of each 3-week cycle) or eribulin mesylate alone. Treatment will continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Patients will be followed for 12 months after the completion of treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients must have histologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer, stage IIIB or IV, not amenable to surgical intervention.  
- Documented evidence of disease progression after at least one standard first-line chemotherapy regimen.  
- Presence of measurable disease as defined by RECIST v1.1.  
- Patients must be aged ≥ 18 years.  
- An Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.  
- Adequate organ function (bone marrow, hepatic, renal).  
- Patients must provide written informed consent prior to participation.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Prior exposure to eribulin mesylate or pembrolizumab.  
- Any prior chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or immunotherapy within 21 days prior to enrollment.  
- Ongoing autoimmune diseases requiring systemic treatment.  
- Patients with active central nervous system metastases.  
- Documented hypersensitivity to any components of the study treatment.  
- Pregnant or nursing women and patients with uncontrolled inter-current illnesses.

**Endpoints:**  
- Primary: Overall survival at 12 months post-treatment, analyzed by the Kaplan-Meier method and compared between intervention groups using log-rank tests.  
- Secondary: Progression-free survival, overall response rate as assessed by imaging, safety profile, and quality of life changes pre- and post-treatment. 

**Follow-Up:**  
Patients will be monitored for safety and efficacy every 6 weeks for the first 6 months followed by every 12 weeks until disease progression, with a final follow-up assessment at 12 months post-treatment.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Descriptive statistics (means, standard deviations) will be utilized for baseline characteristics. Survival analyses will be conducted using the Cox proportional hazards model, and changes in quality of life scores will be analyzed using mixed-model repeated measures.

---

This trial aims to discern the potential benefits of combining eribulin mesylate with an immune checkpoint inhibitor in a patient population previously treated with first-line therapy, thereby facilitating a deeper understanding of treatment options in this challenging malignancy.
