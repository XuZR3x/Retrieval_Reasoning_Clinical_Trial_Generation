<clinical_trial>
  <title>Evaluation of the Efficacy and Safety of Interferon Beta-1a in the Treatment of Patients with Chronic Hepatitis C: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Phase III Trial</title>
  <description>The purpose of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Interferon Beta-1a in patients with chronic hepatitis C. This trial aims to compare the effects of Interferon Beta-1a against a placebo on viral load reduction and progression of liver disease.</description>
  
  <design>Phase III, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study</design>
  
  <inclusion_criteria>
    <criterion>Confirmed diagnosis of chronic hepatitis C based on serological and molecular diagnostic tests</criterion>
    <criterion>Aged 18 years or older</criterion>
    <criterion>Individuals with a baseline viral load of ≥10^5 IU/mL</criterion>
    <criterion>No previous treatment for hepatitis C within the last 6 months</criterion>
  </inclusion_criteria>
  
  <exclusion_criteria>
    <criterion>History of hypersensitivity or severe adverse reactions to interferon or its components</criterion>
    <criterion>Presence of comorbid conditions such as decompensated liver disease or chronic systemic diseases</criterion>
    <criterion>Concurrent use of antiviral agents or immunomodulators</criterion>
    <criterion>History of severe psychiatric disorders or substance abuse within the last year</criterion>
    <criterion>Pregnant or lactating women</criterion>
  </exclusion_criteria>
  
  <primary_outcome>
    <measure>Reduction of serum HCV RNA levels at week 24 of treatment</measure>
    <criterion>Comparison of sustained virological response (SVR) rates at 12 weeks post-treatment cessation</criterion>
  </primary_outcome>
  
  <secondary_outcome>
    <measure>Monitoring of adverse effects, including flu-like symptoms, hematological abnormalities, and psychiatric events</measure>
    <criterion>Assessment of quality of life using established questionnaires at baseline, during treatment, and at follow-up points</criterion>
  </secondary_outcome>
  
  <rationale>The anticipated reasons for study failure include: inadequate sample size leading to insufficient statistical power to detect treatment differences, high dropout rates due to adverse events such as flu-like symptoms associated with interferon beta-1a, and variability in viral response that may not align with previous studies demonstrating interferon efficacy in other conditions.</rationale>
</clinical_trial>
