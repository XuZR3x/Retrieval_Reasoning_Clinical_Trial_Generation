**Title:** A Phase III Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Panobinostat in Combination With Carfilzomib and Dexamethasone in Patients With Relapsed/Refractory Multiple Myeloma

**Objective:** This study is designed to assess the effectiveness and safety of the pan-deacetylase inhibitor panobinostat in combination with carfilzomib, a proteasome inhibitor, and dexamethasone in patients diagnosed with relapsed and/or refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM). This randomized Phase III trial aims to demonstrate the superiority of the panobinostat-containing regimen over the placebo comparator with carfilzomib and dexamethasone in prolonging progression-free survival (PFS).

**Background:** Multiple myeloma remains an incurable malignancy with a poor prognosis. Carfilzomib has shown significant activity as a proteasome inhibitor; its efficacy is improved when combined with panobinostat due to their synergistic cytotoxicity demonstrated in pre-clinical studies. With inadequate treatment options available for RRMM patients, the investigational combination of panobinostat with carfilzomib and dexamethasone could provide an effective therapeutic regimen, potentially improving clinical outcomes.

**Study Design:** A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial will be conducted across several institutions. Patients will be randomly assigned in a 2:1 ratio to receive either the combination of panobinostat, carfilzomib, and dexamethasone (treatment group) or the combination of placebo, carfilzomib, and dexamethasone (control group). 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults ≥ 18 years with a confirmed diagnosis of relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma.
2. Patients must have received at least two prior lines of therapy, including at least one bortezomib-containing regimen and one IMiD (lenalidomide or thalidomide).
3. Patients must demonstrate measurable disease defined by at least one of the following metrics:
   - Serum M-protein ≥ 1 g/dL
   - Urine M-protein ≥ 200 mg/24 hours
   - Serum free light chain assay demonstrating an involved free light chain level ≥ 10 mg/dL.
4. ECOG performance status of 0-2.
5. Adequate hematologic parameters:
   - ANC ≥ 1000/μL
   - Platelets ≥ 70,000/microL
   - AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN, total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN.
6. Patients must provide written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients with primary refractory multiple myeloma.
2. Previous history of severe cardiac dysfunction or known contraindications to any trial medications, including panobinostat, carfilzomib, or dexamethasone.
3. Patients with active or uncontrolled infections, autoimmune diseases, or other confounding co-morbidities.
4. Any significant history of non-compliance to medical regimens.
5. Patients who have undergone allogeneic stem cell transplants or have current active graft-versus-host disease.
6. Pregnant or lactating women, and non-compliant contraceptive use from both genders.

**Endpoints:**
1. Primary Endpoint: Progression-Free Survival (PFS) defined as the time from randomization to disease progression or death from any cause.
2. Secondary Endpoints:
   - Overall Response Rate (complete response + partial response).
   - Duration of Response.
   - Overall Survival.
   - Safety profile and incidence of adverse events graded per CTCAE v5.0 criteria.

**Statistical Analysis:** The sample size is estimated to ensure adequate power (85%) to demonstrate a statistically significant difference in PFS between treatment and control groups, with a two-sided significance level of 0.05. Survival analysis will be performed using Kaplan-Meier curves and stratified log-rank tests.

**Study Duration:** Patients will be treated until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or completion of a maximum of twelve treatment cycles. Follow-up evaluations will occur every three months for a total study duration of two years.

**Conclusion:** This trial will investigate a novel and promising combination therapy of panobinostat, carfilzomib, and dexamethasone, potentially improving treatment outcomes for patients suffering from relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, addressing a critical clinical need in this challenging patient population. The study's findings may affirm the role of panobinostat in standard therapeutic regimens, enhancing quality of life and extending survival in myeloma patients.
