**Title:** Hydroxyurea Versus Standard Care for Prevention of Pain Crises in Children With Sickle Cell Disease - A Multi-Center Phase III Randomized Controlled Trial  

**Study Objective:** The primary objective of this Phase III randomized controlled trial is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of hydroxyurea in reducing the frequency and severity of vaso-occlusive crises (VOCs) in pediatric patients with sickle cell disease (SCD) compared to standard care (oral hydration and analgesics) over a duration of 12 months.

**Study Design:** This study is a double-blind, multicenter trial involving pediatric subjects diagnosed with SCD, specifically HbSS or HbSβ0 thalassemia. Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either hydroxyurea (starting dose of 15 mg/kg/day, titrated to a maximum of 35 mg/kg/day based on tolerance and laboratory parameters) or standard care.

**Rationale:** Despite the growing interest in hydroxyurea as an effective intervention for SCD, current treatment practices largely rely on standard care, which is often insufficient to prevent recurrent VOEs. Hydroxyurea has been proven to decrease the frequency of VOCs, reduce hospitalizations, and improve overall quality of life by increasing fetal hemoglobin (HbF) levels. Additionally, cost-effective therapy options that manage both disease burden and quality of life are urgently needed in pediatric populations. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Pediatric subjects aged 2 to 18 years with a confirmed diagnosis of SCD (HbSS or HbSβ0 thalassemia).
2. History of at least 2 painful VOCs in the previous year.
3. Capacity for independent living or being accompanied by a parent/guardian who can provide informed consent.
4. Ability to comply with study-related assessments and treatment regimens.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Severe organ dysfunction (e.g., renal, hepatic, or cardiac insufficiency).
2. Previous or current malignancy.
3. Participation in another clinical trial within the past 30 days.
4. Known hypersensitivity to hydroxyurea.
5. Pregnancy or lactation.
6. History of recurrent severe infections requiring hospitalization.

**Endpoints:**

* **Primary Endpoint:** Reduction in the incidence of VOCs, quantified by the number of pain episodes requiring medical attention over a 12-month period.
  
* **Secondary Endpoints:**  
  - Change in hemoglobin levels (including HbF percentage) from baseline.
  - Frequency of hospital admissions related to SCD.
  - Health-related quality of life measurement via validated questionnaires (Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory).
  - Assessment of adverse events associated with hydroxyurea treatment, including cytopenias.

**Statistical Analysis:** A sample size of 200 participants (100 in each intervention group) will provide adequate power (80%) to detect a clinically significant difference in the primary endpoint with a two-sided significance level of 0.05. Randomization will be achieved using a computer-generated randomization schedule. Outcome measures will be analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis.

**Expected Outcomes:** The investigators anticipate that the hydroxyurea group will demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in the frequency of VOCs compared to the standard care group, alongside an improvement in hematological parameters and patient-reported outcomes. Safety assessments will ensure that the cytopenic risks associated with hydroxyurea remain manageable in the pediatric population.

**Conclusion:** This trial aims to provide robust evidence in support of hydroxyurea as a standard therapeutic option for pediatric patients with SCD, focusing on improving clinical outcomes while minimizing the frequency of painful crises and the associated healthcare burden. The results of this trial could potentially redefine management strategies for children suffering from SCD on a global scale, particularly in regions with limited healthcare resources.
