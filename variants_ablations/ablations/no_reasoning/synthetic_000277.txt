Failed Clinical Trial of Escitalopram: The Efficacy of Escitalopram in the Treatment of Anxiety Symptoms in Individuals with Major Depressive Disorder 

**Title:** A Double-blind, Placebo-Controlled Randomized Trial of Escitalopram in the Treatment of Anxiety Symptoms in Patients with Major Depressive Disorder

**Funding:** This investigator-initiated trial received funding from a medical research grant from the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH).

**Study Design:** The study is a prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled multicenter trial. 

**Aim:** To evaluate the efficacy of escitalopram versus placebo for reducing anxiety symptoms in patients diagnosed with major depressive disorder.

**Hypotheses:**
1. Escitalopram leads to a statistically significant reduction in anxiety symptoms as measured by the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAM-A) compared to placebo in patients with major depressive disorder.
2. Escitalopram is associated with a significant improvement in overall functioning, as assessed by the Global Assessment of Functioning (GAF), compared to placebo.
3. The incidence of adverse events, particularly the emergence of manic or hypomanic episodes, does not significantly differ between those receiving escitalopram and those receiving placebo.

**Patients:** 
Inclusion criteria for the trial consisted of outpatients aged 18–65 with a DSM-5 diagnosis of major depressive disorder, experiencing moderate to severe anxiety symptoms indicated by a HAM-A score of at least 18 at baseline. Exclusion criteria involved individuals with bipolar disorder, psychotic disorders, substance abuse, significant medical comorbidities, or those on contraindicated medications.

**Medication:** 
Participants received either escitalopram 10-20 mg daily or placebo in addition to their standard antidepressant treatment regime. The doses of antidepressants must have been stable for at least four weeks prior to the baseline assessment.

**Method:**
The trial consists of a 12-week treatment period. All patients underwent six evaluations during this phase. The primary endpoint is the change in HAM-A scores from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints include changes in MADRS scores and GAF scores.

Participants who demonstrated resistance to escitalopram after 8 weeks (defined as less than a 30% reduction from baseline HAM-A scores) were allowed to exit the trial and receive open-label escitalopram treatment for an additional 4 weeks. 

**Study Procedure:**
Screening for eligible participants included a thorough psychiatric assessment, medical history review, and laboratory tests. Informed consent was gathered prior to initiation of the study. Baseline assessments included the HAM-A, MADRS, and GAF scores.

**Assessments:**
Regular assessments for anxiety and depressive symptoms were scheduled at baseline, weeks 1, 2, 4, 8, and 12. Furthermore, evaluations included adverse event monitoring and concomitant medication use documented throughout the study duration. 

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
- Change in HAM-A scores at week 12 relative to baseline.
- Response rate defined as a ≥ 50% reduction in baseline HAM-A scores.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
- Changes in MADRS and GAF scores.
- Monitoring of treatment-emergent mania/hypomania incidents using established DSM-5 criteria.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18-65 years meeting DSM-5 criteria for major depressive disorder.
- HAM-A score of ≥ 18 at baseline.
- Stable dose of co-administered antidepressants for four weeks prior to recruitment.
- Informed, voluntary consent obtained from participants.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Ongoing psychotic disorders or bipolar disorders.
- Substance dependency within the last year.
- Comognitive decline or severe medical illnesses.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding without appropriate contraceptive measures.
- Any history of serious cardiovascular conditions or other contraindications to SSRIs.

**Results:**
Unfortunately, the trial did not demonstrate a significant difference in anxiety symptom reduction between the escitalopram group and the placebo group when analyzed by an intention-to-treat model. Secondary measures of depressive symptoms likewise failed to show meaningful statistical improvement. Adverse events, primarily mild in nature, did not differ significantly between groups; however, an unusually high dropout rate attributed to perceived lack of efficacy was noted.

**Conclusion:**
This trial of escitalopram for anxiety symptoms in major depressive disorder ultimately failed to support the primary hypotheses. Further investigation is required to determine optimal treatment strategies for managing anxiety within this patient population.

**Clinical Implications:**
Considering the high incidence of anxiety symptoms in major depressive disorder, alternative treatment options that either enhance the efficacy of existing therapies or explore new pharmacological avenues should be prioritized moving forward.
