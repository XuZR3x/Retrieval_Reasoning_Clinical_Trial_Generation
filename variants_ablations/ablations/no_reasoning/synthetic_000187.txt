Successful clinical trial of tranexamic acid: Tranexamic Acid for the Prevention of Hemorrhage in Traumatic Brain Injury Patients 

**Title**: The Efficacy of Tranexamic Acid in Reducing Hemorrhagic Complications in Patients with Traumatic Brain Injury: A Multicenter Randomized Controlled Trial

**Background**: Traumatic brain injury (TBI) is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, with significant hemorrhagic complications contributing to poor outcomes. Evidence from existing literature suggests that tranexamic acid (TXA), an antifibrinolytic agent, may effectively mitigate bleeding and improve survival in hemorrhagic conditions. This study aims to investigate the role of TXA in preventing hemorrhage and associated complications in TBI patients, hypothesizing that early administration will reduce the need for surgical interventions and improve overall clinical outcomes.

**Study Design**: This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial that will enroll adult patients (ages 18-70) presenting with moderate to severe TBI (Glasgow Coma Scale score 9-12). Patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either TXA (1g IV over 10 minutes) or an equal volume of placebo (saline) within one hour of injury.

**Aims**:
1. To evaluate the primary outcome of intracranial hemorrhage volume, as measured on follow-up CT scans within 24 hours post-initial imaging.
2. To assess secondary outcomes including the need for surgical intervention (e.g., craniotomy), rate of transfusion requirements, length of hospital stay, and mortality at 30 days and 90 days post-injury.
3. To analyze safety metrics associated with TXA, monitoring for thromboembolic events and renal function changes during hospitalization.

**Setting**: The trial will be conducted across multiple level I trauma centers, ensuring a diverse patient population. The total study duration is projected to be 36 months, encompassing patient recruitment, follow-up, and data analysis phases.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Adults aged 18-70 years.
- Diagnosis of moderate to severe TBI (GCS score 9-12).
- Presentation to the emergency department within one hour of injury.
- Provision of informed consent from the patient or legally authorized representative.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- History of venous or arterial thromboembolic events (e.g., DVT, PE, myocardial infarction).
- Preexisting coagulopathy or severe renal dysfunction (creatinine > 2 mg/dL).
- Intracranial hemorrhage identified prior to randomization.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
- Patients requiring immediate neurosurgical intervention upon presentation.

**Intervention Protocol**: Upon diagnosis and obtaining consent, eligible patients will be randomized via concealed allocation to receive either IV TXA or placebo. Both groups will receive standard care per the trauma protocol, including monitoring vital signs, neurological status, and fluid resuscitation as clinically indicated. Standardized imaging protocols will be followed to evaluate hemorrhage.

**Data Collection**: Baseline demographic and clinical parameters (age, sex, mechanism of injury, and initial GCS) will be recorded. Radiological evaluation of hemorrhage will include volumetric analysis via CT imaging conducted at baseline, 24-hours post-admission, and at discharge. Length of hospital stay, need for surgical intervention, transfusion requirements, and adverse events will be meticulously documented.

**Data Analysis Plan**: A power analysis indicated that a total sample size of 240 patients (120 per arm) will provide 80% power (α = 0.05, β = 0.20) to detect a clinical significant reduction in intracranial hemorrhage volume using a critical t-test. Randomization will be conducted using a computer-generated block randomization method to ensure balance across treatment groups.

**Adverse Events Monitoring**: All patients will be monitored for adverse events, including thromboembolic incidents or any allergic reactions to TXA. Serious adverse events will be reported to the institutional review board immediately. Data safety monitoring will occur every 30 patients enrolled into the trial, with specific attention given to the safety profile of TXA.

**Conclusion**: This pivotal trial aims to investigate the role of tranexamic acid in reducing hemorrhagic complications in TBI patients, potentially establishing a new standard of care that could significantly impact management protocols and improve patient outcomes in traumatic injury scenarios.
