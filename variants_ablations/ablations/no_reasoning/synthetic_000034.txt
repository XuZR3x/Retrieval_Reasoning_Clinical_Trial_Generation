<clinical_trial>
    <title>Evaluation of Perampanel (E2007) for Management of Dyskinesia in Advanced Parkinson's Disease: A Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial</title>
    <objective>Aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of perampanel as an adjunct treatment to standard therapy in reducing dyskinetic episodes in adults with advanced Parkinson's Disease (PD).</objective>
    
    <design>
        <type>Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Multi-Centre Study</type>
        <scope>Patients with advanced idiopathic Parkinson's Disease exhibiting late-stage dyskinesia.</scope>
    </design>
    
    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Male or female subjects aged 40-80 years with a confirmed diagnosis of idiopathic PD based on UKPDSBB criteria, Hoehn and Yahr stages III to IV.</criterion>
        <criterion>Subjects experiencing moderate to severe levodopa-induced dyskinesia, defined as a score of ≥ 2 on the Modified Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale (AIMS).</criterion>
        <criterion>Subjects must be on a stable dose regimen of levodopa combined with an aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase inhibitor for at least 12 weeks prior to randomization.</criterion>
        <criterion>Willingness and ability to provide written informed consent prior to participation.</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    
    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>History of hypersensitivity to perampanel or any of its components.</criterion>
        <criterion>Presence of uncontrolled hypertension or clinically significant cardiovascular, respiratory, hepatic, or renal diseases that may interfere with safety assessments.</criterion>
        <criterion>Past or present history of substance abuse, major depression, or other severe psychiatric disorders.</criterion>
        <criterion>Concurrent use of dopamine agonists, antipsychotics, or other medications known to influence motor control.</criterion>
        <criterion>Pregnant or nursing women, and women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.</criterion>
        <criterion>Evidence of significant cognitive impairment defined by a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score of < 24.</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>

    <primary_outcome>
        <measure>Mean Change From Baseline in the AIMS Dyskinesia Score at Week 12.</measure>
        <description>Evaluation of the frequency and severity of dyskinetic episodes following treatment with perampanel compared to placebo.</description>
    </primary_outcome>
    
    <secondary_outcomes>
        <outcome>Change in Quality of Life as assessed by the Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire (PDQ-39).</outcome>
        <outcome>Evaluate overall safety and tolerability through adverse event reporting and laboratory values.</outcome>
    </secondary_outcomes>
    
    <results>
        <finding>The trial was terminated early due to a lack of statistically significant difference in the primary outcome measure between the perampanel group and placebo, with no notable reduction in AIMS scores at the week 12 endpoint.</finding>
        <finding>Adverse events were more frequent in the perampanel group, including increased incidence of somnolence and dizziness, provoking safety concerns.</finding>
        <finding>Quality of Life metrics showed no significant difference across treatment arms, indicating a potential lack of meaningful clinical impact on the patient population.</finding>
    </results>
    
    <conclusion>This trial underscores the complexity of managing motor fluctuations and dyskinesia in Parkinson's Disease, emphasizing that adjunctive treatment with perampanel does not yield the anticipated therapeutic benefits for dyskinesia reduction and is associated with an increased risk of adverse effects. Further research into alternative therapeutic strategies is warranted.</conclusion>
    
    <ethical_considerations>
        <statement>The study adhered to ethical guidelines for clinical studies with human subjects and obtained approval from the institutional review boards of all participating centers.</statement>
    </ethical_considerations>
    
</clinical_trial>
