**Failed clinical trial of brentuximab vedotin: Evaluation of Brentuximab Vedotin as a Frontline Therapy in Elderly Patients with Advanced Stage Hodgkin Lymphoma**

**Title:** A Phase II Multicenter Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Brentuximab Vedotin in Elderly Patients with Advanced Stage Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma

**Purpose of the Study:** The primary aim of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of brentuximab vedotin, an antibody-drug conjugate, used as a frontline therapy in treating elderly patients diagnosed with advanced stage (IIB, III, and IV) classical Hodgkin lymphoma. The study will also assess the safety profile of this intervention and investigate potential toxicities, particularly in the geriatric population who may have distinct pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics compared to younger cohorts.

**Study Design:** This study is designed as a single-arm, open-label trial that will enroll participants aged 65 years or older who have been newly diagnosed with advanced stage classical Hodgkin lymphoma. The primary endpoint will be the overall response rate (ORR) post-treatment, defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as evaluated by positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET/CT) scans, while secondary endpoints will encompass safety evaluations concerning adverse events in this vulnerable population.

**Study Drug Administration:** Participants will receive brentuximab vedotin via intravenous infusion on Day 1 and Day 15 of a 28-day treatment cycle. Each patient will undergo 6 cycles of the treatment, with assessments for efficacy and safety performed at the completion of each cycle.

**Study Visits:** Assessments will include:
- Comprehensive baseline evaluations, including medical history, physical examination, and vital signs
- Blood tests to monitor hematologic and biochemical parameters
- Assessment of functional status using the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance scale
- Adverse event evaluation via regular monitoring and reporting

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients aged 65 years or older with a confirmed diagnosis of advanced-stage classical Hodgkin lymphoma (IIB, III, IV).
2. Measurable disease as determined by imaging.
3. ECOG performance status of 0-2.
4. Ability to comprehend and voluntarily sign an informed consent form.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior treatment for Hodgkin lymphoma (including chemotherapy and radiation).
2. Presence of significant cardiovascular disease or other comorbidities that could interfere with treatment.
3. Known hypersensitivity to brentuximab vedotin or its components.
4. Pregnancy or lactation status in women of childbearing potential.

**Endpoints and Response Assessment:**
The primary endpoint will be evaluated as the overall response rate (ORR), which is the sum of CR and PR achieved at the end of the 6 proposed treatment cycles. Secondary safety endpoints will involve documenting the frequency and severity of adverse events graded per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Additionally, quality of life metrics will be assessed using standardized questionnaires at baseline and at the conclusion of the treatment.

**Statistical Analysis:** It is anticipated that up to 60 patients will be enrolled. The ORR will be evaluated first with descriptive statistics, with a goal to demonstrate an ORR of at least 60%. If results indicate an ORR significantly lower than the expected threshold, the study may be halted early due to lack of efficacy.

**Results:** The study was prematurely terminated due to suboptimal response rates observed in the initial cohort and reports of significant adverse events, including heightened incidence of peripheral neuropathy and infections among participants. There was no statistically significant improvement in the overall survival or progression-free survival compared to historical controls treated with traditional chemotherapy regimens.

**Conclusion:** The findings underscore the challenges associated with utilizing brentuximab vedotin in an elderly population with advanced stage Hodgkin lymphoma. The outcomes, suggesting a lack of sufficient efficacy and tolerability issues, highlight the necessity for further research in optimizing treatment strategies specifically tailored for older patients in this demographic. Further investigations into pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic parameters in elderly cohorts are warranted to enhance understanding and improve future therapeutic strategies.
