Successful clinical trial of anti-thymocyte globulin: Evaluating Efficacy and Safety of Anti-Thymocyte Globulin Combined with Alemtuzumab for Graft-Versus-Host Disease Prophylaxis in Patients Undergoing Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation for Hematological Malignancies RATIONALE: The prophylactic administration of anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG) has shown promise in attenuating the incidence of graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) following allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT). The addition of alemtuzumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting CD52 on lymphocytes, may further suppress the donor T-cell mediated immune response against host tissues, thereby enhancing engraftment and reducing GVHD incidence. This multi-faceted approach aims to synergistically improve the outcomes of HSCT, especially in patients with active hematological malignancies. PURPOSE: This phase III trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of administering anti-thymocyte globulin alongside alemtuzumab for the prevention of acute and chronic graft-versus-host disease in patients undergoing HSCT for hematological malignancies. OBJECTIVES: Primary - To compare the incidence of acute graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) at 100 days post-transplantation among patients receiving HSCT who are treated with ATG and alemtuzumab versus those treated with standard GVHD prophylaxis. - To assess the overall survival rates at 1 year post-transplantation in both treatment arms. Secondary - To evaluate the incidence of chronic GVHD at 1 year post-transplantation. - To determine the time to engraftment among the different treatment cohorts. - To assess the incidence of infection-related complications, particularly opportunistic infections, at 6 months following transplantation. - To analyze the pharmacokinetics of alemtuzumab and ATG in relation to engraftment and GVHD incidence. - To explore the quality of life outcomes at 6 months and 1 year post-transplantation. OUTLINE: - Pre-transplantation: Patients will be randomized into two arms. Arm A will receive anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG) IV over 6 hours on days -4 to -2, while Arm B will receive alemtuzumab IV on day -1. - Conditioning regimen: All patients will undergo a myeloablative conditioning regimen consisting of busulfan and cyclophosphamide over days -7 to -3. Following conditioning, patients will receive G-CSF-mobilized peripheral blood stem cells on day 0. - Post-transplantation GVHD prophylaxis: Patients in both arms will receive standard post-transplant immunosuppressive therapy including tacrolimus and mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), starting day +1, with dosing adjusted for renal function. - Follow-up: Patients will be monitored for adverse events, GVHD incidence, engraftment, infection complications, and overall survival at specified intervals (weekly for the first month, then monthly for 1 year). PROJECTED ACCRUAL: A total of 250 patients will be enrolled, with 125 patients in each treatment arm. DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS: - Diagnosis of one of the following hematological malignancies: - Acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) in first complete remission - Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) in remission - Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) requiring transplant - Myelodysplastic syndromes with high-risk features - Confirmed availability of an HLA-matched related or unrelated donor. PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS: - Age 18-65 years - ECOG performance status 0-2 - Adequate renal function (creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL) - Adequate hepatic function (bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL; AST/ALT ≤ 2 times normal) - No history of significant autoimmune disease - Negative pregnancy test for women of childbearing age, with effective contraception required. PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY: - No prior extensive immunosuppressive therapy or solid organ transplant - No receipt of prior monoclonal or polyclonal antibody therapies targeting T-cells or B-cells. - No concurrent investigational agents or therapies.
