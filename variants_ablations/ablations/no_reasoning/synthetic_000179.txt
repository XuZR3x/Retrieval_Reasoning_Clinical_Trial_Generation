Failed clinical trial of duloxetine: Efficacy of Duloxetine in Treating Chronic Pain Related to Fibromyalgia  

DULOTRIAL: A Controlled Study Comparing Duloxetine with Placebo for the Management of Fibromyalgia Pain  

The objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of duloxetine, an antidepressant known for its dual action on serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake inhibition, in alleviating chronic pain associated with fibromyalgia compared to a placebo. Despite previous assertions of duloxetine's effectiveness in managing pain, this study aims to rigorously assess its efficacy against a placebo control, accounting for potential placebo effects inherent in chronic pain management. The primary hypothesis is that duloxetine will not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in pain scores when compared to placebo, indicating a lack of efficacy in this patient population.

Inclusion Criteria:  
- Adult subjects aged 18 and above.  
- Diagnosis of fibromyalgia as per the American College of Rheumatology criteria.
- A pain score of ≥ 4 on the 0-10 Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) at screening.
- Sufficient understanding of study requirements and ability to provide informed consent.
- Female participants of childbearing potential must have a negative urine pregnancy test at enrollment and agree to an effective contraceptive method during the study.  

Exclusion Criteria:  
- Current diagnosis of a primary psychiatric disorder, including but not limited to bipolar disorder, major depressive disorder, or generalized anxiety disorder.  
- History of substance use disorder within the past year.  
- Known hypersensitivity to duloxetine or any of its excipients.  
- Enrolled in any investigational drug study within 30 days of screening.  
- Significant medical comorbidities impacting study participation or duloxetine's safety profile, including uncontrolled hypertension or renal impairment.  
- History of seizure disorders or any condition requiring the use of contraindicated medications.  

The primary endpoint is the change in the Brief Pain Inventory (BPI) interference score from baseline to 12 weeks, assessed via a mixed-model analysis to compare the effects of duloxetine versus placebo. Secondary outcomes include the assessment of quality of life using the Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire (FIQ) and evaluation of functional status.

Due to significant difficulties encountered regarding recruitment and retention of eligible participants, combined with emerging concerns regarding duloxetine's tolerability and the limited distinguishing effects over placebo, the study was ultimately terminated after failing to achieve the required enrollment threshold. Additionally, concurrent shifts in treatment paradigms around fibromyalgia management have further complicated the therapeutic landscape in which duloxetine was being evaluated.
