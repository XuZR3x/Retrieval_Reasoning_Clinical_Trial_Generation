**Successful Clinical Trial of Sunitinib:**  
**A Phase II Study of Sunitinib in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) with Targetable Genetic Alterations**  
  
**Study Design:**  
This Phase II, multicenter, open-label trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Sunitinib in patients with advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer harboring specific targetable genetic alterations. The study aims to assess the objective response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) of Sunitinib in this patient population. Sunitinib, a potent oral multitargeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor, targets c-KIT and PDGF receptors, inhibiting angiogenesis and tumor cell proliferation.

**Objectives:**  
**Primary:**  
- To evaluate the ORR in patients with advanced NSCLC with specific genetic aberrations treated with Sunitinib, as per the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v1.1.  
- To assess the PFS at six months after initiation of treatment.  

**Secondary:**  
- To determine the OS at 12 months.  
- To analyze the relationship between specific genetic alterations and treatment response.  
- To evaluate safety and tolerability through adverse event (AE) tracking, using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0.  
- To employ biomarker assays for tracking circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) and evaluating therapeutic response.  

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced NSCLC, stage IIIb or IV with targetable genetic alterations (e.g., EGFR mutations, ALK rearrangements).  
- ECOG performance status 0-2.  
- At least 18 years of age.  
- Measurable disease as per RECIST v1.1 criteria.  
- Adequate hematologic and organ function as specified in the protocol.  
- Participants must provide written informed consent prior to study initiation.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients with active brain metastases or symptomatic central nervous system metastases, unless treated and stable for at least 4 weeks.  
- Presence of severe comorbidities or uncontrolled medical conditions.  
- Prior treatment with Sunitinib or other investigational agents within 4 weeks prior to study enrollment.  
- Any history of intolerance or hypersensitivity to Sunitinib.  
- Pregnant or lactating females.  
- Concurrent treatment with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers.  

**Treatment Administration:**  
Eligible participants will receive Sunitinib at an initial dose of 50 mg orally once daily for four weeks, followed by two weeks off treatment, constituting a full cycle of therapy. This cycle will continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Dosage adjustments will be made based on individual tolerability and side effects. Regular assessments will be conducted every six weeks through imaging studies and clinical evaluations.

**Endpoint Evaluation:**  
The primary endpoint, ORR, will be assessed by comparing baseline tumor measurements against subsequent scans conducted at specified intervals throughout the study. PFS will be defined as the time from treatment initiation to disease progression or death from any cause, and OS will be evaluated from the initiation of treatment until death from any cause.

**Safety Monitoring and Reporting:**  
Continuous monitoring for AEs will be conducted in compliance with protocol-stipulated rules, and all AEs will be reported using CTCAE version 5.0. An independent safety monitoring board will oversee the trial's integrity, focusing on any dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) within the initial treatment cycles.

**Conclusion:**  
This clinical trial aims to elucidate the efficacy of Sunitinib in patients with advanced NSCLC characterized by targetable genetic alterations. By leveraging Sunitinib’s inhibition of critical pathways implicated in cancer progression, the study endeavors to provide a therapeutic benefit and enhance survival outcomes in a select patient population. This cooperative research will further explore the therapeutic promise of Sunitinib in targeting molecular vulnerabilities in NSCLC, providing invaluable insights into the management of this challenging malignancy. Enrollment is projected for up to 100 patients across multiple study sites, ultimately contributing to the advancement of oncological therapies.
