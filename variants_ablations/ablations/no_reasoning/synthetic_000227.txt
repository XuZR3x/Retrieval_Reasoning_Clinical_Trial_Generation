**Failed Clinical Trial of Plerixafor: Plerixafor in Combination with Olaparib for Patients with BRCA-Mutated Platinum-Resistant Ovarian Cancer**

**Phase I Study of Plerixafor in Combination with Olaparib in Patients with BRCA-Mutated Platinum-Resistant Ovarian Cancer**

Plerixafor (AMD3100), a small-molecule inhibitor of CXCR4, is hypothesized to enhance the efficacy of olaparib, a poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor, by potentially improving tumor cell sensitization and engendering an immune response against ovarian cancer cells. Given the encouraging preclinical data suggesting that plerixafor may enhance olaparib's therapeutic index in solid tumors, our objective was to evaluate the combination's safety and efficacy in patients afflicted with BRCA-mutated, platinum-resistant ovarian cancer.

**Study Design:**

This clinical trial is organized into two distinct parts:

**Part 1:** Dose Escalation Study  
The investigators are tasked with identifying the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of plerixafor when co-administered with olaparib. Patients will receive escalating doses of plerixafor administered subcutaneously once daily for 5 consecutive days every 28 days alongside a fixed dose of olaparib (300 mg orally twice daily).

**Part 2:** Efficacy Evaluation  
Upon determining the MTD in Part 1, a cohort of patients will receive the MTD of plerixafor with olaparib in order to evaluate clinical response, measured by objective tumor response according to RECIST criteria, along with progression-free survival (PFS).

**Inclusion Criteria:**

1. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of ovarian cancer with documented BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutations.
2. Progression of disease despite platinum-based therapy.
3. Aged 18 years or older.
4. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
5. Adequate hematologic, renal, and liver function, indicated by specified laboratory values.
6. Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study requirements.
7. Women of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and agree to effective contraception during the trial.

**Exclusion Criteria:**

1. Prior therapy with olaparib or other PARP inhibitors.
2. Concurrent or recent (within 28 days) treatment with cytotoxic chemotherapy or investigational agents.
3. Known hypersensitivity to plerixafor or olaparib.
4. Active central nervous system involvement by cancer.
5. Significant cardiovascular or uncontrolled systemic illness.
6. Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
7. Other malignancies within the past 5 years, excluding non-melanoma skin cancers.

**Objectives:**

- **Primary Objective:** To establish the MTD of plerixafor when combined with olaparib based on the occurrence of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) within the first treatment cycle.
- **Secondary Objectives:** To assess overall response rate, duration of response, and PFS in the cohort receiving the established MTD.

**Dose-Limiting Toxicities Assessment:**

DLTs will be defined according to National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) Version 5.0 and assessed over the first cycle (28 days) of therapy. The MTD will be determined based on observed toxicities, with the MTD defined as the highest dose whereby ≤1 of 6 participants experiences a DLT.

**Study Closure and Outcome:**

Unfortunately, the trial encountered several challenges leading to its termination prior to dosing in Part 2. Factors influencing the trial’s failure included unexpected high rates of DLTs in initial dosing cohorts, particularly hematologic toxicities and gastrointestinal adverse events, leading to difficulty in dose escalation toward an acceptable safety profile. Additionally, inter-institutional variability in patient eligibility and recruitment impeded achieving a sufficient sample size, resulting in an early halt in enrollment and an inability to proceed to efficacy evaluation.

This clinical study reinforces the complexities of combining therapies in advanced ovarian cancer, particularly involving novel agents such as plerixafor. Subsequent investigations are necessary to dissect the pharmacokinetics and underlying mechanisms of CXCR4 inhibition in this context to inform future combination strategies tailored for this challenging patient population.
