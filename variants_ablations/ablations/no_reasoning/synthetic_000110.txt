<clinical_trial>
   <title>
      A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Fixed-Dose, 8-Week Trial of the Efficacy and Safety of Asenapine Compared With Placebo in the Management of Major Depressive Episodes in Patients With Bipolar Disorder Type II
   </title>
   <study_id>
      41023(P05948)
   </study_id>
   <objective>
      The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of asenapine versus placebo in patients with major depressive episodes associated with bipolar disorder, type II, over a treatment period of eight weeks. We hypothesize that asenapine will significantly reduce depressive symptoms compared to placebo, as measured by change in MADRS scores from baseline to endpoint.
   </objective>
   <methods>
      This is an 8-week, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. A total of 120 patients aged 18 to 65 years, diagnosed with bipolar disorder, type II, experiencing major depressive episodes will be enrolled. The recruitment will occur across five clinical sites over a period of sixteen months. Patients will be randomized to receive asenapine (5 mg bid) or placebo, with the possibility of dose adjustment to 10 mg bid based on clinical response. 
      Study visits will occur at baseline (day 0) and at days 7, 14, 28, 42, and 56, during which assessments will be made by a blinded rater. The study will also monitor adverse events throughout the trial duration.
   </methods>
   <inclusion_criteria>
      - Participants must meet DSM-5 criteria for bipolar disorder, type II, with current major depressive episode.
      - Age 18-65 years.
      - A baseline MADRS score of ≥ 24.
      - Each participant must provide written informed consent and understand the study protocols.
      - Participants must have a history of at least one manic or mixed episode prior to the current depressive episode.
      - French-speaking patients included to ensure comprehension of study materials.
   </inclusion_criteria>
   <exclusion_criteria>
      - Pregnant or lactating females.
      - Presence of any comorbid substantial medical or psychiatric condition that could interfere with trial participation or outcomes.
      - History of hypersensitivity or intolerance to asenapine.
      - Patients exhibiting acute suicidality or meeting criteria for mixed episodes as defined in DSM-5.
      - Recent history of substance abuse (excluding tobacco or caffeine) within the last 6 months.
      - Current use of monoamine oxidase inhibitors or other psychotropic medications.
      - Participation in another interventional clinical trial within 30 days of screening.
   </exclusion_criteria>
   <primary_outcome>
      Change in MADRS score from baseline to endpoint at 8 weeks.
   </primary_outcome>
   <secondary_outcomes>
      - MADRS Response Rate: Defined as a≥50% reduction in MADRS score from baseline to endpoint at 8 weeks.
      - Safety and tolerability assessed through reported adverse events and laboratory test abnormalities.
   </secondary_outcomes>
   <conclusion>
      This clinical trial did not meet its primary efficacy endpoint, as asenapine did not demonstrate a statistically significant difference in the decrease of MADRS scores compared to placebo at the conclusion of the 8-week study period. The failure highlights potential limitations of asenapine’s efficacy in a bipolar disorder, type II population, which may be influenced by varying pharmacologic response patterns and underlying neurobiological factors in this patient demographic.
   </conclusion> 
</clinical_trial>
