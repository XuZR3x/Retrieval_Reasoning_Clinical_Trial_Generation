**Successful Clinical Trial of Sativex: Efficacy of Sativex® on Quality of Life in Patients with Advanced Cancer-Related Pain**

**A Randomised, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Parallel Group Study of Sativex® in Relief of Quality of Life and Pain Symptoms in Patients with Advanced Cancer-Related Pain**

The purpose of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of Sativex® in improving quality of life and pain management in patients with advanced cancer-related pain. This 16-week study incorporates a 2-week baseline period followed by a 14-week treatment period. This multicenter, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial aims to compare the quality of life outcomes and pain scores between Sativex® and placebo in participants experiencing inadequately controlled cancer pain.

Subjects will be screened for eligibility following the provision of informed consent. Eligible participants will enter the study (Visit 1, Day 1) and complete a 14-day open-label baseline period to establish stable pain metrics. Following this period, subjects will return for a randomisation visit (Visit 2, Day 15), where they will be allocated to either continue receiving Sativex® or switch to a placebo for the entirety of the treatment phase. Regular assessments for pain, quality of life, and adverse events will occur weekly until Visit 5 (Day 112) or earlier should participants withdraw.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Willing and able to provide written informed consent for participation in the study.
- Male or female, aged 18 years or older.
- Documented diagnosis of advanced cancer (stage III or IV) with persistent pain not adequately controlled by opioid therapy, as per the assessment of the investigator.
- Stability on opioid and adjuvant pain management regimen for at least four weeks prior to screening.
- An average daily pain score of ≥4 on an 11-point Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) during the baseline period.
- Patients of childbearing potential must agree to use effective contraception.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Concomitant pain conditions that may interfere with pain evaluation.
- History of hypersensitivity to cannabinoids, any cannabinoid-containing product, or any excipients in the investigational product.
- Administration of other investigational medicinal products (IMPs) within 12 weeks before Visit 1.
- Known history of psychiatric disorders, particularly severe mood disorders or schizophrenia.
- Patients with a history of substance abuse, defined as a current or previous medically diagnosed substance use disorder.
- Any contraindications to cannabinoid use, including a history of significant cardiac dysfunction or arrhythmia in the last 12 months.
- Females who are pregnant, lactating, or planning a pregnancy during the study duration.

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint is the change from baseline in the Brief Pain Inventory (BPI) worst pain score at the end of the treatment period. Quality of life will be assessed using the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire (EORTC QLQ-C30) at baseline, Week 8, and Week 16. Secondary endpoints include the evaluation of safety through the incidence of adverse events, vital signs, and clinical laboratory tests throughout the study duration.

**Study Statistics:**
Statistical analysis will employ an intention-to-treat framework, with a two-sided significance level of 0.05. Changes in BPI scores between Sativex® and placebo groups will be assessed using an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA). Quality of life outcomes will be analyzed similarly, with appropriate adjustments for baseline scores.

**Sample Size:**
A total of 200 subjects are anticipated to be enrolled across multiple study sites, ensuring adequate power to detect statistically significant differences between the treatment arms. 

This study will contribute to understanding the role of Sativex® in managing cancer-related pain while focusing on the broader implications for quality of life in this patient population, which is often neglected in conventional cancer pain management strategies.
