**Failed Clinical Trial of Oxaliplatin: Oxaliplatin and Pembrolizumab for Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma**

**A Phase II Study of Oxaliplatin Combined with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Treatment-Resistant Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma**

**Introduction:**  
This clinical investigation was conducted in order to analyze the efficacy and safety profile of the combination of oxaliplatin and pembrolizumab for patients suffering from treatment-resistant metastatic renal cell carcinoma (mRCC). The premise of the study was based on emerging evidence suggesting a synergistic interaction between chemotherapy and immune checkpoint inhibitors, hypothesizing that oxaliplatin may enhance the immune response mediated by pembrolizumab.

**Study Objective:**  
The primary objective was to evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) of the oxaliplatin/pembrolizumab regimen in patients with mRCC who have progressed after prior systemic therapy. Secondary objectives included assessment of progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), safety, and quality of life parameters.

**Study Design:**  
This was a multicenter, open-label, single-arm Phase II trial. Patients received oxaliplatin intravenously at a dose of 130 mg/m² on day 1 of each 21-day cycle, combined with pembrolizumab at a standard dose of 200 mg on day 1 of each cycle, for up to six cycles or until disease progression or intolerable toxicity occurred.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically confirmed metastatic renal cell carcinoma.  
- Patients must have evidence of disease progression on one or more prior systemic therapies including an immune checkpoint inhibitor.  
- Performance Status of 0-2 on the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) scale.  
- Adequate organ function as demonstrated by laboratory results.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients with significant cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled hypertension.  
- History of major surgical procedure or significant trauma within 30 days prior to enrollment.  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.  
- Prior exposure to oxaliplatin or hypersensitivity reactions to any component of the study drugs.  
- Active viral, bacterial, or fungal infections.

**Endpoints:**  
The primary endpoint of the study was the ORR, defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as per RECIST 1.1 criteria. Secondary endpoints included PFS, OS, and the incidence of treatment-related adverse events (AEs) categorized by severity.

**Results:**  
Although 120 participants were enrolled, the study was terminated prematurely due to lack of efficacy, evidenced by an ORR of only 15% with none achieving a CR. PFS was notably shorter than anticipated, with a median PFS of 3.4 months, significantly below the historical performance of standard systemic treatments for mRCC. Adverse events were observed in 77% of patients, with common toxicities including neuropathy, fatigue, and gastrointestinal symptoms leading to treatment discontinuation in 30% of the patients.

**Conclusions:**  
The combination of oxaliplatin and pembrolizumab does not appear to improve clinical outcomes for patients with treatment-resistant metastatic renal cell carcinoma compared to historical outcomes with currently approved therapies. The findings underscore the necessity for continued investigation into optimal chemotherapy and immunotherapy combinations, while also indicating that oxaliplatin may not serve as a suitable partner for immune checkpoint inhibition in this patient population.

**Future Directions:**  
Further research is warranted to explore alternative dosing regimens or combinations of oxaliplatin with different targeted therapies or immune modulating agents that may demonstrate improved therapeutic index in mRCC. It may also prove beneficial to investigate patient-specific predictors of response to assist in tailoring treatment approaches in advanced renal cell carcinoma.
