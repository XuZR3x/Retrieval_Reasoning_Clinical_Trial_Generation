**Failed Clinical Trial of OGX-427: A Phase II Study of OGX-427 with Docetaxel in Patients with Relapsed Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma**  

**A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Phase II Trial of OGX-427 in Combination with Docetaxel versus Docetaxel Plus Placebo in Patients with Relapsed Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma**  

**NCT#: [REDACTED]**  
**Principal Investigator: [REDACTED]**  
**Date: [REDACTED]**  

**Study Purpose:**  
This study aims to assess the efficacy and safety of OGX-427, a second-generation antisense oligonucleotide targeting heat shock protein-27 (Hsp27), when administered in conjunction with docetaxel, compared to placebo in adults with relapsed Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL). Given the established role of Hsp27 in cancer cell proliferation and resistance to therapeutic agents, the addition of OGX-427 to a standard chemotherapeutic regimen may enhance treatment outcomes.

**Research Hypothesis:**  
The addition of OGX-427 to the treatment of docetaxel will result in a statistically significant improvement in overall response rates (ORR) and progression-free survival (PFS) in individuals with relapsed NHL compared to docetaxel alone.

**Background:**  
Non-Hodgkin lymphoma represents a diverse group of hematological malignancies with variable responses to chemotherapy, particularly in relapsed cases. Standard approaches often involve single-agent chemotherapy or combination regimens with limited long-term efficacy. Inhibition of Hsp27 has been suggested as a potential strategy to enhance chemosensitivity and could improve outcomes in relapsed disease settings. Preliminary evaluations of OGX-427 suggest it may assist in overcoming resistance pathways through the modulation of Hsp27 expression, thereby facilitating apoptosis in tumor cells.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Patients aged ≥ 18 years with histologically confirmed relapsed NHL, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, or other non-Hodgkin variants.
2. Documented measurable disease per the International Working Group (IWG) criteria.
3. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
4. Adequate hematological function: ANC ≥ 1,500/μL, platelets ≥ 100,000/μL, and hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL.
5. Provision of informed consent and willingness to adhere to study protocols.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Prior treatment with systemic chemotherapy for relapsed disease within 12 months.
2. Known central nervous system involvement or other extramedullary disease.
3. Serious concurrent medical conditions, including uncontrolled infections or unstable cardiovascular disease.
4. Pregnant or breastfeeding women, or those planning to become pregnant during the study period.
5. Active malignancies other than NHL that require treatment.

**Study Design:**  
This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Patients will be stratified based on disease subtype and prior treatment history and randomized to receive either OGX-427 at a dose of [REDACTED] mg combined with docetaxel or docetaxel plus placebo.

**Endpoints:**  
**Primary Endpoint:**  
- Overall Response Rate (ORR) at 24 weeks, defined as the percentage of patients achieving complete or partial response according to IWG criteria.

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
- Progression-free survival (PFS) measured from the date of randomization to the first documented progression or death.
- Safety and tolerability assessed by the frequency and severity of adverse events using CTCAE version 5.0.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
A total of [REDACTED] patients are planned to achieve 80% power for detecting a difference in ORR at a 2-sided significance level of 0.05. Patients will be analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Preliminary data suggested a potential for enhanced efficacy when combining OGX-427 with conventional treatments; however, the trial ultimately aimed to elucidate any significant benefits in terms of overall survival and thus impact clinical management of relapsed NHL. 

**Conclusion:**  
This Phase II clinical trial was developed in the context of exploring novel combinations of existing therapies to improve outcomes for patients suffering from relapsed NH, yet highlight the complexities of translating promising preclinical findings to clinical efficacy in a challenging hematological landscape. 

The trial ultimately failed to meet its endpoints regarding ORR and PFS, suggesting that the integration of OGX-427 did not translate into clinically meaningful benefits in this cohort. Further investigation into the molecular targets involved and alternative combination strategies may offer insights for future trials in NHL.
