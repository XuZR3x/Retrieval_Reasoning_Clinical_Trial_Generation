<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Long-Term Efficacy of Donepezil in Patients with Alzheimer's Disease and Vascular Dementia</Title>
    <Objective>
        The primary aim of this study is to evaluate the long-term safety and efficacy of donepezil in patients diagnosed with a combination of Alzheimer's Disease (AD) and Vascular Dementia (VaD). 
        Secondary objectives include assessing cognitive function, quality of life, and functional performance over a 12-month follow-up period.
    </Objective>
    <Purpose>
        1. To compare the efficacy of donepezil in improving cognitive function as measured by the Alzheimer’s Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive (ADAS-Cog) in patients with AD and VaD.
        2. To elucidate the effects of donepezil on overall quality of life and daily living activities in patients with mixed dementia.
        3. To gather data that could inform optimizing treatment strategies for patients with cognitive decline associated with both AD and VaD.
    </Purpose>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>1. Diagnosis of probable AD and VaD according to established criteria (NINCDS-ADRDA and National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke). </Criterion>
        <Criterion>2. Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) scores between 10 and 26.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>3. Evidence of gradual cognitive decline over a minimum of 6 months.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>4. Availability of a caregiver willing to assist with medication adherence and assessment participation.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>1. Presence of any other significant neurodegenerative disorders (e.g., Parkinson’s disease, Huntington’s disease).</Criterion>
        <Criterion>2. Active psychiatric disorder requiring intervention with psychotropic medications.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>3. History of cerebrovascular events or clinically active cerebrovascular disease.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>4. Physiological conditions necessitating acute medical treatment that may confound efficacy outcomes.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <AssessmentTools>
        <Tool>Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive (ADAS-Cog).</Tool>
        <Tool>Quality of Life in Alzheimer's Disease (QoL-AD) questionnaire.</Tool>
        <Tool>Functional Assessment Staging Test (FAST).</Tool>
    </AssessmentTools>
    <StudyDesign>
        The study will employ a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design over a period of 12 months, with participants receiving either donepezil (10 mg daily, titrated to 23 mg) or placebo. 
        Efficacy will be measured at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months using the aforementioned assessment tools.
    </StudyDesign>
    <SampleSize>
        The study aims to enroll a total of 300 participants, with 150 in the donepezil arm and 150 in the placebo arm, to ensure adequate power to detect clinically significant differences.
    </SampleSize>
    <OutcomeMeasures>
        <Measure>1. Change from baseline in ADAS-Cog scores at 6 and 12 months.</Measure>
        <Measure>2. Assessment of quality of life differences using QoL-AD at 6 and 12 months.</Measure>
        <Measure>3. Evaluation of functional ability measured by FAST scores monthly throughout the study duration.</Measure>
    </OutcomeMeasures>
    <Ethics>
        Written informed consent will be obtained from participants or their legal representatives prior to enrollment. The study protocol will be registered and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and local regulatory requirements.
    </Ethics>
    <ExpectedResults>
        It is anticipated that patients receiving donepezil will demonstrate significant improvements in cognitive function, quality of life, and daily functioning, compared to the placebo group.
    </ExpectedResults>
</ClinicalTrial>
