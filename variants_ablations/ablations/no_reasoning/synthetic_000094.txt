**Study of Aprepitant in Combination with Standard Anti-emetic Therapy in Preventing Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV) in Patients with Solid Tumors: A Phase III, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial**

**RATIONALE:** Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV) remains a significant challenge in the treatment of solid tumors, affecting patient quality of life and treatment adherence. Aprepitant, a neurokinin-1 receptor antagonist, has demonstrated efficacy in reducing CINV in various settings. However, its effectiveness when combined with standard anti-emetic therapies in patients receiving a variety of chemotherapy regimens for solid tumors has yet to be definitively studied.

**PURPOSE:** This study intends to evaluate the efficacy of aprepitant when used in conjunction with standard anti-emetic therapies among patients with solid tumors undergoing highly emetogenic chemotherapy.

**DESIGN:** This is a multi-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized study involving patients with various solid tumors. Subjects will be randomized to receive either aprepitant plus standard anti-emetic therapy (including a 5HT3 receptor antagonist and dexamethasone) or a placebo in conjunction with the same standard therapy.

**OUTLINE:** Subjects will be screened and randomly assigned to one of two groups: Group A receiving aprepitant (125 mg orally on Day 1, followed by 80 mg on Days 2 and 3) in addition to dexamethasone and a 5HT3 antagonist, or Group B receiving a placebo with the same anti-emetic regimen. Chemotherapy will be administered according to each patient's standard care protocol which may include regimens such as FOLFOX, BEP, or EC. 

**ACCRUAL:** An estimated 200 patients will be enrolled over a 12-month period. Follow-up assessments for CINV will occur from pre-chemotherapy (Day 0) through Day 10 of the treatment cycle.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
- To compare the complete response rate (defined as no emetic episodes and no use of rescue medication) in patients receiving aprepitant versus those receiving placebo.
 
**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
- To assess the severity of nausea using the Numeric Rating Scale (0-10) daily during the treatment cycle.
- To evaluate the quality of life throughout treatment, measured via the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire (EORTC QLQ-C30).
- To analyze the incidence of any adverse events related to the aprepitant treatment.

**ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA:**
**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed solid tumors scheduled to receive highly emetogenic chemotherapy.
- Male and female patients aged 18 years and older.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Signed informed consent obtained from the patient or legal guardian.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of severe hypersensitivity to aprepitant or related compounds.
- Participation in another clinical trial involving investigational agents within 30 days before enrollment.
- Significant hepatic or renal impairment (Bilirubin > 1.5 x ULN, Creatinine > 1.5 x ULN).
- Pregnant or lactating women.
- Prior treatment with another neurokinin-1 antagonist.

**DATA COLLECTION AND ANALYSIS:**
Data will be collected through patient diaries for episodes of nausea and vomiting and will be analyzed using appropriate statistical methods to compare the efficacy of aprepitant and placebo on CINV.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES:**
The intervention is anticipated to demonstrate statistically significant improvements in the prevention of CINV as measured by the complete response rate at Days 1-5 of the chemotherapy cycle. Aprepitant is expected to enhance overall patient comfort and facilitate adherence to chemotherapy regimens, thereby improving treatment outcomes in patients with solid tumors. 

**FOLLOW-UP:** Patients will be monitored through Day 30 post-chemotherapy to assess long-term side effects and overall quality of life.

This study aims to further elucidate the role of aprepitant in comprehensive anti-emetic strategies, with the goal of improving care for patients undergoing chemotherapy for solid tumors.
