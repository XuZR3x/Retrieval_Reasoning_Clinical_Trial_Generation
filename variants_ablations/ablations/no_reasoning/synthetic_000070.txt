**Failed Clinical Trial of Dexamethasone: Efficacy of Dexamethasone in Decreasing Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting in Patients Undergoing Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy**

**RATIONALE:** Postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) remains a common yet distressing complication following laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Despite multimodal prophylaxis strategies, many patients continue to experience significant symptoms, compromising recovery and hospital discharge times. Dexamethasone, a corticosteroid, has shown efficacy in reducing PONV in various surgical settings; however, its effectiveness in patients specifically undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy has not been adequately evaluated in the context of a formal clinical trial. This study aims to investigate whether the administration of a single dose of dexamethasone can reduce the incidence of PONV compared to placebo.

**PURPOSE:** The primary objective of this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial is to evaluate the effectiveness of a single intravenous dose of dexamethasone in preventing PONV in patients scheduled for laparoscopic cholecystectomy. 

**OBJECTIVES:**
- To compare the incidence and severity of PONV within 24 hours postoperatively between patients receiving dexamethasone and those receiving a placebo.
- To assess the need for rescue antiemetics in both groups.
- To evaluate the postoperative recovery timeline and length of hospital stay between both groups.
- To monitor for potential adverse effects associated with dexamethasone administration including glucose levels, wound healing, and infection rates.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled trial. After obtaining informed consent, patients are randomized into two treatment arms:
- **Arm I:** Patients will receive a single intravenous dose of dexamethasone (8 mg) administered post-anesthesia induction.
- **Arm II:** Patients will receive an equivalent volume of normal saline (placebo) intravenously during the same timeframe.

Both groups will receive routine perioperative care, including standard antiemetic prophylaxis as per institutional guidelines. Postoperative monitoring will include assessment for nausea and vomiting using a numerical rating scale (0-10) and documentation of any rescue antiemetic use within the first 24 hours post-surgery. 

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 400 patients will be enrolled in this study over a planned duration of 12 months.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy for symptomatic gallstone disease.
- Age ≤ 65 years old.
- ASA (American Society of Anesthesiologists) physical status class I-III.
- Planned overnight observation post-surgery.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Inclusion Criteria: 
    - Patients aged 18-65 years.
    - Able to provide informed consent.
    - Absence of uncontrolled systemic disease or contraindications to surgery/anesthesia.
- Exclusion Criteria:
    - History of chronic nausea or vomiting disorders.
    - Regular use of antiemetics or corticosteroids within the preceding month.
    - Pregnancy or lactation.
    - Allergy to dexamethasone.

**OUTCOMES:** The primary endpoint will be the cumulative incidence of PONV within 24 hours post-operation, while secondary outcomes will include the severity of nausea measured by the NRS, the requirement for rescue medication, length of hospital stay, and any adverse effects noted postoperatively.

**CONCLUSION:** Failure to reduce the incidence of PONV with dexamethasone may provide important insights for clinical practice and inform future research directions. This study will enhance the understanding of dexamethasone's role in managing PONV specifically within the context of laparoscopic cholecystectomy. 

**DATA ANALYSIS:** Treatment effects will be analyzed using Chi-square tests for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous outcomes, with a significance threshold set at p<0.05. 

**FUNDING:** This study will be funded by institutional grants and the Department of Anesthesiology.

**ETHICAL CONSIDERATION:** The trial will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and will receive ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board at participating centers. 

**ACTUAL ACCRUAL:** Follow-up and data analysis are scheduled for completion in the subsequent year for publication and dissemination of results.
