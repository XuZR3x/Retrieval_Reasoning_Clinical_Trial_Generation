**Successful Clinical Trial of Anakinra: Effects of Interleukin-1 Inhibition on Cardiovascular Complications in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes and Chronic Kidney Disease**

**The CARDIO-CKD Trial**

**Background**: Cardiovascular diseases (CVD) are prevalent among individuals with type 2 diabetes (T2D) and chronic kidney disease (CKD). Increased levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, particularly interleukin-1 (IL-1), are associated with endothelial dysfunction, increased vascular inflammation, and cardiovascular events. This trial investigates the efficacy of anakinra, a recombinant IL-1 receptor antagonist, in reducing cardiovascular complications in patients with T2D and CKD.

**Study Design**: The CARDIO-CKD trial is a multicenter, randomized, double-blinded, placebo-controlled trial designed to assess the hypothesis that the inhibition of IL-1 activity with anakinra improves cardiovascular outcomes and renal function in patients with T2D and CKD. This study examines the impact of anakinra on inflammation biomarkers, endothelial function, and cardiovascular event rates.

**Objectives**:  
- **Primary Objective**: Evaluate the reduction in high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) levels, a marker of systemic inflammation, after 12 months of treatment.  
- **Secondary Objectives**: Assess changes in renal parameters (eGFR), endothelial function (measured by flow-mediated dilation), and the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) throughout the study duration.

**Methods**:  
A total of 400 patients with T2D and CKD (stage 2-3), aged 40-75 years, were recruited from 30 clinical sites. Participants were randomized to receive either anakinra (100 mg subcutaneously daily) or placebo for 12 months. The primary endpoint, hs-CRP levels post-treatment, was measured at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months, while secondary endpoints were evaluated quarterly.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
1. Signed informed consent;  
2. Male or female patients aged 40 to 75 years;  
3. Established diagnosis of T2D for at least 2 years;  
4. Stage 2-3 CKD, defined as eGFR 30-89 mL/min/1.73m²;  
5. Elevated hs-CRP levels (>3 mg/L);  
6. No evidence of an active infection or malignancy;  
7. Stable treatment regimen for T2D and CKD for at least 3 months prior to enrollment.

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
1. Uncontrolled hypertension (systolic BP >180 mmHg or diastolic BP >110 mmHg);  
2. Previous history of hypersensitivity to anakinra;  
3. Current treatment with anti-inflammatory or immunosuppressive agents;  
4. Severe comorbid conditions limiting life expectancy <12 months;  
5. Significant liver disease or current drug or alcohol abuse;  
6. Participation in another clinical trial within the last 30 days.

**Results**:  
The primary outcome of reduction in hs-CRP revealed a statistically significant lower level in the anakinra group compared to placebo (p<0.001), indicating a marked anti-inflammatory effect. Additionally, participants receiving anakinra exhibited improved renal function with an eGFR increase of 5 mL/min compared to a decrease observed in the placebo cohort (p<0.05). Notably, the incidence of MACE at 12 months was significantly lower in the anakinra group (4% vs. 12% in placebo, p<0.01).

**Conclusion**: The CARDIO-CKD trial confirms that anakinra effectively reduces systemic inflammation, improves cardiovascular and renal outcomes in patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease. The findings support the role of IL-1 inhibition in mitigating cardiovascular risk among this vulnerable population.
