**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy of Esomeprazole in Patients with Non-Erosive Reflux Disease (NERD)**

A Phase III, Multi-Center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy of Esomeprazole 40 mg Once Daily for 12 Weeks in Patients with Non-Erosive Reflux Disease

**Primary Objective**:   
This study aims to assess the efficacy of esomeprazole 40 mg administered once daily over a 12-week period in inducing symptom relief in patients diagnosed with Non-Erosive Reflux Disease (NERD) compared to placebo.

**Study Design**:  
This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind study designed to enroll patients presenting with typical symptoms of NERD. A total of 500 participants will be recruited from 15 outpatient gastrointestinal clinics across multiple regions. The randomization will ensure equal distribution of participants to receive either esomeprazole 40 mg or matched placebo, following a 1:1 allocation. 

**Study Duration**:  
The intervention period will last 12 weeks, consisting of three mandatory visits: screening (visit 1), mid-study evaluation (visit 2 at week 6), and final assessment (visit 3 at week 12).

**Treatment Administration**:  
Participants will be instructed to take one oral tablet of either esomeprazole 40 mg or placebo once daily before breakfast. A comprehensive dietary and lifestyle counseling will also be provided to maximize treatment outcomes.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
- Adult males and females aged 18 years or older.  
- Patients must report having heartburn and/or regurgitation at least 3 times per week for a minimum of 6 months, without evidence of esophageal mucosal injury confirmed by endoscopy (NERD diagnosis).  
- Able to provide informed consent and complete questionnaires regarding symptom assessment.  
- Subjects must be willing to abstain from other proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) or H2 receptor antagonists for a minimum of 4 weeks prior to randomization. 

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
- History of esophagitis, Barrett's esophagus, or any esophageal stricture.  
- Active gastrointestinal disorders requiring intervention or endoscopy.  
- History of abdominal surgery affecting gastric function, including fundoplication.  
- Current use of medications that may significantly interfere with esomeprazole metabolism, including clopidogrel or warfarin.  
- Pregnancy, lactation, or any condition that could impair the subject's ability to comply with the study protocol.

**Endpoints**:  
The primary endpoint will be the proportion of patients achieving a ≥50% reduction in the frequency of heartburn episodes from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints will include the evaluation of Quality of Life (QoL) measures using the GERD-Q questionnaire and overall patient-reported outcomes.

**Statistical Analysis**:  
Statistical significance will be assessed using a two-sided Chi-square test for categorical variables, with a p-value <0.05 considered significant. Analyses will be performed based on the intention-to-treat principle.

**Expected Outcomes**:  
It is hypothesized that patients treated with esomeprazole 40 mg will experience a significantly greater resolution of heartburn symptoms compared to the placebo group, thus supporting esomeprazole's efficacy in treating patients with NERD.

This study aims to contribute further to the understanding of esomeprazole's therapeutic role in a population often overlooked in reflux management, thereby potentially guiding future clinical practice and treatment guidelines.
