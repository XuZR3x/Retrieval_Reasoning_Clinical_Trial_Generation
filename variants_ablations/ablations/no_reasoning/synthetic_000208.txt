**Failed clinical trial of ranibizumab: Ranibizumab for Early Treatment of Diabetic Macular Edema**  
**A Phase 2 Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Ranibizumab in Patients with Early Diabetic Macular Edema and Preserved Visual Function**  

The purpose of this clinical trial is to determine the efficacy and safety of ranibizumab in the treatment of early diabetic macular edema (DME) in patients exhibiting preserved visual function. Recent discussions in the diabetic retinopathy management community have pointed out that patients with early DME and good visual acuity often fall outside the criteria for established treatment protocols. However, clinicians frequently encounter these patients and anecdotal reports suggest potential benefits from early ranibizumab intervention. 

This study is prompted by the unmet need for a systematic evaluation of early intervention strategies to limit the progression of diabetic macular edema in this specific patient demographic. Despite theoretical support for the use of ranibizumab, the absence of robust clinical trials endorsing early treatment has inhibited changes in clinical practice guidelines. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Diagnosis of Early Diabetic Macular Edema as defined by the presence of retinal thickening associated with diabetes mellitus without substantial visual impairment.
2. Best Corrected Visual Acuity (BCVA) of 65 to 80 letters (approximately 20/25 to 20/50 Snellen equivalent).
3. Central foveal thickness (CFT) measured by OCT between 250 um and 350 um at screening.  
4. Male or female patients aged ≥ 18 years.
5. Informed consent signed by the patient.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. History of treatment for diabetic retinopathy (including but not limited to laser photocoagulation or intraocular injections) in the study eye over the past 12 months.
2. Presence of other ocular pathologies that could confound study outcomes (e.g., age-related macular degeneration, retinal vein occlusion).
3. Stroke or myocardial infarction ≤6 months prior to study entry.
4. Current or anticipated pregnancy or lactation in female patients.
5. Receipt of systemic anti-VEGF therapy within 3 months prior to baseline.

The primary endpoint of this study is the change in BCVA from baseline to 12 months, measured in ETDRS letters. Secondary endpoints include the change in central retinal thickness as measured by OCT, quality of life assessment through validated questionnaires, and evaluation of ocular safety profiles following intravitreal injection of ranibizumab.

The trial's design, while well-intentioned, faced variability in patient responses reflecting the complexity of diabetic macular edema pathology and the biological response to ranibizumab treatment. Preliminary results indicated no significant improvement in BCVA compared to control, with a substantial percentage of subjects displaying minimal change in central retinal thickness, resulting in the trial being deemed a failure. These outcomes reflect the heterogeneous nature of DME progression and suggest that early intervention may not confer the expected benefits seen in advanced stages of the disease. 

The results of this trial highlight the necessity for ongoing research to delineate the appropriate timing for the introduction of anti-VEGF therapies in the management of diabetic macular edema, emphasizing the importance of tailoring treatment approaches to the individual patient's disease classification and progression trajectory.
