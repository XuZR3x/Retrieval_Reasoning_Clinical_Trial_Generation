**Study of Efficacy and Safety of Sunitinib in Combination Therapy for Advanced Hepatocellular Carcinoma**

A Phase II Multi-Center, Open-Label Study Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Sunitinib in Combination with Atezolizumab in Patients with Advanced Hepatocellular Carcinoma

This prospective, multi-center, open-label study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of sunitinib in combination with the immune checkpoint inhibitor atezolizumab in patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ineligible for curative treatment. The rationale for the combination therapy stems from preclinical data indicating that angiogenesis plays a significant role in HCC progression and the potential synergistic effect of sunitinib on T-cell mediated anti-tumor immunity produced by atezolizumab.

A total of 120 subjects will be enrolled in this study. Participants will receive sunitinib at a dose of 50 mg orally once daily, in conjunction with atezolizumab administered via intravenous infusion at a dose of 1200 mg every three weeks. Treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal of consent, whichever occurs first.

The primary endpoint of the study is overall survival (OS), defined as the time from the date of randomization to the date of death from any cause. Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS) measured by RECIST 1.1 criteria, objective response rate (ORR), and quality of life (QoL) assessments using the EORTC QLQ-C30 and EQ-5D.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically or radiographically confirmed diagnosis of advanced hepatocellular carcinoma.
- Patients with at least one measurable lesion according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status of 0 or 1.
- Adequate liver function defined as Child-Pugh class A or B.
- No prior systemic therapy for advanced HCC.
- Patients must provide informed consent and be willing to comply with study requirements.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Active autoimmune disease or a history of autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment within the past 2 years.
- Previous treatment with anti-angiogenic agents or checkpoint inhibitors.
- Ongoing or recent participation in any other investigational drug study within 30 days prior to enrollment.
- Diagnosed with any second malignancy within 5 years, with exceptions for adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancer or cervical carcinoma in situ.
- Known history of hypersensitivity to any component of the study treatments.
- Pregnant or lactating females, and females of childbearing potential must utilize effective contraception.

**Efficacy Assessment:**
Tumor assessments to evaluate treatment response will be performed using computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) at screening and every 8 weeks thereafter until disease progression or the start of subsequent anti-neoplastic therapy. 

Safety will be monitored throughout the study, with adverse events graded according to the NCI Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. 

Expected outcomes from this study include a significant improvement in overall survival and objective response rate compared to historical controls, leveraging the hypothesis that dual targeting of angiogenesis and immune checkpoints will yield synergistic benefits in patients suffering from advanced HCC.

The results of this clinical trial could potentially expand therapeutic options for patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma and further validate the role of sunitinib in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors in this setting.
