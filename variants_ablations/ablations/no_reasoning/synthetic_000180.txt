Failed clinical trial of natalizumab: Efficacy and Safety of Natalizumab in Acute Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) A Phase II, Multicenter, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Intravenous Natalizumab (BG00002) in Patients with Acute Traumatic Brain Injury The primary objective of this study is to assess whether a single 300 mg dose of intravenous natalizumab can reduce neurological deterioration as measured by the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) from Baseline to Day 7 in participants suffering from mild to moderate acute traumatic brain injury administered within 6 hours of the injury. The secondary objectives include: to evaluate the efficacy of natalizumab on neurological outcomes measured by the Extended Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS-E); to assess the effect of natalizumab on functional recovery as indicated by changes in the quality of life scores via the EuroQol-5D (EQ-5D) from Baseline to Day 30; and to determine the safety profile of natalizumab in this patient population. Key Inclusion Criteria: - Age ≥18 years and ≤75 years at enrollment; - Clinical diagnosis of mild to moderate acute traumatic brain injury with a score of 9 to 13 on the GCS at Screening; - Participants must receive treatment within 6 hours of injury; - Presence of acute intracranial hemorrhage on CT scan must not exceed 25 mL; - Participants must be able to provide informed consent or have a legally authorized representative who can consent on their behalf; - Female participants of childbearing potential must agree to effective contraception for the entire study duration. Key Exclusion Criteria: - GCS <9 at the time of randomization; - Evidence of severe intracranial hemorrhage (defined as >25 mL) or mass effect on CT; - History of prior TBI or chronic neurological disorders; - Known hypersensitivity to natalizumab or its components; - Pregnant or lactating women; - Active infection or significant systemic illness at Baseline; - Presence of contraindications to MRI if required for study assessments. NOTE: Other protocol-defined inclusion/exclusion criteria may apply. Primary Endpoint: Change in GCS from Baseline to Day 7 The GCS is a clinical scale that assesses a patient's level of consciousness, based on eye, verbal, and motor responses. Improvements in the GCS will be evaluated to assess the impact of natalizumab on acute brain injury recovery. Secondary Endpoints: - Change in GOS-E score from Baseline to Day 30; - Change in EQ-5D scores, measuring quality of life, from Baseline to Day 30; - Incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs) reported during the study period. 

This clinical trial is designated as failed, as the administration of natalizumab did not demonstrate a significant reduction in neurological deterioration, as reflected in the primary endpoint of change in GCS, compared to placebo. Furthermore, secondary endpoints assessing functional outcomes and quality of life did not meet the predetermined statistical significance thresholds, raising questions about the effectiveness of immunomodulatory approaches in managing acute TBI and emphasizing the complexity of neurological recovery mechanisms in these patients. The safety profile of natalizumab was consistent with prior reports; however, the lack of clinical efficacy highlights the challenges faced in translating theoretical benefits of immune modulation to meaningful clinical outcomes in acute brain injuries.
