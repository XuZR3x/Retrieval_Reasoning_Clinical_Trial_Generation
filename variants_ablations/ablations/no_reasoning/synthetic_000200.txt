Failed clinical trial of telmisartan: Combination Therapy of Telmisartan and Metformin in Reducing Cardiometabolic Risks in Obese Patients with Hypertension (CATT Study)

Objectives: The primary goal of this clinical investigation was to evaluate the efficacy of background therapy with telmisartan alongside metformin in mitigating cardiometabolic risk factors (specifically insulin resistance and hypertension) in a cohort comprising obese patients diagnosed with hypertension. The overarching hypothesis posited that the dual intervention would yield a superior reduction in insulin resistance as measured by homeostasis model assessment (HOMA-IR) compared to monotherapy.

Background: Obesity is a prevalent condition that significantly increases the risk of developing hypertension and metabolic disorders, leading to heightened cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. Insulin resistance, often concomitant with obesity, exacerbates these comorbidities by promoting hypertension and associated metabolic dysfunctions. Telmisartan, an angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB), has shown promise in lowering blood pressure and potentially ameliorating insulin sensitivity through its PPAR-γ agonistic effects. Meanwhile, metformin, a first-line pharmacological agent for type 2 diabetes, enhances insulin sensitivity and promotes weight loss, making it a suitable counterpart for combination therapy aimed at addressing both hypertension and insulin resistance in obese patients. 

Design: This study was structured as a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted over a period of 52 weeks. A total of 400 participants aged 30 to 65 years with a body mass index (BMI) between 30 and 40 kg/m², and stable hypertension (mean BP ≥140/90 mmHg) were recruited. Participants were randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive either telmisartan 80 mg once daily plus metformin 1000 mg twice daily, telmisartan 80 mg once daily plus placebo, or metformin 1000 mg twice daily plus placebo.

Inclusion Criteria: 
- Informed consent obtained.
- Age between 30 to 65 years.
- Body mass index (BMI) 30-40 kg/m².
- Documented diagnosis of essential hypertension.
- Stable regimen of antihypertensive medications for the preceding three months.
  
Exclusion Criteria: 
- Concurrent treatment with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or any antidiabetic medication other than metformin within the last three months.
- History of chronic heart failure (NYHA class III-IV) or significant arrhythmias.
- Renal insufficiency (eGFR < 60 mL/min).
- Known hypersensitivity to telmisartan or metformin.
- Current treatment with systemic corticosteroids or any drugs known to interfere with glucose metabolism.

Results: The primary endpoint assessed the changes from baseline in HOMA-IR at 52 weeks. Secondary endpoints included changes in systolic and diastolic blood pressure, lipid profiles, and body weight. Despite initial hypotheses suggesting a combined therapeutic benefit, the trial was halted after an interim analysis revealed no significant differences between the telmisartan plus metformin group and either control group in terms of primary and secondary outcomes. 

Adverse events related to gastrointestinal disturbances were noted to occur more frequently in the metformin-treated cohorts, leading to higher rates of treatment discontinuation. Furthermore, initial issues with patient compliance were evident, as several participants reported difficulties adhering to the dual medication regimen, likely contributing to the lack of observed efficacy. 

Conclusion: The CATT study underscored the challenges inherent in pharmacological combination therapy for obesity-related hypertension. The lack of detectable benefit in insulin sensitivity despite evidence from prior mechanistic studies indicated a potential disconnect between theoretical benefits and clinical efficacy. Further investigation regarding patient selection, adherence strategies, and alternative therapeutic combinations may be warranted to address the multifaceted nature of obesity and metabolic syndrome hypertension. 

This trial was ultimately categorized as a failure, highlighting the complexities of treating cardiometabolic disorders in the bespoke patient population.
