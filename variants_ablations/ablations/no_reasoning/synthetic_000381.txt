**EFFICACY AND SAFETY OF ESTRADIOL THERAPY ON COGNITIVE FUNCTION IN POSTMENOPAUSAL WOMEN WITH COGNITIVE IMPAIRMENT: A RANDOMIZED, DOUBLE-BLIND, PLACEBO-CONTROLLED, MULTICENTER TRIAL**

The purpose of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of estradiol therapy in enhancing cognitive function in postmenopausal women presenting with mild cognitive impairment (MCI). Prior evidence demonstrates potential benefits of estrogen in neuroprotection; however, this hypothesis remains inadequately validated through rigorous clinical investigations. Estradiol, a key estrogen, has been proposed to exert neuroprotective effects, influencing cognitive decline associated with menopausal transition. The outcome of the study aims to confirm whether estradiol therapy significantly impacts cognitive performance compared to placebo in this demographic group.

This is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled multicenter trial designed to assess the cognitive improvement attributed to estradiol administration compared to an inert substance. Participants will be administered 1 mg of estradiol daily for 24 weeks or a matching placebo. The primary endpoints will include changes in cognitive function measured using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) and secondary outcomes pertinent to mood evaluation via the Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS). 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Healthy postmenopausal women, aged 55-75 years.
- Self-reported cognitive complaints and clinical diagnoses of mild cognitive impairment according to the Mayo Clinic criteria.
- Serum estradiol levels <30 pg/mL prior to enrollment.
- Normal thyroid function tests within 6 months of baseline.
- Warren-Washington Socioeconomic Status scale scores indicative of low to moderate income.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of hormone replacement therapy in the 6 months preceding the study.
- Any severe psychiatric disorders impacting cognitive assessment.
- Significant neurological disorders, including but not limited to dementia and stroke.
- History of breast cancer or other estrogen-sensitive malignancies.
- Medical conditions impeding regular participation or follow-up (e.g., uncontrolled diabetes, significant cardiovascular disease).

Cognitive function, as measured by MoCA, will be the primary outcome. This assessment will be conducted at baseline, 12 weeks, and 24 weeks, with a focus on cognitive domains such as memory, executive function, attention, and visuospatial abilities. Furthermore, secondary measures will monitor mood alterations through GDS scores at the same intervals.

It is hypothesized that estradiol treatment will confer significant improvements in both cognitive performance and mood, with participants exhibiting enhanced scores relative to placebo controls. However, disparities in neurocognitive outcomes have been observed previously, and this trial aims to elucidate the extent of estradiol's influence on the cognitive trajectory of postmenopausal women.
