**Failed Clinical Trial of Atorvastatin: Atorvastatin and Its Effects on Neurocognitive Function in HIV-1 Infected Individuals - A Randomized Controlled Trial**

Atorvastatin and Its Effects on Neurocognitive Function in HIV-1 Infected Individuals - A Randomized Controlled Trial (Atorva-HIV Cognition Study)

Emerging evidence suggests that inflammation associated with chronic HIV-1 infection may impact neurocognitive function, leading to disorders such as HIV-associated neurocognitive disorders (HAND). The Atorva-HIV Cognition Study aimed to investigate the effects of atorvastatin, a well-established statin known for its cholesterol-lowering and potential anti-inflammatory effects, on neurocognitive outcomes among HIV-1 infected patients. Specifically, this study sought to assess whether atorvastatin could mitigate neurocognitive decline through its hypothesized mechanisms of reducing inflammation and improving vascular integrity in patients with controlled HIV.

The primary hypothesis posited that atorvastatin administration would be associated with significant improvements in neurocognitive performance, as measured by standardized neuropsychological assessments, compared to the placebo group. Furthermore, the trial aimed to explore the biological underpinnings of any observed effects by measuring serum inflammatory biomarkers pre-and post-treatment.

Inclusion Criteria:
- HIV-1 infected individuals, aged 18-65 years.
- Documented HIV-1 viral suppression (<50 copies/mL) on a stable antiretroviral therapy regimen for at least 12 months.
- CD4 T-cell count > 300 cells/mm3 within the last 6 months.
- No history of neurological disorders unrelated to HIV or any acute psychiatric condition, including substance abuse, within the last year.
- Signed informed consent.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Current or previous CNS opportunistic infections.
- History of structural brain abnormalities identified by MRI.
- Recent use of statins or other lipid-lowering therapies within 60 days prior to study entry.
- Uncontrolled hypertension (BP > 140/90 mmHg) or significant chronic comorbid conditions that might interfere with study outcomes (e.g., advanced renal disease, hepatic dysfunction).
- Active malignancies except for non-melanoma skin cancers.

The study followed a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled design where participants were assigned to receive either 40 mg of atorvastatin or placebo once daily for 24 weeks. Neurocognitive function was assessed using a comprehensive battery of tests including the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), the HIV Dementia Scale, and neuropsychological measures of executive function, processing speed, and memory.

Despite rigorous study design and enrollment of a demographically diverse cohort of HIV-infected individuals, the Atorva-HIV Cognition Study failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in neurocognitive function in the atorvastatin group compared to placebo after the 24-week intervention period. Additionally, no significant changes in inflammatory markers were observed, undermining the proposed anti-inflammatory hypothesis.

These negative results may be attributed to several factors. Lack of robust neuroprotective effects of atorvastatin in the context of ongoing inflammation in HIV-1 infection could indicate that statin therapy may not sufficiently penetrate the central nervous system or that the neurocognitive deficits arise from mechanisms not exclusively modulated by statin therapy. Furthermore, residual confounding variables such as adherence to the ART regimen and variability in neurocognitive assessments amongst diverse participants may have diluted the potential efficacy signals.

In conclusion, although prior studies have suggested a potential role for atorvastatin in modulating inflammation in other contexts, the Atorva-HIV Cognition Study has conclusively indicated that atorvastatin does not confer neurocognitive benefits in individuals with suppressed HIV-1, warranting reconsideration of its role in neurocognitive outcomes within this patient population. Further research is required to explore alternative therapeutic targets for alleviating HIV-associated neurocognitive decline.
