**Successful Clinical Trial of Raltegravir: Efficacy and Safety of Raltegravir in HIV-1 Infected Patients with Comorbid Cardiovascular Disease**

**Title:** A Phase IV Open-label Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Raltegravir in HIV-1 Infected Patients with Documented Cardiovascular Disease: A Multi-Center, Prospective Clinical Trial

**Objective:** This study aims to assess the clinical efficacy and safety of raltegravir in HIV-1 infected patients with existing cardiovascular conditions. The primary endpoint will be the change in cardiovascular health markers and HIV viral load, while secondary endpoints will include qualitative assessments of patient well-being and side effects.

**Design:** This is a Phase IV, open-label, multi-center, prospective clinical trial designed to monitor the impact of a regimen that incorporates raltegravir in HIV-1 infected patients with cardiovascular disease.

**Intervention:** Eligible HIV-1 infected subjects will switch from their current antiretroviral regimen to:
- Raltegravir 400 mg twice daily 
- Additional supportive therapy consistent with cardiovascular care as per current practice guidelines.

**Duration:** The study will encompass an observation period of 24 weeks, during which health markers will be measured at baseline, 12 weeks, and following completion of therapy.

**Sample Size:** A total of 120 HIV-1 infected individuals aged 30 years and older, with documented cardiovascular disease, will be enrolled from multiple sites.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Males or females aged ≥30 years with confirmed HIV-1 infection.
2. Documented history of cardiovascular disease (e.g., coronary artery disease, congestive heart failure).
3. Signed informed consent.
4. HIV RNA level < 200 copies/mL at screening.
5. Stable antiretroviral therapy for at least 6 months prior to enrollment.
6. No history of prior exposure to raltegravir.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Active opportunistic infections or current malignancies.
2. History of hypersensitivity or severe reactions to raltegravir or its excipients.
3. Uncontrolled hypertension (>160/100 mmHg).
4. Participants on chemotherapeutic agents or non-ARV immunosuppressive medications.
5. Serious psychiatric disorders that preclude participation as determined by the investigator.
6. Known high-risk behaviors for HIV transmission or unsafe practices contrary to study protocols.

**Assessment and Monitoring:**
- At baseline, all subjects will undergo a comprehensive cardiovascular evaluation which includes echocardiography, electrocardiogram, and laboratory work-up for lipid profiles and other cardiovascular risk factors.
- Follow-up assessments will be performed at 12 and 24 weeks post-initiation, focusing on changes in cardiovascular health markers, HIV viral load, tolerance to the regimen, and safety events.
- Patient-reported outcomes will be collected to gauge the quality of life using standardized questionnaires.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Change in HIV viral load (HIV RNA < 50 copies/mL) at the completion of the 24-week treatment.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Change in cardiovascular health markers (lipid profile, inflammatory markers) and quality of life indices based on patient-reported outcomes.

**Data Analysis:** The study will utilize appropriate statistical methods, including paired t-tests or non-parametric equivalents for continuous variables, and Chi-square tests for categorical data. An intent-to-treat analysis will be conducted to evaluate treatment efficacy.

**Ethical Considerations:** This study will comply with ethical principles that govern clinical research, and ethical approval will be obtained from the relevant institutional review boards. All participants will provide written informed consent prior to study enrollment.

This clinical trial is expected to yield valuable data regarding the safety and efficacy of raltegravir in the unique patient population of HIV infected individuals with cardiovascular disease, providing insights into optimizing antiretroviral therapy in patients with comorbid conditions.
