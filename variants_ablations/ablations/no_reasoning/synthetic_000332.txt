**Failed clinical trial of Taxotere: Taxotere Plus Bevacizumab in Advanced Ovarian Serous Carcinoma**

**A Randomized Phase II Study of Taxotere and Bevacizumab in Patients with Advanced Ovarian Serous Carcinoma**

This research study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the combination of Taxotere (docetaxel) and bevacizumab as treatment options for patients diagnosed with advanced ovarian serous carcinoma. Given the historical challenges in managing resistant forms of ovarian cancer, there is a notable requirement for newer chemotherapeutic strategies that may yield improved outcomes. Approximately 220 subjects will participate in this trial, which seeks to assess the objective response rate (ORR) of the combination of Taxotere and bevacizumab in this patient population.

**Inclusion Criteria:**

- Patients must have histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced serous ovarian carcinoma, classified as FIGO stage III or IV.
- Patients must have measurable disease as defined by RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- Age greater than or equal to 18 years.
- An ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ and marrow function, defined as follows:
  - leukocytes greater than or equal to 3,000/microliter
  - hemoglobin greater than or equal to 9.0 g/dl 
  - absolute neutrophil count greater than or equal to 1,500/microliter
  - platelets greater than or equal to 100,000/microliter
- Patients must provide written informed consent prior to study participation.
- Women of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and consent to use effective contraception during the trial and for 6 months following treatment discontinuation.

**Exclusion Criteria:**

- Patients who have received prior chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or targeted therapy for ovarian cancer.
- Previous history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to Taxotere or bevacizumab.
- Patients with central nervous system metastases or significant cardiovascular disease.
- Concurrent therapy with other investigational agents.
- Pregnant or lactating women.
- Patients with active infections or any uncontrolled medical condition.

**Primary Endpoint:**

The primary endpoint of the study is to determine the Objective Response Rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving a Complete Response (CR) or Partial Response (PR) according to RECIST criteria after 6 cycles of treatment.

**Secondary Endpoint:**

Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and the incidence/frequency of adverse events, all evaluated according to NCI Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 4.0.

**Methodology:**

Patients meeting the inclusion criteria will be randomized into two groups: one receiving Taxotere 75 mg/m² plus bevacizumab 15 mg/kg every 21 days, and the other receiving standard single-agent therapy of carboplatin/paclitaxel. Each participant will undergo imaging studies at baseline and then after every two cycles to assess treatment response.

Safety evaluations will be conducted, with monitoring for serious adverse events (SAEs) according to the CTCAE, and patients will be followed for a total duration of 12 months post-treatment.

**Statistical Analysis:**

The analysis will be performed using intention-to-treat principles. The effectiveness will be analyzed using the Chi-square test for categorical variables and Kaplan-Meier methods for survival analysis.

**Anticipated Challenges:**

Previous studies have revealed a lack of significant benefit in therapy modifications when using Taxotere in combination with other agents, often attributed to identified mechanisms of resistance and toxicity profiles that limit patient tolerability. This trial anticipates similar challenges regarding response limitations and severe side effects leading to treatment discontinuation and study failure.

**Disruption of Study:**

Due to lower than anticipated ORR and the emergence of intolerable adverse effects, including significant neutropenia and vascular events associated with the bevacizumab component, the study was terminated after a review of interim data, yielding lower-than-expected survival benefits compared to established treatment paradigms.

The findings of this trial underscore the complexities associated with chemotherapeutic interventions targeting advanced ovarian cancer and highlight the necessity of innovative combinations undergoing rigorous evaluation prior to clinical approval.
