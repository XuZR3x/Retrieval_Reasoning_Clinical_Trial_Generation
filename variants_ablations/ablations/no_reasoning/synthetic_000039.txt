**Failed Clinical Trial of Etoposide: A Randomized Phase II Trial of Etoposide, Doxorubicin, and Oxaliplatin in Patients with Recurrent/refractory Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Study Design**: This phase II open-label, randomized study is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a combination regimen of etoposide, doxorubicin, and oxaliplatin (EDO) versus a standard chemotherapy regimen of etoposide and carboplatin (EC) in patients diagnosed with recurrent or refractory small cell lung cancer (SCLC).

**Objective**: The primary objective is to compare the overall response rate (ORR) of patients treated with the EDO regimen to those receiving the EC regimen, as well as evaluating secondary endpoints including progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and toxicity profiles.

**Treatment Arms**:
1. **Experimental Arm**: Patients receive Etoposide + Doxorubicin + Oxaliplatin
   - Etoposide: 100 mg/m² on days 1-3 intravenously
   - Doxorubicin: 60 mg/m² on day 1 intravenously
   - Oxaliplatin: 130 mg/m² on day 1 intravenously
   - Treatment cycles will last 21 days.
   
2. **Control Arm**: Patients receive Etoposide + Carboplatin
   - Etoposide: 80 mg/m² on days 1-3 intravenously
   - Carboplatin: AUC=5 on day 1 intravenously
   - Treatment cycles will last 21 days.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Histologically confirmed recurrent or refractory small cell lung cancer
- Measurable disease as determined by RECIST criteria 
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2
- Age ≥ 18 years and ≤ 75 years
- Adequate renal (creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min) and hepatic function (bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x upper limit of normal)
- Written informed consent provided

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Prior treatment with doxorubicin, oxaliplatin, or any other anthracycline or platinum-based chemotherapy
- Any significant uncontrolled medical condition or laboratory abnormality
- Known hypersensitivity to etoposide, doxorubicin, or oxaliplatin
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women
- Other active malignancies within the past 5 years, except non-melanoma skin cancers

**Endpoints**:
- **Primary Endpoint**: Overall response rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) at the end of two treatment cycles.
- **Secondary Endpoints**: Progression-free survival (PFS) - time from randomization to disease progression, overall survival (OS) - time from randomization to death from any cause, and toxicity assessment using National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

**Statistical Analysis**: Efficacy will be assessed based on intention-to-treat principles. ORR will be compared between groups using Chi-squared tests, while PFS and OS will be estimated using Kaplan-Meier methods, with group differences analyzed using the log-rank test.

**Report on Findings**: After a recruitment phase of 16 months, with enrollment of 200 participants, the trial was halted due to futility after an interim analysis revealed that the EDO regimen did not demonstrate superior efficacy over the EC regimen. The ORR was 18% in the EDO group compared to 22% in the EC group, with median PFS of 3.2 months versus 4.0 months, respectively. The toxicity profile was comparable between both treatment regimens; however, patients receiving EDO had a higher incidence of grade 3-4 hematologic toxicities. Adverse events included neutropenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia, which were more frequent in the experimental arm (35% versus 20% in the control arm).

In conclusion, the lack of significant efficacy and the increased incidence of adverse hematologic effects led to the conclusion that the EDO combination does not provide a favorable benefit-risk ratio compared to the established EC regimen, resulting in the premature termination of the trial. Further research into alternative combinations or dosing strategies is recommended.
