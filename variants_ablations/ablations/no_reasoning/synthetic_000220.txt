**Failed Clinical Trial of Exparel: Evaluation of Exparel for Analgesia Following Shoulder Surgery**

**Comparison of Exparel to Bupivacaine HCl and Placebo for Post-Surgical Analgesia Following Elective Shoulder Surgery**

**Study Design:**  
This prospective, double-blind, randomized, multicenter trial aims to investigate the safety and efficacy of liposomal bupivacaine (Exparel) compared to 0.25% bupivacaine HCl and saline placebo in providing postoperative analgesia after elective shoulder surgery. The central hypothesis postulates that Exparel will outperform both bupivacaine HCl and placebo in terms of pain relief duration and overall quality of recovery.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adult patients aged 18-80 years  
- Scheduled for elective shoulder surgery (e.g., arthroscopic shoulder repair, total shoulder arthroplasty)  
- American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I-III  
- Ability to provide informed consent and communicate in English  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- History of allergic reactions to amide-type local anesthetics  
- Uncontrolled medical conditions or significant comorbidities affecting surgical recovery  
- Pregnant or nursing females or planning to become pregnant during the study period  
- Previous shoulder surgery or known structural abnormalities impacting surgical technique  
- Use of chronic opioid medications or present substance use disorders  
- Anticipated need for interventional pain management other than prescribed postoperative regimen  

**Objectives:**  
- Primary Objective: To assess pain intensity following surgery using a numerical rating scale (NRS-11), measuring pain levels preoperatively, at 12, 24, and 48 hours postoperatively.  
- Secondary Objectives: To evaluate the total opioid consumption within the first 48 hours post-surgery, monitor adverse events, and ascertain the quality of recovery through a validated questionnaire.

**Trial Procedure:**  
- Subjects will be randomized into three groups: receiving 30 mL of Exparel, 30 mL of 0.25% bupivacaine HCl, or 30 mL of saline.  
- The infiltrate will be administered post-surgically at the incision site by the operating surgeon prior to wound closure. Subjects will remain hospitalized for monitoring for 48 hours post-surgery.  
- Follow-up assessments will be conducted through telephonic interviews at 1-week and 1-month intervals to collect additional data on pain management outcomes and any delayed complications.

**Endpoints:**
1. Change in pain intensity determined by NRS-11 scores from baseline versus time points.
2. Total opioid requirement within 48 hours post-surgery.
3. Incidence of adverse events associated with study drug administration.

**Sample Size:**  
A total of 150 participants will be enrolled, with equal allocation to each treatment group. Statistical significance will be defined at a p-value of <0.05.

**Outcome Measures:**  
- The primary measure of efficacy will be pain scores assessed at the specified post-operative time intervals.  
- Incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events will be documented comprehensively throughout the trial duration.  

**Study Duration:**  
The projected duration of the study is 12 months, encompassing recruitment, data collection, and follow-up periods.

**Conclusion:**  
Despite previous hypothesis-driven assumptions regarding the analgesic efficacy of Exparel in varying surgical settings, this trial aims to rigorously evaluate its performance in shoulder surgery. Ultimately, this study will contribute to elucidating whether liposomal bupivacaine can provide sustained analgesia and improve postoperative recovery, while also considering the potential implications of adverse outcomes.

**Note:** The trial was halted due to interim analysis suggesting that Exparel did not demonstrate superior analgesic outcomes compared to bupivacaine HCl or placebo, with no significant differences in opioid consumption or adverse event profiles.
