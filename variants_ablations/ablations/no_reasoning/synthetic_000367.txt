Failed clinical trial of daclizumab: A Study of Daclizumab for the Prevention of Chronic Rejection in Pulmonary Transplant Recipients. Intervention Number: 20200 This protocol seeks to evaluate the safety and efficacy of daclizumab (Zenapax(R)) in combination with standard immunosuppressive regimens for the prevention of chronic rejection in lung transplant recipients. Despite previous findings suggesting daclizumab's potential utility in maintaining long-term allograft acceptance, recent literature highlights the complexities of chronic rejection pathways that may not be effectively targeted by traditional monoclonal antibody applications. This study is particularly relevant as lung transplant patients face a high incidence of chronic rejection, significantly impacting long-term survival.

The study hypothesizes that the addition of daclizumab to standard regimens—specifically comprising tacrolimus and mycophenolate mofetil—will lead to a reduction in the incidence and severity of chronic rejection episodes, as measured by histopathological assessments. However, early data indicates that the mechanisms underlying chronic rejection differ significantly from acute rejection pathways seen in other organ transplants, potentially limiting the success of utilizing IL-2 receptor antagonists in this context.

Objectives: 
- To investigate the rates of chronic rejection in lung transplant recipients receiving daclizumab in conjunction with standard immunosuppressive therapy.
- To assess the safety profile of daclizumab in this unique patient population, especially concerning lung function and incidence of infections over a period of 12 months.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Patients aged 18 years or older undergoing their first single or bilateral lung transplantation.
- Patients must have established postoperative care and be neurologically competent to consent.
- Positive HIV and Hepatitis B surface antigen tests are not grounds for exclusion.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Recipients of previous lung transplants or other solid organ transplants.
- Patients with a history of hypersensitivity reactions to daclizumab or its components.
- Active malignancy or significant comorbid conditions expected to lead to death within 12 months.
- Concurrent use of investigational immunosuppressive agents not approved for use in lung transplantation within 30 days prior to enrollment.

This multicenter, randomized, double-blind trial will compare the incidence of chronic rejection episodes in the experimental group receiving daclizumab against a control group receiving placebo alongside standard immunosuppressive therapy. The primary endpoint will be the percentage of participants developing chronic rejection as defined by established histopathological criteria within one year post-transplant.

Patients will be closely monitored for adverse events, particularly opportunistic infections and pulmonary function decline. Despite the theoretical advantages of daclizumab due to its modulatory effects on T-cell populations, it is hypothesized that its efficacy in chronic rejection scenarios will prove inadequate based on the underlying complexities of immune response relevant to lung grafts.

The anticipated safety and efficacy outcomes will provide crucial insights into the utility of daclizumab in this setting, with the overarching aim of improving the management protocols for lung transplant recipients facing high rates of chronic rejection.
