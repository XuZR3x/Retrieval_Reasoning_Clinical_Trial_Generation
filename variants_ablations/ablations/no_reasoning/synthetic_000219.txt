Failed clinical trial of bleomycin sulfate: Comparative Evaluation of Bleomycin Combined with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients With Advanced Melanoma A Phase III Trial Assessing the Efficacy of Bleomycin Sulfate in Conjunction with Nivolumab Versus Nivolumab Alone in Patients With Stage III or IV Melanoma RATIONALE: Immune checkpoint inhibitors have revolutionized the treatment paradigm in advanced melanoma by enhancing anti-tumor immunity. However, the combination of bleomycin sulfate, a chemotherapeutic agent with a distinct mechanism of action, may potentially improve therapeutic outcomes by facilitating tumor cell death and increasing neoantigen release. The effectiveness of bleomycin sulfate in conjunction with immune checkpoint inhibitors remains to be elucidated. PURPOSE: This randomized phase III trial aims to compare the efficacy and safety of bleomycin sulfate combined with nivolumab against nivolumab alone in patients presenting with advanced melanoma. OBJECTIVES: - Primary Objective: Evaluate overall survival (OS) in patients receiving nivolumab plus bleomycin sulfate versus those receiving nivolumab alone. - Secondary Objectives: Assess progression-free survival (PFS), overall response rates (ORR), and toxicity profiles in both treatment cohorts. Evaluate quality of life metrics and correlate treatment responses with immune-related biomarkers. OUTLINE: This is a multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled study. Patients are stratified according to stage of melanoma (Stage III vs Stage IV) and prior line of therapy. Upon consent, patients are randomly assigned to one of two treatment arms: - Arm I: Patients receive nivolumab IV on day 1 every 2 weeks. In addition, patients receive bleomycin sulfate IV on days 1 and 15 concurrently for the initial 4 doses of nivolumab. - Arm II: Patients receive nivolumab IV on day 1 every 2 weeks with a placebo infusion on days 1 and 15 for the initial 4 doses. Each treatment cycle spans 12 weeks, with the two arms continuing until disease progression, recurrence, or unacceptable toxicity is noted. Quality of life assessments utilizing validated instruments are conducted at baseline and every 12 weeks thereafter. Patients are followed for a total duration of at least 24 months post-randomization for long-term outcomes and safety evaluation. PROJECTED ACCRUAL: It is anticipated that a total of 600 patients (300 per treatment arm) will be enrolled in the trial. Enrollment is projected to be completed within 5 years. DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS: - Histologically confirmed advanced melanoma (Stage III or IV) - Measurable disease as per RECIST guidelines - No active central nervous system metastases - No prior exposure to immune checkpoint inhibitors - No prior treatment with bleomycin sulfate or other chemotherapy agents. PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS: Age: - 18 years and older Sex: - Male and female Performance status: - ECOG 0-2 Life expectancy: - Not specified Hematopoietic: - WBC ≥ 3,000/mm³ - Platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³ Hepatic: - Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times ULN - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 times ULN if no liver metastases are present; ≤ 5 times ULN for those with liver metastases Renal: - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 times ULN or creatinine clearance ≥ 40 mL/min - No significant electrolyte imbalances Other: - No other malignancy (except basal cell carcinoma) - Not pregnant or nursing - Patients must provide effective contraception during the trial and for 6 months post-treatment PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY: Biologic therapy: - No prior exposure to immune checkpoint inhibitors Chemotherapy: - No prior chemotherapy for advanced melanoma Radiotherapy: - Not received radiotherapy within 4 weeks prior to randomization. EVENT-FREE SURVIVAL: Event-free survival is defined as the time from randomization to disease progression, death from any cause, or the initiation of new systemic therapy. Patients without reported events are censored at last follow-up. 

This clinical trial of bleomycin sulfate demonstrates the challenges of synergistic treatment strategies combining traditional chemotherapy with innovative immunotherapy, ultimately highlighting complexities of tumor heterogeneity and treatment resistance in advanced melanoma populations.
