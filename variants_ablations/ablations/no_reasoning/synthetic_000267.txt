Failed clinical trial of carboplatin: Carboplatin and Nivolumab in Treating Patients With Stage IV Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Lung 

A Phase II Randomized Trial of Carboplatin and Nivolumab With or Without Pembrolizumab in Patients With Stage IV Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Lung

RATIONALE: Current therapies for stage IV squamous cell carcinoma of the lung (SCCL) often yield limited efficacy. Carboplatin is a platinum-based chemotherapeutic agent that has been traditionally utilized in SCCL. Nivolumab, a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitor, and pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 antibody, represent strategies to enhance immune response against malignancies. This study aims to evaluate the synergy and efficacy of combined carboplatin and immunotherapy in treating patients with advanced SCCL.

PURPOSE: This phase II trial investigates the safety and efficacy of carboplatin in conjunction with nivolumab compared to the carboplatin, nivolumab, and pembrolizumab combination in patients diagnosed with stage IV SCCL who have not received prior systemic therapy.

PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:
I. To compare the overall response rate (ORR) of patients receiving carboplatin and nivolumab versus those receiving carboplatin, nivolumab, and pembrolizumab.
II. To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) in both treatment arms.

SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:
I. To assess the safety profile and adverse events associated with each treatment regimen. 
II. To determine the overall survival (OS) rates in both cohorts at 12 and 24 months.
III. To characterize the immunological response of treatment by assessing tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) populations pre- and post-treatment.

OUTLINE: 
This is a multicentric, randomized study where eligible patients are assigned in a 1:1 ratio via a computer-generated randomization process. 

Arm I: Patients receive carboplatin intravenously over 30 minutes on day 1 and nivolumab IV over 1 hour on day 1. 
Arm II: Patients receive carboplatin as in Arm I along with nivolumab and pembrolizumab concurrently. 

Treatment cycles repeat every 21 days for up to 6 cycles, barring disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. After completion, patients are monitored every 3 months for 2 years and every 6 months for an additional 3 years.

INCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of stage IV SCCL per the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) criteria.
- No prior systemic chemotherapy for SCCL.
- Measurable disease as per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.

EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment (i.e., corticosteroids).
- History of interstitial lung disease or other pulmonary dysfunction.
- Prior malignancy other than basal cell or squamous cell carcinoma of the skin, unless in complete remission for >5 years.
- Concurrent enrollment in another investigational drug trial.

FOLLOW-UP: The overall response rate is defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete or partial responses evaluated by independent radiological review. The study utilizes intention-to-treat principles for survival analysis.

Despite the proposed theoretical benefits of combining carboplatin with immune checkpoint inhibitors such as nivolumab and pembrolizumab, this clinical trial ultimately failed to achieve its primary endpoint of improved ORR, demonstrating inconsistent efficacy across both treatment arms and a concerning safety profile characterized by elevated immune-related adverse events. This suggests that the hypothesized immunomodulatory synergy between chemotherapy and checkpoint inhibition in this patient population may not translate effectively into clinical practice.
