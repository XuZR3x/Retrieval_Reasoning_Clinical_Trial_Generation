**Successful Clinical Trial of Candesartan Cilexetil: Efficacy of Candesartan Cilexetil in Combined Hypertension Regimens**

**A Phase IV, Prospective, Randomized, 3-Arm, Multicenter, 12-Week Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Candesartan Cilexetil (CC) Administered Alone Versus in Combination with Amlodipine and Hydrochlorothiazide in Patients with Stage II Hypertension**

**Objective:**  
To assess and compare the antihypertensive efficacy and safety of Candesartan Cilexetil monotherapy (16 mg) with Candesartan Cilexetil (16 mg) in combination with Amlodipine (5 mg) and Hydrochlorothiazide (12.5 mg) in patients diagnosed with Stage II essential hypertension.

**Methods:**  
This study utilized a double-blind design with three parallel treatment arms over a duration of 12 weeks. Patients were randomly assigned to receive either Candesartan Cilexetil monotherapy, the combination therapy of Candesartan Cilexetil and Amlodipine, or the combination of Candesartan Cilexetil, Amlodipine, and Hydrochlorothiazide. Blood pressure was measured at baseline and at 4-week intervals throughout the study period. The primary endpoint was the change in mean sitting diastolic blood pressure (DBP) from baseline at week 12.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adults aged 40 to 75 years with Stage II essential hypertension (SBP ≥ 160 mmHg or DBP ≥ 100 mmHg).
- Untreated or previously treated with no more than 2 classes of antihypertensive medications.
- Signed informed consent provided.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Serum creatinine > 1.5 mg/dL, serum potassium > 5.5 mmol/L.
- Pregnant or lactating women, or women of childbearing potential not practicing adequate contraception.
- History of secondary hypertension or any significant cardiovascular events within the last 6 months.
- Documented hypersensitivity to any components of investigational medications.

**Endpoints:**  
The primary efficacy measure was the reduction in mean sitting DBP from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints included changes in mean sitting systolic blood pressure (SBP) and safety assessments through laboratory tests and adverse event monitoring.

**Results:**  
A total of 450 participants were enrolled across 20 centers. At the end of the study period, participants receiving Candesartan Cilexetil monotherapy demonstrated a mean reduction in DBP of 12.5 mmHg compared to a reduction of 21 mmHg in the group receiving combination therapy with Amlodipine and Hydrochlorothiazide. The combination therapy was statistically superior in lowering both DBP and SBP (p < 0.01). Safety profiles were consistent across treatment arms, with no significant differences in adverse events between monotherapy and combination groups. The most common adverse events included dizziness and mild hyperkalemia, which were transient and managed effectively.

**Conclusion:**  
Candesartan Cilexetil in combination with Amlodipine and Hydrochlorothiazide significantly outperformed Candesartan Cilexetil monotherapy in reducing blood pressure in patients with Stage II hypertension, with a favorable safety profile. These results affirm the therapeutic benefit of combination therapy in this patient population, underlining the importance of individualized treatment regimens for optimal blood pressure management.
