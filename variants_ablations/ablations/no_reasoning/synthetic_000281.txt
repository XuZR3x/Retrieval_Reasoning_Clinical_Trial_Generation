Failed clinical trial of tranexamic acid: Randomized Control Trial of Tranexamic Acid in Patients Undergoing Total Knee Arthroplasty. 

This study aimed to assess the efficacy of tranexamic acid (TXA) in reducing perioperative blood loss and transfusion requirements in patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty (TKA). Recent literature has indicated that TKA is associated with substantial blood loss, which can necessitate blood transfusions, thereby increasing the risk of complications and healthcare costs. Tranexamic acid, an antifibrinolytic agent, has been shown to decrease bleeding in various surgical contexts. However, the utility of TXA in the domain of primary TKA remains contentious, necessitating rigorous investigation.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Patients aged 50 years and older.
- Elective primary total knee arthroplasty procedures indicated for osteoarthritis or rheumatoid arthritis.
- Preoperative hemoglobin levels greater than 10 g/dL.
- Patient’s consent to participate in the study.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Allergy or hypersensitivity to tranexamic acid.
- Current treatment with anticoagulants or antiplatelet therapy such as warfarin or clopidogrel.
- Known history of thromboembolic events including stroke or myocardial infarction.
- Renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min).
- Active bleeding disorders or coagulopathies.
- Participation in another investigational trial during the study period.

The primary objective of the trial was to determine the total blood loss (TBL) in the perioperative setting, with secondary outcomes investigating the need for blood transfusions, postoperative complications including deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, as well as 30-day re-hospitalization or mortality rates. A power calculation suggested a sample size of 200 patients, randomized in a 1:1 fashion to receive intravenous tranexamic acid or placebo prior to surgery and during the surgical procedure.

Despite a structured protocol and rigorous screening, the trial was halted after reaching 75 enrollments due to an interim analysis that revealed no statistically significant reduction in TBL when comparing the TXA group to the placebo group. The predefined stopping criterion of a difference in TBL of greater than 400 mL between groups was not met. Additionally, the analysis indicated no significant differences in transfusion rates or adverse events related to the administration of tranexamic acid.

In conclusion, the study outcomes suggested that, contrary to expectations, tranexamic acid did not achieve its intended effect of reducing perioperative blood loss in patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty. The findings contributed valuable data to the existing scientific literature, indicating that further research is required to delineate the role of TXA in orthopedic surgery, particularly in joint arthroplasties. The study reinforces the complexities associated with multifactorial causes of bleeding during TKAs and the necessity for alternative strategies to mitigate blood loss.
