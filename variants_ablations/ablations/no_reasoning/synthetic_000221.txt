**Failed Clinical Trial of Tamoxifen Citrate: Combination Therapy with Tamoxifen and Everolimus for Advanced Hormone Receptor Positive Breast Cancer**

**Phase II Study of Tamoxifen and Everolimus in Patients with Advanced Hormone Receptor Positive Breast Cancer**

RATIONALE: Tamoxifen citrate, a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), is widely used in treating estrogen receptor (ER) positive breast cancer. Everolimus, an mTOR inhibitor, has demonstrated potential efficacy by disrupting various cellular pathways that drive cancer proliferation. Preclinical studies have suggested that the combination of these agents may result in enhanced anti-tumor effects through simultaneous blockade of ER signaling and mTOR pathways. However, clinical validation of such combinations remains ambiguous and requires thorough investigation. 

PURPOSE: This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of the combination of tamoxifen citrate and everolimus in delaying disease progression among patients with advanced hormone receptor-positive breast cancer that has progressed on prior therapy.

OBJECTIVES: 
- The primary endpoint of this trial is progression-free survival (PFS) assessed from the start of treatment until documented disease progression or death from any cause. 
- Secondary endpoints include overall response rate (ORR), overall survival (OS), and safety profile of the combination treatment.

DESIGN: This is a multicenter, open-label, phase II trial designed to assess the efficacy and safety of tamoxifen combined with everolimus. Patients will be stratified based on prior therapies (one prior hormonal therapy vs. more than one hormonal therapy) and disease stage (local advanced vs. metastatic).

INCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Histologically confirmed metastatic or locally advanced hormone receptor-positive breast cancer.
- Disease progression during or after treatment with at least one hormonal agent.
- Patients must be 18 years or older with a performance status score of 0-2 per Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) criteria.
- Adequate organ function, defined as:
  - Hematopoietic: Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³, and hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL.
  - Liver: Serum AST and ALT < 2.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).
  - Renal: Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL or creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.
- Ability to provide informed consent.

EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Patients with prior treatment involving everolimus or any mTOR inhibitors.
- Concurrent use of other anti-neoplastic agents or investigational agents.
- Significant uncontrolled medical conditions or psychological disorders.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
- Allergies to study components.

TREATMENT PROTOCOL: Patients will receive tamoxifen citrate 20 mg orally once daily and everolimus 10 mg orally once daily. The treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent. 

FOLLOW-UP: Patients will be followed up every month for the first 6 months and every 3 months thereafter. Evaluations will include physical examinations, vital signs, laboratory tests, and imaging studies (CT or MRI) to assess tumor response.

PROJECTED ACCRUAL: A total of 150 patients will be enrolled over 18 months.

RESULTS: Unfortunately, the study concluded that the combination of tamoxifen citrate and everolimus failed to demonstrate a significant improvement in progression-free survival compared to historical controls. Moreover, adverse events were more frequent than anticipated, leading to treatment discontinuation in a substantial subset of patients. Consequently, the trial was terminated due to lack of benefit and due to the high incidence of adverse effects, including severe stomatitis and metabolic disturbances.

CONCLUSION: The combination of tamoxifen and everolimus did not provide additive clinical benefit in patients with advanced hormone receptor-positive breast cancer, marking this trial as a failed intervention in the current oncology landscape. Further research is warranted to explore other potential combinations leveraging distinct molecular targets for optimally managing resistant ER-positive breast cancers.
