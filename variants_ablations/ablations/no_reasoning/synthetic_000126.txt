**Failed clinical trial of fulvestrant: A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Fulvestrant in Combination With Chemotherapy in Patients With Advanced Pancreatic Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** Pancreatic cancer has been associated with resistance to conventional therapy, with recent studies suggesting that estrogen may play a role in tumor progression. This trial aims to evaluate whether the addition of fulvestrant to standard chemotherapy regimens can improve therapeutic efficacy in patients with advanced pancreatic cancer.

**PURPOSE:** This Phase II trial investigates the effectiveness, tolerability, and safety of fulvestrant when administered in combination with a standard chemotherapeutic regimen in patients diagnosed with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objective:** To assess the overall response rate (complete and partial response) in patients treated with fulvestrant combined with standard chemotherapy compared to historical controls.
- **Secondary Objectives:** To evaluate the progression-free survival and overall survival rates, as well as the safety profile of the combination therapy.

**OUTLINE:** This study is an open-label, multicenter, Phase II trial. Patients will receive fulvestrant via intramuscular injection at a dose of 500 mg on days 1 and 15, in combination with a standard chemotherapy regimen consisting of gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel administered intravenously on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle. Treatment will continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity is observed.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 60 patients will be enrolled across multiple sites to meet the study objectives effectively.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed pancreatic adenocarcinoma
- Unresectable locally advanced or metastatic disease
- Measurable disease by RECIST v1.1 criteria
- No prior systemic treatment for metastatic disease

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Performance status: ECOG 0-2
- Life expectancy of ≥ 4 months
- Age ≥ 18 years
- Adequate hematologic and organ function, with parameters including:
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL
  - Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
  - Bilirubin < 1.5 x upper limit of normal (ULN), and AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN
  - Creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL or estimated creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- History of prior treatment with fulvestrant
- Presence of brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease
- Active uncontrolled infections
- History of other malignancies within the past 5 years (with exceptions for non-melanoma skin cancer)
- Participation in another clinical trial within 30 days prior to enrollment
- Significant cardiovascular disease or acute coronary syndrome within the past 6 months
- Known hypersensitivity to fulvestrant or any of its excipients

**PRIMARY ENDPOINTS:**
- Overall response rate, defined as the percentage of patients achieving a complete or partial response per RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**SECONDARY ENDPOINTS:**
- Progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) measured from the first day of treatment until disease progression or death from any cause.
- Assessment of adverse events using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE v4.0).

**RECRUITMENT AND STUDY TIMELINE:** The estimated duration of recruitment is set to 18 months, with an expected follow-up period of 12 months for each enrolled patient, allowing for adequate assessment of outcomes.

**OUTCOMES:** Preliminary findings indicate an overall response rate of less than 5%, significantly below the predefined threshold for clinical significance. The combination therapy resulted in a notable incidence of gastrointestinal toxicities and treatment-related complications, including febrile neutropenia and liver function abnormalities, leading to termination of the trial due to lack of therapeutic benefit and safety concerns.

This trial underscores the need for careful patient selection and reconsideration of the role of hormonal therapy in the context of aggressive malignancies such as pancreatic cancer. Further investigation is warranted to explore alternative combinations or dosing regimens that may enhance therapeutic outcomes.
