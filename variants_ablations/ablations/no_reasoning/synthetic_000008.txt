**Failed Clinical Trial of Fingolimod: Efficacy and Safety of Fingolimod 0.5 mg in Patients With Active Autoimmune Encephalitis**

**Study Title**: A Double-blind, Randomized, Multicenter, Placebo-controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Fingolimod 0.5 mg Administered Orally Once Daily Versus Placebo in Patients With Active Autoimmune Encephalitis 

**Study Design**: This study is a double-blind, randomized, multicenter, placebo-controlled trial aimed at assessing the efficacy and safety of fingolimod in individuals diagnosed with autoimmune encephalitis. Participants will be randomized in a ratio of 1:1 to receive either oral fingolimod (0.5 mg/day) or matching placebo, with a treatment duration of 24 weeks.

**Background and Rationale**: Autoimmune encephalitis is characterized by neuroinflammation and can lead to significant cognitive dysfunction and psychiatric symptoms. Advances in our understanding of the immunological basis of the condition have sparked interest in immunomodulatory therapies as potential treatments. Fingolimod, originally developed for multiple sclerosis, acts as a sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor modulator, promoting lymphocyte sequestration in lymphoid organs, thereby reducing systemic inflammation. However, prior studies have indicated variable responses to fingolimod in other autoimmune contexts. 

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Written informed consent must be obtained from participants capable of providing consent
2. Adults aged 18-65 years with a confirmed diagnosis of autoimmune encephalitis as per established diagnostic criteria 
3. Active disease, defined as ongoing neurological symptoms for less than 6 months and evidence of significant neuroinflammation on MRI or CSF analysis (presence of anti-neuronal antibodies)
4. Prior treatment with corticosteroids or intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) is permitted, though must have been stable for at least 2 weeks prior to randomization
5. Patients must exhibit a clinical decline measured by relevant cognitive and functional scales (e.g., Clinical Global Impression - Severity [CGI-S] ≥ 4)

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Presence of primary neurological disorders (e.g., multiple sclerosis, epilepsy) distinct from autoimmune encephalitis 
2. Known history of hypersensitivity to fingolimod or its constituents 
3. Patients requiring chronic immunosuppressive therapies, except for stable low-dose corticosteroids
4. Any severe uncontrolled medical condition, including significant cardiovascular disease, active malignancy, or severe infections 
5. Pregnant or breastfeeding women, or those planning to conceive within the study period
6. History of suicide ideation or attempts within the 12 months prior to screening 

**Primary Endpoint**: The primary endpoint is the change in cognitive function as assessed by the Repeatable Battery for the Assessment of Neuropsychological Status (RBANS) from baseline to week 24. 

**Secondary Endpoints**:
1. Changes in quality of life measured by the EuroQol-5 Dimension (EQ-5D) scale
2. Incidence of serious adverse events (SAEs) during the double-blind treatment period
3. Neuroimaging outcomes including changes in MRI-derived volumetric measurements of brain atrophy 

**Statistical Analysis Plan**: All statistical analyses will be performed according to an intention-to-treat principle. Continuous variables will be analyzed using the appropriate parametric or non-parametric tests, and categorical data will be assessed using chi-square tests. A significance threshold of p < 0.05 will be established for all analyses.

**Monitoring and Safety**: The study will have an independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) that will review all adverse events and clinical outcomes throughout the trial. Participants will be contacted regularly for safety assessments during the treatment period.

The study seeks to elucidate the potential efficacy of fingolimod in modulating neuroinflammation and improving neurological function in patients suffering from autoimmune encephalitis. However, the study ultimately concluded with disappointing results, revealing no significant differences in cognitive function or quality of life between the fingolimod and placebo groups at the conclusion of the trial.
