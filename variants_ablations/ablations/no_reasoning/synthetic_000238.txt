Failed clinical trial of rituximab: Rituximab in Combination with Methotrexate for Early Systemic Sclerosis (SSc) A Phase II Study of Rituximab in Combination with Methotrexate for the Treatment of Early Systemic Sclerosis Systemic sclerosis (SSc), also known as scleroderma, is a multisystem connective tissue disorder characterized by fibrosis, vascular alterations, and autoimmunity, resulting in significant morbidity and mortality. It affects approximately 0.2-0.5% of the population, with varying clinical presentations. Current management strategies involve symptomatic treatment and, in some cases, disease-modifying agents; however, there remains an unmet need for effective therapies in the early disease phase to arrest disease progression and prevent irreversible damage. Rituximab is a humanized monoclonal antibody that targets CD20 on B cells, leading to their depletion, and has previously demonstrated efficacy in autoimmune disorders characterized by B cell dysregulation. This multi-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of rituximab in combination with methotrexate (MTX) compared to MTX alone in patients with early diffuse SSc. The primary endpoint will be the change in modified Rodnan skin score (mRSS) from baseline to 24 weeks of treatment, with secondary endpoints including the assessment of lung function, quality of life metrics, and biomarkers of autoimmune activity. Preliminary evidence suggests that B cell activity, as mediated by autoantibody production, plays a pivotal role in the immunopathogenesis of SSc, and rituximab may ameliorate skin and pulmonary manifestations by depleting autoreactive B cells. Inclusion Criteria: - Diagnosis of early diffuse SSc as defined by the American College of Rheumatology criteria - Symptom duration of less than 18 months - mRSS of at least 10 - Active disease, evidenced by the presence of organ involvement (e.g., interstitial lung disease, renal crisis) - Adequate organ function (lung, renal, cardiac) to tolerate treatment - No prior treatment with rituximab or other B cell-depleting therapies - Written informed consent Exclusion Criteria: - Active uncontrolled infections - History of malignancy within the last five years - Ongoing immunosuppressive therapy, including systemic glucocorticoids exceeding 10 mg/day - Pregnant or breastfeeding women - Hepatitis B or C positivity - Allergy to MTX or rituximab - Other significant comorbidities that may preclude participation or confound study results The experimental arm will receive intravenous rituximab on days 1 and 15, combined with weekly MTX, while the control arm will receive weekly MTX and placebo infusions. Participants will undergo assessments at baseline, week 12, and week 24, with particular focus on skin involvement, pulmonary function tests (PFTs), and adverse events. A total of 100 participants are planned to enroll. Despite the theoretical rationale and previous reports indicating potential benefit, the trial failed to meet the primary endpoint of a statistically significant reduction in mRSS at 24 weeks when comparing the rituximab plus MTX group to the MTX alone group. Additionally, secondary endpoints revealed no meaningful differences in functional outcomes or quality of life metrics between groups. The investigation highlighted the complex nature of SSc, where B cell targeting alone may not address its multifactorial pathogenesis. The trial was terminated early due to futility, underscoring the challenges of developing effective interventions in early systemic sclerosis. Adverse events were consistent with existing literature on rituximab, including infusion reactions, but were not significantly different from placebo. Future research may aim to explore combination therapies targeting divergent pathways in SSc pathophysiology.
