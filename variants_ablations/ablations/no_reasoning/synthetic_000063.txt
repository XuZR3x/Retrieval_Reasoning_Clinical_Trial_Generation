<ClinicalTrial>
  <Title>Evaluation of Chlorambucil in Combination With Immunotherapy in Previously Untreated Patients With Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia: A Phase III Randomized Study</Title>
  
  <Background>
    This multicenter, randomized, open-label Phase III clinical trial evaluates the efficacy and safety of chlorambucil in combination with an immunotherapeutic agent versus chlorambucil alone in previously untreated patients diagnosed with chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL). Prior studies have indicated that chlorambucil has a well-established role in the management of CLL, particularly for patients with comorbid conditions. Immune modulation has been shown to synergize with conventional therapies, positing potential improvements in treatment outcomes.</Background>
  
  <Objective>
    To compare the overall response rate, progression-free survival (PFS), and safety profile of chlorambucil combined with an immunotherapy agent to chlorambucil monotherapy in a cohort of previously untreated CLL patients.</Objective>
  
  <Methodology>
    This trial enrolled a total of 300 participants across several centers. Eligible participants include adult patients diagnosed with previously untreated CLL, with a CIRS score greater than 6 and adequate organ function. Participants are randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either chlorambucil (10 mg/m² orally on Days 1-7 of a 28-day cycle for 6 cycles) combined with an approved immunotherapy agent or chlorambucil alone. 

    Primary endpoints include overall response rate associated with both treatment regimens assessed at the end of six cycles, defined according to IWCLL 2018 criteria. Secondary endpoints will include PFS, overall survival (OS), and quality of life (QoL) measures established via validated questionnaires. Follow-up assessments will occur every three months for up to five years post-treatment completion.</Methodology>
  
  <InclusionCriteria>
    - Adult individuals aged 18 years and above
    - Documented diagnosis of CLL requiring treatment, according to IWCLL criteria
    - Cumulative Illness Rating Scale (CIRS) score > 6
    - Adequate renal and hepatic function
    - Life expectancy greater than 6 months
    - Signed informed consent obtained from participant</InclusionCriteria>
  
  <ExclusionCriteria>
    - Previous treatment for CLL
    - Active autoimmune disorders or history of significant autoimmune hemolytic anemia
    - Known central nervous system involvement by CLL
    - Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) history
    - Pregnant or breastfeeding individuals
    - Concurrent malignancies (except for basal cell carcinoma) or significant intercurrent infections
    - Known hypersensitivity to chlorambucil or the immunotherapy agents used in the trial</ExclusionCriteria>

  <OutcomeMeasures>
    Primary endpoint: Overall response rate assessed by investigator review at the end of 6 cycles.
    Secondary endpoints: 
      1. Progression-free survival (PFS) at 12 months, determined by time from randomization to disease progression or death.
      2. Overall survival (OS) at 24 months, defined as time from randomization to death from any cause.
      3. Quality of life changes, assessed using appropriate questionnaires at baseline, mid-treatment, and at study conclusion.
  </OutcomeMeasures>
  
  <StatisticalConsiderations>
    A sample size of 300 participants provides 80% power to detect a statistically significant improvement in overall response rate between treatment arms, using a two-sided significance level of 0.05. Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline characteristics, while comparisons between groups will be conducted using the Chi-square test for categorical variables and the Kaplan-Meier method for survival analysis.</StatisticalConsiderations>
  
  <ExpectedResults>
    This study anticipates a favorable outcome, demonstrating a statistically significant improvement in overall response rates and PFS in the chlorambucil plus immunotherapy cohort relative to the chlorambucil monotherapy group. Additionally, it is expected to provide valuable safety and tolerability data concerning the combination therapy approach in this specific patient population.</ExpectedResults>
</ClinicalTrial>
