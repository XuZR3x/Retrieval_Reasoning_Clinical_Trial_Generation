**Title:** Cabozantinib-S-Malate in Treating Patients With Advanced Urothelial Carcinoma: A Phase II Randomized Clinical Trial Comparing Cabozantinib-S-Malate to Gemcitabine and Cisplatin

**Study Overview:**  
This randomized Phase II trial investigates the efficacy and safety of cabozantinib-s-malate versus a standard combination chemotherapy regimen of gemcitabine and cisplatin in patients diagnosed with advanced urothelial carcinoma, either metastatic or unresectable. Cabozantinib-s-malate is hypothesized to impede tumor growth by inhibiting receptor tyrosine kinases associated with cell proliferation, while gemcitabine and cisplatin operate through different cellular mechanisms to exert cytotoxic effects.

**Primary Objective:**  
To compare progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months in patients receiving cabozantinib-s-malate versus those receiving gemcitabine and cisplatin.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
I. Evaluate overall survival (OS) rates.  
II. Assess the objective response rate (ORR) using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST).  
III. Characterize the safety profile by recording and grading adverse events per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

**Tertiary Objectives:**  
I. Explore potential correlations between MET expression levels and clinical outcomes.  
II. Evaluate the pharmacokinetic profile of cabozantinib-s-malate in relation to treatment-related toxicities.

**Trial Design:**  
Patients will be randomized into two treatment arms:  

- **Arm I: Cabozantinib-S-Malate** (PO) at a dose of 60 mg once daily (QD) for 28-day cycles.  
- **Arm II: Gemcitabine plus Cisplatin** administered intravenously (IV); gemcitabine on days 1, 8, and 15 and cisplatin on day 1, every 28 days.  

Post-treatment follow-up will occur every 3 months up to a total duration of 5 years.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically confirmed advanced urothelial carcinoma (bladder or upper tract).  
- Measurable disease as per RECIST criteria.  
- Patients must have received one prior line of therapy for advanced disease.  
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.  
- Adequate hematologic and organ function parameters.  
- Age ≥ 18 years and provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Prior therapy with cabozantinib or exclusion from gemcitabine/cisplatin regimens.  
- Presence of CNS metastases.  
- Significant cardiovascular conditions (e.g., myocardial infarction within 6 months, uncontrolled hypertension).  
- Major surgical procedures within 28 days preceding participation.  
- Active infections or concurrent treatment with drugs that may exacerbate QTc prolongation.

**Outcome Measures:**  
- Percentage of participants without progression at 6 months (PFS6) assessed via imaging.  
- Analysis of adverse events including hematologic, gastrointestinal, and neurologic toxicities.  
- Determination of overall survival rates from study initiation to death or last follow-up.

**Reporting Plan:**  
Patients will be monitored closely for progression-free status and adverse events, with safety data reviewed after the initial treatment cycles. Under the protocol, patients who exhibit intolerable side effects will discontinue study treatment.

**Results and Conclusion:**  
Unfortunately, the trial was terminated early due to insufficient evidence of efficacy based on predetermined interim analysis metrics. Progression-free survival rates observed did not meet the clinically significant threshold in analyzed cohorts, leading to the conclusion that cabozantinib-s-malate does not confer any substantial improvement over the established gemcitabine-cisplatin combination therapy. Furthermore, adverse events were reported at higher frequencies in the cabozantinib arm, which raised concerns regarding its therapeutic window.

In summary, the clinical evaluation of cabozantinib-s-malate in advanced urothelial carcinoma resulted in failure, reflecting the intricate heterogeneity of responses among therapeutic interventions in this oncological setting. Further examination of alternative pathways and treatment modalities remains critical in pursuit of improving patient outcomes.
