**Failed clinical trial of galantamine: Evaluation of Galantamine for Anxiety Disorders in Adult Populations**

**Title:** A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Galantamine in Patients with Generalized Anxiety Disorder

**Objective:** The purpose of this clinical trial was to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of galantamine in adults diagnosed with Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD), as defined by DSM-5 criteria. Secondary objectives included assessing the impact of galantamine on associated cognitive impairments and overall quality of life in this patient population.

**Background:** Anxiety disorders, particularly GAD, are prevalent psychiatric conditions characterized by excessive and persistent worry significantly impacting daily functioning. Current pharmacological treatments often yield suboptimal outcomes, with numerous patients seeking alternatives. Galantamine, an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, has previously shown limited success in modulating neurotransmitter activity, suggesting potential anxiolytic properties through the enhancement of cholinergic function, which could alleviate anxiety symptoms while also addressing cognitive deficits associated with the disorder. Despite this theoretical background, prior investigations have not firmly established galantamine's role or efficacy in treating anxiety disorders.

**Methodology:** This double-blind, placebo-controlled study was conducted over a 24-week period, including a 4-week baseline assessment followed by a treatment phase categorized into three distinct groups: an experimental group receiving 16 mg of galantamine twice daily, a placebo group, and an active comparator group receiving standard SSRI treatment. A total of 150 participants, aged 18-65 years, were enrolled. Inclusion criteria required a diagnosis of GAD, as confirmed by the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HARS) with a minimum score of 18. Participants underwent a titration schedule over the first month to assess tolerability and determine the maintenance dose, whereas those displaying intolerable side effects were allowed to withdraw early. 

**Endpoints:** The primary outcome measure was the change from baseline on the HARS at the conclusion of the 24-week treatment period. Secondary endpoints included changes in Cognitive Performance Scale (CPS) scores, and assessments of quality of life as measured by the Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item (GAD-7) scale and the World Health Organization Quality of Life Assessment (WHOQOL-BREF).

**Results:** Out of 150 enrolled participants, only 56 completed the full study protocol. Preliminary analysis revealed no statistically significant difference in HARS scores between the galantamine and placebo groups at 24 weeks. Additionally, secondary outcomes demonstrated no meaningful changes in cognitive performance or quality of life indices. Adverse events associated with galantamine led to discontinuations in nearly 25% of the participants, with reported side effects including nausea, dizziness, fatigue, and gastrointestinal disturbances.

**Conclusion:** The findings from this clinical investigation do not support the hypothesis that galantamine is an effective treatment option for Generalized Anxiety Disorder in adult populations. Notably, there were no observed improvements in the primary symptomatology associated with anxiety disorders or the cognitive deficits commonly seen in this patient group. Further research is warranted to iterate on findings and explore alternative pharmacological strategies in managing GAD.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18-65 years
- Diagnosis of Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) per DSM-5 criteria
- HARS score ≥ 18 at screening
- Ability to give informed consent

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of substance use disorder (excluding nicotine)
- Significant comorbid psychiatric illnesses (e.g., bipolar disorder, schizophrenia)
- Concurrent treatment with other psychoactive medications
- Uncontrolled medical comorbidities (e.g., cardiovascular, endocrine disorders)
- Pregnancy or lactation
- Known hypersensitivity to cholinesterase inhibitors

**Study Status:** This study was terminated prior to completion after failing to meet predefined efficacy benchmarks as stated in the protocol. Further analysis indicated that the role of cholinergic enhancement via galantamine does not confer the anticipated therapeutic benefits in the management of generalized anxiety disorders.
