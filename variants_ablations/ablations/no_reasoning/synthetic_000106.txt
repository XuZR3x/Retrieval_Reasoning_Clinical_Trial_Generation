Failed clinical trial of montelukast sodium: Montelukast in Adult Asthmatic Patients with Chronic Rhinosinusitis (0476-400)(COMPLETED)  
A Multicenter, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Randomized, Parallel-Group Study to Assess the Efficacy of Oral Montelukast Versus Placebo in Adult Patients with Asthma and Coexisting Chronic Rhinosinusitis  
This study aims to evaluate the potential benefit of montelukast sodium in improving asthma control in adults concurrently suffering from chronic rhinosinusitis. This is a 12-week trial assessing the change in asthma control through quality of life measures alongside respiratory function metrics.  

Inclusion Criteria:  
- Males and females aged 18 to 65 years with established diagnosis of asthma  
- Patients must have concomitant persistent chronic rhinosinusitis as confirmed by endoscopic findings  
- Participants should demonstrate inadequate asthma control despite standard inhaled corticosteroid therapy  

Exclusion Criteria:  
- Patients with history of any other significant respiratory diseases, including but not limited to chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)  
- Presence of evident acute infections or any other acute debilitating conditions during the screening phase  

Primary Efficacy Endpoints:  
- Change in Asthma Control Test (ACT) score from baseline to Week 12 as a measure of asthma control quality  
- Percentage change in Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 Second (FEV1) from baseline to Week 12  

Secondary Efficacy Endpoints:  
- Frequency of asthma exacerbations leading to healthcare utilization (hospitalization, emergency department visits) during the treatment period  
- Change in daily use of rescue β-agonist medication from baseline to Week 12  

In this study, it is hypothesized that the addition of montelukast sodium will not yield a statistically significant improvement in asthma control or respiratory function when compared to placebo, similar to previous trials that indicate a lack of efficacy in specific patient populations with persistent asthma.
