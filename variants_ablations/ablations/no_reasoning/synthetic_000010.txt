**Failed Clinical Trial of LDK378: Phase II Trial of LDK378 in Patients With ALK-Rearranged Advanced Small Cell Lung Cancer**

A Phase II Trial of LDK378 as a Targeted Therapy in Patients With Anaplastic Lymphoma Kinase (ALK) Rearrangements in Advanced Small Cell Lung Cancer

**Primary Objective:** To evaluate the efficacy of LDK378 in achieving an objective response rate in patients with ALK-rearranged advanced small cell lung cancer (SCLC) who have previously received at least one line of systemic therapy.

**Secondary Objectives:** 
1. To assess progression-free survival (PFS).
2. To determine the overall survival (OS) rates at defined intervals.
3. To evaluate the disease control rate (DCR).
4. To document the toxicity profile of LDK378.
5. To identify potential predictive biomarkers correlating with clinical outcomes.

**Sample Size:** A total of 40 patients with recurrent ALK-positive advanced SCLC will be enrolled, accounting for a 15% dropout rate.

**Study Design:** 
- **Step 1:** Eligibility assessment and confirmation of ALK-rearrangement status through next-generation sequencing (NGS). 
- **Step 2:** Administer LDK378 at a dose of 750 mg orally, once daily, in 28-day cycles until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.
- **Step 3:** Tumor response evaluation every 6 weeks utilizing RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of small cell lung cancer with confirmed ALK rearrangement (≥15% tumor cells positive via NGS).
2. Patients must have locally advanced or metastatic disease unsuitable for curative treatment.
3. ECOG performance status of 0-2.
4. Aged 18 years or older and no more than 75 years at the time of consent.
5. Adequate organ function as defined by specified laboratory values.
6. At least one measurable lesion according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.
7. Patients must give informed consent and be willing to comply with all study procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients with symptomatic central nervous system (CNS) metastases requiring ongoing therapy or increasing steroid doses.
2. Known hypersensitivity to any components of LDK378.
3. Prior ALK inhibitor therapy including but not limited to crizotinib and alectinib.
4. Other concomitant malignancies, except for adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancer or localized carcinoma in situ.
5. Clinically significant cardiac disease or uncontrolled hypertension.
6. Co-administered medications noted for strong inhibition or induction of CYP3A4/5 that cannot be ceased at least one week prior to treatment initiation.
7. Presence of interstitial lung disease or significant prior pulmonary radiotherapy.

**Study Endpoints:**
- **Primary endpoint:** Objective response rate defined by the proportion of patients achieving a complete or partial response.
- **Secondary endpoints:** Progression-free survival, overall survival, disease control rate, safety and tolerability based on CTCAE v5.0 criteria, and analysis of biomarker correlations.

**Outcome Measures:**
- Response evaluations will be conducted at baseline and participants will be followed every 6 weeks with imaging studies (CT or MRI); endpoints will include rates of complete response, partial response, stable disease, and progressive disease as per RECIST criteria.

**Failure Summary:**
Despite initial hope based on preclinical data suggesting the activity of LDK378 in the inhibition of ALK activity, this trial did not demonstrate a meaningful increase in objective response rates compared to historical controls. Adverse effects, primarily concerning gastrointestinal disturbances and pulmonary complications, reached levels leading to premature treatment discontinuation in a substantial cohort of participants. Furthermore, the anticipated therapeutic effect did not correlate with ALK-rearrangement status, which was presumed to confer sensitivity to ALK inhibitors. Consequently, the trial was prematurely terminated due to lack of clinical efficacy and overwhelming safety concerns, with an overall recommendation against further development in this patient population.

This report reflects the importance of robust patient selection criteria, monitoring for systemic effects, and potentially exploring alternative concurrent therapies to optimize outcome when utilizing ALK inhibitors in small cell lung cancer settings. Further studies may elucidate the complexities inherent in targeting ALK within the SCLC landscape.
