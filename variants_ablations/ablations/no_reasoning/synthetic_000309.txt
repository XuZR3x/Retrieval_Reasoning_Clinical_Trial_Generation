**Cabozantinib in Recurrent/Metastatic Cholangiocarcinoma: A Phase II Study**

**Background:**
Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) is a rare and aggressive malignancy arising from the biliary epithelium. It poses significant therapeutic challenges due to its late presentation and poor prognosis, with an estimated five-year survival rate of less than 10%. The development of targeted therapies is critical, as current treatment options are limited primarily to chemotherapy and surgical resection. Cabozantinib is a multi-tyrosine kinase inhibitor with potential activity against cholangiocarcinoma through its inhibition of MET and VEGFR pathways. Previous studies have suggested aberrant expression of these receptors in CCA. The aim of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of cabozantinib in patients with advanced, recurrent, or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma previously treated with chemotherapy.

**Objectives:**
- To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of cabozantinib in patients with recurrent/metastatic cholangiocarcinoma.
- To determine the progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in the study population.
- To evaluate the safety and tolerability profile of cabozantinib.

**Design:**
This is a multicenter, open-label, non-randomized Phase II trial that will enroll approximately 100 participants, aged 18 years or older, with histologically confirmed recurrent or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma. Participants will receive cabozantinib orally at a dose of 60 mg once daily in 28-day treatment cycles.

**Eligibility:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma that is recurrent or metastatic.
- Patients must have received at least one line of prior systemic therapy, including at least one chemotherapy regimen.
- Measurable disease, defined as at least one lesion measuring greater than or equal to 20 mm on imaging studies or greater than or equal to 10 mm for spiral CT.
- Adequate hematologic and organ function.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- ECOG performance status ≤ 2.
- Participants must have normal liver, renal, and bone marrow function (defined by specific laboratory thresholds).
- Women of childbearing potential must have negative pregnancy tests and agree to use effective contraceptive measures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Recent treatment with cytotoxic chemotherapy or radiotherapy within 4 weeks prior to enrollment.
- Patients with previous treatment with any MET or VEGFR inhibitors.
- Active contraindicated comorbidities including severe cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled hypertension.
- History of significant coagulopathy or bleeding disorders.
- Evidence of active brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease.
- Pregnancy or lactation.

**Outcome Measures:**
The primary endpoint will be the ORR, defined as the proportion of participants achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as per RECIST v1.1 criteria. Secondary endpoints will include:
- PFS, determined from the start of treatment to the date of documented disease progression or death.
- OS, measured from trial enrollment until patient death or last follow-up.

**Monitoring and Follow-up:**
Participants will undergo imaging studies every 8 weeks during the treatment phase to assess response. Safety evaluations will be conducted via regular clinical assessments and laboratory tests. Follow-up will persist for 30 days after discontinuation of therapy or until adverse events resolve.

**Analysis Plan:**
The data will be analyzed using descriptive statistics. A two-stage design will be employed to assess interim efficacy after 50 patients, with the possibility to halt the trial if the response rate falls below a predefined threshold.

**Expected Outcomes:**
Given previous trials of cabozantinib in other malignancies and the biological rationale for its use in CCA, it was hypothesized that a significant proportion of patients would experience disease control. However, it is anticipated that suboptimal response rates, as observed in previous studies involving similar patient cohorts, may contribute to trial failure.

**Conclusion:**
Cabozantinib in this cohort of cholangiocarcinoma patients represents a critical investigation into the viability of targeting angiogenic pathways. Despite the molecular basis for potential efficacy, the expectation based on historical data suggests a high likelihood of unfavorable outcomes in overall response rates leading to trial failure. Further research is warranted to explore alternative therapeutic avenues in this difficult-to-treat malignancy.
