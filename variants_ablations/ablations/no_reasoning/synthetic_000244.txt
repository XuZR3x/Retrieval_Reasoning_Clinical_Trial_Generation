**Successful Clinical Trial of Entinostat: Phase II Study of Entinostat and Pembrolizumab in Patients With Advanced Melanoma**

*A Randomized, Double-Blind, Multicenter Phase II Study Assessing the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of Entinostat in Combination With Pembrolizumab in Adults With PD-L1 Positive Advanced Melanoma Who Have Progressed Following Anti-PD-1 Therapy*

This phase II trial investigates the safety and efficacy of entinostat in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced melanoma expressing programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) who have exhibited disease progression after prior anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody therapy. This study hypothesizes that the combination of entinostat, a histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor, with pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 therapy, may enhance the immune response against melanoma cells and lead to improved clinical outcomes.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) to entinostat in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with PD-L1 positive advanced melanoma who have progressed following anti-PD-1 therapy.
II. To assess the safety and tolerability of the combination therapy through the incidence of treatment-related adverse events (AEs).

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To determine progression-free survival (PFS) in patients treated with the combination versus historical controls receiving pembrolizumab alone.
II. To evaluate changes in tumor and circulating immune cell profiles pre- and post-treatment to assess correlative biomarkers associated with treatment response.

**EXPLORATORY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess tumor microenvironment changes following treatment with entinostat and pembrolizumab through immune cell profiling within tumor tissues.

**OUTLINE:**
This is a randomized, double-blind study in which patients are assigned to receive either entinostat orally (PO) on days 1 and 15 of each 28-day cycle in conjunction with pembrolizumab intravenously (IV) on day 1 of each cycle. Randomization will allocate patients to receive either the active combination or a placebo-Controlled regimen with pembrolizumab. Patients will continue therapy until disease progression or intolerable toxicity is observed. Clinical assessments, CT imaging, and blood-based immune profiling will be performed at designated intervals to evaluate treatment efficacy.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced melanoma with documented PD-L1 positivity (≥ 1% expression as determined by IHC).
- Patients must have previously received anti-PD-1 monotherapy and experienced disease progression.
- Age ≥ 18 years with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Participants must provide informed consent and have a life expectancy of at least 6 months.
- Adequate laboratory values: Hemoglobin ≥ 9.0 g/dL; platelets ≥ 100 x 10^9/L; neutrophil count ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L; liver function tests (ALT, AST) must be ≤ 2.5 × ULN.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior treatment with any HDAC inhibitors.
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment or a history of pneumonitis requiring corticosteroids.
- Serious concomitant medical conditions that might compromise safety or compliance.
- Pregnancy, lactation, or failure to use adequate contraception during the study.
- Past or active malignancies other than melanoma within the last 5 years (with exceptions).

**RESULTS AND OUTCOMES:**
The primary outcome measure will evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) defined by RECIST criteria. Safety will be monitored continuously throughout the study, evaluating for any dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) or serious adverse events that would compromise patient safety. Correlative studies will assess any immune modulation resulting from entinostat administration.

This innovative combination therapy aims to address the unmet needs of patients with advanced melanoma, potentially improving the effectiveness of immunotherapy strategies through modulation of epigenetic mechanisms.

**Study Closure:**
After completion of treatment, patients will be followed for an additional 6 months to assess long-term outcomes and evaluate the sustainability of radiological and immunological responses. The proposed study offers a promising approach to enhancing the efficacy of existing therapies for advanced melanoma.
