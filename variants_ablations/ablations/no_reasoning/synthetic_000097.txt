**Failed clinical trial of botulinum toxin type A: Efficacy and Safety Study of Botulinum Toxin Type A for Chronic Migraine Prevention**

A Phase II, International, Multi-centre, Prospective, Randomised, Parallel-group, Double-blind, Dose-ranging, Placebo-controlled, 16-week, Princeps Study to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of Multiple Injection Cycles of Botulinum Toxin Type A (BOTOX® 100, 200, or 400 Units) Compared to Placebo for the Prophylactic Treatment of Chronic Migraine in Adult Patients. The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the optimal dosing of botulinum toxin type A to reduce the frequency and severity of migraines, while also assessing the impact on quality of life metrics and associated headache-related disability.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult subjects (aged 18 years and older) diagnosed with chronic migraine, defined as experiencing ≥15 headache days per month with ≥8 days of migraine per month.
- Subjects must have demonstrated inadequate response to at least two previous prophylactic migraine treatments over the last 12 months.
- Eligible subjects must agree to maintain a stable regimen of acute migraine medications throughout the study duration.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with botulinum toxin type A within the last 12 months for migraine prophylaxis.
- Presence of a history or current diagnosis of medication overuse headaches.
- Patients with a history of neuromuscular disorders (e.g., myasthenia gravis, Lambert-Eaton syndrome) known to affect muscle tone.
- Pregnancy or lactation, or subjects not complying with effective contraceptive measures.

The primary endpoint is the change in the average number of headache days per month from Baseline to the end of the 16-week treatment period. Secondary endpoints include the evaluation of migraine severity, use of acute medications, and quality of life as measured by validated questionnaires.

Despite the well-established mechanism of action of botulinum toxin type A, the intervention failed to demonstrate a statistically significant superiority over placebo in either the reduction of headache days or secondary efficacy measures. This necessitated the discontinuation of the trial due to insufficient efficacy and the need for a reconsideration of dosing protocols and patient selection criteria in future investigations of this intervention in chronic migraine.
