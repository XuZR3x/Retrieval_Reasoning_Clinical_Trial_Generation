<clinicalTrial>
  <title>Evaluating the Efficacy of Olaparib in Patients with Advanced Urothelial Carcinoma: A Phase II Study</title>
  <objective>
    To evaluate the efficacy of olaparib in patients with advanced urothelial carcinoma who have received prior chemotherapy and to determine progression-free survival (PFS) in this cohort.
  </objective>
  <design>
    A multicenter, randomized, double-blinded, placebo-controlled Phase II trial comparing olaparib to placebo in combination with best supportive care in patients with advanced urothelial carcinoma who have progressed after first-line chemotherapy.
  </design>
  <inclusionCriteria>
    <criteria>
      - Histologically confirmed advanced urothelial carcinoma.
      - Patients with measurable disease as defined by RECIST v1.1 criteria.
      - Documented progression following at least one line of platinum-containing chemotherapy.
      - Provision of archival tumor tissue for biomarker analysis.
      - Age ≥ 18 years.
    </criteria>
  </inclusionCriteria>
  <exclusionCriteria>
    <criteria>
      - More than one prior chemotherapy regimen for advanced urothelial carcinoma.
      - Previous treatment with any PARP inhibitor, including olaparib.
      - Known symptomatic brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease.
      - Serious uncontrolled medical conditions or infections.
      - Patients with significant comorbidity that may interfere with study drug administration or follow-up.
    </criteria>
  </exclusionCriteria>
  <primaryOutcome>
    Overall survival measured from the date of randomization until death from any cause.
  </primaryOutcome>
  <secondaryOutcomes>
    <outcome>
      - Progression-free survival as assessed by imaging every 6 weeks.
    </outcome>
    <outcome>
      - Objective response rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients with complete or partial response.
    </outcome>
    <outcome>
      - Safety profile assessed by incidence of adverse events and laboratory abnormalities.
    </outcome>
  </secondaryOutcomes>
  <hypothesis>
    Given the lack of robust biomarker-driven patient selection, the study anticipates significant challenges in demonstrating an efficacy signal for olaparib. The wide variance in prior treatment history may lead to confounding effects, further complicating the interpretation of clinical outcomes.
  </hypothesis>
  <expectedResults>
    The study expects that a lack of patient stratification based on actionable biomarkers such as DNA repair deficiencies, combined with the extensive prior treatment history and resultant acquired resistance to therapies, will impede olaparib's ability to demonstrate a significant therapeutic effect.
  </expectedResults>
  <conclusion>
    The anticipated heterogeneity among included patients based on tumoral characteristics and treatment backgrounds is likely to dilute the observed efficacy of olaparib, mirroring previous failures in studies of similarly targeted therapies in advanced urothelial carcinoma.
  </conclusion>
</clinicalTrial>
