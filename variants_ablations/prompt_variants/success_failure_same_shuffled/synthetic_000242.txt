**Failed Clinical Trial of Alisertib: Alisertib in Patients With Advanced Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma**

**Phase II Study of MLN8237 (Alisertib) in Patients With Advanced Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma** 

This phase II trial evaluates the efficacy and safety of alisertib in patients diagnosed with advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma. The study aims to assess whether alisertib can enhance clinical outcomes in a cohort experiencing the severe burden of disease progression, particularly focusing on those who have received prior chemotherapeutic regimens.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To determine the objective response rate (ORR) (comprising complete response [CR] + partial response [PR]) among patients treated with alisertib, as assessed by RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
II. To evaluate the disease control rate (DCR) defined as the proportion of patients exhibiting CR, PR, or stable disease for at least 12 weeks.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To assess overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in patients receiving alisertib.  
II. To monitor the frequency and severity of adverse events related to alisertib treatment.  

**TERTIARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To explore the potential correlation between biomarkers of aurora kinase activity and clinical response using archival tumor specimens.  
II. To examine the impact of alisertib on metabolic activity through changes in fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG)-positron emission tomography (PET) standard uptake values (SUVs) before and after the treatment.  

**OUTLINE:**  
Patients receive alisertib orally (PO) at a dose of 50 mg twice daily (BID) for the first 7 days of a 21-day cycle. Treatment will continue in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Post-treatment, patients will be followed for response assessment every 6 weeks for a duration of 18 months.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Patients must have histologically confirmed advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma and measurable disease per RECIST 1.1.  
- Patients must have received at least one prior systemic therapy.  
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2.  
- Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function as per established guidelines.  
- Aged ≥ 18 years and capable of providing informed consent.  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Patients with prior therapy targeting aurora kinases or those who have had concomitant use of investigational agents within 4 weeks prior to enrollment.  
- Known hypersensitivity reactions to benzodiazepines or similar compounds.  
- Active infection or coexisting malignancy.  
- History of severe cardiovascular conditions as defined in the protocol.  
- Inability to comply with oral administration or maintain fasting requirements.  

**EFFICACY ASSESSMENT:**  
The primary endpoint will be evaluated based on confirmed tumor responses assessed on two consecutive imaging evaluations at least 6 weeks apart. An ORR of less than 10% among patients will be considered non-promising for further clinical evaluation.

The study, designed under a two-stage Simon design, anticipates that an ineffective treatment response (defined as less than 10% ORR) would warrant discontinuation, owing to insufficient efficacy observed in prior studies with alisertib against the backdrop of pancreatic adenocarcinoma. Compounding factors such as tumor heterogeneity may contribute to the diminished therapeutic response, which is characterized by the diverse genetic landscape within pancreatic tumors.

**STATISTICAL ANALYSIS:**  
Statistical analysis will utilize Kaplan-Meier methodology for PFS and OS estimation, and the log-rank test will be applied for comparison between groups. The study anticipates a high dropout rate due to adverse effects, as reported in prior trials of alisertib, contributing to the challenges in maintaining treatment doses and patient persistence in this regimen.

This trial is expected to reinforce the limitations previously demonstrated regarding alisertib's efficacy in malignancies characterized by significant resistance mechanisms and pre-existing treatment history, potentially leading to underlying ineffective therapeutic interventions.
