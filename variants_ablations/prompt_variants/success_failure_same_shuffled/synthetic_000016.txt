**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy of Cilostazol in Reducing Cardiovascular Events in High-Risk Patients with End-Stage Renal Disease on Hemodialysis**

**Trial Name**: Cilostazol for Cardiovascular Event Reduction in Hemodialysis Patients (CCER-H)

**Background**: Patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) on hemodialysis face a significantly elevated risk of cardiovascular events, including myocardial infarction and cerebrovascular accidents. Cilostazol, a phosphodiesterase III inhibitor, has shown promise in enhancing vascular health and reducing platelet aggregation. The CCER-H trial aimed to investigate cilostazol's efficacy in decreasing the incidence of cardiovascular events in this high-risk population.

**Design**: This was a randomized, double-blind, multicenter clinical trial. A total of 350 patients aged 18 to 85 years with ESRD currently undergoing hemodialysis were recruited from 15 dialysis centers across the country. Participants were randomized to receive cilostazol (100 mg BID) or placebo for a duration of 12 months.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Adults aged 18-85 years diagnosed with end-stage renal disease requiring hemodialysis.
2. Patients with a history of at least one cardiovascular event (MI, stroke, or cardiovascular-related hospitalization) within the preceding 12 months.
3. Patients providing informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
1. Pregnant or nursing women.
2. Known hypersensitivity to cilostazol or any of its components.
3. History of bleeding disorders or active gastrointestinal bleeding.
4. Patients with malignancy or severe hepatic/renal impairment.
5. Concurrent use of anticoagulants or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.

**Endpoints**: The primary endpoint was the composite incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), including cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, and stroke, assessed at 12 months. Secondary endpoints included hospitalization rates for cardiovascular causes and quality of life measures using the EQ-5D scale.

**Results**: Out of 350 enrolled patients, 317 completed the trial, with a comparable dropout rate between both groups (9% in the cilostazol group vs. 10% in the placebo group). The trial revealed a 35% reduction in the composite rate of MACE in the cilostazol group compared to placebo (14% vs. 22%, p < 0.01). Additionally, there was a statistically significant improvement in quality of life metrics (mean EQ-5D score increase of 0.15 vs. 0.02 in placebo).

**Safety Profile**: Cilostazol was generally well-tolerated. Notably, the incidence of major bleeding episodes was comparable between the cilostazol and placebo groups (3% vs. 2%), supporting cilostazol's favorable long-term safety profile. 

**Discussion**: The CCER-H trial was underpinned by the mechanistic efficacy of cilostazol, targeting the prevention of vascular events through dual action as a vasodilator and an anti-platelet agent. The robust randomized controlled design minimized bias, enhancing the validity of the findings. Comprehensive endpoints evaluation allowed for an integrative assessment of cilostazol's impact on cardiovascular health in a uniquely vulnerable demographic.

**Conclusion**: This trial convincingly demonstrated that cilostazol significantly reduces cardiovascular events in patients with end-stage renal disease undergoing hemodialysis, establishing it as a valuable therapeutic option in a high-risk population. Further studies may explore long-term outcomes and optimal dosing strategies.

**Keywords**: Cilostazol, cardiovascular events, end-stage renal disease, hemodialysis, clinical trial.
