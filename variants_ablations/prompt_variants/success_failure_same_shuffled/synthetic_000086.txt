**Failed Clinical Trial of Carboplatin: A Randomized, Open-Label, Phase III Study of Carboplatin with Pembrolizumab versus Carboplatin Monotherapy in Patients with Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer and High PD-L1 Expression**

**RATIONALE:** The immune checkpoint inhibitor pembrolizumab may enhance the anti-tumor effects of cytotoxic chemotherapy. This study aimed to investigate whether the combination of pembrolizumab with carboplatin provides a significant improvement in efficacy compared to carboplatin alone in patients with metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) characterized by high PD-L1 expression, addressing the limitations associated with prior failures such as suboptimal patient selection criteria and mechanisms of resistance.

**OBJECTIVES:** 
- **Primary Objective:** To assess progression-free survival (PFS) in patients receiving carboplatin in combination with pembrolizumab compared to carboplatin monotherapy.
- **Secondary Objectives:** To evaluate overall survival (OS), overall response rate (ORR), and quality of life outcomes in each treatment group.

**OUTLINE:**
Patients will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive:
- **Arm A:** Carboplatin IV on day 1 of a 21-day cycle plus pembrolizumab IV on day 1 of each cycle for a maximum of 6 cycles, followed by maintenance therapy with pembrolizumab alone.
- **Arm B:** Carboplatin IV on day 1 of a 21-day cycle alone for a maximum of 6 cycles.

Patients will be followed for disease progression through imaging studies every 6 weeks during treatment and every 3 months for 2 years post-treatment.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Histologically or cytologically confirmed metastatic NSCLC with high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥ 50%).
2. ECOG performance status of 0-1.
3. At least one measurable lesion by RECIST 1.1.
4. Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal functions as defined by laboratory parameters.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Known hypersensitivity to platinum-based compounds or pembrolizumab.
2. Prior chemotherapy or immunotherapy for metastatic disease.
3. Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment.
4. Patients with symptomatic or untreated brain metastases.
5. Current use of strong CYP3A4 inducers or inhibitors affecting pembrolizumab metabolism.

**PRIMARY OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- Progression-Free Survival: Time from randomization to documented disease progression or death from any cause.
  
**SECONDARY OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- Overall Survival: Duration from randomization to death due to any cause.
- Overall Response Rate: Proportion of patients experiencing a complete or partial response at the first imaging assessment.

**PROPOSED ACCRUAL:** A target enrollment of 250 patients is established over a period of 18 months, aiming to achieve sufficient power for survival analysis.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Patients must have stage IV NSCLC without available curative surgical or radiation options.
- Presence of measurable disease as defined per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Prior history of malignancy other than NSCLC is permissible if in remission for at least 5 years.

**POTENTIAL FACTORS CONTRIBUTING TO TRIAL FAILURE:**
- **Suboptimal Patient Selection:** Failure to adequately identify a cohort of patients who might benefit from the combination therapy, potentially including individuals with more advanced disease states who could show a lower likelihood of treatment efficacy.
- **Resistance Mechanisms:** The potential for existing tumor cell resistance to combined carboplatin and pembrolizumab, including PD-L1 expression inconsistencies leading to varied responses in efficacy.
- **Inadequate Dosing Strategies:** The carboplatin dosing schedule could deviate from optimal pharmacokinetic parameters necessary for synergy with pembrolizumab, impacting overall response rates.
- **Toxicity Burden versus Therapeutic Benefit:** Increased incidence of adverse events such as respiratory distress or hematologic toxicity leading to treatment discontinuation may have exacerbated outcomes and limited patient adherence to the treatment regimen.
- **Pharmacogenomic Influences:** Variability in individual metabolism of carboplatin due to genetic polymorphisms could have complicated efficacy assessments, resulting in inconclusive survival data.

**FOLLOW-UP:** Patients will be monitored for toxicity and efficacy every 3 months for 2 years post-treatment completion, with long-term survival data captured for up to 5 years. 

Overall, the trial's results failed to demonstrate a significant benefit of dual therapy with carboplatin and pembrolizumab, leading to early termination based on interim analyses showing lack of improvement in both primary and secondary endpoints compared to historical controls treated with carboplatin monotherapy.
