**Successful Clinical Trial of Exemestane: Exemestane and Olaparib in Treating Postmenopausal Women With Hormone Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer and BRCA Mutations**

**Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Exemestane and Olaparib in Postmenopausal Women With Metastatic Hormone-Receptor Positive Breast Cancer Harboring BRCA1/2 Mutations**

**RATIONALE:** Hormone receptor-positive breast cancers are often driven by estrogen, with growth facilitated by its signaling pathways. Exemestane functions as an aromatase inhibitor, significantly reducing estrogen synthesis, while Olaparib, a PARP inhibitor, enhances the cytotoxic effects through the promotion of synthetic lethality in BRCA-mutated tumor cells. This combination may improve patient outcomes through synergistic mechanisms, targeting both hormonal and DNA repair pathways.

**PURPOSE:** This randomized phase II trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the combination of exemestane and olaparib in treating postmenopausal women diagnosed with hormone receptor-positive metastatic breast cancer with BRCA1/2 mutations.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objective:** Assess the progression-free survival (PFS) rates at 6 months for patients treated with exemestane and Olaparib compared to exemestane alone.
- **Secondary Objectives:** 
  - Determine overall response rate (ORR) and clinical benefit rate (CBR).
  - Evaluate quality of life (QoL) measures throughout the treatment phase.
  - Analyze biomarker correlations with response and resistance.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, double-blind, randomized study. Patients will be stratified based on prior chemotherapy exposure (yes vs no) and specific BRCA mutation status (BRCA1 vs BRCA2). Treatment will occur over a 12-month period. Follow-ups will happen every 6 weeks during treatment and at 3-month intervals post-treatment.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed metastatic breast cancer
- Hormone receptor-positive (ER+/PR+)
- Presence of BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation confirmed by genomic profiling
- No prior treatment with aromatase inhibitors or PARP inhibitors

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: Postmenopausal women (≥ 50 years with no menstrual periods for at least 12 months)
- Karnofsky performance status ≥ 70%
- Adequate renal and hepatic function as per institutional criteria
- No history of other malignancies within the preceding five years, with certain exceptions

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Distant metastases in the central nervous system
- Previous treatment with either exemestane or olaparib
- Active uncontrolled infections
- Other concurrent cancer therapies

**INTERVENTION**
- **Experimental Arm:** Patients receive exemestane orally (25 mg once daily) in combination with Olaparib (300 mg twice daily) for 12 months.
- **Control Arm:** Patients receive exemestane alone (25 mg once daily) for 12 months.

**EFFICACY MEASURES:**
- **Progression-Free Survival (PFS):** Time from randomization to disease progression or death from any cause.
- **Overall Response Rate (ORR):** Proportion of patients with complete or partial responses to treatment as per RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- **Quality of Life (QoL):** Assessed using the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire at baseline and every three months during the trial.

**SAFETY MEASURES:**
- Adverse events (AEs) will be monitored and recorded per the NCI CTCAE version 4.03. Serious AEs will be documented, with an emphasis on hematologic and gastrointestinal toxicities, particularly given the combined treatment regimen.

The stratified randomization based on BRCA mutation type and chemotherapeutic history is expected to enhance the precision of the therapeutic approaches and minimize confounding variables. Regular monitoring of quality of life metrics integrated with clinical outcomes will ensure that treatment not only improves survival but also addresses the patients' quality of life aspirations.

**CONCLUSION:** This trial aims to elucidate the benefits of combining exemestane with Olaparib in managing metastatic hormone receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women with BRCA mutations, potentially establishing a new standard of care in this patient demographic through mechanistic synergy and tailored treatment strategies.
