**Hydrocortisone for Improving Postoperative Recovery in Cardiac Surgery Patients: A Prospective, Randomized, Double-blind Controlled Trial** 

**Objective**: The study aims to evaluate the efficacy of hydrocortisone in enhancing postoperative recovery in patients undergoing cardiac surgery by assessing its effects on inflammatory markers, cognitive function, and overall recovery metrics.

**Rationale**: Cardiac surgery often precipitates significant physiological stress and inflammatory responses, which can compromise recovery outcomes. Previous trials have established hydrocortisone's role in modulating inflammation and improving patient recovery in various surgical contexts. Despite this, there remains a critical gap in exploring its specific application in the cardiac surgery population. This trial will explore if hydrocortisone supplementation leads to improved recovery metrics in postoperative settings, thereby enhancing patient outcomes.

**Intervention**: A total of 150 patients scheduled for elective cardiac surgery will be randomized into two groups: one group will receive hydrocortisone (100 mg IV) administered at induction and then daily for three consecutive days post-surgery, while the control group will receive a placebo solution. Patients will receive standard postoperative care as per institutional protocols.

**Study Design**: This is a multicenter, prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial across four tertiary care centers known for cardiovascular surgery. The trial will employ rigorous methodology, including thorough patient screening and exclusion criteria to ensure reliable results.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Aged 18 to 80 years.
- Scheduled for elective cardiac surgery (e.g., coronary artery bypass grafting or valve surgery).
- Ability to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Documented endocrine disorders (e.g., Cushing’s syndrome, Addison's disease).
- Current use of systemic corticosteroids or immunosuppressive therapy.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding patients.
- Pre-existing cognitive impairment or psychiatric disorders.
- Documented history of severe adverse reactions to glucocorticoids.

**Endpoints**: The primary endpoint is the change in postoperative inflammatory markers (specifically C-reactive protein and interleukin-6) measured at baseline and 72 hours post-surgery. Secondary endpoints include cognitive function assessed by validated neuropsychological tests (e.g., the Mini-Mental State Examination) at baseline, 1 week, and 1 month postoperatively, as well as quality of life metrics measured by the EQ-5D scale at those same intervals.

**Methodology**: Consistent with successful trial designs, this study employs a double-blind approach to mitigate bias, with both participants and investigators blinded to treatment allocation. Statistical analyses will be conducted using intention-to-treat principles, with appropriate methods employed for the handling of missing data.

Following a scheduled surgery, participants will undergo laboratory assessments, including serum inflammatory markers, and will be monitored meticulously for recovery parameters. Group evaluations will occur at designated time points to assess the primary and secondary endpoints systematically.

**Expected Outcomes**: It is hypothesized that hydrocortisone administration will significantly lower inflammatory markers compared to placebo, resulting in improved cognitive recovery scores and enhanced overall patient quality of life in the postoperative period. The results of this trial could substantively contribute to enhanced recovery protocols in cardiac surgery, promoting the use of hydrocortisone as a therapeutic adjunct.

**Enrollment Timeline**: Recruitment will commence in January 2024, with an anticipated conclusion date by December 2025, subject to the accrual of participants.

With this trial, we aim to enhance the understanding of hydrocortisone’s role in the surgical recovery landscape, identifying specific mechanisms and patient populations that would benefit most from its administration in cardiac surgical contexts.
