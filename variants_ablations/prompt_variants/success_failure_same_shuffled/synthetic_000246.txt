**Failed clinical trial of rituximab**: **Rituximab in Combination with Nivolumab for Patients with Relapsed Follicular Lymphoma**

**Phase II Study of Rituximab and Nivolumab in Patients with Relapsed Follicular Lymphoma: The FOL-RES Study**

**RATIONALE**: An increasing number of studies have demonstrated that the combined use of monoclonal antibodies, such as rituximab, and immune checkpoint inhibitors like nivolumab can lead to enhanced immune responses against tumor cells. Despite theoretical synergies, this trial assesses whether this combination confers a clinically meaningful benefit in patients with relapsed follicular lymphoma, particularly across diverse genetic backgrounds and disease characteristics.

**PURPOSE**: The primary aim of this Phase II trial is to evaluate the overall response rate and safety profile of rituximab administered in conjunction with nivolumab for patients diagnosed with relapsed follicular lymphoma.

**OBJECTIVES**:
- Determine the overall response rate (ORR) of rituximab in combination with nivolumab in patients with relapsed follicular lymphoma.
- Assess the safety profile and incidence of dose-limiting toxicities associated with this combination therapy.
- Investigate the impact of prior therapies and genetic markers on treatment efficacy.

**OUTLINE**: This multicenter clinical trial will be designed as a single-arm study. Patients will receive:
- Rituximab 375 mg/m^2 intravenously on Day 1,
- Nivolumab 3 mg/kg intravenously on Day 1 of each 21-day cycle.
Treatment will continue for a maximum of 6 cycles, followed by a follow-up period of 12 months to monitor long-term outcomes.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL**: A total of 50 patients with relapsed follicular lymphoma will be enrolled for this study.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Histologically confirmed follicular lymphoma (Grade 1-3a),
- Relapsed after at least one line of systemic therapy,
- Measurable disease per the International Working Group (IWG) criteria,
- No history of central nervous system involvement.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Age ≥ 18 years,
- Performance status ECOG 0-2,
- Life expectancy > 6 months,
- Adequate bone marrow function, defined as:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm^3,
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm^3,
- No active autoimmune disease or significant comorbid conditions.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY**:
- More than 4 weeks since prior chemotherapy or monoclonal antibody therapy (other than rituximab),
- No systemic corticosteroids or other immunosuppressive therapy within 14 days before enrollment,
- No recent major surgery or radiation therapy to the target lymph nodes.

**FAILURE ANALYSIS**:
Despite the initial hypothesis that rituximab paired with nivolumab would target multiple mechanisms of action against follicular lymphoma, the trial faced significant challenges:

1. **Limited Efficacy in High-Risk Populations**: Early analyses revealed suboptimal response rates in patients with high-risk disease features, which were prevalent in the participant pool. The heterogeneous nature of follicular lymphoma combined with treatment-resistant clones limited the overall efficacy of this combined regimen.

2. **Dose-Limiting Toxicities**: The combination of rituximab and nivolumab was associated with a higher incidence of grade 3 and 4 toxicities compared to historical controls, particularly immune-related adverse events. These toxicities led to treatment discontinuation in a significant subset of patients, thus confounding efficacy assessments.

3. **Suboptimal Patient Selection Criteria**: While the eligibility criteria aimed to enrich the cohort for potential responders, they inadvertently excluded a more representative sample of the population. The lack of inclusivity regarding patients with different genetic mutations (e.g., mutations in the BCL2 or TP53 genes) potentially limited the generalizability of findings.

4. **Variability in Tumor Biology**: The trial faced significant inter-patient variability in tumor microenvironments, which could have diminished the expected enhancement of therapeutic efficacy through immune engagement. T-cell exhaustion or an unfavorable immune landscape might have further curtailed the potential benefits of the combination therapy.

5. **Lack of Previous Data Supporting Combined Regimens**: Prior studies that combined monoclonal antibodies with checkpoint inhibitors in lymphoma indicated mixed results, which were not adequately addressed in this trial’s design. The absence of robust evidence for the efficacy of these combinations in previously treated populations may have decreased the reliability of this approach.

In conclusion, the FOL-RES trial illustrated profound complexities inherent in treating relapsed follicular lymphoma with novel personalized approaches and highlights the critical need for further exploration of patient stratification and combination strategies to enhance therapeutic outcomes in this patient demographic. The trial was officially terminated after enrollment of 42 patients with no significant efficacy observed during interim analysis.
