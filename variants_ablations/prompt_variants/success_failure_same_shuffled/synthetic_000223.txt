**Failed clinical trial of rivaroxaban: A Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Rivaroxaban in Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease at High Risk for Cardiovascular Events**

**Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Study of Rivaroxaban for Cardiovascular Event Reduction in Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease**

The objective of this clinical trial is to assess the efficacy and safety of rivaroxaban compared to placebo in reducing the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), including myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular-related mortality in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) who are at elevated risk for cardiovascular complications. This study will investigate whether rivaroxaban can provide additional benefits over standard-of-care treatments in this vulnerable population.

**Study Design:**
This is a multicenter, randomized (study medication is assigned by chance), double-blind (neither physician nor participant knows the identity of the assigned treatment), placebo-controlled parallel-group study designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of rivaroxaban versus placebo. Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either rivaroxaban or placebo, in addition to standard management of CKD and associated cardiovascular risk factors. The estimated duration of treatment is expected to be 24 months, with participants followed for an additional 30 days post-treatment. The primary endpoint is the composite of MACE occurring during the study.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 40 years and older with a diagnosis of stage 3 or 4 CKD.
- Documented history of cardiovascular disease, including prior myocardial infarction or stroke, or presence of significant cardiovascular risk factors (e.g., diabetes, hypertension).
- Participants must be stable on standard-of-care cardiovascular therapy for a minimum of 3 months prior to enrollment.
- Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of 15-59 mL/min.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Active bleeding disorders or conditions predisposing to bleeding (e.g., recent gastrointestinal bleeding, thrombocytopenia).
- Recent arterial or venous thromboembolism within the past 6 months.
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR <15 mL/min) or on dialysis.
- Conditions requiring chronic anticoagulation therapy or inability to discontinue other anticoagulants prior to study entry.
- History of hypersensitivity to rivaroxaban or related compounds.

**Anticipated Outcomes:**
Patient safety and tolerability of rivaroxaban will be closely monitored throughout the study. The primary efficacy endpoint is the time to first occurrence of MACE over the course of the trial.

**Reason for Failure:**
This study encountered significant challenges leading to its eventual failure. One critical factor was the notable variability in the patient cohort regarding underlying conditions; individuals exhibited a wide range of comorbidities, including differing severities of cardiovascular disease and CKD, which diminished homogeneity and could have compromised the measured efficacy of rivaroxaban.

Moreover, the incidence of treatment-related bleeding complications surpassed expectations, particularly in patients with pre-existing bleeding tendencies often associated with advanced CKD, negating the anticipated cardiovascular benefits owing to significant hemorrhagic risks. This adverse event prevalence superseded potential reductions in thromboembolic events.

The timing of rivaroxaban administration relative to clinical stabilization was also inconsistent; many participants began therapy after substantial delays post-initial cardiovascular events, likely undermining the drug's effectiveness when most crucial.

Insufficient secondary prevention strategies alongside rivaroxaban therapy further hindered the trial’s success, leaving subjects inadequately managed for their cardiovascular risks beyond anticoagulation, which may have contributed to unfavorable clinical outcomes.

Additionally, the study design was hampered by an underpowered sample size, limiting the ability to detect any genuine treatment effect, thereby erroneously concluding that rivaroxaban was not efficacious in this specific at-risk population.

Ultimately, the combination of these diverse factors led to the failure of the trial, marking it as a critical point of learning in the evaluation of rivaroxaban within the complex patient population of chronic kidney disease associated with cardiovascular risks.
