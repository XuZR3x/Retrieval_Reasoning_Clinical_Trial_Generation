**Failed Clinical Trial of Dabigatran: Efficacy and Safety of Dabigatran Versus Apixaban in Patients with Non-Valvular Atrial Fibrillation and Active Cancer**  

**Study Identifier:** DABICAN

**Study Design:** This is a phase 3, multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of dabigatran compared to apixaban in patients diagnosed with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (AF) who are also undergoing active cancer treatment. Major endpoints reflect the primary focus on stroke prevention and major bleeding events.

**Background:** Previous studies indicated that dabigatran was associated with a significant rate of gastrointestinal bleeding and a particular lack of synergy when considered alongside dual antiplatelet therapies. This study aimed to determine whether dabigatran could demonstrate comparable effectiveness and safety in a unique population with additional co-morbidities, specifically focusing on cancer patients.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Adults aged 18 to 80 years with a confirmed diagnosis of AF.
2. Patients concurrently undergoing treatment for active malignancy.
3. CHA2DS2-VASc score of ≥2 indicating a moderate to high risk for stroke.
4. Signed informed consent obtained from participants.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. History of significant bleeding disorders or active bleeding.
2. Renal impairment with estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <30 ml/min.
3. Prior intracranial hemorrhage or severe uncontrolled hypertension.
4. Previous use of anticoagulants or antiplatelet medications incompatible with trial medication in the last 30 days.
5. Patients with acute thromboembolic events in the past month.

**Endpoints:**  
Primary endpoint was the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events, including ischemic stroke and systemic embolism, occurring within a follow-up period of 12 months. Secondary endpoints included the assessment of major bleeding events and thromboembolic events post-treatment initiation.

**Randomization and Treatment Regimen:**  
Patients were randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to receive either dabigatran at a dose of 150 mg twice daily or apixaban at a dose of 5 mg twice daily, with randomization achieved via a secure web-based system. The allocation sequence was generated by a computerized algorithm and concealed from recruiting investigators.

**Trial Oversight:**  
The study adhered to Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines, with ethical approval from relevant institutional review boards. An independent data monitoring committee (DMC) oversaw patient safety and trial integrity.

**Follow-Up and Data Collection:**  
Regular assessments were scheduled at 30, 90, and 180 days, as well as at the end of the study period (12 months), where both clinical events and laboratory parameters were evaluated. Complications such as vascular occlusions, stroke symptoms, or any significant bleeding were documented thoroughly.

**Preliminary Results and Conclusions:**  
From an enrolled population of 1,000 participants, demographics indicated a predominance of older adults with multiple comorbidities, including various stages of cancer. Initial findings revealed that dabigatran did not exhibit efficacy superior to apixaban in the prevention of thromboembolic events and presented a notably higher incidence of major bleeding complications, particularly in individuals concurrently receiving chemotherapy. Furthermore, a significant number of patients experienced gastrointestinal adverse effects, which raised substantial concerns regarding treatment adherence and safety, ultimately limiting the therapeutic applicability of dabigatran in this population.

The outcome of this trial aligns with earlier observations where the side effect profile of dabigatran proved detrimental compared to alternative anticoagulants within populations requiring enhanced safety protocols, particularly those undergoing complex cancer therapies. As such, this trial has underscored the need for cautious consideration of dabigatran in patients with multifactorial health challenges, contributing to the overall conclusion of failure under the inclusive experimental conditions.

*This report firmly establishes the limitations of dabigatran, particularly in addressing the nuanced healthcare needs of patients with concurrent medical conditions such as cancer and highlights potential areas for further investigation to improve anticoagulant strategies in similarly vulnerable populations.*
