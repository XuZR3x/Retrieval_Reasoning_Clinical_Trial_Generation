Failed clinical trial of montelukast: Efficacy and Safety of Montelukast in Adults with Moderate to Severe Asthma in the Presence of Comorbidities. 

A Phase III, Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Montelukast in Adults with Moderate to Severe Asthma and Comorbid Conditions

This study aims to assess the efficacy and safety of montelukast in adults diagnosed with moderate to severe asthma complicated by comorbid conditions including obesity, rhinitis, and gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD). The primary hypothesis posits that montelukast will demonstrate superior efficacy over placebo in alleviating asthma-related symptoms and improving overall control despite the intrusiveness of comorbidities. 

Inclusion Criteria:
- Adults aged 18-65 years
- Physician-confirmed diagnosis of moderate to severe persistent asthma, with an Asthma Control Questionnaire (ACQ) score ≥ 1.5
- Patients must have been on a stable regimen of inhaled corticosteroids for at least 3 months prior to enrollment 
- Documented history of comorbid obesity (BMI ≥ 30), allergic rhinitis, or GERD 

Exclusion Criteria:
- History of asthma exacerbation requiring hospitalization or systemic corticosteroids within the past 6 months 
- Past or current history of significant respiratory diseases other than asthma
- Patients who are currently smokers or have a smoking history of > 10 pack years 
- Any chronic infection affecting the respiratory tract 

The efficacy of montelukast will be measured primarily through changes in the ACQ score at 12 weeks, in conjunction with secondary endpoints including pulmonary function tests (PFTs) and assessments of rescue medication use. Given the potential challenges presented by concurrent conditions which may obscure the efficacy of monotherapy with a leukotriene receptor antagonist, the study also aims to explore correlations between comorbidity severity and therapeutic outcomes to ascertain the impact of these variables.

As previously indicated in other trials involving montelukast, several factors may contribute to the potential failure of this study. First, the primary hypothesis relies heavily on the assumption that montelukast will exhibit substantial therapeutic effects despite the presence of intercurrent comorbidities that could significantly alter the symptomatology and severity of asthma, a notion that has not consistently borne fruit in prior research. 

Secondly, strict inclusion criteria may unduly limit participant diversity, potentially predisposing the study to a demand for a homogenous population that does not accurately reflect the complexity of asthma in the general population, which frequently encompasses various comorbidities.

Furthermore, the methodology lacks a comprehensive assessment of confounding factors intrinsic to the subjects' comorbidities that may mitigate the effectiveness of montelukast, particularly in individuals with obesity whose conditions are associated with more pronounced inflammation leading to reduced therapeutic response.

Lastly, the use of ACQ as a primary endpoint may not fully encapsulate the nuanced improvements in asthma control as it is subject to the biases inherent in self-reported symptoms, thus potentially underrepresenting montelukast’s efficacy when perceived against a backdrop of chronic comorbid conditions.

This investigational study is poised to offer valuable insights into the intricacies of montelukast’s therapeutic profile in a cohort marked by multifaceted health issues and their implications on asthma management, although the risk of failure persists due to aforementioned potentially confounding factors.
