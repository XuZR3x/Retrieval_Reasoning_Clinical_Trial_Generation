**Failed Clinical Trial of Entinostat: A Study of Entinostat With Nivolumab in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Entinostat in Combination With Nivolumab for Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

The purpose of this phase II clinical trial is to ascertain the effectiveness and safety of the histone deacetylase inhibitor entinostat when administered in combination with nivolumab in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer previously treated with platinum-based chemotherapy. This trial specifically aims to assess overall response rates, progression-free survival (PFS), and investigate the potential mechanistic limitations leading to a failure to demonstrate therapeutic efficacy.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) based on Response Evaluation Criteria In Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria in patients with advanced NSCLC receiving entinostat and nivolumab.
  
**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess tumor progression-free survival (PFS) among patients undergoing the combination therapy.
II. To evaluate the safety profile and toxicity of entinostat in conjunction with nivolumab.
III. To explore changes in tumor methylation and histone modifications, aiming to identify potential biomarkers for response.

**TERTIARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To correlate molecular changes in circulating tumor DNA with clinical response.
II. To analyze the association between treatment-induced toxicities and patient compliance.

**OUTLINE:**
This multi-center, randomized, double-blind phase II trial will enroll patients who have histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC that is measurable and progressing post-platinum-based chemotherapy. Patients will be assigned to receive either nivolumab in combination with entinostat or nivolumab with placebo. Entinostat dosing will occur orally at 10 mg per day on a 5-day schedule each week, while nivolumab will be administered intravenously at 240 mg every 2 weeks. The treatment will continue until disease progression or the occurrence of unacceptable toxicity.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced non-small cell lung cancer.
- Measurable disease according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Previous treatment with at least one line of platinum-based chemotherapy regimen.
- ECOG performance status of 0-1.
- Adequately controlled hypertension and no active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to nivolumab or entinostat.
- Previous treatment with investigational immune-oncology agents or histone deacetylase inhibitors.
- Evidence of untreated CNS metastases or significant comorbidities prohibiting patient participation.
- Any contraindication to scheduled treatment administration as determined by the Investigative team.

**EVALUATION OF OUTCOMES:**
Confirmed tumor responses will be categorized as either complete response (CR), partial response (PR), or stable disease (SD) according to RECIST criteria. ORR will be calculated as the proportion of patients achieving CR or PR. PFS will be assessed from the initiation of treatment to disease progression or death by any cause, with data analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method.

**SAFETY MONITORING:**
Adverse events (AEs) will be systematically recorded and graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0. Toxicity related to combination therapy may lead to interruptions or reductions of the entinostat dosage, influencing patient retention and compliance throughout the trial.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES AND MECHANISTIC CONSIDERATIONS:**
Despite the anticipated synergistic effects of entinostat and nivolumab, challenges stemming from mechanistic limitations, including inherent tumor heterogeneity and inadequate biomarkers, may hinder achieving the efficacy endpoints. The anticipated difficulties associated with an insufficient biomarker correlation and the suboptimal design of the dosing regimen could substantially impact the study’s overall success.

Following the completion of study therapy, patients will be followed for overall survival, disease progression, and health-related quality of life assessments at 3, 6, and 12 months post-treatment. Notably, robust data collection on histone modification and DNA methylation may yield insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying treatment failure in advanced NSCLC.
