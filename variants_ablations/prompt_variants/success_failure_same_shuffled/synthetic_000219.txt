**Study Title: A Phase I Clinical Trial Evaluating the Safety and Efficacy of Alisertib in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Study Objectives:**
This phase I trial aims to evaluate the safety and tolerability of alisertib in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have progressed after prior therapies. The primary objective will be to determine the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of alisertib when administered alongside pembrolizumab. Secondary objectives include assessing the efficacy of this regimen in terms of overall response rate and duration of response, as well as evaluating pharmacokinetic (PK) profiles and correlating biomarkers of response.

**Background:**
Alisertib (MLN8237), an investigational Aurora A kinase inhibitor, has demonstrated favorable absorption and distribution characteristics. Previous studies suggest that alisertib may enhance the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors by promoting apoptosis in tumor cells and overcoming resistance pathways typical in NSCLC. Pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody, has shown clinical benefit in NSCLC. Given the synergistic potential stemming from concurrent inhibition of oncogenic pathways by alisertib and immune modulation through pembrolizumab, this combination is hypothesized to enhance therapeutic efficacy.

**Study Design:**
The trial will utilize a non-randomized dose-escalation design. Eligible patients will receive alisertib orally twice daily for 7 days of a 21-day treatment cycle in addition to standard doses of pembrolizumab, administered intravenously every 3 weeks. Treatment will continue until disease progression or intolerable toxicity occurs.

**Study Population:**
This multi-center study will enroll patients with histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC who have experienced disease progression following one or more lines of systemic therapy. The inclusion criteria will prioritize a cohort of diverse backgrounds and demographics to ensure a comprehensive assessment of the treatment's safety and efficacy across different populations.

**Eligibility Criteria:**
*Inclusion Criteria:*
- Histological confirmation of advanced non-small cell lung cancer.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0 or 1.
- At least one measurable lesion per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1.
- Adequate organ function, as defined by laboratory assessments.
- Signed informed consent provided.

*Exclusion Criteria:*
- Previous treatment with aurora kinase inhibitors.
- Uncontrolled intercurrent illness including active infections.
- CNS metastases that are symptomatic or require treatment.
- Any prior malignancies within the last 5 years, except for adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancer.

**Endpoints:**
*Primary Endpoint:*
- Determine the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of alisertib in conjunction with pembrolizumab.

*Secondary Endpoints:*
- Assess overall response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete or partial response.
- Evaluate the duration of response and pharmacokinetic profile of alisertib in combination with pembrolizumab.
- Analyze tumor biopsy specimens for biomarkers correlating with response, including Aurora A kinase expression, PD-L1 status, and immune cell infiltration.

**Results:**
Enrollment will target approximately 50 patients across multiple centers. Given the favorable pharmacokinetic profile of alisertib and the inclusive assessment of diverse populations, it is anticipated that the carefully defined eligibility criteria will yield robust safety and efficacy analyses. The adaptive trial design will allow for real-time monitoring and adjustments based on preliminary efficacy findings.

**Follow-Up:**
Patients will be monitored with regular clinic visits throughout their treatment period and will undergo follow-up assessments for a maximum of 30 days after the last dose of study medication. Safety will be evaluated using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0, with comprehensive tracking of adverse events to inform future trial designs.

**Conclusion:**
This phase I trial seeks to build on promising outcomes observed with alisertib and immunotherapy combinations in other malignancies. By enrolling a heterogeneous patient population with advanced NSCLC, this study aims to elucidate the safety profile and efficacy of a novel therapeutic combination, potentially leading to new treatment paradigms in managing this challenging disease.
