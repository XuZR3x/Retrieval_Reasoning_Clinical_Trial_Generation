<clinical_trial>
    <title>Evaluation of Intravenous Lidocaine for Enhanced Recovery After Surgery in Robotic-Assisted Prostatectomy</title>
    <objective>
        The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the effect of perioperative intravenous lidocaine infusion on postoperative pain control and opioid consumption in patients undergoing robotic-assisted prostatectomy.
    </objective>
    <background>
        The use of intravenous lidocaine has been shown to exhibit significant analgesic, anti-hyperalgesic, and anti-inflammatory effects, making it a compelling adjunct in multimodal analgesia regimens. Its efficacy in reducing opioid consumption and associated side effects, such as nausea and vomiting, as well as its role in mitigating inflammatory pain responses post-surgery, supports its integration into enhanced recovery protocols following major surgical procedures. 
    </background>
    <hypothesis>
        The administration of intravenous lidocaine will lead to a statistically significant reduction in total opioid consumption and improved pain control during the first 48 hours postoperatively in patients undergoing robotic-assisted prostatectomy.
    </hypothesis>
    <methods>
        This prospective, double-blind, randomized controlled trial will enroll 80 male patients (ASA PS I and II, aged 50-75 years) scheduled for robotic-assisted prostatectomy. Eligible patients will be randomized into two groups: Group A (lidocaine group) will receive an initial IV bolus of lidocaine (1.5 mg/kg) followed by a continuous infusion of 2 mg/kg/h for 48 hours postoperatively. Group B (control group) will receive an equivalent volume of normal saline. 
    </methods>
    <intervention>
        Primary outcomes will include total opioid consumption (measured in morphine milligram equivalents) during the first 48 hours post-surgery, with secondary outcomes assessing pain scores via the Numeric Pain Rating Scale (NRS) at rest and during activity at specified intervals (PACU, 2, 4, 12, 24, and 48 hours). Additionally, assessments will include the incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), quality of recovery as evaluated by the QoR-40 questionnaire at 48 hours post-surgery, and the assessment of satisfaction rates using postoperative satisfaction surveys.
    </intervention>
    <inclusion_criteria>
        Patients must be male, aged 50 to 75 years, and possess an ASA physical status of I or II.
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
        Patients with pre-existing neurological disorders, severe comorbidities (e.g., unstable cardiac disease), or those who are allergic to local anesthetics will be excluded.
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <blinding>
        Both participants and assessors will be blinded to group assignments to ensure impartiality in outcome measurement.
    </blinding>
    <sample_size_calculation>
        Previous studies indicated that a 20% reduction in opioid consumption can be clinically significant. The sample size was calculated to detect this difference at a power of 80% and an alpha level of 0.05, yielding 80 participants (40 per group) to account for a dropout rate of approximately 10%.
    </sample_size_calculation>
    <results>
        Preliminary findings exhibited a statistically significant decrease in morphine consumption in the lidocaine group compared to the control group (mean difference of 35 mg, p < 0.05). Furthermore, mean pain scores on the NRS at 24 hours post-surgery were significantly lower in the lidocaine group (3.5 vs 5.8, p < 0.01). Reduced PONV incidents were also reported in the lidocaine group with satisfaction rates surpassing the control group by 25% (85% vs 60%).
    </results>
    <conclusion>
        The incorporation of intravenous lidocaine as part of a multimodal analgesic approach substantially improves postoperative pain management and minimizes opioid requirements following robotic-assisted prostatectomy, suggesting its utility in enhanced recovery pathways.
    </conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
