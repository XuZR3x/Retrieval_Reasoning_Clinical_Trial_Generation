<ClinicalTrial>
  <Title>A Phase II, Multicenter, Randomized Trial Evaluating Lenalidomide in Combination with Nivolumab in Patients with Relapsed/Refractory Hodgkin Lymphoma</Title>
  
  <Objective>
    To assess the safety and efficacy of lenalidomide in combination with nivolumab in patients with relapsed or refractory Hodgkin lymphoma (HL), hypothesizing a synergistic effect on tumor response rates due to lenalidomide's immunomodulatory properties and nivolumab's programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) blockade.
  </Objective>

  <Design>
    This is a Phase II, multicenter, open-label, randomized study designed to evaluate the efficacy of lenalidomide plus nivolumab compared to nivolumab alone. Patients will be stratified based on prior treatment regimens and disease stage.
  </Design>
  
  <InclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>
      1. Age ≥ 18 years with histologically confirmed relapsed or refractory Hodgkin lymphoma.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      2. Previous treatment with at least two lines of systemic therapy for Hodgkin lymphoma, including an anti-PD-1 agent.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      3. At least one measurable lesion as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      4. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      5. Normal organ function: absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,000/mm³, platelet count ≥ 75,000/mm³, liver function tests ≤ 3 times the upper limit of normal, and creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      6. Written informed consent must be obtained from each participant.
    </Criterion>
  </InclusionCriteria>
  
  <ExclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>
      1. Known active central nervous system involvement by HL or other malignancies.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      2. Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to lenalidomide or nivolumab.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      3. Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy or significant immunodeficiency.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      4. Concurrent use of systemic glucocorticoids greater than 10 mg/day of prednisone or equivalent.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      5. Pregnancy or lactation.
    </Criterion>
  </ExclusionCriteria>
  
  <PrimaryEndpoint>
    Objective Response Rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR), unconfirmed complete response (CRu), or partial response (PR) as assessed by the independent radiology committee.
  </PrimaryEndpoint>
  
  <SecondaryEndpoints>
    <Endpoint>
      1. Progression-free survival (PFS).
    </Endpoint>
    <Endpoint>
      2. Overall survival (OS).
    </Endpoint>
    <Endpoint>
      3. Safety profile assessed by the frequency and severity of adverse events.
    </Endpoint>
    <Endpoint>
      4. Quality of life assessed by standard questionnaires at baseline and during the study.
    </Endpoint>
  </SecondaryEndpoints>
  
  <StatisticalAnalysis>
    The study will utilize a two-stage Fleming’s design to assess efficacy. A total of 60 patients are to be enrolled. If two or more patients experience an objective response out of the first 18 patients treated within the lenalidomide and nivolumab cohort, the study will continue to fully enroll. Statistical significance will be established at a p-value of <0.05.
  </StatisticalAnalysis>
  
  <Rationale>
    The successful design of inclusion criteria ensures the study selectively targets patients likely to derive benefit from lenalidomide, based on previous treatment responses as well as measurable lesions prior to therapy. Furthermore, the combination of lenalidomide and nivolumab exploits the known synergistic effects of immunomodulation and immune checkpoint inhibition. Initial pilot studies indicated that lenalidomide improves T-cell function and enhances anti-tumor immunity, thereby anticipated enhancing assessment outcomes. The structured trial design with an adaptive model allows for efficient evaluation of treatment efficacy while rather reducing the risk of exposing patients to ineffective therapies. Meticulous monitoring for potential adverse effects, including immune-related adverse events pertinent to nivolumab, aligns with rigorous safety standards and regulatory compliance, directly contributing to the study's ethical standing. Finally, the emphasis on patient education with respect to teratogenic risk management safeguards participants’ health while contributing to the high integrity required for trial completion.
  </Rationale>

  <ExpectedResults>
    It is anticipated that the combination of lenalidomide and nivolumab will yield a superior objective response rate compared to nivolumab alone, thereby reinforcing the potential for enhanced efficacy in patients with difficult-to-treat relapsed or refractory Hodgkin lymphoma.
  </ExpectedResults>
  
</ClinicalTrial>
