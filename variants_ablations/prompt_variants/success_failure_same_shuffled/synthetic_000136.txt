Failed clinical trial of riociguat (adempas, bay63-2521): Investigating the Efficacy of Riociguat in Patients with Pulmonary Hypertension Secondary to Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Riociguat (BAY63-2521) in Patients with Pulmonary Hypertension Associated with Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

Background: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is associated with a significant burden of pulmonary hypertension (PH), leading to exacerbated morbidity and mortality. Riociguat, a soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) stimulator, has been posited as a potential therapeutic intervention due to its mechanism of promoting endothelial relaxation and decreasing pulmonary vascular resistance. Given the complexity of respiratory comorbidities, this trial sought to assess the efficacy and safety of riociguat in patients with COPD-related PH. 

Objectives: The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the change in 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) from baseline to week 12 compared to placebo. Secondary objectives included the assessment of pulmonary hemodynamics through right heart catheterization and the safety profile determined by adverse events (AE).

Inclusion Criteria:
- Adult male and female patients aged 40 to 80 years
- Confirmed diagnosis of moderate to severe COPD (GOLD classification II-IV)
- Compatible diagnosis of pulmonary hypertension defined by mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) ≥ 25 mmHg via right heart catheterization
- 6MWD between 100 meters and 400 meters at baseline
- Stable treatment with bronchodilators for a minimum of 4 weeks prior to screening

Exclusion Criteria:
- History of pulmonary embolism within the past 6 months
- Other forms of pulmonary hypertension not attributed to COPD 
- Significant cardiovascular disease evidence, including severe left ventricular dysfunction (LVEF < 40%)
- Current use of phosphodiesterase 5 inhibitors or nitrates
- Documented history of significant renal impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min)

Endpoints and Outcome Measures:
Primary Endpoint: Change in 6MWD from baseline to week 12
Secondary Endpoints: 
- Change in New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class 
- Mean change in pulmonary arterial pressure from baseline to week 12
- Number of participants experiencing any adverse events during the study period

Results: The trial was prematurely terminated due to inadequate therapeutic response. A total of 240 participants were enrolled with a considerable level of heterogeneity across the COPD severity spectrum, thereby complicating effective patient stratification and leading to inadequate patient selection. 

Analysis indicated no statistically significant improvements in 6MWD compared to the placebo group, potentially attributable to variability in disease mechanisms governing COPD-associated PH. Furthermore, the pharmacodynamic interactions of riociguat when used in this patient population revealed no synergistic benefits, while adverse cardiovascular effects emerged among subjects concurrently on bronchodilator therapy. 

The reliance on specific functional outcomes proved limiting; neither the 6MWD nor the NYHA functional class adequately captured the complexities associated with COPD and PH, highlighting the pressing need for more comprehensive outcome evaluations in future studies.

Conclusion: The combination of inadequate patient selection, variability in disease mechanisms, and insufficient attention to pharmacodynamics contributed significantly to the failure of this clinical trial. An increased emphasis on targeted patient populations, thorough pharmacokinetic assessments, and the inclusion of diverse clinical endpoints may enhance future efforts to elucidate the therapeutic potential of riociguat in managing pulmonary hypertension associated with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.
