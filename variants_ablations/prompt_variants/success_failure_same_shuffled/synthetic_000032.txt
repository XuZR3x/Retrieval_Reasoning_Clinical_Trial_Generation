**Successful Clinical Trial of Cilostazol: Efficacy of Cilostazol on Glycemic Control and Endothelial Function in Type 2 Diabetes Patients With Metabolic Syndrome** 

**Study Title**: Efficacy of Cilostazol on Glycemic Control and Endothelial Function in Type 2 Diabetes Patients With Metabolic Syndrome: A Multi-Center, Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Study 

**Objective**: The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the effects of cilostazol on glycemic control, endothelial function, arterial stiffness, and inflammatory markers in patients diagnosed with type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome. This study intends to elucidate cilostazol's mechanism of action, particularly its role in enhancing vasculoprotection and improving long-term cardiovascular outcomes.

**Background**: Cilostazol, a selective phosphodiesterase III inhibitor, has shown promise in improving vascular health via mechanisms involving vasodilation, insulin sensitivity enhancement, and anti-inflammatory activity. Prior studies have indicated improved outcomes in cardiovascular and metabolic parameters; however, further investigations into its roles in glycemic control and endothelial function are warranted.

**Study Design**: This study is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted across multiple centers. A total of 300 participants aged 30-70 with validated type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome, as per the American Diabetes Association and the International Diabetes Federation criteria, will be recruited.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
1. Age between 30 and 70 years.
2. Diagnosis of type 2 diabetes (HbA1c ≥ 6.5%).
3. Presence of metabolic syndrome defined by the presence of at least three of the following:
   - Elevated fasting glucose (≥110 mg/dL).
   - Increased waist circumference (≥94 cm for men, ≥80 cm for women).
   - Elevated triglycerides (≥150 mg/dL).
   - Decreased HDL cholesterol (<40 mg/dL for men and <50 mg/dL for women).
   - Blood pressure ≥130/85 mmHg.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
1. Known hypersensitivity to cilostazol.
2. History of major bleeding events.
3. Severe hepatic or renal dysfunction.
4. Cardiovascular events within the past 6 months.
5. Chronic inflammatory conditions or malignancy.
6. Use of CYP3A4 or CYP2C19 inhibitors.
7. Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Primary Endpoint**: The primary outcome will assess changes in glycemic control as measured by HbA1c levels at 12 months. Secondary outcomes include changes in endothelial function assessed by flow-mediated dilation and arterial stiffness measured by pulse wave velocity, as well as alterations in inflammatory markers such as high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP) and adiponectin levels.

**Statistical Analysis**: Statistical analysis will be performed using intention-to-treat principles. Continuous variables will be analyzed using independent t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests, and categorical variables will be assessed using Chi-square tests. A p-value <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Results**: Preliminary data analysis indicates that cilostazol treatment led to a statistically significant reduction in HbA1c levels in comparison to placebo (p < 0.01). In addition, notable decreases in pulse wave velocity and hsCRP were observed in the cilostazol group, underscoring its vasculoprotective effects. Participants exhibited enhanced endothelial function, as demonstrated by improved flow-mediated dilation responses compared to baseline measurements.

**Conclusion**: Cilostazol demonstrated efficacy in improving glycemic control and enhancing vascular function in patients with type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome. These findings provide a compelling argument for considering cilostazol as an adjunctive therapy in managing metabolic dysregulation and mitigating cardiovascular risks in this high-risk population. Longitudinal studies are warranted to establish long-term cardiovascular outcomes associated with cilostazol therapy.
