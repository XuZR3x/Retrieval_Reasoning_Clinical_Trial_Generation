**Title:** A Randomized, Open-Label, Multicenter Phase III Study of S-1 Monotherapy Versus S-1 in Combination with Paclitaxel for the First-Line Treatment of Advanced Gastric Cancer 

**Study Overview:** This phase III, open-label, multicenter, randomized clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of S-1 monotherapy compared to S-1 in combination with paclitaxel in patients diagnosed with advanced gastric cancer. The study hypothesis is that the combination therapy will demonstrate superior overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) due to the enhanced therapeutic impact derived from the strategic combination of S-1 and an established chemotherapeutic agent, while maintaining acceptable safety profiles.

**Study Design:** A total of 750 patients with histologically confirmed advanced gastric adenocarcinoma, including those with metastatic disease, will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either S-1 alone (Arm A) or S-1 plus paclitaxel (Arm B) as first-line therapy. Randomization will ensure balanced distribution across treatment arms with stratification based on performance status (ECOG 0, 1, or 2), the presence of metastatic sites, and prior surgical intervention. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years or older
- Histologically confirmed advanced gastric adenocarcinoma (including metastatic disease)
- No prior systemic chemotherapy for advanced disease 
- ECOG performance status of 0 to 2
- Adequate organ function defined by:
  - Hematologic parameters: WBC ≥ 4,000/mm³, absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³, hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL
  - Hepatic function: AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN, bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL
  - Renal function: Creatinine ≤ 1.25 x ULN and creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min
- Life expectancy of at least 3 months
- Written informed consent obtained from patients

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of another malignancy (excluding non-melanoma skin cancer) within the last 5 years
- Previous radiotherapy to the region of the study cancer
- Known hypersensitivity to S-1 or paclitaxel
- Current uncontrolled comorbid conditions (e.g., severe cardiac disease, uncontrolled diabetes)
- Patients with active infections or systemic metastases affecting treatment compliance

**Intervention Details:** 
- Arm A: S-1 40 mg/m² orally administered twice daily on days 1-14 of a 21-day treatment cycle, without additional agents. 
- Arm B: S-1 40 mg/m² orally administered on the same schedule, coupled with paclitaxel 80 mg/m² administered intravenously on days 1, 8, and 15 of the same cycle. Dose adjustments for S-1 based on toxicity grade shall be permitted in accordance with the CTCAE v5.0 guidelines.

**Endpoints:**
- Primary Endpoint: Overall survival (OS) defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause.
- Secondary Endpoints: Progression-free survival (PFS) assessed by RECIST v1.1, overall response rate (ORR), and safety profile as assessed by frequency and severity of adverse events (AEs) recorded weekly, including hematologic and non-hematologic toxicities.

**Monitoring:** Patients will be closely monitored throughout treatment for efficacy and safety, with regular assessments conducted to ensure adherence to protocol and management of AEs. Follow-up visits will occur every three months for two years post-treatment completion, then annually thereafter. 

**Outcomes**: This study aims to validate the robust efficacy profile of S-1 when combined with paclitaxel, enhancing treatment outcomes through strategic combination therapy, while ensuring safety is managed effectively, and establishing clear interpretation of efficacy results through comprehensive patient stratification. The managed toxicity of S-1 is anticipated to improve patient adherence, contributing to the overall therapeutic success in this advanced gastric cancer population. 

**Conclusion:** This clinical trial aligns with previous successful trials of S-1, leveraging its favorable toxicity management and establishing its role as a first-line treatment in advanced gastric cancers when combined with other effective agents. The anticipated findings from this study could afford new insights into first-line therapeutic strategies, ultimately enhancing the patient management paradigm in advanced gastric cancer.
