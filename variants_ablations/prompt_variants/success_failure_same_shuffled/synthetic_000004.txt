**Successful Clinical Trial of Aprepitant: Efficacy of Aprepitant with Ondansetron and Dexamethasone in Preventing Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting in Patients with Breast Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** The combination of aprepitant, ondansetron, and dexamethasone may synergistically enhance the prevention of chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV) through the blockade of multiple emetic pathways, especially in patients undergoing treatment for breast cancer with highly emetogenic chemotherapy (HEC).

**PURPOSE:** This phase III clinical trial investigates the efficacy and safety of aprepitant in conjunction with ondansetron and dexamethasone for preventing CINV in patients receiving HEC for breast cancer treatment.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** To evaluate the efficacy of the triplet regimen (aprepitant, ondansetron, and dexamethasone) in reducing the incidence of acute and delayed vomiting in patients treated with HEC for breast cancer.
- **Secondary:** To assess the proportion of patients achieving complete response (no vomiting and no rescue medication) and to evaluate patient-reported outcomes related to nausea severity and quality of life over the treatment period.

**OUTLINE:** This open-label, randomized, multicenter study involves approximately 250 participants diagnosed with breast cancer scheduled for HEC. Patients will receive oral aprepitant on days 1-3, intravenous ondansetron on day 1, and oral dexamethasone on days 1-4. Nausea and vomiting will be evaluated daily, with patient-reported outcomes captured via validated questionnaires.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 250 patients will be accrued to ensure enough power to detect significant differences in treatment efficacy.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Diagnosis of breast cancer
- Scheduled to receive one of the following chemotherapy regimens classified as highly emetogenic, including doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide or paclitaxel/carboplatin
- No history of significant emesis from prior chemotherapy regimens.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2
- No chronic antiemetic therapy within 30 days prior to enrollment
- Adequate organ function (WBC > 3,000/mm³, ANC > 1,500/mm³, platelet count > 100,000/mm³, bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times upper limit of normal, AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 times upper limit of normal).

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- No prior treatment with neurokinin-1 receptor antagonists
- Patients with a history of allergic reactions to any components of the regimen are excluded.
- No concurrent investigational protocols or treatments.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- The primary outcome will assess the number of patients who report no vomiting and no use of rescue therapy within the first five days following chemotherapy.
- Secondary outcomes will include patient-reported daily assessments of nausea severity via numerical rating scales and quality of life evaluations conducted at baseline, during treatment, and at follow-up.

**RESULTS:**
The primary endpoint indicated that 70% of patients experienced no emesis and required no rescue medication during the initial cycle of chemotherapy. Moreover, secondary results showed a significant reduction in nausea severity and improved overall quality of life scores among participants receiving the combination therapy compared to historical controls receiving standard antiemetics.

**CONCLUSION:**
The results underscore the efficacy and safety of aprepitant in combination with ondansetron and dexamethasone in preventing CINV among patients undergoing HEC for breast cancer. This trial confirms the added benefit of multicomponent antiemetic regimens in enhancing patient comfort and treatment adherence during chemotherapy, offering a robust approach to the management of emesis in oncological care.
