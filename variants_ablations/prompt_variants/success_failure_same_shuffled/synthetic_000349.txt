**Phase III Study of Plitidepsin in Combination With Dexamethasone and Carfilzomib in Patients With Relapsed and Refractory Multiple Myeloma**

**Study Title:** A Randomized, Multicenter, Open-label Phase III Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Plitidepsin in Combination With Dexamethasone and Carfilzomib vs. Dexamethasone and Carfilzomib Alone in Patients With Relapsed and Refractory Multiple Myeloma

**Purpose:** This study is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of plitidepsin when administered in conjunction with dexamethasone and carfilzomib compared to the control arm consisting of dexamethasone and carfilzomib alone, with primary outcomes measured in terms of progression-free survival (PFS) and secondary outcomes focusing on overall response rates (ORR), overall survival (OS), and safety profile.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Age ≥ 18 years.
2. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status (PS) ≤ 2.
3. Diagnosed with relapsed and/or refractory multiple myeloma after at least two prior therapeutic regimens, including a bortezomib-containing regimen.
4. Patients must possess measurable disease with defined serum monoclonal protein criteria.
5. Documented informed consent, with adherence to local ethical guidelines.
  
**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior treatment history with plitidepsin.
2. Known hypersensitivity to plitidepsin, dexamethasone, carfilzomib, or any excipients.
3. Congestive heart failure or significant cardiovascular disease not controlled by standard medical therapy.
4. Active systemic infections, primary malignancy other than multiple myeloma, or acute hepatitis B or C infection.
5. Patients requiring any prohibitive medications affecting study outcomes or safety.

**Study Design:** This multicenter, open-label, randomized phase III trial will consist of an estimated 300 participants stratified based on prior treatment experiences with bortezomib and lenalidomide. Subjects will be randomized to receive either plitidepsin (5 mg/m^2 via intravenous infusion over 3 hours) combined with dexamethasone and carfilzomib or a control arm of dexamethasone and carfilzomib alone in a 28-day cycle for up to 12 cycles.

**Objectives:**
- Primary: To determine the difference in PFS between the plitidepsin combination arm and the control arm.
- Secondary: To evaluate the ORR (including complete response (CR), partial response (PR), and minimal response (MR)), safety and tolerability adverse events (AEs), and exploratory pharmacokinetic (PK) assessments of plitidepsin and carfilzomib.

**Observations:** 
- The integration of plitidepsin into the regimen is hypothesized to enhance antitumor activity due to its distinct yet complementary action alongside dexamethasone and carfilzomib, targeting crucial survival pathways of myeloma cells. 
- The infusion regimen has been structured to allow for optimal pharmacokinetic profiles and mitigate severe toxicities, congruent with previously successful outcomes where sustained systemic exposure was linked to significant therapeutic benefits.

**Endpoints:** 
- The co-primary outcomes to be assessed are overall response rates (ORR) per the International Myeloma Working Group criteria and progression-free survival (PFS). Safety will be measured by the frequency and severity of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), hospitalization due to AEs, and quality of life assessments.

Correlation of treatment outcomes with quality of life measures, laboratory parameters, and imaging will further delve into the therapy's impact on patients' overall well-being and disease management. Comprehensive monitoring and rigorous clinical assessments will ensure efficacy and safety protocols uphold the integrity of the trial.

**Conclusion:** This study embarks on elucidating the potential synergistic effects of plitidepsin when integrated with established therapeutic agents, aiming to provide additional therapeutic options for a population characterized by refractory disease states, ultimately enhancing clinical care standards in multiple myeloma treatment regimens. The trial is a strategic step toward innovating treatment paradigms, reinforcing the positive outcomes observed in preceding studies of plitidepsin.
