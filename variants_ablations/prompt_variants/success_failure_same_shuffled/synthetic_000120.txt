**Phase II Clinical Trial of Metformin Adjunctive Therapy in Patients with Hormone Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer**

**Trial Title**: A Phase II, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Metformin as an Adjunctive Therapy in Patients with Hormone Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer Undergoing Standard Endocrine Therapy

**Background**: Hormone receptor-positive breast cancer constitutes a significant subset of breast malignancies, with a notable proportion of patients experiencing recurrence despite standard endocrine therapies. Emerging evidence suggests that metformin, primarily known as an antihyperglycemic agent, may exert anticancer effects through various mechanisms, including decreased cancer stem cell (CSC) populations and enhanced therapeutic responses to endocrine treatments. The overarching hypothesis is that metformin will improve recurrence-free survival in patients with hormone receptor-positive breast cancer receiving standard endocrine therapy by targeting CSCs and enhancing overall treatment efficacy.

**Study Design**: This multicenter, double-blind, randomized Phase II clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of metformin as an adjunct therapy to hormonal treatment in patients with early-stage hormone receptor-positive breast cancer.

**Primary Objective**: To evaluate the impact of metformin on recurrence-free survival (RFS) at 24 months compared to a placebo control group in patients receiving standard endocrine therapy.

**Secondary Objectives**: 
1. To assess the change in circulating tumor cell (CTC) counts and CSC markers pre- and post-treatment in both groups.
2. To determine the overall survival (OS) at 36 months, stratified by metformin-related changes in metabolic parameters.
3. To evaluate the tolerability and safety of metformin in patients without diabetes, including adverse event profiles.
4. To explore potential predictive biomarkers, particularly the relationship between metformin efficacy and the presence of specific genetic mutations (e.g., PIK3CA, PTEN) in tumor samples.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
1. Histologically confirmed hormone receptor-positive breast cancer (ER+/PR+) at an early stage (Stage I-III).
2. Scheduled to receive standard adjuvant endocrine therapy (tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors).
3. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
4. Age greater than 18 and less than 75 years.
5. Adequate renal function (serum creatinine <1.3 mg/dL) and liver function (AST/ALT <2.5 times the upper limit of normal).
6. Ability to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
1. History of diabetes mellitus or metformin use in the past 6 months.
2. Prior treatment with other investigational agents for breast cancer.
3. Current or planned simultaneous treatment with other chemotherapeutic agents.
4. Pregnancy or lactation.
5. Presence of active, uncontrolled medical conditions that could interfere with protocol adherence.

**Treatment Protocol**: Eligible participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either metformin (850 mg bid) or placebo, in conjunction with their standard endocrine therapy for a duration of 24 months. 

**Outcomes**: 

**Primary Endpoint**: 
- RFS at 24 months defined as the time from the start of treatment to the first occurrence of progressive disease or recurrence.

**Secondary Endpoints**: 
- Change in CTC count, as assessed using liquid biopsy analysis.
- Median overall survival (OS) determined from randomization until death from any cause.
- Assessment of adverse events through Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.
- Identification of biomarkers predictive of response through genetic profiling of tumor samples, focusing on mutations associated with treatment resistance.

**Statistical Analysis**: A two-sided significance level of 0.05 will be used. Survival curves will be generated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and comparisons performed using the log-rank test. 

**Conclusion**: This trial leverages a robust mechanistic understanding of metformin’s role as a potential anticancer agent in targeting CSC populations within hormone receptor-positive breast cancer. Supported by strong epidemiological evidence, the combination of metformin with endocrine therapy aims to enhance treatment outcomes while upholding patient safety through rigorous selection and monitoring protocols. The adaptation of this investigational treatment paradigm paves the way for future research endeavors, including potential Phase III trials, should the results indicate a meaningful benefit in recurrence-free survival.
