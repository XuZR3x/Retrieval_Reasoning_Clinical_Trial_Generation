**Phase II Study of Vandetanib in Patients with Nodal Metastatic Melanoma**

**Background:**
Melanoma, a malignant neoplasm of melanocytes, represents one of the most aggressive forms of skin cancer, particularly when associated with nodal metastasis. Despite recent advancements in targeted therapies and immunotherapy, the prognosis for patients with advanced disease remains dismal, necessitating exploration of novel therapeutic agents. Vandetanib, a multi-kinase inhibitor targeting vascular endothelial growth factor receptor-2 (VEGFR2), epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), and rearranged during transfection (RET), was investigated for its potential to reduce tumor burden in patients with metastatic melanoma.

**Objectives:**
- Primary: To evaluate the efficacy of vandetanib in producing a measurable clinical response (overall response rate) in patients with nodal metastatic melanoma, utilizing Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST v1.1).
- Secondary: To assess safety, tolerability, and adverse event profiles in the study population.

**Eligibility:**
- Adults aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed metastatic melanoma exhibiting nodal involvement.
- Patients must possess at least one measurable lesion as per RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- Adequate organ function defined by specified laboratory parameters (including renal, hepatic, and hematological functions).

**Design:**
This was a multicenter, open-label Phase II study. Eligible participants were administered oral vandetanib at a dose of 300 mg daily in 28-day cycles, until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Baseline imaging was conducted, followed by periodic assessments every eight weeks to monitor tumor size via computed tomography (CT) scans.

Participants underwent comprehensive evaluations for cardiotoxic events, specifically monitoring QTc interval prolongation, necessitating frequent electrocardiogram (ECG) assessments given the known risk profile associated with vandetanib usage.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed nodal metastatic melanoma.
- Measurable disease via RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- ECOG performance status 0-2.
- At least 4 weeks following any prior systemic therapy, immunotherapy, or surgical intervention.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with symptomatic or uncontrolled central nervous system metastasis.
- History of significant cardiac events, including myocardial infarction or sustained arrhythmia.
- Previously documented prolonged QTc intervals or concurrent medications with known cardiotoxic potential.

**Assessment Methods:**
- Clinical outcomes were evaluated primarily through radiographic response as determined by RECIST v1.1.
- Adverse events were continuously monitored according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0.

**Results:**
This trial failed to meet the primary endpoint of objective clinical response, with an overall response rate observed at only 5% among 40 enrolled patients. A notable incidence of adverse events, particularly dose-dependent cardiotoxicities including marked QT prolongation, led to premature withdrawal from the study in 30% of the patient cohort due to intolerable side effects.

Patient eligibility limitations further impacted recruitment numbers, revealing a significant rate of exclusion due to cardiac comorbidities and performance status criteria. The inherent pathophysiological variability among melanoma subtypes also contributed to the challenge of achieving target engagement, particularly within the resistant nodal metastatic population. Variations in tumor microenvironments highlighted the inadequate targeting of key signaling pathways that govern melanoma progression.

**Conclusion:**
The Phase II study of vandetanib in patients with nodal metastatic melanoma resulted in insufficient clinical efficacy lacking meaningful therapeutic response while underscoring significant safety concerns. The study's design and execution were adversely affected by inadequate patient population recruitment stemming from restrictive eligibility criteria and the heterogeneous nature of melanoma. The findings warrant further investigation into alternative therapeutic strategies that may enhance response rates in this difficult-to-treat population and consider optimizing patient selection to identify those more likely to benefit from targeted therapies.
