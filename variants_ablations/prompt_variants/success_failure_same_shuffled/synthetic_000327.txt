<clinical_trial>
    <title>A Phase II Study of Romidepsin in Patients With Relapsed or Refractory Hodgkin Lymphoma</title>
    <phase>II</phase>
    <intervention>Romidepsin</intervention>
    <objective>
        <primary>
            To evaluate the efficacy of romidepsin in achieving a complete or partial response in patients with relapsed or refractory Hodgkin Lymphoma.
        </primary>
        <secondary>
            To assess the safety profile and tolerability of romidepsin in the studied patient population, and to document overall survival rates and progression-free survival outcomes.
        </secondary>
    </objective>
    <background>
        Romidepsin, a potent histone deacetylase inhibitor (HDACi), demonstrates a theoretical mechanism for inhibiting tumor proliferation through the alteration of chromatin dynamics; however, previous clinical trials in diverse malignancies have shown significant limitations in efficacy, particularly regarding insufficient response rates, leading to trial failure. Notably, challenges include complexities of patient cohort selection, substantial Grade 3-4 toxicities, and an incomplete understanding of the biological mechanisms underpinning treatment resistance.
    </background>
    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Histologically confirmed Hodgkin lymphoma with relapsed or refractory disease following at least two lines of prior therapy.</criterion>
        <criterion>Measurable disease, defined as at least one lesion that can be accurately measured in one dimension ≥ 20 mm by conventional techniques or ≥ 10 mm by spiral CT scan.</criterion>
        <criterion>Aged ≥ 18 years with an ECOG performance status of 0-2.</criterion>
        <criterion>Patients must have adequate organ function: absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500 cells/uL, platelets ≥ 100,000 cells/uL, total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN, and AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN.</criterion>
        <criterion>Women of childbearing potential and men must agree to use adequate contraception during the study.</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Prior exposure to romidepsin or other HDAC inhibitors.</criterion>
        <criterion>History of cardiac arrhythmias or significant cardiovascular disease.</criterion>
        <criterion>Active or uncontrolled infections, including HIV positivity.</criterion>
        <criterion>History of severe allergic reactions to compounds with similar biological compositions as romidepsin.</criterion>
        <criterion>Concurrent malignancy other than adequately treated basal cell or squamous cell skin cancer.</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <treatment_outline>
        Patients will receive romidepsin administered as a 4-hour intravenous infusion on days 1, 8, and 15 of a 28-day cycle. Treatment will continue for a maximum of 6 cycles in the absence of disease progression or intolerable toxicity.
    </treatment_outline>
    <failure_analysis>
        <reason_1>
            Limited Efficacy: The study showed poor response rates, with only 15% of patients achieving either a complete or partial response, failing to meet the pre-defined threshold of 30% as clinically significant.
        </reason_1>
        <reason_2>
            Patient Cohort Limitations: The enrollment criteria were overly restrictive, selecting a patient population with advanced disease stages and previous extensive therapy histories, which may have influenced treatment responsiveness negatively.
        </reason_2>
        <reason_3>
            Associated Toxicity Concerns: The incidence of severe drug-related toxicities, including thrombocytopenia and severe fatigue, led to treatment discontinuation in 35% of subjects, impacting overall tolerability and long-term assessment of the intervention.
        </reason_3>
        <reason_4>
            Incomplete Mechanism Exploration: No comprehensive biomarker analysis was conducted, preventing identification of subgroups that may have better responded to romidepsin, and limiting the biological understandings necessary to refine clinical applications.
        </reason_4>
        <reason_5>
            Incomplete Treatment Duration: Due to early disease progression or adverse effects, only 40% of patients completed the planned treatment regimen, limiting the efficacy analysis and preventing adequate assessment of sustained therapeutic outcomes from romidepsin.
        </reason_5>
    </failure_analysis>
    <projected_accrual>Expected enrollment of approximately 60 patients across multiple sites over an estimated duration of 18 months.</projected_accrual>
    <followup>
        Patients will be followed post-treatment for a duration of 12 months, assessing overall survival, progression-free survival, and ongoing safety profiles.
    </followup>
</clinical_trial>
