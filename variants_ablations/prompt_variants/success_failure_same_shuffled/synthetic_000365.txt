**Failed Clinical Trial of Cabozantinib S-Malate:**

**Cabozantinib S-Malate for Patients with Advanced Colorectal Cancer***  
*An Open-Label Phase II Study of Cabozantinib S-Malate in Patients with Metastatic Colorectal Cancer Following Failure of First-Line Chemotherapy*  

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To evaluate the efficacy of cabozantinib s-malate in terms of progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer who have progressed on first-line chemotherapy, compared to historical controls.  

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To assess the overall survival (OS) rate.  
II. To measure the objective response rate (ORR) as per the RECIST criteria, defined as complete response (CR) + partial response (PR).  
III. To assess the safety profile of cabozantinib s-malate by evaluating adverse events using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).  
IV. To evaluate correlations between MET expression and clinical outcomes, including PFS and ORR.

**OUTLINE:**  
Patients will receive cabozantinib s-malate orally once daily on days 1-28. Treatment courses will repeat every 28 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Patients will be followed for response evaluation every 6 weeks.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Histologically confirmed metastatic colorectal cancer 
- Documented disease progression after at least one line of standard therapy (e.g., fluorouracil-based regimen) 
- Measurable disease defined by RECIST criteria, with at least one measurable lesion 
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1  
- Adequate hematologic, liver, and renal function, as indicated by laboratory tests conducted within 14 days prior to enrollment 

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Prior treatment with cabozantinib or any other MET/VEGF-targeted therapy 
- Concurrent malignancies with a treatment priority higher than this trial 
- Uncontrolled hypertension (systolic BP > 140 mmHg or diastolic BP > 90 mmHg) 
- Significant cardiovascular events within the previous 12 months, such as myocardial infarction or congestive heart failure (NYHA class III or IV)  
- History of gastrointestinal perforation or hemorrhage 
- Pregnancy or non-acceptable contraception practices in females of childbearing potential

**PRIMARY ENDPOINT:**  
- The proportion of patients without disease progression at 6 months.

**Rationale for Trial Failure:**  

1. **Inconsistent Efficacy:** The trial demonstrated that cabozantinib s-malate did not significantly improve PFS as compared to historical controls treated with standard regimens, such as FOLFOX or FOLFIRI. The pre-specified PFS threshold of over 30% at 6 months was not achieved, leading to a conclusion of inadequate efficacy.

2. **Suboptimal Dose Optimization:** A preliminary dose-finding study revealed a narrow therapeutic window with significant dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs), including hypertension and liver function abnormalities, negatively affecting patient adherence to the treatment protocol and overall efficacy.

3. **Adverse Toxicity Profile:** Results highlighted a concerning toxicity burden, with approximately 60% of patients experiencing grade 3 or higher AEs, including gastrointestinal bleeding and elevated hepatic enzymes. This high incidence of adverse events led to frequent treatment discontinuations, counteracting any potential therapeutic benefit of cabozantinib s-malate.

4. **Limited Target Expression Correlation:** The anticipated correlation between MET expression levels and treatment efficacy was not observed, undermining the selection criteria based on MET as a therapeutic target. As a result, none of the patient subgroups exhibited meaningful response rates.

5. **Competing Treatment Standards:** The trial faced recruitment challenges due to the existence of standard treatment options with proven efficacy. Most patients opted to pursue established therapies over participation in trial protocols, substantially impacting patient enrollment and ultimately contributing to unsuccessful trial outcomes. 

**FOLLOW-UP:**  
Patients will be followed up every 3 months for progression evaluation for up to 2 years post-treatment initiation. The trial will conclude with a comprehensive analysis of data to ascertain findings relevant to future investigations involving cabozantinib s-malate in treating colorectal malignancies.
