**Successful Clinical Trial of Cilostazol: Cilostazol in the Management of Peripheral Artery Disease**

**The Double-Blind, Randomized, Multi-Center, and Active-Controlled Trial for Efficacy and Safety of Cilostazol in Patients with Peripheral Artery Disease**

**Purpose of the Study:** This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of cilostazol in improving functional outcomes in patients with intermittent claudication associated with peripheral artery disease (PAD). Medical interventions in PAD primarily aim at relieving symptoms and preventing disease progression. Current therapies, such as cilostazol, possess unique pharmacological properties that may yield improved clinical results when compared to conventional treatments.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients aged 40 to 85 years.
- Diagnosed with intermittent claudication secondary to PAD confirmed via duplex ultrasound.
- Ankle-brachial index (ABI) measurement of <0.90.
- Patients providing informed consent after receiving a full explanation of the study.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Presence of critical limb ischemia or history of limb-threatening ischemic ulcers.
- Concurrent use of other antiplatelet agents within the past month.
- History of major bleeding within the last 6 months.
- Pregnant or lactating women.
- Uncontrolled hypertension (SBP > 180 mmHg or DBP > 110 mmHg).
- Renal impairment defined as creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL or significant hepatic dysfunction.

**Design and Methodology:** This trial employs a double-blind, randomized, parallel-group design to minimize biases and ensure validity. The inclusion of specific patient populations allows for the identification of individuals who are most likely to benefit from cilostazol treatment as supported by prior successful outcomes in targeted cohorts. Participants will undergo randomization to receive either cilostazol or placebo for a duration of 6 months, with further stratification based on baseline functional capacity assessed via the 6-minute walk test.

**Primary and Secondary Endpoints:**
- Primary Endpoint: Improvement in walking distance assessed by the 6-minute walk test at 6 months.
- Secondary Endpoints: Changes in quality of life measured by the Walking Impairment Questionnaire (WIQ), frequency of adverse events, and assessment of ABI pre- and post-treatment.

**Safety and Tolerability:** A comprehensive safety evaluation will be integrated into the study design, highlighting the importance of monitoring adverse effects throughout the trial. The favorable risk-benefit profile of cilostazol, previously documented in other studies, will be used to underscore its potential utility as a safe therapeutic option among individuals with PAD.

**Data Analysis:** Statistical analyses will be performed using intention-to-treat principles. Differences between treatment groups will be calculated using appropriate tests to determine the significance of outcomes. This methodological rigor will facilitate an accurate understanding of cilostazol's therapeutic efficacy in PAD management.

**Conclusion:** The proposed trial is designed to evaluate cilostazol in a well-defined population presenting with peripheral artery disease. Rigorous trial design, meticulous endpoint definitions, and comprehensive safety assessments aim to underlie a potential breakthrough in the therapeutic landscape. Results from this study may substantiate cilostazol as a first-line pharmacotherapy for improving functional capabilities and enhancing the quality of life in patients afflicted with PAD, paralleling the success observed in previous cilostazol trials.
