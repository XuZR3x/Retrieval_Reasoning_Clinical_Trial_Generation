**Failed Clinical Trial of Candesartan Cilexetil: Candesartan in Acute Hypertensive Crisis Trial (CAHCT)**

**Candesartan in Acute Hypertensive Crisis Trial**  
The purpose of this trial is to evaluate the efficacy of candesartan cilexetil (an angiotensin II receptor blocker) in the management of patients presenting with an acute hypertensive crisis and assessment of organ function. 

**Hypothesis:**  
AT1 receptor blockade with candesartan in patients with acute hypertensive crisis will:  
1. Reduce the incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI) at 72 hours post-initiation of treatment compared to placebo.  
2. Lead to a significant decrease in systolic blood pressure within 24 hours of intervention, reducing the overall need for additional antihypertensive medications.

There remains substantial debate regarding the promptness and degree of blood pressure reduction during acute hypertensive crises. Observational data suggest that overly aggressive control may precipitate adverse neurological outcomes. Existing studies have shown mixed results, and the literature lacks a sufficiently powered randomized controlled trial assessing the efficacy of candesartan specifically within this population. The CAHCT was thus initiated to clarify these uncertainties, with a target recruitment of 1,800 participants.

Patients aged 18 years or older presenting with an acute hypertensive episode (systolic blood pressure ≥ 180 mmHg) will be randomly assigned to receive either candesartan cilexetil (16 to 32 mg, orally administrated) or matching placebo for 48 hours. Follow-up assessments will occur at 72 hours and one month after initial treatment onset.

The trial is being coordinated from the Medical University of Ghent, Belgium. Over 80 centers around Belgium, Netherlands, and Germany have pledged their collaboration. The financial contributors include the Belgian Health Ministry, AstraZeneca, and the Medical University of Ghent. AstraZeneca will provide candesartan cilexetil and placebo for the duration of the trial.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adults aged ≥18 years  
- Documented acute hypertensive crisis with systolic blood pressure ≥180 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥120 mmHg  
- Ability to provide informed consent (directly or from a legal representative)  
- No preexisting renal insufficiency (serum creatinine < 150 µmol/L)  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Markedly reduced consciousness or altered mental status (defined by Glasgow Coma Scale score < 14)  
- Pre-existing stage IV-V chronic kidney disease (CKD)  
- Known intolerances or contraindications to angiotensin II receptor blockers  
- Recent history of cerebrovascular accident (within 3 months)  
- Evidence of a secondary cause of hypertension (e.g., pheochromocytoma, renovascular disease)  
- Pregnancy or lactation  
- Any serious concomitant illness that would interfere with trial participation  

**Primary Endpoints:**  
1. Incidence of acute kidney injury at 72 hours post-administration, defined by a change in serum creatinine levels (≥ 50% from baseline).  
2. Change in systolic blood pressure at 24 hours post-intervention.  

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
- Need for rescue antihypertensive therapy within the initial 48 hours.
- Composite outcomes including all-cause mortality, myocardial infarction, or further cerebrovascular events over a 6-month follow-up.

**Rationale for Failure:**  
The CAHCT encountered several factors leading to its inadequate effectiveness:

1. **Inadequate Population Selection:** The exclusion criteria limited recruitment from a broader spectrum of hypertensive crisis presentations. Patients with altered mental status or severe comorbidities, frequently encountered in real-world settings, were omitted.

2. **Lack of Therapeutic Window:** The controversy surrounding the optimal timing for initiating antihypertensive treatment may have limited the opportunity for candesartan to exert its intended effects, especially given the focus on rapid control of blood pressure within the first hours.

3. **Underpowered Study Design:** Initial power analyses were inadequate for the high-complexity outcomes expected from heterogenous patient responses, resulting in observed events being insufficient to achieve statistical significance for primary endpoints.

4. **Biochemical Limitations:** The underlying physiology of patients presenting with acute hypertensive crises indicated variations in vascular response, potentially diminishing the hypothesized neuroprotective benefits typically attributed to candesartan.

5. **Variability in Treatment Adherence:** The open-label design of the study encountered challenges in maintaining consistency of patient adherence, as evidenced by variable changes in the systolic blood pressure response, leading to diluted results.

Ultimately, the CAHCT failed to demonstrate the anticipated benefits of candesartan in the acute treatment of hypertensive crises, underscoring the need for further investigation into its role within varied patient demographics and clinical scenarios.
