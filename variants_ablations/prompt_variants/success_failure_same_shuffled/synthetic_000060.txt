<!-- Successful Clinical Trial of Interferon Beta-1a: An Evaluation of Interferon Beta-1a Versus Placebo in Patients with Active Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis - A Longitudinal Study --> 
<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>A Multi-Center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Interferon Beta-1a in Patients with Active Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis</Title>
    <Objective>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of interferon beta-1a compared to placebo in reducing the frequency of relapses in patients with active relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis (MS) over a treatment period of 48 weeks.</Objective>
    <StudyDesign>This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Patients will be randomly assigned to receive either interferon beta-1a (Rebif) 44 mcg subcutaneously three times weekly or matching placebo for a total of 48 weeks. 
    Assessments of clinical outcomes will include relapse rate, Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) scores, and quality of life indices as defined by the MS Quality of Life 54 (MSQOL-54).</StudyDesign>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Diagnosis of relapsing multiple sclerosis, based on the revised McDonald criteria (2010)</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Documented evidence of at least 2 relapses in the preceding 2 years or 1 relapse within the last 12 months</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Neurological stability for at least 30 days prior to screening</Criterion>
        <Criterion>EDSS score between 0 and 5.5 inclusive</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Age between 18 to 60 years</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>History of progressive forms of multiple sclerosis</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Current pregnancy or lactation</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Significant cardiovascular, hepatic, or renal diseases that would preclude the safe use of interferon beta-1a</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Known immunodeficiency disorders or active infections</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Recent treatment with systemic corticosteroids or immunomodulatory therapies within the last 3 months</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <OutcomeMeasures>
        <PrimaryOutcome>Annualized relapse rate (ARR) at 48 weeks, defined as the total number of relapses for all participants in the interferon beta-1a group divided by the total participant-years of exposure to that treatment</PrimaryOutcome>
        <SecondaryOutcome>Changes in EDSS scores from baseline to week 48</SecondaryOutcome>
        <SecondaryOutcome>Correlation between MSQOL-54 scores and treatment response at weeks 12, 24, and 48</SecondaryOutcome>
    </OutcomeMeasures>
    <StatisticalAnalysis>A two-sided significance level of 0.05 will be used. Continuous variables will be analyzed using appropriate parametric or non-parametric tests, while categorical variables will be assessed using Chi-square or Fisher's exact tests as appropriate. The primary endpoint (ARR) will be compared using Poisson regression models, adjusting for baseline characteristics.</StatisticalAnalysis>
    <StudyDuration>The study will span a total duration of 48 weeks for double-blind treatment, followed by an optional open-label extension phase for an additional 48 weeks for all participants who complete the initial treatment.</StudyDuration>
    <ResultsExpected>It is anticipated that treatment with interferon beta-1a will demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in the ARR compared to placebo, thus reinforcing the established efficacy of interferon beta-1a in managing relapsing forms of MS, as supported by previous trials showing favorable outcomes regarding relapse rates and quality of life improvements. The use of stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria, validated outcome measures, and a robust longitudinal study design are expected to provide comprehensive insights into the efficacy and safety of the treatment.</ResultsExpected>
</ClinicalTrial>
