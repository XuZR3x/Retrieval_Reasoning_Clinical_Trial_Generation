**Successful Clinical Trial of Perampanel: A Randomized Multicenter Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Perampanel in Patients with Refractory Focal Seizures**

**Title: A Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Multicenter Study to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of Perampanel as Adjunctive Therapy in Patients with Refractory Focal Seizures**

**Summary:** The primary objective of this clinical trial is to determine the efficacy and safety of perampanel when administered as an adjunctive therapy in adult and adolescent patients with refractory focal seizures. The study features a robust double-blind, placebo-controlled design with a predefined open-label phase, ensuring minimal bias and comprehensive assessment of treatment outcomes.

**Inclusion Criteria:**

1. Males and females aged 12 years and older, capable of providing informed consent or having a legal guardian consent on their behalf.
2. Confirmed clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy with refractory seizures, defined as inadequate seizure control despite treatment with at least two other antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) in the previous 2 years.
3. Seizure frequency of at least 4 focal seizures per month over the last 3 months, confirmed by a patient-reported seizure diary as verified by electronic diary or investigator review.
4. Participants should have undergone neuroimaging (CT or MRI) within the preceding 10 years that excluded significant structural abnormalities.
5. Patients must be on stable doses of a maximum of three concomitant AEDs, with only one potential inducer AED permitted (e.g., carbamazepine) for 30 days prior to baseline.

**Exclusion Criteria:**

1. History of non-focal seizures or any other generalized seizure types, including absence or myoclonic seizures.
2. Presence of progressive neurological disorders or significant comorbid conditions that may interfere with study conduct or safety.
3. Documented history of status epilepticus within the previous year or seizure clustering that precludes accurate seizure counting.
4. Pregnant or lactating females, or those not using adequate contraceptive measures.
5. Participation in another investigational drug trial within the last 30 days prior to screening.

**Study Design:** 

The study comprises a screening phase of up to 4 weeks, a 6-week prerandomization phase, and a 24-week double-blind treatment phase followed by a 52-week open-label extension phase. During the double-blind phase, eligible participants are randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either perampanel or placebo, with dose titration occurring over the initial 8 weeks, followed by a maintenance phase of 16 weeks.

**Objectives:**

- Primary Objective: To evaluate the change from baseline in the frequency of focal seizures, reported as the percent change in seizure frequency per 28 days, during the double-blind phase.
- Secondary Objectives: To assess the safety profile of perampanel, evaluate responder rates (≥50% reduction in seizure frequency), and evaluate the quality of life measures using validated scales.

**Endpoints:**

The primary endpoint assessment consists of the change in seizure frequency from baseline to the end of the 24-week double-blind phase, based on patient-recorded data from seizure diaries. Secondary endpoints include safety assessments through adverse event reporting and laboratory tests, alongside subjective quality-of-life evaluations.

**Statistical Analysis:** 

Statistical analysis will involve the use of descriptive statistics for baseline characteristics and inferential statistics to compare treatment groups with respect to the change in seizure frequency. An intention-to-treat analysis will be conducted to account for participant dropouts.

**Conclusion:** 

This clinical trial aims to provide comprehensive evidence supporting the efficacy and safety of perampanel as a crucial adjunctive therapy in patients suffering from refractory focal seizures. The rigorous methodology implemented—comprising stringent participant selection criteria, a robust double-blind design, standardized measurement protocols, and adequate assessment of long-term outcomes—will strengthen the reliability of trial findings.
