**Failed Clinical Trial of Cabozantinib S-Malate: A Multi-Center Phase II Study Evaluating Cabozantinib S-Malate in Combination With Standard Radiation Therapy in Patients With Locally Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**SYNOPSIS**: This phase II, open-label, multi-center study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of cabozantinib S-malate administered concurrently with standard radiation therapy in patients with unresectable locally advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The primary objective was to determine progression-free survival (PFS) at the six-month mark, while secondary objectives included overall survival (OS), response rates, and adverse event profiles.

**RATIONALE**: Previous studies of cabozantinib S-malate have suggested potential benefits in inhibiting tumor growth via c-Met inhibition. However, historical analyses revealed substantial reasons contributing to the failures associated with its efficacy and safety profiles (see below).

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES**:  
I. To assess the progression-free survival rate at 6 months in patients receiving cabozantinib S-malate plus radiation therapy compared to standard care radiation therapy alone.  
II. To evaluate response rates assessed via Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) after 3 and 6 months of treatment.  

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES**:  
I. To determine overall survival rates at 12 months.  
II. To characterize and report the toxicity profile of cabozantinib S-malate combined with radiation.  
III. To analyze pharmacokinetic parameters of cabozantinib S-malate in the context of combined therapy.

**OUTLINE**: Participants were randomized into two arms:  
- **Arm I**: Patients received cabozantinib S-malate orally once daily during radiation therapy sessions.  
- **Arm II** (Control): Patients received standard radiation therapy alone.  

Participants were monitored at baseline, week 4, and every 8 weeks thereafter until disease progression or intolerable toxicity. Follow-up continued for up to two years.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA**:  
1. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of locally advanced NSCLC, not amenable to surgical resection.  
2. Measurable disease as determined by CT imaging.  
3. Estimated life expectancy of greater than 12 weeks.  
4. Performance status: Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) 0-2.  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA**:  
1. Prior use of cabozantinib S-malate or other c-Met inhibitors.  
2. Uncontrolled hypertension or significant cardiovascular events within six months.  
3. Any history of significant adverse reactions or hypersensitivity to the study drug.  
4. Active autoimmune diseases or significant neurologic disorders.  
5. Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**RESULTS**:  
The trial was terminated early due to insufficient efficacy profiles and concerning adverse event rates. **Key findings included**:

1. **Insufficient Efficacy Profiles**: The primary endpoint, PFS at 6 months, demonstrated lack of improvement with cabozantinib S-malate combined with radiation. PFS was not statistically significant when compared to the control arm (p=0.65).  
2. **Adverse Event Profiles**: A marked increase in adverse events, including grade 3-4 toxicities such as hypertension, gastrointestinal bleeding, and severe fatigue, was reported. The combined therapy group experienced over a 30% rate of treatment discontinuation due to adverse effects, compared to less than 5% in the control arm.  
3. **Pharmacokinetic Variability**: The variability in cabozantinib S-malate pharmacokinetics was significant, with higher plasma concentrations leading to augmented toxicity without a corresponding therapeutic gain.
4. **Compromised Patient Cohorts**: High rates of prior therapies, notably chemotherapy and radiation, limited the effective therapeutic window for cabozantinib S-malate in this cohort, resulting in diminished efficacy against an already complex cancer landscape.
5. **Biomarker Limitations**: Analysis of c-MET expression revealed no significant correlation with outcomes, suggesting that the expected molecular target engagement did not yield the hypothesized clinical benefit.

**CONCLUSION**: Due to the challenges associated with efficacy, tolerability, and significant toxicity, this clinical trial illustrates the limitations of cabozantinib S-malate when combined with radiation therapy in treating locally advanced NSCLC. These findings further underscore the need for rigorous evaluation of pharmacodynamic and safety parameters, as well as careful consideration of patient selection criteria, in future studies involving this agent.
