**Failed Clinical Trial of Insulin Degludec: A Multicenter, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial Investigating the Efficacy and Safety of Insulin Degludec in Insulin-Naïve Patients With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus**  

**Study Protocol Identifier**: NCTXXXXXXX  
**Date of Last Update**: October 2023  
**Study Completion Date**: July 2023  

**Objectives**:  
The primary objective of this clinical trial was to evaluate the efficacy of insulin degludec relative to placebo in achieving a statistically significant reduction in glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c) across a 24-week treatment duration in insulin-naïve patients diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus. A secondary objective included the assessment of safety parameters, primarily the incidence of hypoglycemia and other adverse events attributable to treatment.

**Study Design**:  
This was a 24-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial conducted across multiple sites in North America, Europe, and Asia. A total of 800 participants aged 18–70 years with a diagnosis of type 2 diabetes for at least 12 months were enrolled. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either insulin degludec (10 dU/mL) nightly or placebo.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
- Male or female subjects aged 18 to 70 years.  
- Clinically diagnosed type 2 diabetes mellitus for a minimum of 12 months.  
- HbA1c level of 7.0% to 10.0% (both inclusive) at screening.
- Insulin-naïve patients (previous short-term use of insulin during hospitalization for up to 14 days was permitted).  
- Body Mass Index (BMI) ≤ 40.0 kg/m².  
- Current treatment with metformin with a stable dose ≥ 1500 mg/day for at least 3 months prior to screening.  

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
- Previous use of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists, thiazolidinediones, or any other anti-diabetic agents, excluding metformin, in the last 3 months prior to the study.  
- Recent history (within the last 6 months) of cardiovascular events (e.g., myocardial infarction, stroke).  
- Uncontrolled hypertension with systolic blood pressure ≥ 180 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 100 mmHg at screening.  
- Any prior history of severe hypoglycemic events.

**Endpoints**:  
- Primary Endpoint: Change in HbA1c from baseline to week 24.  
- Secondary Endpoints: Incidence of adverse events, changes in body weight, bolus insulin requirements, and patient-reported outcomes on quality of life and treatment satisfaction.

**Results**:  
The primary endpoint demonstrated no statistically significant difference in HbA1c reduction when comparing insulin degludec to placebo (mean change from baseline: −0.5% vs. −0.4%; p = 0.63). The absence of superior efficacy relative to placebo raises significant questions regarding the therapeutic benefit of insulin degludec in this patient demographic.

Furthermore, an increased incidence of hypoglycemic events was noted in the insulin degludec group (22% vs. 13% in the placebo group; p = 0.04), leading to concerns about the safety profile of the intervention, potentially impacting participant adherence and altering primary outcomes.

**Conclusion**:  
This study faced critical challenges, including insufficient efficacy relative to the placebo comparator, which hindered insulin degludec's establishment as an effective treatment for type 2 diabetes. The increased incidence of adverse events, primarily hypoglycemia, and variability in patient compliance undermined the robustness of the results. The pharmacokinetic profile of insulin degludec may not align well with patients' needs across diverse geographical populations, leading to diminished overall treatment success and generalizability of findings. Subsequent trials may benefit from a different study design focusing on specific subpopulations within diabetic patients, thereby enhancing the potential for clearer outcomes regarding insulin degludec's efficacy and safety.
