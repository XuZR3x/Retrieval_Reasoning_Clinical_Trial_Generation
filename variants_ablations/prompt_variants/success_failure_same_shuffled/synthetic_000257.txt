<clinical_trial>
    <title>Evaluation of Candesartan Cilexetil in the Treatment of Hypertensive Patients with Mild to Moderate Chronic Kidney Disease: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Multi-Center Trial</title>
    <objective>
        The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of candesartan cilexetil compared to placebo in reducing urinary protein excretion rates in hypertensive patients diagnosed with mild to moderate chronic kidney disease (CKD). Secondary objectives include assessing the change in estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) and the incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI) during the treatment period.
    </objective>
    <design>
        This trial will follow a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design over a duration of 24 weeks across multiple centers specializing in nephrology. Participants will be assigned to receive either candesartan cilexetil (starting dose of 8 mg, titrated to 16 mg based on tolerability) or an identical placebo, administered once daily.
    </design>
    <inclusion_criteria>
        - Adults aged 40-75 years.
        - Diagnosed with essential hypertension (SBP ≥ 140 mmHg or DBP ≥ 90 mmHg).
        - Diagnosed with mild to moderate CKD (Stage 1 or 2, eGFR 45-90 mL/min/1.73 m²) and displayed a baseline urinary protein/creatinine ratio between 300 mg/g and 2000 mg/g.
        - Ability to provide informed consent.
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
        - Current or previous treatment with any Angiotensin II receptor antagonists or ACE inhibitors.
        - Known severe renal impairment (eGFR < 45 mL/min/1.73 m²) or serum potassium > 5.5 mmol/L at baseline.
        - History of hypersensitivity to candesartan or related compounds.
        - Presence of secondary forms of hypertension (e.g., renal artery stenosis).
        - Significant comorbidities (e.g., NYHA Class III or IV heart failure, malignancy) that may interfere with trial assessments.
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <primary_endpoint>
        The primary endpoint will be the change in urinary protein/creatinine ratio from baseline to week 24, analyzed using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) to assess the effect of treatment while adjusting for baseline values.
    </primary_endpoint>
    <secondary_endpoints>
        - Change in eGFR measured at baseline and at week 24.
        - Incidence of AKI, defined as an increase in serum creatinine of ≥ 0.3 mg/dL from baseline.
    </secondary_endpoints>
    <rationale>
        Previous studies have indicated that candesartan cilexetil may not effectively target renal protection in specific populations due to pharmacogenomic variations that affect its metabolism, potentially limiting the efficacy observed in polypharmacy scenarios or in patients exhibiting concomitant diseases. The strict inclusion and exclusion criteria will result in a more homogeneous population; however, this could hinder the external validity given the limited diversity. Moreover, a lack of a statistically significant result in prior trials assessing renal outcomes and hypertensive conditions may further influence the potential for success in this particular study.
    </rationale>
    <anticipated_outcomes>
        We hypothesize that participants in the candesartan cilexetil group will demonstrate a significant reduction in urinary protein excretion compared to the placebo group, aligning with the potential protective benefits of angiotensin II receptor blockers observed in different populations. However, we remain cautious, reflecting on historical efficacy challenges and the need for nuanced interpretations of genetic variability and concurrent pathologies that may obscure outcomes.
    </anticipated_outcomes>
</clinical_trial>
