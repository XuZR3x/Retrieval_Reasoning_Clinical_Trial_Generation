**Successful Clinical Trial of Aldesleukin: Combination Immunotherapy in Patients With Metastatic Melanoma**

**Phase II Study of Aldesleukin in Combination With Nivolumab in Patients With Metastatic Melanoma**

**RATIONALE:** Metastatic melanoma remains a significant clinical challenge, characterized by poor prognosis and resistance to conventional therapies. The combination of immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as nivolumab, which is an anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody, with aldesleukin, an immunomodulatory cytokine that enhances T cell activation and proliferation, has demonstrated promise in improving antitumor responses and patient outcomes. The rationale for this study is based on the hypothesis that the combination of aldesleukin and nivolumab can achieve enhanced synergistic effects in stimulating immune-mediated tumor cell death.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aims to evaluate the safety, efficacy, and tolerability of aldesleukin in combination with nivolumab in treating patients with metastatic melanoma, focusing on determining overall survival and progression-free survival rates compared to historical controls.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** To assess the overall response rate (ORR) and progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with metastatic melanoma receiving aldesleukin in combination with nivolumab.
- **Secondary:** 
  - To evaluate overall survival (OS) compared to historical data of patients treated with nivolumab alone.
  - To characterize the safety profile and incidence of adverse events associated with combination therapy, particularly focusing on immune-related adverse events.
  - To identify pharmacodynamic biomarkers that correlate with clinical response, including T cell activation profiles and circulating cytokine levels.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, open-label study will involve patients stratified by baseline disease staging (AJCC 7th edition) and prior treatments. Patients will receive aldesleukin intravenously on days 1, 3, and 5 of each 28-day cycle, and nivolumab will be administered intravenously on days 1 and 15 of each cycle. Treatment will continue for up to 12 cycles, followed by follow-up for survival assessments every 3 months for 2 years.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 80 patients will be enrolled in this study.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed metastatic melanoma with measurable disease.
- No prior systemic anti-cancer therapy for metastatic melanoma.
- No history of autoimmune disorders or active infections requiring therapy.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- ECOG performance status 0-2.
- Adequate organ function as indicated by labs (absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³, AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN, and creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min).
- No prior history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to recombinant human IL-2 or nivolumab.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- Patients must not have received any therapy targeting PD-1 or CTLA-4 prior to entering the study.
- Systemic corticosteroid use is prohibited within 14 days prior to study enrollment, except for low-dose use for non-autoimmune hosts.

**METHODOLOGY:**
Comprehensive assessments including clinical evaluations, imaging studies, and laboratory tests will be performed at baseline, before each treatment cycle, and during follow-up visits to monitor response and adverse events. The correlation of immune biomarker profiling with clinical outcomes will provide insights into the mechanisms of activity for the combination therapy.

By harnessing the synergistic effects of aldesleukin's cytokine-mediated immune activation and nivolumab's checkpoint blockade, this study aims to provide a novel therapeutic option for patients with metastatic melanoma, potentially increasing survival rates and improving quality of life for this challenging patient population.
