**Title**: Efficacy and Tolerability of Olanzapine in the Treatment of Residual Symptoms in Bipolar I Disorder Following Stabilization: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Multi-Center Study

**Abstract**: This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and tolerability of olanzapine in patients diagnosed with Bipolar I Disorder who exhibit residual symptoms after a period of stabilization. The study proposes that olanzapine facilitates significant improvement in mood stabilization compared to placebo, particularly in patients exhibiting persistent depressive or anxious symptoms post-stabilization phase. 

**Objectives**: To assess the change in the severity of residual symptoms in Bipolar I disorder patients after treatment with olanzapine versus placebo, and to determine the safety profile of the drug throughout the treatment duration.

**Primary Outcome Measure**: The primary outcome will be measured using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS) and Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale (GAD-7) to quantify the reduction in residual depressive and anxiety symptoms.

**Secondary Outcome Measures**: Secondary outcomes include overall functionality evaluated through the World Health Organization Disability Assessment Schedule (WHODAS 2.0), as well as adverse events recorded using the Systematic Assessment for Treatment Emergent Effects (SAT-E).

**Design**: This is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted across multiple centers. 

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Male or female patients aged 20-65 years.
- Diagnosed with Bipolar I Disorder, with a history of at least two mood episodes, including one manic episode.
- Currently stable (defined as no significant mood episode for the past 4 weeks), yet experiencing residual depressive or anxiety symptoms as evidenced by HDRS and GAD-7 scores higher than baseline pre-treatment values.
- Appropriate women must provide a negative pregnancy test and utilize adequate contraception.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- History or current diagnosis of schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder.
- Significant medical conditions such as diabetes, cardiovascular disorders, or any major organ dysfunction.
- Prior treatment with olanzapine or any depot antipsychotic in the last 3 months.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Randomization and Blinding**: Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the olanzapine group (5-20 mg daily, flexible dosing based on tolerability) or the placebo group.   

**Sample Size**: A total of 300 participants will be enrolled across various sites, allowing for sufficient statistical power to detect clinical significance in primary and secondary outcome measures.

**Statistical Analysis**: Efficacy analyses will employ repeated measures ANOVA to evaluate changes in HDRS and GAD-7 scores over time while accounting for baseline characteristics. Continuous data will be presented as mean (SD), and categorical data as n (%).

**Conclusion**: This study aims to demonstrate the efficacy and safety of olanzapine as an intervention for patients experiencing residual symptoms in Bipolar I Disorder. This is expected to further reinforce evidence-based practices in treating chronic mood disorders, addressing the therapeutic scope of olanzapine beyond management of acute episodes, and integrating the value of longitudinal treatment strategies designed to enhance patient quality of life.
