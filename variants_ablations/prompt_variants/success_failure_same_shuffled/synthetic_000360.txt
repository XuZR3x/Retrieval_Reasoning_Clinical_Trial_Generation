**Clinical Trial Report: A Phase 2 Study of Brentuximab Vedotin in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Therapy for CD30-Positive Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Trial ID:** BV-NSCLC-002

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm Phase 2 clinical trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of brentuximab vedotin in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors (pembrolizumab) in patients with CD30-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The study aims to demonstrate the overall response rate (ORR) and assess the duration of response, progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) in this population.

**Background:** Brentuximab vedotin is an antibody-drug conjugate targeting CD30, an established therapeutic target in various malignancies, including lymphoproliferative disorders. The integration of brentuximab vedotin with immune checkpoint inhibitors is hypothesized to enhance anti-tumor immune responses and improve patient outcomes, utilizing distinct yet complementary mechanisms of action.

**Primary Objectives:**
1. To evaluate the ORR of brentuximab vedotin in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with CD30-positive NSCLC.
2. To assess the safety and tolerability of the combination treatment as per the National Cancer Institute's Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

**Secondary Objectives:**
1. To measure the duration of response (DoR) in patients achieving a confirmed objective response.
2. To estimate progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) of the study population.
3. To explore potential predictive biomarkers of response, including changes in serum TARC levels and CD30 tumor expression pre-and post-treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer with CD30-positive expression as assessed by immunohistochemistry.
- Patients aged 18 years and older.
- Measurable disease according to RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function defined by standard laboratory values.
- Capable of providing informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior exposure to brentuximab vedotin or immune checkpoint inhibitors.
- Known active central nervous system (CNS) metastases.
- Uncontrolled intercurrent illness including uncontrolled infection, psychiatric conditions, or significant cardiovascular, renal, or hepatic impairment.
- History of hypersensitivity to any component of the study drugs.

**Study Intervention:**
Patients will receive brentuximab vedotin at a dose of 1.8 mg/kg administered intravenously every three weeks, combined with pembrolizumab at a dose of 200 mg administered intravenously every three weeks. Treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or completion of up to 24 months of therapy.

**Endpoints:**
- The primary endpoint is the ORR assessed by an independent radiologic review based on RECIST.
- Secondary endpoints include DoR, PFS, OS, and the identification of biomarkers relevant to treatment response via detailed genomic and proteomic analysis.

**Study Outcome and Significance:**
Anticipated findings from this trial may provide pivotal insights into the role of targeted therapies in conjunction with immunotherapy in the treatment landscape of CD30-positive NSCLC, potentially guiding future treatment paradigms through defined pathways. The study is powered based on the successful outcomes evidenced in prior clinical trials employing brentuximab vedotin, contributing to the rationale for this combination strategy.

**Conclusion:**
This Phase 2 study, by leveraging the mechanisms of action of both brentuximab vedotin and immune checkpoint inhibitors, aims to enhance therapeutic outcomes in a novel patient population, aligning with previous findings showcasing favorable response rates in malignancies with CD30 expression. The structured approach to patient selection and response assessment is geared toward achieving meaningful clinical results and further validating the hyperefficacy hypothesis when combining targeted and immunotherapeutic strategies.
