<clinicalTrial>
    <title>Phase II Study of Perifosine and Anti-Programmed Cell Death Protein 1 (Anti-PD-1) Therapy in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)</title>
    <background>The aim of this study is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of perifosine in combination with anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibodies in patients diagnosed with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Preclinical data have indicated that perifosine may enhance anti-tumor immune responses when combined with checkpoint inhibitors, potentially augmenting their clinical efficacy.</background>
    
    <design>This is a multi-center, open-label, single-arm, Phase II trial designed to enroll 100 patients with histologically confirmed, unresectable stage III or IV NSCLC who have not received prior systemic therapy. The study will assess overall response rates (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and safety profiles associated with the combination treatment.</design>
    
    <treatmentRegimen>Eligible patients will receive oral perifosine at a dose of 100 mg daily, administered concomitantly with anti-PD-1 therapy, typically delivered every 2 or 3 weeks according to the prescribing information. Treatment will continue for a maximum of 24 months, or until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.</treatmentRegimen>
    
    <objectives>
        <primaryObjective>To determine the overall response rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete response (CR) and partial response (PR) per RECIST 1.1 criteria.</primaryObjective>
        <secondaryObjectives>
            <objective>To assess progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months.</objective>
            <objective>To evaluate the safety and tolerability of the combination therapy.</objective>
            <objective>To analyze pharmacodynamic markers using serum and tumor biopsies to correlate with clinical outcomes.</objective>
        </secondaryObjectives>
    </objectives>
    
    <inclusionCriteria>
        <criterion>Histologically confirmed advanced non-small cell lung cancer (stage III or IV).</criterion>
        <criterion>ECOG performance status of 0-2.</criterion>
        <criterion>Patients must not have received previous systemic therapy for metastatic disease.</criterion>
        <criterion>Age ≥ 18 years.</criterion>
        <criterion>Life expectancy of at least 12 weeks.</criterion>
        <criterion>Ability to provide informed consent.</criterion>
        <criterion>Prior adjuvant therapy is permissible if completed at least 6 months prior to enrollment.</criterion>
    </inclusionCriteria>
    
    <exclusionCriteria>
        <criterion>Active brain metastases or carcinomatous meningitis.</criterion>
        <criterion>Uncontrolled or active infections requiring systemic antibiotics.</criterion>
        <criterion>History of autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy.</criterion>
        <criterion>Current use of systemic corticosteroids (≤10 mg/day of prednisone equivalent permitted).</criterion>
        <criterion>Major surgery within 4 weeks of starting treatment.</criterion>
        <criterion>Hypersensitivity to perifosine or anti-PD-1 agents.</criterion>
    </exclusionCriteria>
    
    <monitoringAndEvaluation>Regular assessments of overall response will be conducted through imaging studies every 8 weeks. Toxicity will be monitored using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.</monitoringAndEvaluation>
    
    <statisticalAnalysis>
        <analysisPlan>The primary endpoint ORR will be analyzed using a two-sided exact binomial test. Progression-free survival will be estimated using Kaplan-Meier methodology. Statistical significance will be set at α=0.05.</analysisPlan>
    </statisticalAnalysis>
    
    <clinicalSignificance>The successful outcome of this trial may support the combination of perifosine with immune checkpoint inhibitors as a viable therapeutic option, potentially leading to improved clinical outcomes in patients with advanced NSCLC while utilizing the mechanisms of action outlined in previous trials, particularly the specific targeting of tumor microenvironments to enhance immune responses.</clinicalSignificance>

</clinicalTrial>
