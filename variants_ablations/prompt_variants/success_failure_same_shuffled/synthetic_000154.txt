**Failed Clinical Trial of Cyclophosphamide: Efficacy of Cyclophosphamide in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Title:** A Phase II Study of Cyclophosphamide in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**Background:** Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, with considerable heterogeneity in tumor behavior and response to therapy. Recent advancements in immunotherapy, particularly the use of programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors like pembrolizumab, have shown promise in treating this malignancy. This trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy of low-dose cyclophosphamide when used in conjunction with pembrolizumab, hypothesizing synergistic effects in immune modulation that could enhance treatment outcomes.

**Objective:** The primary objective was to assess the overall response rate (ORR) in patients with advanced NSCLC receiving cyclophosphamide and pembrolizumab compared to pembrolizumab alone. We aimed for an ORR of 30% in the intervention cohort based on the anticipated immune response due to cyclophosphamide’s potential in reducing regulatory T cells (Tregs) and enhancing anti-tumor immunity.

**Methods:** 

**Design:** Multicenter, open-label, phase II clinical trial.

**Inclusion Criteria:** 
1. Adults aged ≥ 18 years with histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC (stage IIIB/IV).
2. Patients must have received no more than two prior lines of systemic therapy.
3. Measurable disease as per RECIST v1.1 criteria.
4. ECOG Performance Status of 0-1.
5. Adequate organ function, with laboratory values:
   - WBC ≥ 3,000/mm³
   - Platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³
   - Creatinine ≤ 1.5 x ULN
   - AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN

**Exclusion Criteria:** 
- History of autoimmune disease.
- Concurrent use of immunosuppressive agents.
- Prior treatment with agents targeting PD-1 or PD-L1.
- Active central nervous system metastases.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Treatment:** Participants received low-dose cyclophosphamide (300 mg/m²) intravenously on day 1 and pembrolizumab (200 mg) intravenously on day 8 of a 21-day cycle for a total of 8 cycles.

**Endpoints:** The primary endpoint was the ORR as defined by complete or partial response documented on imaging studies. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety/tolerability.

**Results:** The trial enrolled 120 patients, achieving a disappointing ORR of 15%, significantly below the hypothesized 30%. The lack of synergistic immune modulation between cyclophosphamide and pembrolizumab was evident, as immune profile assessments indicated no significant decrease in peripheral Treg populations post-treatment. Furthermore, the stratification of patients by prior treatment revealed that accumulated toxicities from prior regimens manifested in a higher incidence of adverse events (AEs), leading to protocol deviations and ultimately incomplete treatment.

Pharmacokinetic analysis revealed unanticipated interactions between cyclophosphamide and pembrolizumab, diminishing the therapeutic index for cyclophosphamide and amplifying systemic toxicity, thus challenging the proposed dosage regimen. 

**Discussion:** The failure of this clinical trial can be attributed to several key factors. First, the anticipated immunomodulatory effect of cyclophosphamide on Tregs was not observed, failing to enhance the therapeutic efficacy of pembrolizumab in this context. Second, patient selection criteria inadvertently led to a heterogeneous cohort with varying histories of prior therapies, which negatively impacted the overall response rate. Third, significant pharmacokinetic interactions between cyclophosphamide and pembrolizumab were identified, corroborating previous concerns regarding diminished efficacy due to systemic toxicity.

**Conclusion:** The combination of cyclophosphamide with pembrolizumab did not provide the desired enhancement in response rates for patients with advanced NSCLC, attributed to inadequate immune modulation, patient heterogeneity, and significant pharmacokinetic interactions. Future trials should explore alternative low-dose strategies and pre-treatment screening to optimize patient selection, potentially incorporating biomarkers predictive of response to immunotherapy.
