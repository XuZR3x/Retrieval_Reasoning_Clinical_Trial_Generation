**ZD6474 Combined with Chemotherapy in Treatment-Emergent Colorectal Cancer**

**A Phase II Randomized Trial of ZD6474 in Combination with Standard Chemotherapy Versus Standard Chemotherapy Alone in Patients with Recurrent Metastatic Colorectal Cancer**

**Abstract:**  
This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the investigational drug ZD6474, used in combination with standard chemotherapeutic agents (capecitabine and oxaliplatin), compared to chemotherapy alone in patients suffering from recurrent metastatic colorectal cancer. The dual inhibition of VEGFR and EGFR by ZD6474 is hypothesized to significantly enhance the therapeutic benefits by disrupting critical pathways associated with angiogenesis and tumor proliferation.

**Introduction:**  
Colorectal cancer remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related deaths globally. Traditional chemotherapy yields limited efficacy in advanced disease settings. Leveraging the unique mechanism of ZD6474 to inhibit VEGF and EGFR signaling could be pivotal in improving clinical outcomes. The study will explore this by combining ZD6474 with established agents, capecitabine and oxaliplatin, while also considering pharmacodynamic markers to evaluate the therapeutic response.

**Study Design:**  
This is a multicenter, double-blind, randomized, Phase II trial involving 200 patients diagnosed with recurrent metastatic colorectal cancer. Eligible participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either ZD6474 in combination with chemotherapy or chemotherapy alone. The trial consists of an initial 8-week treatment phase followed by an evaluation phase to assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall response rate (ORR).

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Histologically confirmed metastatic colorectal cancer with measurable disease per RECIST criteria.
2. Age ≥ 18 years with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status of 0-2.
3. Prior treatment with one to two systemic therapies for metastatic disease.
4. Adequate organ function assessed by laboratory tests.
5. Provision of informed consent, agreeing to study conditions.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Prior therapy with ZD6474 or other EGFR inhibitors.
2. Active gastrointestinal disorders affecting absorption.
3. Significant cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled hypertension.
4. Pregnant or lactating women and those not using effective contraception.

**Endpoints:**  
- **Primary Endpoint:**  
Comparison of PFS between the two study groups at 8 weeks of treatment. PFS is defined as the time from treatment initiation to disease progression or death, whichever occurs first.

- **Secondary Endpoints:**  
* Overall response rate (complete response + partial response) according to RECIST criteria.
* Safety profile measured by the incidence and type of adverse events (AEs).
* Assessment of biomarker modulation related to treatment efficacy and response.

**Methodology:**  
Patients will begin treatment with ZD6474 (dose ranging from 200 mg to 300 mg) orally once daily in conjunction with standard therapies of capecitabine and oxaliplatin administered bi-weekly. The study applies an adaptive design, offering dose adjustments based on adverse events and treatment tolerability.

**Monitoring and Follow-Up:**  
Participants will undergo routine evaluation including imaging studies at baseline and every two cycles, alongside periodic laboratory testing to monitor hematological and biochemical parameters. Patients will be followed up for disease status every 4 weeks post-treatment cycle.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
We hypothesize that the addition of ZD6474 to chemotherapy will enhance the PFS and ORR over chemotherapy alone by leveraging the synergistic effects of dual pathway inhibition. Furthermore, rigorous patient selection and adaptive monitoring are anticipated to optimize both safety and efficacy outcomes, thereby supporting the clinical utility of ZD6474 in the management of recurrent metastatic colorectal cancer.

**Conclusion:**  
This trial aims to elucidate the therapeutic advantage of ZD6474 in combination with standard chemotherapy among a carefully selected cohort of patients with recurrent metastatic colorectal cancer, thereby potentially addressing an unmet need in oncological therapeutics.
