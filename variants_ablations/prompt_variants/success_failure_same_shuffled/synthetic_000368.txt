**Successful clinical trial of cilostazol: The Cilostazol for Peripheral Artery Disease and Quality of Life (CAPA-QOL) Study**

The Prospective, Double-Blind, Randomized, Multi-Center Study Assessing the Efficacy of Cilostazol on Quality of Life and Symptoms in Patients with Intermittent Claudication Due to Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD) 

This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of cilostazol in improving quality of life and alleviating symptoms in patients diagnosed with PAD experiencing intermittent claudication. Peripheral artery disease significantly impacts daily activities and overall quality of life, characterized by exercise-induced leg pain due to inadequate blood flow. Current therapies primarily focus on lifestyle modifications and conventional antiplatelet agents, which frequently yield suboptimal outcomes. Cilostazol, as a phosphodiesterase III inhibitor, has demonstrated potential benefits in enhancing exercise tolerance and mitigating ischemic-related symptoms through vasodilation and inhibition of platelet aggregation.

Inclusion Criteria:
1. Clinical
   1. Patients aged 40 to 85 years with a confirmed diagnosis of PAD determined by lower extremity Doppler ultrasound with an ankle-brachial index (ABI) of <0.90.
   2. Patients experiencing intermittent claudication defined as calf pain on exertion that resolves within 10 minutes of rest.
2. Functional
   1. Patients who are capable of providing informed consent and comprehend study requirements.
   2. Baseline walking distance less than 200 meters on a standardized treadmill test.

Exclusion Criteria:
1. Chronic conditions with significantly elevated risk of bleeding, including history of bleeding diathesis, coagulopathy, or prior major hemorrhagic events.
2. Uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >180 mmHg or DBP >110 mmHg).
3. Patients on prior anticoagulation or antiplatelet therapy, except for aspirin at a stable dose.
4. Significant comorbidities limiting life expectancy to less than 12 months.
5. Renal dysfunction (creatinine levels >2.0 mg/dL) or severe hepatic dysfunction (ALT/AST >3 times the upper limit of normal).

Endpoints:
1. Primary Endpoint: Improvement in the Walking Impairment Questionnaire (WIQ) scores at 12 weeks post-treatment as compared to baseline assessments, focusing on perception of limitations due to claudication.
2. Secondary Endpoints: 
   1. Change in walking distance assessed by treadmill exercise tests.
   2. Comparison of patient-reported outcomes via the Short Form Health Survey (SF-36) focusing on physical functioning and mental health.
   3. Incidence of adverse events, including any bleeding complications and other treatment-related side effects during the treatment phase.

Rationale:
Utilization of cilostazol as an adjunct to therapy for PAD may enhance patient quality of life, specifically addressing the debilitating symptoms associated with intermittent claudication. The double-blind, randomized design of the study will ensure minimization of bias, enhancing the validity and generalizability of results across diverse demographics. Given cilostazol's superior safety profile relative to conventional therapies, particularly its lower incidence of bleeding complications, it presents as a favorable primary treatment option for patients with PAD at elevated risk for adverse events. This study aims to build upon previous findings demonstrating cilostazol's efficacy in enhancing exercise capacity and metabolic parameters, thus establishing its role as a multifaceted therapeutic agent in the management of PAD.

Overall, through this structured clinical investigation, we anticipate the clear demonstration of cilostazol's significant impact on alleviating symptoms related to intermittent claudication, ultimately leading to improved functional outcomes and enhanced patients' quality of life.
