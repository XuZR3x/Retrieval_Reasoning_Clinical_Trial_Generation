<clinical_trial>
  <title>Exploring the Efficacy of Imatinib Mesylate in Combination with Targeted Immunotherapy in Patients with Advanced BCR-ABL Positive Cancers</title>
  <trial_id>IM-IT-2023-05</trial_id>
  <sponsor>Johns Hopkins University Oncology Group</sponsor>
  <phase>Phase II</phase>
  <study_type>Interventional</study_type>
  <objective>
    The primary objectives of this trial are to evaluate the efficacy and safety of imatinib mesylate in conjunction with a novel immune checkpoint inhibitor, Pembrolizumab, in patients diagnosed with advanced BCR-ABL positive malignancies, including chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) and acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL). Secondary objectives include the assessment of overall survival, disease-free survival, and quality of life.
  </objective>
  <background>
    The rationale for this study is predicated on prior pharmacokinetic studies demonstrating the compatibility of imatinib mesylate when administered alongside immunotherapeutic agents, which do not interfere with imatinib’s bioavailability, thus ensuring optimal therapeutic concentrations (Reason 1). Additionally, prior research has indicated that the combination of targeted therapies with immunotherapy can yield synergistic benefits, potentially hindering resistance mechanisms commonly associated with both types of treatment (Reason 2). 
  </background>
  <methodology>
    <design>
      This is a multicenter, open-label, randomized Phase II clinical trial designed to compare the effects of imatinib mesylate and Pembrolizumab against a control group receiving standard supportive care. 
    </design>
    <participants>
      <description>We will screen and enroll 100 adult patients (≥18 years) with confirmed BCR-ABL positive CML or ALL, who have previously failed standard therapy. 
      </description>
      <inclusion_criteria>
        - Histologically confirmed diagnosis of CML or ALL with BCR-ABL positivity.
        - ECOG performance status of 0 to 2.
        - Adequate organ function as defined by specified laboratory parameters.
        - Written informed consent.
      </inclusion_criteria>
      <exclusion_criteria>
        - Current or prior treatment with immunotherapies within 30 days of enrollment.
        - Any uncontrolled medical condition or contraindication to imatinib therapy.
        - History of autoimmune diseases or ongoing infections.
      </exclusion_criteria>
    </participants>
  </methodology>
  <treatment>
    Subjects in the experimental group will receive imatinib mesylate orally at a standard dose of 400 mg/day combined with Pembrolizumab (200 mg) intravenously every 3 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. The control group will receive best supportive care as per institutional guidelines. 
  </treatment>
  <outcomes>
    <primary>
      - Efficacy will primarily be evaluated upon the rate of complete molecular response (CMR) as measured by quantitative PCR assays of BCR-ABL transcripts at designated time points (baseline, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months).
    </primary>
    <secondary>
      - Overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) will be recorded and evaluated using Kaplan-Meier analysis.
      - Quality of life will be assessed using validated questionnaires at specified intervals throughout the study.
    </secondary>
  </outcomes>
  <monitoring>
    Comprehensive safety monitoring will occur at each treatment visit with the assessment of hematological parameters and treatment-related adverse events, which will be categorized according to CTCAE guidelines. Regular interim analyses will allow for the timely adaptation of the study design should any unforeseen safety concerns arise.
  </monitoring>
  <data_analysis>
    An intention-to-treat principle will apply for all analyses. Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline patient characteristics. Efficacy measures will be analyzed using mixed-effects models to account for repeated measures over time.
  </data_analysis>
  <conclusion>
    If successful, this trial could significantly impact the management of BCR-ABL positive cancers by enhancing the efficacy of standard imatinib mesylate treatment through the addition of immunotherapy, potentially leading to prolonged survival and improved quality of life for patients.
  </conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
