<ClinicalTrial>
    <TrialID>MC-MIBMRI-01</TrialID>
    <Title>A Multicenter Randomized Controlled Trial to Discover the Efficacy and Safety of Mirabegron in Adults With Overactive Bladder Symptoms Not Controlled by Antimuscarinics</Title>
    <Purpose>The present study aims to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of mirabegron in adults suffering from overactive bladder (OAB) symptoms that remain inadequately controlled despite previous treatment with antimuscarinic agents.</Purpose>
    <Background>Overactive bladder is a significant clinical issue characterized by urgency, increased frequency, and urge incontinence. Current therapies primarily utilize antimuscarinic agents; however, numerous patients experience suboptimal responses or adverse effects. Mirabegron's unique mechanism as a β3-adrenoceptor agonist offers a novel therapeutic strategy, targeting the detrusor muscle to enhance bladder relaxation. Prior studies have shown promising outcomes across pediatric and adult populations. The current trial seeks to expand knowledge regarding mirabegron's applicability as a second-line therapy among adults whose symptoms remain refractory.</Background>

    <StudyDesign>
        <Type>Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Controlled</Type>
        <Duration>12 weeks</Duration>
        <Participants>300 adults aged 18-75 years</Participants>
        <Randomization>1:1 to treatment with mirabegron (50 mg) or placebo</Randomization>
        <PrimaryEndpoints>Efficacy measured by changes in the Total OAB Symptom Score (OABSS) after 12 weeks</PrimaryEndpoints>
        <SecondaryEndpoints>Safety profile, patient-reported outcomes, and adherence rates assessed through micturition diaries and validated symptom questionnaires</SecondaryEndpoints>
    </StudyDesign>

    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Adult patients aged 18-75 with a clinical diagnosis of OAB for at least 6 months</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Prior inadequate response or intolerable side effects from at least one antimuscarinic therapy</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Participants experiencing an average of ≥ 8 micturitions per day and at least 3 episodes of urgency per week</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Ability to comply with study requirements and medication protocols</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Written informed consent obtained from the patient</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>

    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Patients with known hypersensitivity to mirabegron or any of its components</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Presence of urinary tract infection at screening</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Significant cardiovascular conditions, severe hypertension, or other major systemic illnesses</Criterion>
        <Criterion>History of bladder outlet obstruction, severely reduced renal function (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min), or clinically significant abnormalities detectable via ECG</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Pregnant or lactating women and those without an effective contraception method</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>

    <Results>
        <Efficacy>
            <ChangeInOABSS>Significant reduction in Total OAB Symptom Score observed in mirabegron group compared to placebo (p<0.001)</ChangeInOABSS>
            <ContinenceRate>62% of patients in the mirabegron group achieved at least a 50% reduction in urge incontinence episodes</ContinenceRate>
        </Efficacy>
        <Safety>
            <AdverseEvents>Adverse events reported were mild and consistent with prior studies, including headache (3.5%), dry mouth (2.1%), and transient increases in blood pressure</AdverseEvents>
            <SeriousAdverseEvents>No serious adverse events attributed to mirabegron were reported</SeriousAdverseEvents>
        </Safety>
    </Results>

    <Conclusion>Mirabegron demonstrates substantial efficacy and a favorable safety profile in adults with OAB symptoms not adequately controlled by antimuscarinics. This trial supports its relevance as a viable second-line therapeutic option, reinforcing its utilization in clinical practice.</Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
