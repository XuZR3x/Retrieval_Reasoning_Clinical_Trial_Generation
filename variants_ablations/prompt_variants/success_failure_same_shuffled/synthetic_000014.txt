**Study of Brexpiprazole as a Primary Treatment for Treatment-Resistant Major Depressive Disorder in Adults: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial** 

*Protocol ID: BRX-TRD-2023-01*  

**Background**: Treatment-resistant major depressive disorder (TRD) presents a considerable challenge in clinical practice, with only a subset of patients achieving satisfactory symptom relief from standard antidepressant therapies. Brexpiprazole, a serotonin-dopamine activity modulator, has shown potential efficacy in improving depressive symptoms and addressing comorbid irritability in such patient populations. This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of brexpiprazole in a population diagnosed with TRD compared to placebo.

**Objective**: The primary objective is to assess the efficacy of brexpiprazole in reducing depressive symptoms in adults with TRD, measured by changes from baseline in the Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) scores.

**Study Design**: This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving two treatment arms: brexpiprazole and placebo. Participants will be administered either brexpiprazole or a matching placebo for 12 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Male and female patients aged 18-65 years with a confirmed diagnosis of Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) per DSM-5 criteria.
- Patients must have exhibited an inadequate response to at least two different classes of antidepressant medications (SSRIs, SNRIs, atypical antidepressants) during their current depressive episode.
- Baseline MADRS score ≥ 24.
- Patients must provide written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- A diagnosis of any other primary psychiatric disorder, including bipolar disorder or schizophrenia, as determined by the DSM-5.
- History of substance use disorder within the past six months.
- Recent (within 3 months) hospitalization related to psychiatric illness.
- Significant risk of suicide as assessed by the Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS).

**Methods**:
After a screening phase, eligible patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either brexpiprazole (starting at 2 mg/day, adjustable up to 4 mg/day) or placebo. The primary efficacy endpoint will be the change in MADRS total score from baseline to week 12.

Secondary endpoints will include evaluations of safety and tolerability, assessed through the frequency and severity of adverse events, as well as assessments of cognitive functioning using the Cognitive Assessment Screening Instrument (CASI).

**Statistical Analysis**:
Efficacy will be evaluated using a mixed-model repeated measures (MMRM) approach, with a significance level set at 0.05. Safety analyses will include descriptive statistics for adverse events and laboratory values.

**Expected Outcomes**: This trial anticipates demonstrating the targeted mechanism of action of brexpiprazole as a modulator of serotonergic and dopaminergic activity, hypothesizing a greater reduction in MADRS scores as compared to placebo. Additionally, we predict that the robust clinical design, incorporating standardized measurement tools and comprehensive patient selection criteria, will yield generalizable results. 

Furthermore, cognitive assessments are hypothesized to reveal potential improvements, affirming previously observed cognitive enhancing properties of brexpiprazole reflected in earlier studies.

**Conclusion**: This study aims to provide critical evidence for the efficacy and safety of brexpiprazole as a viable treatment option for adults with TRD, addressing a significant gap in current therapeutic strategies for this patient population and highlighting brexpiprazole’s favorable tolerability profile, which may enhance adherence and improve long-term management outcomes.
