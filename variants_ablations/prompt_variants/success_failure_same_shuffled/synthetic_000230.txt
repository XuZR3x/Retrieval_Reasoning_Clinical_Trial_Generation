**Successful Clinical Trial of Tadalafil: A Study of Tadalafil in Combination With Lifestyle Interventions for Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms Associated With Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia in Older Adults**

**A Phase 3, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Tadalafil in Combination With Lifestyle Interventions for 12 Weeks in Older Adults With Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms Suggestive of Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia**

The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy and safety of tadalafil as an adjunctive therapy to lifestyle modifications (including increased physical activity and dietary adjustments) in older adults presenting with lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) secondary to benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH). This trial designs to test the hypothesis that tadalafil, when combined with targeted lifestyle interventions, leads to a greater reduction in total International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) compared to placebo.

**Inclusion Criteria:**

- Adults aged 65 years and older.
- Diagnosis of benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH-LUTS) with an IPSS of ≥14 at screening.
- Demonstration of bladder outlet obstruction (Qmax of ≥4 and ≤12 mL/sec) at baseline.
- Agreement to engage in a structured lifestyle intervention program (including bladder training and physical activity) for the duration of the study.
- Consent to abstain from using any medications for BPH other than study drugs for at least 4 weeks prior to screening.

**Exclusion Criteria:**

- History of prostate cancer or current evidence of malignancy (PSA >10 ng/mL).
- Significant cardiovascular disease (including myocardial infarction or angina within the past year) or unstable medical conditions.
- History of serious renal or hepatic impairment as defined by standard clinical assessments.
- Concurrent participation in any other clinical trial or use of investigational drugs in the preceding 30 days.

**Endpoints:**

The primary endpoint is the change in total IPSS from baseline at 12 weeks, with secondary endpoints including quality of life assessments utilizing the BPH Impact Index (BPH-II) and participant compliance with lifestyle interventions evaluated through weekly journals. Safety assessments will include adverse event reporting and laboratory monitoring.

**Statistical Analysis:**

Using a mixed-model repeated measures (MMRM) approach, statistical significance of the treatment effects comparing tadalafil plus lifestyle intervention to placebo plus lifestyle intervention will be determined. A sample size of 300 participants, based on projected effect sizes and dropout rates, will provide adequate power to evaluate the hypothesis.

**Expected Outcomes:**

It is anticipated that the combination of tadalafil with prescribed lifestyle modifications will yield a statistically significant improvement in total IPSS scores compared to the placebo group. The study also aims to explore the potential of lifestyle factors to augment the symptomatic relief afforded by tadalafil, based on evidence from previous trials demonstrating the positive impact of concurrent lifestyle modifications.

This robust focus on efficacy, coupled with the methodological rigor of a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design, is expected to provide compelling data on the role of tadalafil in the comprehensive management of symptomatic BPH in older adults.
