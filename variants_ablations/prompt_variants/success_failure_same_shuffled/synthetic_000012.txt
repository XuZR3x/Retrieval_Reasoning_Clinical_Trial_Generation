<clinical_trial>
    <trial_title>A Phase II Open-label Study of BIBW 2992 (Afatinib) in Patients With Advanced Biliary Tract Carcinoma Harboring EGFR Mutations Post-Platinum Therapy</trial_title>
    <objective>
        This Phase II open-label study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of BIBW 2992 in patients with advanced biliary tract carcinoma (BTC) harboring specific mutations in the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) following progression on platinum-based chemotherapy. The primary endpoint of the study is to assess the overall response rate (ORR) as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.
    </objective>
    
    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Patients aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed advanced biliary tract carcinoma (i.e., intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, gallbladder cancer).</criterion>
        <criterion>Presence of documented EGFR activating mutations within exons 18 to 21 confirmed by next-generation sequencing of tumor tissue.</criterion>
        <criterion>Progressive disease following one or more lines of systemic therapy including at least one platinum-based chemotherapy regimen.</criterion>
        <criterion>Measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria.</criterion>
        <criterion>Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.</criterion>
        <criterion>Life expectancy of at least 3 months.</criterion>
        <criterion>Informed consent must be obtained according to ICH-GCP guidelines.</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    
    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Patients with severe comorbid conditions, active infectious disease, or significant organ dysfunction.</criterion>
        <criterion>Prior treatment with EGFR inhibitors (including BIBW 2992) or any investigational drugs within 30 days prior to enrollment.</criterion>
        <criterion>Patients with symptomatic brain metastases or uncontrolled interstitial lung disease.</criterion>
        <criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding women, or those not willing to use effective contraception during the trial.</criterion>
        <criterion>Patients with any history of other malignancies within the past 5 years, except for adequately treated non-melanomatous skin cancer or in situ cervical cancer.</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>
    
    <design>
        This is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm trial. Eligible patients will receive BIBW 2992 at a predefined dose of 40 mg orally once daily until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Tumor assessments will be performed at baseline and every 8 weeks thereafter until disease progression as per RECIST criteria. The trial employs an adaptive design to allow for continuous enrollment and assessment of response to treatment.
    </design>
    
    <endpoints>
        <primary_endpoint>Overall response rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) based on RECIST 1.1 criteria.</primary_endpoint>
        <secondary_endpoint>Progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and analysis of treatment-emergent adverse events recorded throughout the study.</secondary_endpoint>
    </endpoints>
    
    <rationale>
        <reason>Utilizing robust patient selection criteria, the study population is optimized to identify individuals with EGFR mutations who are most likely to derive clinical benefit from BIBW 2992.</reason>
        <reason>The mechanistic targeting of EGFR by BIBW 2992 has shown promise in regulating tumor growth and improving patient outcomes, notably in patients with advanced malignancies carrying specific EGFR mutations.</reason>
        <reason>Favorable tolerance and manageable safety profiles observed in previous studies allow for sustained administration of the treatment, thus enhancing potential efficacy through prolonged exposure.</reason>
        <reason>Significant tumor shrinkage as demonstrated in earlier trials supports the hypothesis of BIBW 2992's efficacy in altering disease dynamics, potentially leading to meaningful clinical benefits.</reason>
        <reason>The adaptive study design enables flexibility in response to patient outcomes, maximizing the chance of identifying effective treatment strategies for patients unresponsive to prior therapies.</reason>
    </rationale>
    
    <conclusion>
        This Phase II trial aims to establish the clinical utility of BIBW 2992 in patients with advanced biliary tract carcinoma, leveraging robust scientific rationale stemming from prior studies to propel forward the therapeutic potential of targeted EGFR inhibition in this challenging patient population.
    </conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
