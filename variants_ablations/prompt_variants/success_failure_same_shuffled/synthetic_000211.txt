**Title**: Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Atazanavir in Combination with Dolutegravir in Treatment-Naive HIV-1 Infected Adults: A Multicenter, Randomized Controlled Trial (ATDOL)

**Abstract**: This investigational study aims to determine the efficacy and safety of atazanavir in combination with dolutegravir compared to atazanavir monotherapy in treatment-naive individuals infected with HIV-1. The trial will emphasize the pharmacokinetic benefits observed in prior research as well as the potential for enhanced patient adherence due to the once-daily dosing regimen. Long-term safety outcomes will also be explored to determine atazanavir's long-term tolerability in a diverse population.

**Introduction**: HIV presents a significant global health challenge, necessitating effective antiretroviral therapies that enhance viral suppression, minimize morbidity, and are safe for long-term use. Atazanavir has previously shown robust efficacy in viral load suppression and has a favorable safety profile. However, as treatment paradigms evolve, there is a need to explore its efficacy in novel combinations, particularly in resource-limited settings. This trial seeks to build on existing evidence regarding the tolerability of atazanavir and its ability to fortify adherence among newly initiated antiretroviral therapy (ART) regimens.

**Methods**: 

**Study Design**: This multicenter, randomized, open-label trial will engage approximately 1,200 treatment-naive HIV-1 infected adults in diverse geographical locations, including sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and Eastern Europe. 

**Participants**: Eligible participants will be adults aged 18-65 years with confirmed HIV-1 infection, a baseline plasma viral load > 1,000 copies/mL, and CD4 counts between 200 and 600 cells/mm³. 

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
- Confirmation of HIV-1 infection via serological testing
- CD4 count within specified ranges
- Consent to adhere to study protocols and follow-up visits

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
- Previous ART exposure exceeding 7 days
- Chronic medical conditions that may interfere with study conduct
- Known hypersensitivity to atazanavir or dolutegravir

**Randomization and Intervention**: Participants will be assigned to one of two arms:
1. **Arm A**: Atazanavir (300 mg once daily) combined with dolutegravir (50 mg once daily)
2. **Arm B**: Atazanavir monotherapy (300 mg once daily) 

**Outcomes**: The primary outcome will be the proportion of participants achieving viral suppression (plasma HIV-1 RNA < 50 copies/mL) at week 48. Secondary endpoints will include the assessment of safety (measured by the incidence of adverse effects), CD4 count improvements, quality of life assessments, and long-term adherence rates.

**Sample Size Calculation**: Using an estimated effect size based on previous studies, a sample size of 1,200 participants will ensure sufficient power to detect statistically significant differences in the primary outcome.

**Data Analysis**: Efficacy will be assessed using the intention-to-treat principle. Safety data will be analyzed descriptively. Comparisons between arms will utilize chi-square tests for categorical outcomes and t-tests for continuous outcomes.

**Safety Monitoring**: A Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will oversee trial safety, with predefined stopping rules related to efficacy and adverse events.

**Expected Results**: Retaining and enhancing the beneficial impact of atazanavir on viral suppression through optimal combination therapy may reduce the incidence of treatment failures, fostering improved public health outcomes in regions disproportionately affected by HIV.

**Conclusion**: The ATDOL trial aims to provide evidence supporting atazanavir's efficacy in combination with dolutegravir, addressing the dual necessities of sustained viral suppression and long-term safety in a diverse cohort of HIV-infected individuals.

**Registration**: The study will be registered with clinical trial registries prior to enrollment commencement, ensuring adherence to global standards for clinical research transparency.
