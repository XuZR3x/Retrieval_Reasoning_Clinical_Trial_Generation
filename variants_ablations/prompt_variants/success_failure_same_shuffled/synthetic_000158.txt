**A Randomized Phase II Study of Aldesleukin and Nivolumab for the Treatment of Patients With Refractory Metastatic Squamous Cell Carcinoma**

**RATIONALE:**
Recent advancements in immunotherapy have demonstrated that the combination of immune checkpoint inhibitors with cytokine therapy can enhance anti-tumor responses. Nivolumab, a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitor, has shown efficacy in treating patients with metastatic squamous cell carcinoma (SCC). Aldesleukin, a recombinant interleukin-2, is hypothesized to further augment immune responses by enhancing lymphocyte proliferation and effector function when combined with nivolumab. This study aims to evaluate the clinical efficacy, safety, and biomarker correlates of this combination therapy in refractory metastatic SCC.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the progression-free survival (PFS) in patients receiving combination therapy with aldesleukin and nivolumab compared to nivolumab alone.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) and overall survival (OS) in both treatment arms.
II. To characterize the safety and tolerability profiles of the combination treatment.
III. To investigate changes in immune markers correlating with treatment response, focusing on T cell activation, regulatory T cell (Treg) populations, and cytokine production.

**OUTLINE:**
Patients are randomly assigned to one of two treatment arms.

**ARM I:** Patients receive nivolumab intravenously (IV) every 2 weeks and aldesleukin subcutaneously (SC) every 8 hours for 5 doses, repeated every 21 days for up to 3 courses in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**ARM II:** Patients receive nivolumab IV as above. Patients are treated for up to 3 courses in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

After completion of study treatment, patients are followed every 3 months for 2 years.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:**
A total of 120 patients will be accrued for this study.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed locally advanced or metastatic squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) with no curative treatment options available.
- Measurable disease defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
- Absolute neutrophil count > 1,500/mm^3, platelets ≥ 100,000/mm^3, and hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL.
- Creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.
- No active autoimmune disease that requires systemic treatment.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Patients must provide written informed consent.
- Age 18 years or older.
- Patients with any history of anti-PD-1 therapy are eligible provided they have completed treatment at least 4 weeks prior to the study.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Previous treatment with systemic interleukin-2 or nivolumab within 4 weeks prior to study entry.
- History of severe allergic reaction to aldesleukin, nivolumab, or any of the study medications.
- Active infection or significant comorbid conditions that may preclude study participation.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- Progression-free survival (PFS) is estimated using Kaplan-Meier methods.
- Overall response rate determined by RECIST version 1.1 criteria.
- Safety assessed using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

**BIOMARKER EXPLORATION:**
- Blood samples collected pre- and post-treatment to evaluate levels of cytokines and changes in immune cell populations, particularly focusing on Treg cells and activated T cells.

**ADVERSE EVENTS MONITORING:**
Comprehensive tracking of adverse events and severe toxicities to inform ongoing risk-benefit assessment of the treatment regimen.

**SUMMARY:**
This randomized phase II trial aims to elucidate the combined therapeutic potential of aldesleukin and nivolumab in patients with refractory metastatic squamous cell carcinoma. By employing a rigorous biomarker-oriented design, the study looks not only to improve PFS and ORR but also to deepen the understanding of the mechanisms underpinning immune response modulation in SCC treatment. The stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria will ensure a well-defined patient population, ultimately contributing to the generation of high-quality clinical evidence.
