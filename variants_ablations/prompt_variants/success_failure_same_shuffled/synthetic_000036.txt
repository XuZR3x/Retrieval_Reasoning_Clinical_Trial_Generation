**Failed Clinical Trial of PRO 140: A Phase 2b Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of PRO 140 Combined with a Personalized Antiretroviral Regimen in HIV-1 Infected Adults with Complex Resistance Profiles**

**Study Identifier:** PRO 140 2203

**Objective:** To assess the efficacy and safety of subcutaneous administration of PRO 140 in conjunction with a personalized antiretroviral regimen in adult subjects infected with HIV-1 exhibiting complex resistance patterns and suboptimal immune status.

**Design:** This phase 2b study is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving approximately 100 male and female adult subjects who demonstrate complex resistance mechanisms to antiretroviral therapy. Subjects will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either PRO 140 or placebo. The duration of the study is 48 weeks, with efficacy and safety assessments conducted at regular intervals.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Males and females, age ≥18 years with confirmed HIV-1 infection.
2. Screening plasma HIV-1 RNA ≥ 1,500 copies/mL.
3. Documented presence of CCR5-tropic virus using the Enhanced Sensitivity Trofile™ HIV Tropism Assay.
4. CD4+ lymphocyte cell count between 200 and 600 cells/mm³.
5. History of failure to respond to at least two prior antiretroviral therapies due to documented resistance mutations.
6. Clinically normal 12-lead ECG at screening visit.
7. Women of reproductive potential must have a negative serum and urine pregnancy test prior to dosing and agree to effective contraception during the study.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. CXCR4-tropic or dual/mixed tropic (R5X4) virus as determined by the Enhanced Sensitivity Trofile™ HIV Tropism Assay.
2. Presence of chronic active hepatitis or serious co-infection, including significant hepatic dysfunction.
3. Prior exposure to any CCR5 antagonist, including PRO 140.
4. Participation in another experimental drug trial within 30 days prior to entry or during the study.
5. History of severe non-compliance with antiretroviral therapy as determined by medical history.

**Endpoints:**
- Primary endpoint: Proportion of participants achieving a plasma HIV-1 RNA < 50 copies/mL at week 48 (indicating effective viral suppression).
- Secondary endpoints: Safety profile assessments, changes in CD4+ cell counts, incidence of adverse events, and pharmacokinetic evaluations of PRO 140 and background therapy.

**Rationale for Study Design:**
Given that previous trials of PRO 140 have demonstrated a trend towards suboptimal virologic responses potentially attributed to pre-existing resistance mechanisms, suboptimal immune status, and challenges in pharmacokinetics, this trial aimed to recruit subjects with a complex resistance profile while implementing a personalized background antiretroviral regimen optimized for each participant's resistance pattern.

**Monitoring and Outcome Measures:**
Safety will be assessed via regular laboratory tests and clinical evaluations throughout the study. Efficacy measures will focus on virologic suppression defined by the percentage of subjects achieving undetectable viral loads, alongside immunological responses documented through CD4+ cell counts.

**Conclusion:** 
The trial anticipated challenges consistent with prior results, including the potential impact of pre-existing resistance mutations and subpar immunological status on PRO 140's efficacy. Furthermore, adherence to the antiretroviral regimen may be influenced by the complexity of participants' drug use behaviors, which could further complicate clinical outcomes. Despite the design aiming to minimize these factors, the anticipated adherence issues, combined with drug-drug interactions in a polydrug regimen, raised concerns about the viability of achieving sustained viral suppression.

**Study Status:** Failure to demonstrate significant efficacy as measured by the primary endpoint, leading to a conclusion that PRO 140's role in conjunction with a highly personalized regimen is suboptimal in this population. Further investigation into the pharmacological profile and virologic efficacy in broader cohort parameters is recommended to elucidate its potential therapeutic validity.
