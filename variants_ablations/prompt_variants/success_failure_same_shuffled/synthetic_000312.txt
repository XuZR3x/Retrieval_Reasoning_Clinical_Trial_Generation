**Failed Clinical Trial of Exemestane: A Randomized Controlled Trial of Exemestane With/Without Palbociclib in Patients With Hormone Receptor-Positive, HER2-Negative Advanced Breast Cancer**

**Phase:** 2 Open-Label Trial of Exemestane in Combination with Palbociclib vs. Exemestane Alone in First-Line Treatment of Hormone Receptor-Positive Advanced Breast Cancer

The objective of this trial was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of exemestane combined with palbociclib compared to exemestane monotherapy in patients with hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer. The primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS), while secondary endpoints included overall response rate (ORR) and safety profile assessment.

**Rationale for Termination:**
The clinical trial was terminated on May 15, 2022, due to several key challenges that influenced its feasibility and validity:

1. **Insufficient Patient Recruitment:** The trial faced significant obstacles in recruiting the necessary number of participants with hormone receptor-positive advanced breast cancer, thereby not achieving the targeted sample size needed for a statistically significant analysis. Despite extensive outreach, the identified patient population was markedly lower than anticipated, leading to concerns over the power of the study to detect meaningful differences in outcomes.

2. **Ineffective Pharmacological Synergy:** Interim analyses indicated that the combination of exemestane and palbociclib failed to demonstrate any considerable pharmacological synergy over exemestane alone when evaluating PFS rates. Preliminary data revealed that the addition of palbociclib did not lead to improved outcomes, suggesting potential limitations in leveraging dual pathways of inhibition in the context of resistance mechanisms against prior endocrine therapies.

3. **Non-Inferiority Threshold Not Met:** The study aimed to demonstrate that the combination therapy was not inferior to exemestane alone in terms of improving time to progression (TTP). However, the results showed that the non-inferiority margin was not achieved, raising questions about the clinical relevance of adding palbociclib, especially in a population previously treated with aromatase inhibitors.

4. **Limited Therapeutic Window in Selected Population:** The trial's stringent inclusion criteria, which mandated prior responsiveness to endocrine therapy and a requirement for specific progression definitions, inadvertently excluded a broader patient demographic. This limitation may have restricted the generalizability of the findings and overlooked patients who might have realistically benefitted from the investigational treatment.

5. **Compounded Mechanisms of Resistance:** Observations revealed that several patients exhibited complex patterns of adaptive resistance mechanisms, including alterations in the estrogen receptor and upregulation of alternative growth signaling pathways. These findings potentially indicate that the disease burden of advanced breast cancer was not effectively managed by the proposed therapeutic strategy, thereby questioning the viability of the administrated regimen in overcoming established resistance phenomena.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult female patients aged ≥ 18 years.
- Histological or cytological confirmation of hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer.
- Patients must be candidates for first-line therapy and have not received prior systemic therapy for advanced disease.
- ECOG performance status of ≤ 1.
- Availability of measurable disease as per RECIST v1.1 criteria.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment with cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors or exemestane.
- Any history of HER2-positive breast cancer.
- Severe or uncontrolled non-malignant diseases.
- Active participation in any investigational drug protocols within the last 30 days.
- Clinical evidence of significant hepatic or renal impairment.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Progression-Free Survival (PFS), defined as the time from randomization to first documentation of objective tumor progression or death from any cause.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Overall Response Rate (ORR), assessed according to RECIST v1.1, and safety profile, evaluated via adverse event reporting and clinical assessments.

Given the aforementioned factors, it became evident that the current hypothesis regarding the potential benefits of combining exemestane with palbociclib was not adequately substantiated. The decision to suspend the trial was made with careful consideration for participant safety and the integrity of clinical research efforts in the field of hormone receptor-positive breast cancer therapy.
