**Title:** A Phase II, Randomized, Double-Blind, Multicenter Study of Entinostat with Nivolumab and Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

**Abstract:** This clinical trial investigates the safety and efficacy of entinostat in combination with checkpoint inhibitors, nivolumab and pembrolizumab, in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who progress after prior therapy, including a platinum-based regimen. This study aims to explore the synergistic effects of entinostat in modulating immune response pathways alongside immune checkpoint inhibition, thereby potentially improving therapeutic outcomes compared to checkpoint inhibitor monotherapy.

**Introduction:** Advances in molecularly targeted therapies have led to significant improvements in the treatment landscape of NSCLC. However, resistance to current treatments denotes the need for novel combinations aiming to enhance tumor response rates. The combination of entinostat, a histone deacetylase inhibitor, with nivolumab and pembrolizumab is hypothesized to overcome resistance mechanisms by reshaping the tumor microenvironment and enhancing immune recognition of tumor cells.

**Objectives:**
- **Primary Objectives:**
  1. To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) in patients receiving entinostat combined with nivolumab and pembrolizumab.
  2. To assess progression-free survival (PFS) in patients treated with entinostat and immune checkpoint inhibitors compared to historical controls receiving nivolumab or pembrolizumab alone.

- **Secondary Objectives:**
  1. To determine the safety profile and incidence of adverse events associated with the combination therapy.
  2. To analyze the modulation of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and PD-L1 expression in tumor biopsies assessed at baseline and post-treatment.

**Methods:** This multicenter, double-blind study enrolled patients aged ≥ 18 with histologically confirmed stage IIIb or IV NSCLC who experienced disease progression after standard treatment. Patients with contraindications to checkpoint inhibitors and those currently enrolled in other investigational studies were excluded.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with measurable disease as defined by RECIST v1.1.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0-1.
- Adequate organ function (defined as hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL, platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³, creatinine < 1.5 times upper limit of normal (ULN), hepatic transaminases < 2.5 times ULN).
- Prior treatment must have included at least one platinum-based chemotherapy regimen and proof of tumor progression.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment.
- Known history of hypersensitivity to entinostat, nivolumab, or pembrolizumab.
- Participation in any other clinical trial within 30 days prior to enrollment.

**Study Design:** Participants were randomized (1:1:1) to receive either:
1. Entinostat plus nivolumab,
2. Entinostat plus pembrolizumab, or
3. Nivolumab or pembrolizumab alone as a control (historical cohort).

Entinostat was administered orally on days 1, 8, and 15 of each 28-day cycle, while nivolumab and pembrolizumab were given intravenously on days 1 and 15.

**Endpoints:** ORR and PFS were assessed as per RECIST criteria, with efficacy related to immune modulation evaluated through previous pathways affected by combination therapy, such as TILs and PD-L1 expression, explored in correlative studies.

**Results:** Within the primary endpoint evaluation, the ORR observed in the treatment cohort receiving entinostat with nivolumab was 42%, significantly higher than the historical ORR of 27% for nivolumab alone. The addition of entinostat to pembrolizumab yielded an ORR of 39%. Median PFS was recorded at 8.5 months for nivolumab plus entinostat and 7.9 months for pembrolizumab plus entinostat.

**Conclusion:** The findings from this study underscore the potential of combining entinostat with immune checkpoint inhibitors in treating advanced NSCLC, supported by the observed modulation of oncogenic signaling pathways and immune response indicators. Further phased studies are warranted to confirm these findings and explore the long-term outcomes associated with this combination regimen.

**Follow-Up:** Patients will be followed for safety evaluations for a period of 6 months post-treatment, ensuring the resolution of any adverse events and assessment of long-term outcomes. Regular health assessments will be performed bi-monthly until the end of follow-up.
