**Successful Clinical Trial of SR58611A: A Study on the Efficacy and Safety of SR58611A for the Treatment of Generalized Anxiety Disorder in Elderly Patients**

**A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of SR58611A (350 mg q12) in Elderly Outpatients (Age ≥ 65) with Generalized Anxiety Disorder**

The purpose of this clinical trial is to ascertain the efficacy and safety of SR58611A in elderly patients diagnosed with Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD). The primary objective is to evaluate the change in Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAM-A) scores from baseline to Day 56 while assessing the treatment's impact on quality of life using the GAD-7 scale as a secondary outcome measure.

**Study Design:**
This is a multicenter, multinational study design incorporating a parallel-group, double-blind, placebo-controlled methodology. The trial includes two phases: phase one consists of a 1-week single-blind placebo run-in, while phase two represents the 8-week double-blind intervention period where participants are randomized to receive either SR58611A (350 mg q12) or a matched placebo. A post-study follow-up assessment is scheduled one week after the administration of the last dose of study medication.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Elderly outpatients aged 65 and older.
- Patients diagnosed with Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) based on DSM-IV-TR criteria, confirmed by the Mini International Neuropsychiatric Interview (MINI).
- A total HAM-A score of ≥ 20 at baseline.
- Written informed consent provided voluntarily.
- Ability to follow study protocols and provide feedback during evaluations.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Diagnosis of Major Depressive Disorder or other comorbid anxiety disorders (including panic disorder, social phobia, PTSD) as defined by DSM-IV-TR within the preceding 6 months.
- High suicide risk as assessed by clinical judgment.
- Significant comorbid medical conditions which may affect participation.
- Pregnant or lactating females, or those not adhering to contraception.
- Positive screen for illicit substances or participation in a clinical trial of an experimental therapy within 3 months prior to screening.

**Primary Outcome:**
The primary endpoint is the change from baseline to Day 56 in the total score on the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAM-A).

**Secondary Outcomes:**
1. Change from baseline to Day 56 in the GAD-7 scale scores.
2. Assessment of safety through the frequency and severity of adverse effects reported during the study duration.
3. Changes in the Clinical Global Impression Improvement (CGI-I) scale scores.

The trial emphasizes rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria, ensuring the participant population adheres strictly to the target demographic, thus enhancing the clarity of treatment outcomes (Reason 1). A well-defined primary outcome measure anchored by established scales such as the HAM-A allows precise evaluation of SR58611A's efficacy in reducing anxiety symptoms (Reason 2). By employing a double-blind, randomized design, potential biases are mitigated, aligning with the best practices for clinical investigation (Reason 3). The extended duration of follow-up permits a comprehensive evaluation of both immediate and sustained therapeutic effects while addressing safety concerns over time (Reason 4). Finally, a consistent dosage regimen at a fixed dose of 350 mg q12 guarantees uniformity in drug exposure, a principal element contributing to the favorable outcomes observed (Reason 5).

This multi-faceted approach directed at a homogenous elderly cohort with GAD in this clinical setting supports the notion that SR58611A, at the tested dosage, not only effectively reduces symptoms of anxiety but does so with an acceptable safety profile. Thus, paving the way for further extensive exploration into the pharmacotherapeutic capacity of SR58611A in managing anxiety disorders among varied populations.
