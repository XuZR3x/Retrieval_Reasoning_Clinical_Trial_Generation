**Title:** Efficacy of Entinostat Combined with Immunotherapy in Patients with Advanced Melanoma: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Phase II Study

**Purpose:** This study aims to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and clinical efficacy of entinostat in combination with programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors in patients with advanced melanoma, assessing its potential for enhancing immunotherapy responses.

**Design:** This is a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase II trial designed to elucidate the therapeutic impact of entinostat as an adjunct treatment with PD-1 inhibitors. The study will feature a lead-in phase assessing entinostat alone, followed by a randomized phase evaluating the combination therapy with the PD-1 inhibitor.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed stage III or IV melanoma with measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Patients must have received at least one prior therapy for metastatic disease and progressed.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function defined by laboratory parameters: Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL, Platelets ≥ 100 x 10^9/L, Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L.
- Paraffin-embedded tumor specimen available for correlative studies to assess histone acetylation markers.
- Both male and female patients aged ≥ 18 years willing to use effective contraception during the trial.
- Written informed consent obtained prior to the initiation of any study-specific procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with PD-1 inhibitors or histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitors (including entinostat).
- Active central nervous system metastases or significant cardiac conditions.
- Uncontrolled infections or serious concomitant medical conditions that could preclude participation.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- Patients with a known hypersensitivity to benzamide derivatives.

**Primary Objectives:**
1. To evaluate the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of entinostat in combination with PD-1 inhibitors and determine the recommended phase III dose.
2. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) as determined by radiographic imaging following the RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**Secondary Objectives:**
1. To analyze the safety profile and tolerability of the combination treatment as measured by common adverse event grading.
2. To characterize pharmacokinetic profiles of entinostat when administered concomitantly with anti-PD-1 therapy.
3. To evaluate the change in histone acetylation in tumor samples pre- and post-treatment, providing insight into the pharmacodynamics of the intervention.

**Study Treatment:** Patients will receive oral entinostat once weekly combined with a PD-1 inhibitor administered according to standard treatment regimens. The treatment cycles will last 28 days, with response assessments at the end of every two cycles.

**Assessment Schedule:** Tumor response will be evaluated radiologically using CT or MRI every 8 weeks. Patient-reported outcomes, including quality of life and symptom burden, will be monitored through validated questionnaires at baseline and every two cycles.

**Correlative Studies:** Integrating biomarker analysis, this study will feature correlative studies to assess histone acetylation levels in peripheral blood and tumor tissues, along with immune profiling to elucidate potential mechanisms related to the combined therapeutic benefit of entinostat and immunotherapy.

**Statistical Considerations:** This trial will utilize a stratified randomization method with an estimated sample size of 150 participants to provide sufficient power for detecting significant differences in objectives. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Endpoints:** Primary endpoints will include MTD, ORR, and safety profile, while secondary endpoints will encompass pharmacokinetic characteristics and changes in histone acetylation as biological markers of response.

**Conclusion:** The current study is predicated on successful outcomes from previous trials demonstrating the efficacy of entinostat in diverse malignancies. By integrating entinostat with advanced immunotherapy protocols, the study aims to enhance treatment responses in advanced melanoma, addressing a critical need in patient care management. This comprehensive assessment anticipates illustrating how entinostat may significantly modulate the tumor microenvironment, potentially augmenting immune responses against melanoma cells.
