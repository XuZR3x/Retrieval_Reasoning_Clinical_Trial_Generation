**Evaluating the Effect of Raltegravir on Metabolic Outcomes in HIV-Infected Patients: A Phase IV Study**

**Study Title:** Metabolic Effects of Raltegravir in HIV-Infected Individuals Co-managing Hepatic Steatosis

**Primary Objective:** To evaluate the impact of raltegravir substitution on metabolic outcomes, particularly hepatic steatosis and lipid profiles, in HIV-infected patients with a history of metabolic complications receiving antiretroviral therapy.

**Hypothesis:** Transitioning patients who have experienced metabolic complications related to their current antiretroviral therapy to a regimen including raltegravir will lead to significant improvements in hepatic steatosis and overall metabolic health, without compromising viral suppression.

**Design:** This study is a multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial designed for assessing the efficacy and safety profile of raltegravir among HIV-infected patients with documented hepatic steatosis. Eligible patients will be randomized to receive either raltegravir combined with background nucleoside analogs or to continue with their current regimen, which lacks integrase inhibitors.

**Condition:** HIV infection in the context of metabolic complications.

**Intervention:** Patients on current antiretroviral therapy will be randomly assigned to switch to raltegravir (400 mg BID) combined with two nucleoside analogues or to remain on their existing regimen.

**Study Population and Sample Size:** A total of 120 patients will be recruited across multiple sites, with 60 patients allocated to each treatment arm. Participants must be 18 years or older, and both genders are eligible.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Written informed consent must be obtained.
2. Age ≥ 18 years.
3. Documented diagnosis of HIV infection.
4. Evidence of hepatic steatosis calculated via controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) ≥ 238 dB/m.
5. Patients currently undergoing stable antiretroviral therapy for a minimum of 24 weeks.
6. HIV RNA must be below 50 copies/mL for at least two consecutive assessments over the previous 24 weeks and present during screening.
7. Patients should have previously experienced metabolic complications related to antiretroviral therapy prior to enrollment.
8. All sexually active females of childbearing potential must agree to use adequate contraception during the study.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Known allergies to investigational products or their excipients.
2. Active HIV-related opportunistic infections or severe comorbidities.
3. Previous treatment with integrase strand transfer inhibitors, including raltegravir.
4. History of malignancies or other significant medical conditions that could interfere with the study outcomes.
5. Any uncontrolled psychiatric disorders or substance abuse issues.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** The primary endpoint is the change in hepatic steatosis as measured by CAP from baseline to 48 weeks of treatment.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** 
  1. Proportion of patients who maintain viral control (HIV RNA < 50 copies/mL) at 48 weeks.
  2. Changes in fasting lipid profiles, including total triglycerides, LDL, and HDL cholesterol levels from baseline to 48 weeks.
  3. Assessment of quality of life using a validated HIV-specific questionnaire at baseline and at 48 weeks.
  4. Safety profile documented through adverse event reporting throughout the study duration.

**Statistical Analysis:** Continuous outcomes will be analyzed using appropriate statistical methods (e.g., paired t-tests or ANOVA), while categorical outcomes will be analyzed using Chi-square tests. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

This study is particularly important as it addresses the metabolic complications often presenting in HIV patients, aligning with recent findings that support raltegravir’s favorable effects on hepatic steatosis and metabolic health. Given the robust pharmacokinetic data showing raltegravir's efficacy, combined with its well-documented safety profile, this trial will provide critical insights into the long-term management of HIV-infected individuals facing metabolic challenges.
