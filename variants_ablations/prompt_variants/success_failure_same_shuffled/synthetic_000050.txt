**Failed clinical trial of pemetrexed: Assessment of Pemetrexed Versus Standard Chemotherapy in Patients with Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Lung** 

**A Phase 3 Randomized Trial of Pemetrexed Compared to Standard Etoposide and Cisplatin in Patients with Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Lung Who Have Failed First-Line Therapy**

The aim of this study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of pemetrexed in comparison to the standard chemotherapy regimen of etoposide and cisplatin for the treatment of squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the lung in patients who have previously received at least one line of chemotherapy. The study hypothesis posited that pemetrexed would demonstrate non-inferior overall survival (OS) to the standard regimen. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologic diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma of the lung (Stage IIIB or IV)
- Patients must have received at least one prior chemotherapy for metastatic or recurrent disease
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 to 2
- Measurable disease according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment with pemetrexed
- Presence of other malignancies or clinically significant comorbidities
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women
- Patients unable or unwilling to comply with folic acid and vitamin B12 supplementation

**Overall Survival**
Overall survival was defined as the duration from randomization to death from any cause. For those patients who were alive at the assessment date, survival time was censored at the date of last known contact. Preliminary end-point results indicated no significant benefit in OS between the two treatment arms. Adjustments to the trial design were necessitated following interim analysis, which revealed high rates of inherent resistance mechanisms within the study population, particularly among those with altered folate metabolism.

The anticipated efficacy of pemetrexed was ultimately not realized, with results highlighting the impact of limited generalizability due to the recruitment of a predominantly male population with specific genetic backgrounds. Additionally, the lack of synergistic effect when combined with concurrent therapies underscored the suboptimal performance of pemetrexed compared to standard-of-care regimens. Furthermore, observed variability in tumor histology led to heterogenous responses; suboptimal outcomes were common, leading to early termination of the study.

**Progression-Free Survival**
Progression-Free Survival (PFS) was assessed as the interval from randomization to documented disease progression or death due to any cause. PFS was significantly lower than expected, attributed to the rapid disease progression observed in patients not adequately managed with the proposed maintenance therapy, following initial treatments with pemetrexed.

In conclusion, this clinical trial failed to demonstrate the anticipated advantages of pemetrexed over established therapeutic regimens, leading to a reassessment of its role within the treatment landscape of squamous cell carcinoma of the lung. Further investigation into optimizing combination therapy approaches and incorporating consistent follow-up care is warranted to enhance treatment efficacy.
