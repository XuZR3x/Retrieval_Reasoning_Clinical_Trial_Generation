**Ranolazine in CHF: A Multinational, Multicentric Study of Ranolazine in Patients with Chronic Heart Failure (CHF)**

**Study Overview:**
The primary aim of this study is to investigate the efficacy and safety of ranolazine in patients with chronic heart failure (CHF) with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) to alleviate symptoms and enhance exercise capacity, alongside assessing its impact on hemodynamic parameters and microvascular function.

**Background:**
Chronic heart failure is an escalating global health crisis characterized by complex pathophysiology and symptomatology. Recent studies have highlighted ranolazine's ability to improve diastolic function and coronary microvascular dysfunction, indicating its potential therapeutic utility in this context. This study builds upon the promising findings of enhanced global hemodynamics and efficacy in diverse ethnic subgroups observed in previous ranolazine trials.

**Study Design:**
This is a randomized, double-blind, multicenter, placebo-controlled trial designed to evaluate the effect of ranolazine on exercise tolerance measured through the Six-Minute Walk Test (6MWT), quality of life as assessed by the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ), and significant improvement in LVEDP and tau during right heart catheterization.

**Patient Population:**
A total of 200 participants aged 45 years and older with symptomatic HFpEF (NYHA class II-III) will be enrolled from multiple sites across North America, Europe, and Asia. All participants must have documented coronary artery disease and demonstrate an average resting LVEDP greater than 18 mm Hg, with NT-pro-BNP levels exceeding 300 pg/mL.

**Treatment Protocol:**
Upon meeting eligibility criteria and providing consent, participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either ranolazine (1000 mg/day) or placebo for 12 weeks. Treatment compliance will be monitored through weekly check-ins and electronic diary entries. 

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Outcome:** Change in 6MWT distance from baseline to week 12.
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Changes in KCCQ scores, NT-pro-BNP levels, LVEDP, and tau measured with right heart catheterization pre- and post-treatment, alongside adverse event monitoring.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Males or females aged ≥ 45 years.
2. Documented symptomatic heart failure (NYHA class II-III) with preserved ejection fraction (LVEF ≥ 50%).
3. Documented coronary artery disease with an average resting LVEDP >18 mm Hg and NT-pro-BNP >300 pg/mL at screening.
4. Ability to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Acute decompensated heart failure requiring hospitalization.
2. Significant comorbidities affecting cardiac function (e.g., severe valvular disease, cardiomyopathy).
3. Hypotension defined as blood pressure < 90/50 mm Hg.
4. Known contraindications to ranolazine (e.g., history of long QT syndrome).
5. Concurrent use of potent CYP3A inducers/inhibitors.

**Analytical Approach:**
Statistical analyses will be performed to assess differences in primary and secondary endpoints between treatment and placebo groups using appropriate parametric and non-parametric methods, considering confounding variables based on demographic and physiological characteristics.

**Conclusion:**
This trial aims to elucidate ranolazine’s role as a transformative intervention in patients with chronic heart failure, providing evidence for its efficacy in improving exercise capacity and enhancing quality of life, further supporting its safety profile across diverse populations. The study is anticipated to generate insights into mechanisms underlying microvascular function and diastolic dysfunction, reinforcing ranolazine’s therapeutic potential in chronic heart failure management.
