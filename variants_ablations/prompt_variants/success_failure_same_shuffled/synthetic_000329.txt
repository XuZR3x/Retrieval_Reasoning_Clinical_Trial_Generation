**Clinical Trial Report: The Evaluation of Letrozole in Preventing Recurrent Disease in Postmenopausal Women with Hormone Receptor-Positive Early-Stage Breast Cancer - The RELY Study**

**RATIONALE:** Letrozole, an aromatase inhibitor, has been widely recognized for its role in decreasing estrogen levels and subsequently inhibiting the proliferation of hormone receptor-positive breast cancer cells. This trial sought to investigate the efficacy of letrozole in preventing disease recurrence in postmenopausal women with early-stage hormone receptor-positive breast cancer who had completed standard adjuvant therapy.

**PURPOSE:** This randomized phase III trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy of letrozole in maintaining disease-free survival (DFS) among postmenopausal women diagnosed with early-stage, hormone receptor-positive breast cancer after undergoing a standard course of adjuvant therapy. Given previous discrepancies in treatment outcomes related to patient selection and adherence, this trial aimed to establish clearer endpoints and patient protocols.

**OBJECTIVES:**  
**Primary Objective:** To compare the disease-free survival (DFS) at three years for patients treated with letrozole versus those who received observational management post-adjuvant therapy.  
**Secondary Objectives:**  
- To assess overall survival (OS) differences between treatment groups across a five-year follow-up period.  
- To analyze the incidence of disease recurrence (local and distant) and second malignancies in both groups.  
- To evaluate the patient adherence rate to the letrozole regimen and its impact on treatment outcomes.  
- To investigate the correlation between reported side effects and treatment persistence.

**OUTLINE:** This was a multi-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled study. Eligible patients were randomly assigned to one of two arms:  
- **Arm I:** Patients received oral letrozole 2.5 mg daily for 5 years.  
- **Arm II:** Patients were placed under observation without any intervention, serving as a control group.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Confirmed diagnosis of operable, non-inflammatory breast cancer.  
- Tumors must have been estrogen receptor-positive with no evidence of distant metastases post-treatment.  

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Female postmenopausal.  
- No unresolved comorbid conditions that would interfere with treatment adherence.  
- Clinical performance status of ECOG 0-1.  
- Completion of prior cancer treatments must have occurred at least 6 months before enrollment.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**  
- No ongoing endocrine therapy at study enrollment.  
- Any prior chemotherapy must have been completed at least six months prior to trial participation.

**TRIAL RESULTS:** The RELY study was undertaken with an expected accrual of 600 patients, however, only 350 patients were ultimately randomized. Several factors contributed to challenges encountered during recruitment and retention, including overly stringent selection criteria and patient reluctance arising from previous treatment experiences.

Primary efficacy analysis demonstrated that there was not a statistically significant difference in DFS rates between patients receiving letrozole and the control group (p=0.12). This lack of robust endpoint efficacy corroborated findings from past studies, raising concerns regarding the efficacy of letrozole in this specific population. Furthermore, variability in patient adherence was noted, with only 60% of patients maintaining compliance with the prescribed regimen over five years, a factor which may have further obscured potential treatment benefits.

**CONCLUSION:** The RELY study elucidated significant shortcomings in employing letrozole as a preventive treatment in this cohort, mirroring previous failures wherein restrictive patient selection and adherence challenges contributed to inconclusive results. The affirmation of insufficient endpoint efficacy alongside inadequate treatment duration calls for reconsideration of the therapeutic strategies involving letrozole, emphasizing the necessity for tailored approaches in managing hormone receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal populations.

The findings of this trial will ultimately serve as critical data to guide future research directions, incorporating broader patient demographics and reinforced adherence strategies to uphold treatment integrity and thereby assess the true efficacy of letrozole in combating breast cancer recurrence.
