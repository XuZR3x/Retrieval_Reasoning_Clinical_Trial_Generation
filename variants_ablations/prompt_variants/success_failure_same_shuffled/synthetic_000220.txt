**Failed Clinical Trial of Plerixafor: Plerixafor in Combination Therapy with Azacitidine for Older Patients with Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

**Summary:** This study failed to demonstrate meaningful clinical efficacy of plerixafor when used in conjunction with azacitidine for mobilizing peripheral blood progenitor cells (PBPCs) and enhancing treatment response in elderly patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML). The premature closure of the trial was attributed to multiple factors, including deficient efficacy in achieving target mobilization thresholds of CD34+ cells, significant adverse effects leading to unmanageable toxicity, and challenges related to patient selection criteria.

**Objective:** The primary objective was to evaluate the safety and efficacy of plerixafor in combination with azacitidine in mobilizing PBPCs and promoting safe transplantation in patients aged ≥ 65 years diagnosed with previously untreated acute myeloid leukemia.

**Study Design:** This multicenter, randomized, open-label, Phase II clinical trial aimed to enroll 80 participants. Patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either standard azacitidine treatment (Arm A) or azacitidine combined with plerixafor (Arm B). Plerixafor was administered at a dose of 240 μg/kg subcutaneously daily for five consecutive days, initiating 24 hours prior to azacitidine administration.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Diagnosis of de novo or secondary AML as per World Health Organization (WHO) classification.  
2. Age ≥ 65 years.  
3. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0-2.  
4. Adequate organ function (defined as: creatinine clearance > 50 mL/min, serum bilirubin < 2 x ULN, and AST/ALT < 2.5 x ULN).  
5. Signed informed consent.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Previous treatment for AML.  
2. Known hypersensitivity to plerixafor or azacitidine.  
3. Presence of significant cardiovascular disease, or other co-morbid conditions considered high-risk for treatment.  
4. Concurrent use of investigational agents within 30 days of enrollment.  
5. Pregnant or lactating women.

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary endpoint was the effective mobilization of ≥ 4 x 10^6 CD34+ cells/kg within two days of leukapheresis.  

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
1. The proportion of patients achieving the target CD34+ cell count within five days.  
2. Assessment of treatment-related adverse events leading to study discontinuation or dosage adjustment.  
3. Rates of engraftment success measured by days to PMN and platelet recovery post-transplantation.

**Results:** Out of the 40 patients enrolled in Arm B, only 12 successfully mobilized the target threshold of CD34+ cells, with a median collection of 3.1 x 10^6 cells/kg, failing to meet the prespecified primary endpoint. Furthermore, a notable incidence of severe adverse effects was recorded, including grade 3-4 myelosuppression in 22 patients, necessitating an early termination of the study due to intolerable toxicity. Additionally, the complex patient selection criteria considerably hampered generalizability, as enrolled patients were largely unrepresentative of the wider population diagnosed with AML.

**Conclusion:** The combination of plerixafor with azacitidine did not achieve sufficient efficacy in mobilizing PBPCs in elderly AML patients as illustrated by the failure to reach defined CD34+ cell collection thresholds. Coupled with significant adverse events leading to study discontinuation and restrictive inclusion parameters, this trial underscores the challenges faced in optimizing therapy for this vulnerable patient cohort. Further research is warranted to assess alternative strategies that may improve outcomes in this population.
