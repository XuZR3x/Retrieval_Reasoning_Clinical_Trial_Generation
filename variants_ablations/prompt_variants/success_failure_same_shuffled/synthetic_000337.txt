**Failed Clinical Trial of Imatinib Mesylate: Imatinib Mesylate in Treating Patients With Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)**

**Phase II Study of Imatinib Mesylate in Patients With Relapsed or Refractory Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

This trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of imatinib mesylate in patients diagnosed with relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia (AML). The study focuses on assessing potential therapeutic outcomes associated with the administration of imatinib mesylate, particularly for patients exhibiting specific molecular aberrations believed to be susceptible to tyrosine kinase inhibition.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To determine the overall response rate of imatinib mesylate in patients with relapsed or refractory AML that harbors c-KIT mutations or demonstrates overexpression of PDGFR.
  
**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the duration of overall survival (OS) in patients treated with imatinib mesylate.
II. To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) of treated patients.
III. To identify the toxicity profile and tolerability of imatinib mesylate in this patient population.
IV. To explore potential molecular markers associated with treatment-resistant subpopulations within the cohort.

**OUTLINE:** 
Participants receive oral imatinib mesylate at a standard dosage of 400 mg once daily for a period of 28 days, with treatment cycles to repeat every 28 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Efficacy is monitored through regular evaluation of blood counts and bone marrow biopsies at designated intervals.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Confirmed diagnosis of acute myeloid leukemia, relapsed or refractory.
- Documentation of c-KIT mutations or PDGFR overexpression via molecular profiling of tumor samples.
- Age ≥ 18 years with an ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Participants must have previously received at least one standard chemotherapy regimen.
- Adequate hepatic and renal function, defined as normal bilirubin levels, AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal, and a creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.
- Signed informed consent indicating understanding of the trial's nature.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of allergic reactions to imatinib mesylate or related compounds.
- Prior treatment with investigational agents or substances affecting the cytochrome P450 system within 30 days prior to enrollment.
- Concurrent malignancies requiring active treatment, except for non-melanoma skin cancers.
- Active central nervous system involvement.

**RESULTS:**
Despite initial hypotheses positing that imatinib mesylate might prove effective against AML characterized by specific mutations, the trial was terminated due to lack of significant therapeutic efficacy among the enrolled patients. **Limited Biomarker Expression** proved problematic, as a substantial number of trial subjects lacked sufficient expression of the targetable pathways (c-KIT, PDGFR), which rendered the therapeutic rationale ineffective, leading to preliminary endpoints not being met. 

**Patient Heterogeneity** was also a notable factor; varying molecular profiles and treatment histories resulted in a disproportionate response rate across the cohort. The anticipated uniformity in treatment response was hindered due to intrinsic tumor resistance mechanisms prevalent in subpopulations of AML, particularly those not dependent on kinase-mediated pathways, which ultimately facilitated **Intrinsic Tumor Resistance** to imatinib treatment.

In addition, analysis indicated that **Inadequate Drug Exposure** could have adversely affected outcomes; patients frequently experienced dosage alterations owing to **Adverse Effect Profile**, including significant myelosuppression and gastrointestinal toxicity, prompting reduced dosing regimens that regrettably impacted drug efficacy.

This clinical intervention highlights crucial challenges in targeted therapy within heterogeneous malignancies such as AML and emphasizes the necessity for biomarker-focused patient selection to optimize trial outcomes for future investigational pathways.
