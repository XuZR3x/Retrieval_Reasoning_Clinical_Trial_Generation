**Failed Clinical Trial of OSI-906: Evaluation of OSI-906 with Cytotoxic Agents in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**A Randomized, Phase 2 Study of Maintenance OSI-906 Plus Conventional Chemotherapy in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) after First-Line Treatment Failure**

This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of OSI-906 in combination with conventional cytotoxic agents (gemcitabine or carboplatin) in patients with advanced NSCLC who have failed first-line therapy. The study aimed to assess whether the addition of OSI-906 would improve progression-free survival (PFS) as compared to placebo with standard chemotherapy.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed locally advanced or metastatic stage IIIB or IV NSCLC.
- Documented disease progression following first-line platinum-based chemotherapy.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function, defined as neutrophil count ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L, platelet count ≥ 100 x 10^9/L, total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN, AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN or ≤ 5 x ULN if liver metastases are present, and serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 x ULN.
- Patients must be able to take oral medication.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior exposure to insulin-like growth factor receptor inhibitors or EGFR-targeted therapies (e.g., erlotinib, gefitinib).
- Active infection requiring systemic treatment or known history of central nervous system metastases.
- Significant cardiovascular disease, including uncontrolled hypertension or arrhythmias.
- Laboratory test abnormalities indicative of impaired organ function.

**Primary Endpoint:**
The primary endpoint of this study was to evaluate the effect of OSI-906 on progression-free survival (PFS). PFS was defined as the time from randomization to disease progression, based on RECIST v1.1 criteria, or death due to any cause, whichever occurred first.

**Study Design:**
Eligible patients were to be randomized in a 1:1 fashion to receive either OSI-906 plus chemotherapy or placebo plus chemotherapy, stratified by prior treatment type and ECOG performance status.

**Results:**
The trial faced several critical challenges leading to its premature termination:

1. **Insufficient Therapeutic Efficacy:** Interim analyses revealed that the combination of OSI-906 with gemcitabine or carboplatin did not result in statistically significant improvements in PFS compared to the placebo group. The lack of observable efficacy limited the potential benefits of OSI-906 and raised concerns about the drug's therapeutic activity in this advanced NSCLC cohort.

2. **Adverse Safety Profile:** Patients in the OSI-906 arm exhibited a higher incidence of adverse events, including gastrointestinal toxicities and metabolic disturbances, leading to increased rates of treatment discontinuation. This unacceptable safety profile prompted early termination of the trial, as the risk to patient safety outweighed any potential benefits.

3. **Patient Selection Challenges:** The predetermined strict eligibility criteria resulted in a limited pool of patients. The study did not capture the broader demographic of advanced NSCLC patients, potentially skewing results and contributing to the failure to observe positive treatment outcomes.

4. **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** The administration of OSI-906 alongside conventional cytotoxic agents such as gemcitabine showed complicated pharmacokinetic interactions, which may have diminished the efficacy of both OSI-906 and the chemotherapy agents. These interactions posed challenges to understanding the therapeutic potential of OSI-906.

5. **Limitations in Model Validation:** Preclinical data supporting the efficacy of OSI-906 were insufficiently predictive of clinical outcomes in humans, undermining the rationale for the trial. The trial's design relied on models with known limitations in translating preclinical findings to clinical benefits.

In conclusion, this Phase 2 trial aimed at evaluating the combination of OSI-906 with conventional chemotherapy in advanced NSCLC was terminated due to insufficient therapeutic efficacy, significant safety concerns, challenges in patient selection, pharmacodynamic interactions, and limitations in preclinical model validation. Further exploration into the therapeutic potential of OSI-906 may be warranted, but approaches must be refined to address the identified challenges.
