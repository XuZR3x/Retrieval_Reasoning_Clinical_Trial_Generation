**Title**: An Investigational Study of Perifosine in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Patients with Advanced Metastatic Solid Tumors

**Abstract**: This clinical trial evaluates the safety and efficacy of perifosine in combination with pembrolizumab (a PD-1 inhibitor) in patients with advanced metastatic solid tumors. The study examines whether the molecular targeting of perifosine can enhance the immune-mediated responses induced by pembrolizumab, potentially improving patient outcomes in solid tumors known for immune evasion. The trial is structured in two phases: phase I focuses on maximizing the tolerated dose (MTD) of perifosine, while phase II evaluates the overall response rates (ORR) in eligible patients.

**Introduction**: Perifosine, an oral alkylphospholipid agent, has shown promise in modulating apoptotic pathways and enhancing immunogenicity in various malignancies. The novel combination of perifosine with pembrolizumab seeks to address the challenges of immune resistance in advanced solid tumors. Through optimal dosing and real-time molecular assessments, we hypothetically aim to exploit synergistic effects that may arise from concurrent administration, thereby enhancing clinical efficacy and patient safety.

**Methods**: This study is a multi-center, open-label, randomized phase I/II trial. 

**Phase I**: 
- **Objective**: Determine MTD of perifosine when administered with pembrolizumab.
- **Participants**: Patients with advanced metastatic solid tumors who have progressed on standard therapies. 
- **Design**: Patients will receive escalating doses of perifosine (50 mg, 100 mg, and 150 mg) daily alongside a fixed dose of pembrolizumab (200 mg IV every 21 days).
- **Monitoring**: Safety assessments will include toxicity profiles, adverse events (AEs), and immune-related AEs (irAEs) monitored weekly via clinical evaluations and laboratory tests.

**Phase II**:
- **Objective**: Assess ORR following treatment with the established MTD of perifosine plus pembrolizumab.
- **Objectives**: The primary endpoint is the ORR (CR + PR + MR) assessed by RECIST 1.1 criteria every 8 weeks.
- **Secondary endpoints** include progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
1. Histologically confirmed advanced solid tumors.
2. Patients must be ≥ 18 years of age and possess an ECOG performance status of 0–2. 
3. Must have received at least one prior line of systemic therapy.
4. Adequate organ function defined as:
   - Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 × ULN
   - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 × ULN
   - Creatinine clearance ≥ 50 mL/min

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Individuals with prior immunotherapy within the last 4 weeks.
2. Active autoimmune diseases, history of severe allergic reactions to monoclonal antibodies.
3. Peripheral neuropathy ≥ grade 2.
4. Pregnancy or lactation.

**Outcome Measures**:
- **Primary**: The percentage of patients achieving CR, PR, or MR at 6-month intervals.
- **Secondary**: Evaluation of immunological parameters and biomarker assessments via serial blood draws and tumor biopsies.

**Discussion**: The combination of perifosine and pembrolizumab harnesses both cytotoxic and immune modulation pathways. Phase I results indicating an established MTD without prohibitive toxicity allow for an expanded analysis in phase II, where patient stratification based on molecular profiling may enhance response rates. Real-time pharmacodynamic assessments and continuous monitoring of immune responses could facilitate tailored interventions, potentially mitigating adverse outcomes.

**Conclusion**: This trial seeks to elucidate the role of perifosine as a complimentary agent to enhance the effectiveness of immune checkpoint inhibitors in solid tumors, optimizing patient care through a robust clinical research framework that prioritizes safety and efficacy. 

**Ethics**: The study protocol has been approved by the institutional review boards (IRBs) at all participating sites, and written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.
