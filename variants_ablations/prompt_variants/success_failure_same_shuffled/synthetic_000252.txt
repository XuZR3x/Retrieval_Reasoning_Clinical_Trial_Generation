**Title: Phase II Study of Regorafenib in Combination With Immunotherapy in Patients With Metastatic Colorectal Cancer**  

**Study Design:**  
The purpose of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of regorafenib in conjunction with an immune checkpoint inhibitor in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) who have progressed following standard therapies. This multicenter, open-label, phase II trial will examine the treatment's effect on progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and biomarker changes associated with treatment response.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Written informed consent must be obtained prior to participation.  
2. Patients aged 18 years or older.  
3. Histologically or cytologically confirmed metastatic colorectal carcinoma that is refractory to at least one line of standard therapy, including fluoropyrimidines and oxaliplatin.  
4. Documented disease progression per RECIST version 1.1 within the last 6 months.  
5. At least one measurable lesion as determined by imaging studies.  
6. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 to 2.  
7. Adequate organ function as defined by the following laboratory criteria:  
    - Neutrophils > 1500/mm³  
    - Platelets > 100,000/mm³  
    - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN  
    - ALT and AST ≤ 2.5 x ULN (≤ 5 x ULN for patients with liver involvement).  
8. Life expectancy of at least 3 months.  
9. Patients with defined biomarkers, such as MSI-high or dMMR status or PD-L1 expression, will be favored for enrollment.  
10. Patients must commit to compliance with oral medication and undergo monitoring throughout the study.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Prior treatment with regorafenib or any immune checkpoint inhibitors.  
2. Concurrent active malignancy requiring treatment.  
3. Significant cardiac disease, including uncontrolled hypertension (blood pressure > 150/90 mmHg).  
4. History of interstitial lung disease or active pulmonary conditions.  
5. Presence of severe comorbid conditions that, in the opinion of the investigator, could compromise patient safety or compliance.  
6. Pregnant or breastfeeding women, or those not using adequate contraception.  
7. Any prior serious allergic reaction to regorafenib or immune-oncology agents.  
8. Presence of brain metastases or other significant neurological conditions.  
9. Known active infection requiring systemic therapy.
  
**Study Objectives:**  
- Primary: To evaluate the disease control rate (DCR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR), partial response (PR), or stable disease (SD) lasting at least 12 weeks according to RECIST v1.1.  
- Secondary: To assess PFS, OS, safety profiles, and quality of life measurements using validated tools.    

**Intervention:**  
Patients will receive oral regorafenib at 160 mg daily in combination with an intravenous immune checkpoint inhibitor administered every 3 weeks for up to 12 months or until progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**Study Outcomes:**  
- At 6 months, the trial aims for a DCR of at least 50% in the overall patient population, hypothesizing the synergistic effect of regorafenib's dual mechanism of action—targeting angiogenesis and immune modulation—contributing to enhanced therapeutic efficacy in this progressive disease state.  
- Safety and tolerability will be evaluated through reported adverse events, laboratory values, and clinical assessments throughout the treatment period.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
The efficacy analysis will be conducted on an intent-to-treat basis, with statistical significance assessed via Kaplan-Meier survival curves for PFS and OS. Two-sided p-values of < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant. 

**Conclusion:**  
This study seeks to delineate the complementary action of regorafenib in conjunction with immunotherapy in a population enriched with specific biomarkers. By employing rigorous enrollment criteria, precise quantification of therapeutic responses, and standardizing assessment protocols, this trial stands to contribute significantly to the existing landscape of treatments for mCRC, particularly for patients who are frail or unfit for conventional chemotherapy.
