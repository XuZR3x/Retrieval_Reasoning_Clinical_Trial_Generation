**Successful clinical trial of carvedilol: CARVE-PAH Carvedilol for the Reduction of Ventricular Dysfunction in Patients with Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension**

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of carvedilol in enhancing right ventricular function and reducing cardiac workload in patients diagnosed with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). This study will specifically measure improvements in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR), and cardiac glucose metabolism as assessed by FDG-PET over a period of 12 months.

**Background:** Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is characterized by increased pulmonary artery pressure leading to right ventricular dysfunction and ultimately contributes significantly to morbidity and mortality. Traditional therapies predominantly focus on vasodilation of the pulmonary vasculature, leaving a treatment gap in addressing right ventricular pathology. Recent findings suggest that beta-adrenergic receptor blockade may reverse maladaptive remodeling in the heart, thereby improving cardiac function in PAH. Carvedilol's unique dual-action mechanism of beta-1 and beta-2 receptor blockade enables a reduction in heart rate and myocardial oxygen demand, which may facilitate favorable outcomes in PAH patients exhibiting intrinsic heart dysfunction.

**Study Design:** This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial will include 240 adult participants (18-65 years) diagnosed with PAH classified under WHO functional classes II-III. Subjects will be stratified based on baseline characteristics and randomly assigned to receive either carvedilol or placebo for 12 months. Comprehensive baseline evaluations will involve echocardiographic assessments, hemodynamic measurements through right heart catheterization, and FDG-PET imaging for cardiac glucose uptake assessment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults, aged 18 to 65 years
- Confirmed diagnosis of pulmonary arterial hypertension (WHO Class II-III)
- Stable on conventional PAH therapy for at least 30 days prior to enrollment
- Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study protocols

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Significant comorbidity, including untreated severe pulmonary or cardiac conditions
- Recent hospitalization for acute decompensated heart failure within the past 30 days
- Allergy or contraindication to carvedilol or other beta-blockers
- Current participation in other interventional clinical trials or recent (<30 days) administration of investigational drugs
- Pregnant or lactating women

**Outcomes:**
1. Primary Endpoint: Change in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) from baseline to 12 months, defined as an improvement of ≥8% at 12 months or ≥5% at 3 months in the absence of adverse clinical outcomes.
2. Secondary Endpoints: Changes in right ventricular function, pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR), and cardiac glucose utilization as evaluated by FDG-PET standardized uptake values (SUVs) at designated time points.

**Statistical Analysis:** An intent-to-treat approach will be utilized for analysis. Continuous variables will be assessed using appropriate statistical tests and will be expressed as mean (± standard deviation). Categorical variables will be analyzed using chi-square tests. A two-sided significance level of p<0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Expected Outcomes:** We hypothesize that the administration of carvedilol will facilitate significant improvements in LVEF among participants suffering from PAH, additionally demonstrating enhanced right ventricular function and a reduction in myocardial glucose uptake as measured by FDG-PET. These outcomes will substantiate the role of carvedilol as a novel therapeutic approach for treating right ventricular dysfunction in the context of pulmonary arterial hypertension.

**Clinical Significance:** With the potential to address the often-overlooked aspect of right ventricular dysfunction in PAH management, this trial underscores carvedilol's capacity not only to improve systolic function but also to effectuate favorable remodeling—thus proposing a paradigm shift in the therapeutic landscape of PAH treatment.
