**Failed Clinical Trial of SAR302503: Phase II Study in Patients With Myelofibrosis and Spleen Enlargement**

**A Phase II, Multicenter, Open-Label, Non-Comparative Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of SAR302503 in Patients With Myelofibrosis Diagnosis and Significant Splenomegaly**

**Primary Objective:**
- To assess the efficacy of SAR302503 administered once daily over a treatment period of 6 months, targeting a ≥35% reduction in spleen volume as assessed by Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) or Computed Tomography (CT) in participants diagnosed with primary myelofibrosis (PMF), post-polycythemia vera myelofibrosis (Post-PV MF), or post-essential thrombocythemia myelofibrosis (Post-ET MF) who were previously treated with ruxolitinib.

**Secondary Objectives:**
- To evaluate SAR302503's impact on myelofibrosis-related symptoms as determined by the modified Myelofibrosis Symptom Assessment Form (MFSAF).
- To explore the durability of any splenic response during the treatment period.
- To measure changes in spleen size by physical examination palpation at the conclusion of Cycle 6.
- To assess therapeutic effects on the JAK2 V617F allele burden post-treatment.
- To monitor the safety and tolerability profile of SAR302503 in this treated cohort.
- To document plasma concentrations of SAR302503 for pharmacokinetic analysis if deemed necessary.

**Study Duration:**
The study duration for each participant includes an assessment period of up to 28 days for screening eligibility, followed by a treatment phase of 6 months (6 cycles), concluding with an end-of-treatment (EOT) evaluation. Follow-up visits will occur approximately 30 days post-treatment cessation for participants who discontinue treatment before completion.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Confirmed diagnosis of myelofibrosis: PMF, Post-PV MF, or Post-ET MF according to the 2008 WHO classification.
- Prior treatment with ruxolitinib for a minimum duration of 28 days, with treatment cessation a minimum of 14 days prior to trial initiation.
- A clinical status of intermediate-1 with symptoms, intermediate-2, or high-risk classification via the Dynamic International Prognostic Scoring System.
- Baseline spleen size ≥5 cm below the costal margin.
- Adult participants (≥18 years) providing signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of splenectomy.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status > 2 at enrollment.
- Relevant laboratory or hepatic function abnormalities within 14 days prior to treatment initiation.
- Presence of active infections requiring antibiotic therapy prior to the start of treatment.
- Uncontrolled cardiovascular or other chronic conditions that may jeopardize participant safety.
- Prior participation in any investigational drug studies within 30 days before SAR302503 administration.

Given the prior trials' noted failures related to insufficient efficacy in spleen volume reduction, limited symptomatic improvement, tolerability concerns due to adverse events, lack of adequate pharmacodynamic outcomes, and inherent treatment resistance due to prior ruxolitinib therapy, this study anticipates potential challenges similar to its predecessors.

**Expected Outcomes:**
- The primary endpoint defines success as a response rate characterized by the proportion of participants achieving a ≥35% reduction in spleen volume following 6 treatment cycles.
- Expected evaluation of reducing symptom burdens and overall health improvement as captured through MFSAF scoring, contrasting with historical data to identify significant deviations.

**Status:**
This trial was terminated prematurely due to suboptimal efficacy outcomes observed during interim analyses. The data underscored the consistent failure of SAR302503 to elicit substantial reductions in spleen volume or symptom relief, correlating with previously documented pharmacological inefficacies within this patient population. Additionally, adverse events led to participant discontinuations that further compromised the integrity of the treatment protocol and overall study objectives. The results reinforce the critical need for innovative therapeutic avenues to address the complexities of myelofibrosis pathophysiology effectively.
