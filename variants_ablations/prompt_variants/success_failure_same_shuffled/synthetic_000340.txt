**Failed clinical trial of fluorouracil: Evaluation of Fluorouracil Combined With Atezolizumab for Patients With Metastatic Colorectal Cancer and Available Tumor Tissue**

**RATIONALE**: The combination of fluorouracil with atezolizumab, an anti-programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) monoclonal antibody, was hypothesized to improve clinical outcomes by eliciting a robust antitumor immune response in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC). Despite the theoretical basis for this combination, emerging data suggests that inherent tumor biology and pre-existing treatment histories may undermine treatment efficacy and patient benefit. 

**PURPOSE**: This randomized phase II trial aimed to assess the overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) of fluorouracil in combination with atezolizumab compared to fluorouracil monotherapy in a cohort of patients with RAS and BRAF wild-type metastatic colorectal cancer previously treated with anti-EGFR agents.

**OBJECTIVES**:  
**Primary Objective**:  
I. To evaluate the overall survival (OS) between patients receiving fluorouracil plus atezolizumab versus those receiving fluorouracil alone in the context of existing treatment resistance mechanisms.

**Secondary Objectives**:  
I. To analyze the progression-free survival (PFS) rates in both treatment arms.  
II. To assess the safety and tolerability profiles of both regimens, focusing on adverse events as defined by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).  
III. To evaluate the response rate (RR), including complete response, partial response, and stable disease, using RECIST v1.1 criteria.  

**OUTLINE**:  
Patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive:  
**ARM I**: Fluorouracil IV bolus over 15 minutes on days 1, 8, and 15 combined with atezolizumab IV every 2 weeks.  
**ARM II**: Fluorouracil IV bolus over 15 minutes on days 1, 8, and 15 without atezolizumab.  
Courses were repeated every 28 days, contingent upon disease stability and absence of severe toxicity. Follow-up occurred every 3 months post-treatment.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA**:  
- Histologically confirmed metastatic colorectal cancer with RAS and BRAF wild-type status.  
- Prior treatment with at least one anti-EGFR therapy.  
- Measurable disease based on RECIST v1.1.  
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.  
- Adequate hematologic parameters, including ANC ≥ 1,500 µL, Hgb ≥ 9 g/dL, and PLT ≥ 100,000 µL.  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA**:  
- History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to fluorouracil or atezolizumab.  
- Prior treatment with PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors.  
- Presence of uncontrolled intercurrent illnesses or psychiatric conditions limiting compliance.  
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES**:  
Primary and secondary endpoints included OS and PFS rates compared through Kaplan-Meier analysis, alongside safety evaluations focusing on toxicity profiles within each arm. However, this trial was terminated due to insufficient likelihood of meeting predefined criteria for clinical benefit among participants, largely influenced by the following failure factors:

1. **Treatment Resilience of Tumor Biology**: Tumor specimens indicated significant genetic heterogeneity, potentially allowing for evolutionary paths that confer resistance to both fluorouracil and immune-based therapies, particularly in patients previously exposed to multiple lines of treatment. 

2. **Acquired Resistance to Prior Therapies**: Patients exhibited a frequent history of resistance to anti-EGFR therapies leading to diminished expected responses to fluorouracil, further challenging the therapeutic potential of the added immunotherapy component.

3. **Suboptimal Patient Selection**: The intricate selection criteria could not adequately discern the biological nuances related to RAS and BRAF mutations and their subsequent impact on treatment response, verifying that resistance mechanisms persisted independent of previous treatment responses.

4. **Inadequate Therapeutic Synergy**: Observational data suggested a disconnect in the synergistic potential of combining fluorouracil with atezolizumab; patients underscored the potential for antagonistic effects which ultimately overshadowed the anticipated cumulative benefit of the dual regimen.

5. **High Toxicity and Associated Morbidity**: The combination therapy resulted in a high incidence of adverse events, leading to detrimental impacts on quality of life and treatment adherence, ultimately hindering the endpoint measures for survival and response assessment.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL**: A total of 150 patients were envisaged for enrollment; however, study closure occurred following the initial evaluation phase once interim analyses supported insufficient efficacy and safety profiles.

**CONCLUSION**: This clinical trial underscores the complexities inherent in treating metastatic colorectal cancer, accentuating the need for robust genetic profiling and enhanced understanding of the evolving tumor microenvironment to inform future therapeutic strategies.
