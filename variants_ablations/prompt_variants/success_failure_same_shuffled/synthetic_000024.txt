**Failed Clinical Trial of Azacitidine: Azacitidine With or Without Pembrolizumab in Treating Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) and High PD-L1 Expression**  
*A Randomized Phase II Trial of Azacitidine Plus Pembrolizumab Versus Pembrolizumab Alone in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) Exhibiting High Programmed Death-Ligand 1 (PD-L1) Expression*

This trial aimed to assess the efficacy of azacitidine when combined with pembrolizumab in treating patients diagnosed with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who exhibit high PD-L1 expression. The hypothesis underpinning this study was that azacitidine could reprogram the immune microenvironment and enhance the efficacy of pembrolizumab, a programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) axis inhibitor. 

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To estimate the overall response rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete or partial response according to RECIST 1.1 criteria in patients treated with azacitidine and pembrolizumab versus those receiving pembrolizumab alone.  

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in both treatment arms.  
II. To assess the safety profile and adverse event rates associated with the combination of azacitidine and pembrolizumab relative to pembrolizumab monotherapy.  
III. To investigate changes in tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) populations and associated biomarkers of response through serial tumor biopsies.

**OUTLINE:**  
Eligible participants were randomized to receive one of the following treatment regimens:  
- **Arm A (Azacitidine + Pembrolizumab):** Patients received azacitidine intravenously (IV) at 50 mg/m^2 for five consecutive days followed by a 28-day break, concomitantly with pembrolizumab administered via IV infusion at a dose of 200 mg every three weeks, for up to two years in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.  
- **Arm B (Pembrolizumab Alone):** Patients received pembrolizumab at the same specified intervals.

Following completion of therapy, patients were monitored for clinical response and adverse effects for a minimum of 24 months.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Histologically confirmed NSCLC with at least 50% of tumor cells expressing PD-L1 by IHC.  
- Patients must have received no prior systemic therapy for advanced disease.  
- ECOG performance status 0-2.  
- Adequate organ function, as defined by laboratory assessments.  
- Written informed consent to participate in the study.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Prior treatment with azacitidine, decitabine, or agents targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway.  
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy or a history of severe adverse reactions to monoclonal antibodies.  
- Presence of CNS metastases requiring treatment or with significant complications.  
- History of hypersensitivity to azacitidine or any component of the study drug formulations.

**RESULTS:**  
Despite the initial premise based on theoretical dual targeting immuno-oncology and epigenetic modulation through azacitidine, the clinical trial yielded suboptimal outcomes. The anticipated enhancement of immunogenicity and subsequent response rates was not realized. A significant proportion of patients in both arms exhibited rapid disease progression, reflecting the multifaceted resistance mechanisms present in NSCLC, including the development of adaptive resistance despite PD-L1 overexpression. Moreover, adverse demographic factors such as the inclusion of older adults with significant comorbidities likely contributed to the overall diminished therapeutic window for combined therapies. The lack of significant synergistic effects indicated that azacitidine failed to modulate the immune response adequately to improve outcomes when combined with pembrolizumab. Ultimately, the trial was terminated prematurely due to insufficient evidence of clinical benefit and concerns surrounding patient safety.

**CONCLUSION:**  
The failure of this clinical trial underscores the complex interplay between metastatic lung cancer biology and the immune microenvironment. It illustrates the necessity for refined selection criteria, including patient characteristics and biomarker stratification, to improve therapeutic efficacy in the realm of epigenetic agents and immune checkpoint inhibitors. Insights gained from this study will inform future investigation designs and therapeutic strategies aimed at enhancing the potential of azacitidine in oncological applications.
