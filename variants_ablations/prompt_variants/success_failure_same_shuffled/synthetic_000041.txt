**Failed Clinical Trial of Entinostat: Evaluation of Entinostat in Combination With Pembrolizumab in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Study Title:** A Phase 1b/2 Study of Entinostat in Combination With Pembrolizumab in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Who Have Progressed After Previous Therapies

**Clinical Trial Identifier:** SNDX-275-0701

**Background:** Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality. Immunotherapeutic approaches, particularly immune checkpoint inhibitors, have transformed the treatment landscape. Prior studies involving histone deacetylase inhibitors, including entinostat, in combination with immunotherapies have demonstrated limited efficacy, thus prompting further investigation into the pharmacodynamics and patient-related variables impacting treatment response.

**Study Overview:** This trial aimed to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of entinostat when combined with pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 therapy, in patients with advanced NSCLC. Despite initial hypotheses regarding the potential for synergistic therapeutic effects, outcomes indicated that entinostat did not significantly enhance clinical benefit when partnered with pembrolizumab.

**Primary Objectives:**
1. To determine the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of entinostat in combination with pembrolizumab.
2. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) in patients treated with the combination therapy.

**Secondary Objectives:**
1. To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) rates in the study population.
2. To analyze the pharmacokinetic interactions between entinostat and pembrolizumab.
3. To explore the relationship between minimal residual disease (MRD) markers and treatment outcomes.

**Design:** The study consisted of two phases: a dose-escalation phase followed by a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase. Eligible participants were assigned to receive either the entinostat-pembrolizumab combination or pembrolizumab alone.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC not amenable to curative surgery or radiation.
2. At least one prior systemic therapy regimen, including chemotherapeutic agents and/or anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapies.
3. ECOG performance status of 0-1.
4. Measurable disease per RECIST v1.1 criteria.
5. Adequate hematologic and organ function as defined in the protocol.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients with a history of autoimmune disease or those requiring chronic immunosuppressive therapy.
2. Prior treatment with entinostat or other histone deacetylase inhibitors.
3. Presence of active CNS metastases.
4. Significant comorbidities that may interfere with study participation or safety.

**Safety Evaluation:** Adverse events (AEs) were closely monitored throughout the trial. Frequent laboratory testing and clinical evaluations were performed to detect toxicities. Adverse events of special interest included immunologic events and gastrointestinal disturbances consistent with previous findings regarding entinostat.

**Results:** The study included 120 participants, of whom 58 were randomized to the entinostat-pembrolizumab group while 62 were assigned to pembrolizumab alone. Analysis revealed that entinostat did not enhance the efficacy of pembrolizumab, with the ORR being 18% in the combination arm compared to 21% in the control arm. Pharmacokinetic assessments showed no significant alterations in pembrolizumab pharmacodynamics due to entinostat co-administration, pointing towards limited effective immune modulation.

**Discussion:** The combination regimen failed to achieve the anticipated therapeutic synergy, primarily due to:
1. Limited efficacy of entinostat, which did not translate into improved outcomes when combined with an immunotherapeutic variant.
2. A discernible adverse event profile, including gastrointestinal complications and fatigue, leading to treatment discontinuation in 25% of the study population.
3. A highly specific patient cohort that may have resulted in non-generalizable findings across the broader NSCLC population.

**Conclusions:** The Phase 1b/2 clinical trial of entinostat in conjunction with pembrolizumab reached a premature cessation due to lack of efficacy and safety concerns. Future clinical explorations should consider alternative combinations or dosing strategies to enhance potential therapeutic outcomes in patients with advanced NSCLC, while taking heed of the pharmacokinetic insights garnered from this investigation.

**Follow-Up:** Participants will be monitored for 30 days following treatment discontinuation to assess long-term safety and any delayed-onset adverse effects, with results reported in a subsequent longitudinal analysis.
