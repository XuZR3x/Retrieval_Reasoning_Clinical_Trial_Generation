<ClinicalTrial>
  <Title>Randomized Double-Blind Assessment of Asenapine in Combination with Psychosocial Intervention for Patients with Bipolar I Disorder</Title>
  <StudyID>A7502001</StudyID>
  <Phase>Phase 3</Phase>
  <Objective>This trial aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of asenapine in patients with bipolar I disorder experiencing acute manic or mixed episodes, when combined with a structured psychosocial intervention.</Objective>
  
  <Design>
    <Type>Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind</Type>
    <Duration>12 weeks</Duration>
    <Methodology>The trial will include a psychosocial component, incorporating cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) alongside pharmacologic treatment with asenapine or placebo. Periodic assessments based on established scales will monitor patient progress.</Methodology>
  </Design>
  
  <InclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>- Age between 18 and 65 years</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- Diagnosed with bipolar I disorder, presenting with acute manic or mixed episodes as per DSM-5 criteria</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- Able and willing to provide informed consent</Criterion>
  </InclusionCriteria>
  
  <ExclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>- Current unstable medical condition or clinically significant laboratory abnormality</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- History of hypersensitivity to asenapine or other antipsychotic medications</Criterion>
    <Criterion>- Concurrent treatment with any medication that could confound study results</Criterion>
  </ExclusionCriteria>
  
  <PrimaryOutcome>
    <Measure>Change in Young Mania Rating Scale (Y-MRS) score from baseline to week 12</Measure>
    <Details>The Y-MRS will be utilized to quantitatively measure the severity of manic symptoms over the course of the study. </Details>
  </PrimaryOutcome>
  
  <SecondaryOutcomes>
    <Outcome>Change in Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale - Excited Component (PANSS-EC) scores</Outcome>
    <Outcome>Assessment of Clinical Global Impression (CGI) scores for both severity and improvement</Outcome>
    <Outcome>Patient Functional Status evaluated through the Global Assessment of Functioning (GAF) scale</Outcome>
  </SecondaryOutcomes>
  
  <ExpectedResults>
    <Result>It is anticipated that the combination of asenapine with a structured psychosocial intervention will yield statistically significant improvements in manic symptomatology, as indicated by a greater decrease in Y-MRS scores compared to placebo.</Result>
    <Result>The integration of psychosocial strategies is expected to enhance patient engagement and long-term outcomes, reinforcing the initial findings regarding the rapid effects of asenapine observed in previous studies.</Result>
  </ExpectedResults>

  <Rationale>
    <Point>The inclusion of well-defined diagnostic criteria facilitates homogeneous patient selection, minimizing variability and enhancing efficacy detection for asenapine.</Point>
    <Point>Employing rigorous exclusion criteria reinforces the fidelity of observed outcomes, eliminating confounding comorbidities that could impact efficacy and safety.</Point>
    <Point>A double-blind, placebo-controlled methodology across multi-tiered assessments boosts the validity of findings, effectively reducing potential biases.</Point>
    <Point>The addition of a psychosocial intervention, as a component of the study design, could potentially amplify treatment effects, addressing both pharmacological and psychosocial dimensions of acute mania.</Point>
    <Point>Measured rapid efficacy could establish asenapine as a versatile treatment option in both medication and comprehensive multidisciplinary frameworks for managing acute agitation and manic episodes.</Point>
  </Rationale>
</ClinicalTrial>
