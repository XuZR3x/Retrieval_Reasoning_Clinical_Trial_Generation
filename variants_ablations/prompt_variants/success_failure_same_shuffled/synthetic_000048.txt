**Title**: Multicenter Phase II Trial of Sorafenib with Pembrolizumab for Advanced Hepatocellular Carcinoma in Patients with Unresectable Disease

**Abstract**: This multicenter, open-label, Phase II clinical trial aims to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of sorafenib in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The rationale for this study is grounded in the successful integration of sorafenib with checkpoint inhibitors, which has shown promise in enhancing therapeutic outcomes by targeting various immune pathways and tumor growth simultaneously.

**Study Design**: This is a Phase II, open-label, multicenter trial involving a total of 150 participants diagnosed with unresectable HCC. Eligible patients will be stratified based on their baseline disease characteristics and prior treatment history. The study will consist of a treatment phase followed by a follow-up phase.

**Intervention**: Participants will receive sorafenib at a dose of 400 mg orally twice daily, concurrently with pembrolizumab administered intravenously at a fixed dose of 200 mg every three weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Confirmed diagnosis of unresectable HCC based on imaging and histological evidence.
- ECOG Performance Status 0-1.
- Adequate hematologic, renal, and hepatic function as assessed by:
  - Hemoglobin > 9.0 g/dL.
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) > 1,500/mm³.
  - Platelet count > 100,000/mm³.
  - Total bilirubin < 1.5 times ULN.
  - ALT and AST < 2.5 times ULN (≤ 5 x ULN for patients with liver involvement).
  - GFR > 30 ml/min.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Known history of autoimmune disease.
- Active cardiac disease, including congestive heart failure (CHF) > Class II NYHA.
- Current participation in another clinical trial involving investigational agents.
- Uncontrolled hypertension or significant bleeding diatheses.
- History of organ transplant or significant non-healing wounds.
- Evidence of other malignancies within the last 5 years, with exceptions defined.

**Primary Objectives**:
1. To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) to the combination therapy.
2. To assess the safety and tolerability profile of the treatment regimen.

**Secondary Objectives**:
1. To determine the duration of response (DoR).
2. To analyze overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS).
3. To establish biomarker correlations with treatment outcomes.

**Study Procedures**: Participants will undergo baseline assessments, including imaging studies, serum tests, and biomarker analysis to identify predictive factors related to sorafenib responsiveness. Efficacy will be assessed via imaging at 6-week intervals, while safety will be monitored through frequent blood tests and clinical evaluations.

**Endpoints**: The primary endpoint is the overall response rate (ORR) defined by the proportion of participants achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) per RECIST 1.1 criteria. Secondary endpoints comprise duration of response (DoR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety assessments.

**Statistical Analysis**: Descriptive statistics will be utilized to summarize baseline characteristics and clinical outcomes. The ORR will be evaluated using a 95% confidence interval. Survival analyses will be conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method.

**Rationale**: Previous studies have indicated that combining sorafenib with immune checkpoint inhibitors could potentially yield synergistic effects by enhancing anti-tumor immunity while simultaneously inhibiting tumor growth pathways. The trial aims to broaden the therapeutic scope of sorafenib, particularly in the setting of advanced HCC, where effective treatment options remain limited.

**Expected Outcomes**: It is hypothesized that the combination regimen will demonstrate an improved ORR and extend PFS and OS compared to historical controls receiving sorafenib monotherapy. Findings may provide critical insights into personalized treatment strategies involving targeted and immunotherapeutic approaches in HCC management.

**Conclusion**: This trial is poised to elucidate the role of sorafenib in conjunction with pembrolizumab for advanced HCC, potentially establishing a new standard of care for patients with unresectable disease.
