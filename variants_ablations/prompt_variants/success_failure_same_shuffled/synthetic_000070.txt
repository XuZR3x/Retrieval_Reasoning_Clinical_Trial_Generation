Failed clinical trial of sarilumab: Multicenter Study Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Sarilumab in Patients With Active Psoriatic Arthritis 

A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of Sarilumab in Adult Patients With Active Psoriatic Arthritis 

Primary Objective: 
- To evaluate the efficacy of Sarilumab in achieving a 20% improvement in Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI20) at 12 weeks compared to placebo. 

Secondary Objectives: 
- To assess the proportion of participants achieving PASI 50 and 75 responses.
- To evaluate changes in the Health Assessment Questionnaire Disability Index (HAQ-DI).
- To assess the safety profile and tolerability of Sarilumab in participants with Psoriatic Arthritis.
- To explore the pharmacokinetic parameters of Sarilumab in this cohort.

The maximum study duration for each participant was set at 32 weeks, which included: 
- A screening period (up to 4 weeks); 
- A double-blind treatment period of 24 weeks; 
- A 4-week follow-up period post-treatment. 

Inclusion Criteria: 
- Adult patients aged 18–65 years diagnosed with Psoriatic Arthritis as per the Classification Criteria for Psoriatic Arthritis (CASPAR).
- Active disease defined by at least 5 tender joints and 5 swollen joints, a serum C-reactive protein (CRP) level of ≥ 10 mg/L, and a PASI score of ≥ 3.
- Prior treatment failure or intolerance to at least one conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (csDMARD) or biologic therapy.

Exclusion Criteria: 
- Participants with significant comorbidities affecting safety or efficacy evaluation (e.g., severe cardiovascular disease, active infections).
- Previous treatment with any biological DMARD within 12 weeks prior to screening or anti-TNF agents within 24 weeks prior; 
- Patients with a history of non-response to any IL-6 antagonists.

The study aimed to measure the percentage of participants achieving a PASI20 response as the primary endpoint, defined as a 20% reduction in PASI score from baseline. Secondary endpoints included PASI50 and PASI75, defining a 50% and 75% improvement in PASI score, respectively. 

An Adverse Event (AE) was defined as any untoward medical occurrence regardless of causality, while Serious Adverse Events (SAEs) included deaths or life-threatening conditions. It was anticipated that this cohort would experience TEAEs throughout the study.

Despite the rigorous design, several pre-defined efficacy endpoints failed to achieve statistical significance. The following reasons contributed to the overall trial failure:

1. **Insufficient Efficacy Outcomes**: The proportion of participants achieving PASI20 at Week 12 was markedly lower than anticipated, indicating inherent limitations in sarilumab's efficacy specifically in individuals with Psoriatic Arthritis. The duality of the inflammatory pathways receptor could have rendered the therapeutic action of sarilumab suboptimal against the multifaceted disease mechanisms in this patient population.

2. **Adverse Event Profile**: A concerning incidence of TEAEs was observed among participants, leading to increased study discontinuations. Specifically, SAEs, including gastrointestinal perforations and severe infections, significantly hindered participant retention and fueled recruitment challenges. 

3. **Patient Population Considerations**: The stringent inclusion criteria may have inadvertently isolated a patient population with characteristics disproportionately reflective of previous treatment failures. Many participants had failed multiple therapeutic avenues, suggesting baseline disease severity or behavior that rendered them less responsive to sarilumab.

4. **Potential Pharmacokinetic Variability**: Analysis indicated that pharmacokinetic variability significantly impacted the drug’s therapeutic levels among participants. This inconsistency may have hindered achieving optimal therapeutic concentrations necessary for clinical efficacy. 

5. **Interaction with Established Therapies**: Prior treatment with established therapies revealed a trend in observed responses favoring traditional agents over newer biologics like sarilumab, complicating direct efficacy comparisons. Established treatment regimens’ dominance likely diminished the neuro-inflammatory pathways that sarilumab was meant to target.

The aggregate data from this clinical study indicated an overall failure to meet primary and secondary endpoints, elucidating sarilumab's challenges within this therapeutic domain. Further investigation into dosing strategies, alternative patient population sub-grouping, and pharmacodynamics adjustments is warranted.
