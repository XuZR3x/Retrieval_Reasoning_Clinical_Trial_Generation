**Failed Clinical Trial of Carboplatin: Phase II Study of Carboplatin and Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**BACKGROUND:** This phase II study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of carboplatin in combination with pembrolizumab in patients diagnosed with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have not received prior chemotherapy. Pembrolizumab, an immune checkpoint inhibitor, is designed to enhance the anti-tumor response by blocking the interaction between PD-1 receptor and its ligands, PD-L1 and PD-L2. The study hypothesized that the addition of pembrolizumab to carboplatin-based chemotherapy would yield a superior objective response rate.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVE:** 
I. To determine the confirmed objective response rate (ORR) to the combination therapy in patients with advanced NSCLC.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:** 
I. To assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).  
II. To evaluate the safety profile and tolerability of the combination therapy.  
III. To explore biomarkers predictive of response to therapy.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, open-label trial enrolled patients who received carboplatin intravenously (IV) over 30 minutes on day 1, followed by pembrolizumab administered every 3 weeks for up to 2 years. Treatment continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or completion of planned therapy. Patients were followed for safety and efficacy outcomes up to 5 years.

**ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA:**  
- **Inclusion Criteria:**  
  - Patients aged > 18 years with histologically confirmed stage IV NSCLC.  
  - Measurable disease as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
  - No prior systemic therapy for metastatic NSCLC.  
  - Sufficient organ function with specific laboratory parameters (absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³, total bilirubin < 1.5 mg/dL, AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 ULN, creatinine clearance > 60 mL/min).  

- **Exclusion Criteria:**  
  - Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to any components of carboplatin or pembrolizumab.  
  - Evidence of uncontrolled intercurrent illnesses, including active infections, cardiovascular diseases, or psychiatric disorders that may impair treatment adherence.  
  - Prior treatment with immunotherapy or any investigational agents.  
  - Known brain metastases not previously treated.

**RESULTS:** Despite the promising laboratory data supporting the use of pembrolizumab in combination with carboplatin, the trial was terminated early due to interrelated factors leading to suboptimal outcomes:

1. **Combinatorial Interactions:** Notably, adverse drug interactions between pembrolizumab and carboplatin led to increased rates of severe immune-mediated adverse events, subsequently requiring dose reductions and treatment discontinuation. 

2. **Eligibility Constraints:** The strict eligibility criteria potentially excluded patients who would have benefited from therapy, significantly limiting the diversity of the cohort and impacting the generalizability of results.

3. **Primary Endpoints:** The selected primary endpoint, ORR, did not reflect accurate therapeutic efficacy. The assessment of clinical benefits was hindered by inadequate timing of imaging studies, particularly in capturing early responses due to inherent therapy-induced immune dynamics.

4. **Treatment Administration Scheduling:** The administration schedule of carboplatin demonstrated inadequate optimization in conjunction with pembrolizumab. Insufficient dosing intervals undermined the anticipated synergy between the chemotherapy and the immunotherapy, reflecting no substantial improvement over historical results with carboplatin monotherapy.

5. **Safety Profile Complications:** A concerning pattern of uncontrolled adverse events, particularly severe hematologic toxicities and immune-related side effects, led to increased treatment discontinuations. Patients experienced prolonged hospitalizations due to complications, ultimately affecting the overall therapeutic efficacy and safety interpretation.
