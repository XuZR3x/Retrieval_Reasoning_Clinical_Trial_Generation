**Failed Clinical Trial of Raltegravir: Evaluation of Raltegravir’s Impact on Cardiovascular and Immunological Outcomes in HIV-Infected Patients with Prominent Comorbidities**

**Study Title:** Effects of Raltegravir on Cardiovascular Risk Factors and Immune Activation in HIV-Infected Patients with Comorbid Conditions: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Background:** The link between HIV infection and increased cardiovascular disease (CVD) incidence remains a critical concern, especially among individuals with existing comorbidities. Studies indicate that comorbidity can confound the immunological and cardiovascular profiles of HIV-infected patients. Raltegravir, as an integrase inhibitor, has previously been associated with improved immunological responses, yet its efficacy in this particular cohort has not been adequately evaluated. 

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study was to assess the impact of a raltegravir-based regimen on cardiovascular risk factors, such as platelet reactivity and immune activation levels, compared to continuing non-integrase inhibitor-based antiretroviral therapy in HIV-infected patients with significant comorbid conditions.

**Study Design:** A multicentric, open-label, randomized controlled trial was designed to enroll patients from multiple infectious disease clinics. The study was intended to be double-blinded; however, the nature of participant monitoring resulted in unblinded assessments.

**Population:** The inclusion criteria targeted HIV-infected adults aged 18 years or older with documented severe comorbidities (cardiovascular disease, diabetes, kidney dysfunction). Patients were required to have stable HIV viral suppression (<50 copies/mL) alongside significant immune activation (elevated CD8 T-cell activation markers). The power analysis suggested an enrollment of 300 participants, divided into two arms: a switch group (n=150) receiving raltegravir addition and a control group (n=150) continuing their current regimen.

**Intervention:** Participants in the switch group were administered a raltegravir-containing regimen for a 24-week period, while the control group continued their pre-existing non-integrase inhibitor ART without modification.

**Endpoints:** The primary endpoint was the assessment of platelet reactivity and associated inflammation markers (P-selectin and CD40L levels) measured at baseline and at 24 weeks. Secondary outcomes included changes in CD4 recovery rates and soluble P-selectin levels.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Documented HIV infection
- Age ≥ 18 years
- Stable ART for at least 12 months with sustained HIV RNA < 50 copies/mL
- Significant baseline comorbidities (cardiovascular disease, renal impairment)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Hypersensitivity to raltegravir or components of the regimen
- Active opportunistic infections or malignancies
- History of any cardiovascular surgeries in the past year
- Participants on chronic anticoagulant therapy

**Results:** The trial ultimately experienced pronounced challenges during implementation. The anticipated sample size was reduced due to participant recruitment difficulties arising from stringent exclusion criteria and patient aversion to switching regimens. Moreover, high heterogeneity was encountered due to underlying comorbid conditions across participants, leading to substantial variability in clinical responses. 

Statistical analyses indicated that, while there were numerically favorable trends observed in the switch group, the difference between the groups was not statistically significant (p>0.05) due to inadequate sample size and power. Many individuals exhibited inconsistent platelet reactivity patterns attributed to pre-existing comorbidities, which could not be accounted for adequately in the study's design.

**Conclusion:** The study was prematurely halted due to the inability to meet the predetermined statistical criteria for significance. The findings underscored critical challenges, such as inadequate sample size attributed to stringent eligibility criteria, which led to insufficient power for detecting potential benefits of raltegravir in a complex patient population. Additionally, the multifaceted nature of comorbidities significantly confounded the study outcomes, emphasizing the need for more comprehensive trial designs that can accommodate the complexity of real-world HIV-infected populations.

This trial serves as a reminder of the importance of addressing underlying patient heterogeneity and multimorbidity in designing future studies evaluating antiretroviral therapies like raltegravir, aimed at improving cardiovascular and immunological outcomes among HIV-infected individuals.
