**Clinical Trial Title: Efficacy of Estradiol in Alleviating Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause: A Randomized Controlled Trial**

**Protocol Number:** ESTR-GSM-2024

**Study Design:** A Phase 2, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled, Multi-center Trial assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Estradiol in Postmenopausal Women with Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause (GSM)

**Objectives:** 
The primary objective is to evaluate the efficacy of a low-dose formulation of estradiol delivered via a novel transdermal patch in alleviating symptoms associated with Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause. Secondary objectives involve assessing the safety profile of the intervention and conducting detailed analyses of subjective and objective measures of vaginal health.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Female subjects aged 45 to 75 years at the time of randomization.
2. At least 12 months of spontaneous amenorrhea or surgical menopause (hysterectomy with or without bilateral oophorectomy).
3. Clinical diagnosis of moderate to severe genitourinary symptoms as assessed by the Genitourinary Symptom Assessment Index (GSAI) during the screening visit.
4. Vaginal pH greater than 5.0 and at least 5% superficial epithelial cells on cytology.
5. Subjects exhibiting significant distress from genitourinary symptoms as identified by self-report at screening visit.
6. Comprehension of and willingness to participate in the study protocol.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Current or past history of estrogen-dependent neoplasia, endometrial hyperplasia, or any malignancy within the last five years (excluding basal cell carcinoma).
2. Uncontrolled hypertension or thromboembolic disorders.
3. History of liver dysfunction, nephropathy, or any conditions that the investigator deems unsuitable for participation.
4. Use of hormonal treatment (including SERM, progestins, or hormonal contraceptives) within the last 8 weeks or any vaginal products within the past 4 weeks before baseline screening.
5. Pregnancy or lactation at the time of enrollment, or positive urine pregnancy test.

**Intervention:**
Subjects will be randomized to receive either the estradiol transdermal patch (delivering 0.05 mg/day) or a placebo patch applied once weekly for a duration of 12 weeks.

**Outcome Measures:**
1. Primary outcome will be the change in GSAI scores from baseline to 12 weeks, providing a clear quantitative measure of symptom relief.
2. Secondary outcomes will include improvements in vaginal atrophy assessments, including vaginal pH, cytology, and patient-reported outcomes on sexual function and quality of life measured at 4, 8, and 12 weeks.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Descriptive statistics will be generated for all baseline characteristics and outcome measures. Between-groups comparisons of primary and secondary endpoints will utilize ANOVA for continuous outcomes and Chi-square tests for categorical variables. Significance will be set at p < 0.05.

**Ethical Considerations:**
The trial will adhere to Good Clinical Practice guidelines and will be conducted following approval by Institutional Review Boards. Informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

**Conclusion:**
With a meticulously designed randomized, double-blind study structure and a targeted demographic, it is anticipated that the data yielded will substantiate the efficacy of estradiol in ameliorating the symptoms of Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause, corroborated by a comprehensive safety assessment approach. The study emphasizes rigorous recruitment aligned with stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria, facilitating a clear evaluation of estradiol's therapeutic profile in a clinically relevant population.
