**Success Clinical Trial of Prednisone: Assessment of Oral Prednisone in the Treatment of Acute Exacerbations of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD).**  
**A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Oral Prednisone in Patients with Acute Exacerbations of COPD.**

This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of oral prednisone in the management of acute exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) compared to placebo. This investigation is motivated by previous findings that corticosteroids, particularly prednisone, have shown promise in improving pulmonary function and reducing hospitalization rates among patients experiencing exacerbations.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adults aged 40 years and older with a clinical diagnosis of COPD (Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease GOLD stage II-IV).  
- Documented history of at least one exacerbation in the previous year requiring systemic corticosteroids or antibiotics.  
- Current exacerbation characterized by increased dyspnea, volume and/or purulence of sputum, and/or increased cough.  
- FEV1/FVC ratio < 70%.  
- Signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Recent hospitalization (within 30 days) for exacerbation of COPD.  
- Previous known allergy or hypersensitivity to prednisone.  
- Patients with active infections requiring antibiotics or those recently treated with systemic corticosteroids (within 4 weeks of screening).  
- Patients with significant comorbidities such as congestive heart failure, active malignancy, or uncontrolled diabetes that may interfere with study outcomes.  
- Unable to provide informed consent.

**Objective:**  
To determine the efficacy of oral prednisone (40 mg once daily for 5 days) in improving clinical outcomes during acute COPD exacerbations compared to placebo, as measured by change in dyspnea scores using the Modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) scale, and the time to recovery of baseline pulmonary function (as determined by FEV1).

**Endpoints:**  
- Primary endpoint: Reduction in mMRC dyspnea score from baseline to Day 7.  
- Secondary endpoints include:  
  - Change in FEV1 from baseline to Day 7.  
  - Time to recovery of baseline pulmonary function (FEV1).  
  - Rates of hospital admission and re-exacerbation within 30 days post-treatment.  
  - Safety profile as assessed by the incidence of adverse effects related to treatment.

**Sample Size:**  
A total of 200 patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive oral prednisone or placebo for 5 days.

This study utilizes a robust double-blind, randomized controlled trial design, which minimizes confounding variables and enhances the reliability of the outcomes. Rigorous selection of subjects based on well-defined inclusion criteria ensures that the cohort represents a population likely to benefit from corticosteroid treatment, thereby aligning with previous successful interventions utilizing similar methodologies.

Statistical analyses will assess pivotal outcomes, with anticipated significant reductions in dyspnea scores and improved pulmonary function, correlating with the established benefits of optimized corticosteroid dosing protocols. The results of this trial aim to enhance treatment guidelines and therapeutic approaches for managing acute exacerbations in COPD, thereby contributing to improved patient outcomes and clinical acceptance of prednisone as a foundational therapy in exacerbation management.
