**Clinical Trial Report: Nebivolol Efficacy in Reducing Post-Prandial Hypertension and Endothelial Dysfunction in Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Patients**

**Title:** Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy of Nebivolol in Reducing Post-Prandial Hypertension and Improving Endothelial Function in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

**Background:** The correlation between post-prandial hyperglycemia and elevated blood pressure has been well-documented in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), leading to a significant increase in cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. This study aims to assess the impact of nebivolol on blood pressure regulation during the post-prandial state and its effects on endothelial function, hypothesizing that the unique pharmacological profile of nebivolol can enhance nitric oxide bioavailability, thereby improving vascular health.

**Objectives:** 
1. To evaluate the efficacy of nebivolol in reducing systolic and diastolic blood pressure during the post-prandial period compared with placebo in patients with T2DM.
2. To assess the change in endothelial function, measured by flow-mediated dilation (FMD), from baseline to twelve weeks of treatment with nebivolol.

**Study Design:** This will be a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted over a period of 12 weeks. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either 5 mg of nebivolol or placebo once daily.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Males and females aged 30 to 65 years.
- Diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus for a minimum of 2 years.
- Current treatment regimen includes dietary management and oral hypoglycemic agents but not insulin.
- Blood pressure readings of 130-160 mmHg systolic and/or 80-100 mmHg diastolic at baseline.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Secondary causes of hypertension, including renal artery stenosis.
- History of significant cardiovascular disease, including myocardial infarction or stroke within the past year.
- Contraindications to beta-blockers, including asthma or severe bradycardia (HR < 60 bpm).
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- Patients currently involved in other clinical trials or previously enrolled in this study.

**Outcome Measures:**
- **Primary Outcome:** Change in post-prandial blood pressure measured at 1-hour intervals post-meal over a 12-week period.
- **Secondary Outcome:** Improvement in endothelial function as measured by flow-mediated dilation (FMD) at baseline and after 12 weeks.

**Statistical Analysis:** A power analysis will be conducted to determine the appropriate sample size to detect significant differences between groups. Data will be analyzed using ANCOVA to account for baseline values, with a significance level set at p < 0.05.

**Study Rationale:** The design employs stringent eligibility criteria that create a homogenous population of patients with T2DM, minimizing confounding variables and enhancing result reliability. The unique mechanism of action of nebivolol, particularly its ability to enhance nitric oxide release, positions it as a favorable agent for targeting the pathophysiological abnormalities associated with hypertension in T2DM. Furthermore, the longitudinal follow-up allows for assessment of sustained antihypertensive effects and endothelial function improvement, vital in managing chronic diabetic complications.

**Anticipated Results:** It is hypothesized that treatment with nebivolol will result in statistically significant reductions in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure during the post-prandial period, alongside marked improvements in endothelial function as indicated by increased FMD values. Advanced biomarkers, including plasma nitric oxide levels, will be measured to further substantiate clinical findings with underlying biochemical correlates.

**Conclusion:** This trial aims to provide robust evidence on the efficacy of nebivolol for managing post-prandial hypertension and improving endothelial health in patients with type 2 diabetes, which may have profound implications in reducing cardiovascular risk and enhancing patient quality of life.
