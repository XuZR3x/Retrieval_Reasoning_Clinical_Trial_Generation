**Failed Clinical Trial of PRO 140: Efficacy and Safety Assessment in a Cohort with Variable Viral Load and Suboptimal Adherence**

**PRO 140 2104: A Phase 2b, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study of PRO 140 for Treatment of HIV Infection in Patients with High Variability in Viral Load and Non-Adherence to Antiretroviral Therapy**

This study was designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of PRO 140 (anti-CCR5 monoclonal antibody) when administered subcutaneously in conjunction with an optimized background antiretroviral regimen (OBR) in HIV-infected individuals exhibiting significant viral load fluctuations and a history of poor adherence to previous antiretroviral therapies.

**Study Overview**  
*Design*: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled  
*Population*: Approximately 120 HIV-infected adults with a history of non-adherence and fluctuating viral loads  
*Duration*: 24 weeks  
*Endpoints*: Primary endpoint was the percentage of participants achieving sustained virologic suppression (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) at week 24.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
1. Individuals aged ≥18 years with confirmed R5-tropic HIV-1 (Enhanced Sensitivity Trofile™ HIV Tropism Assay)
2. Documented high variability in plasma HIV-1 RNA levels (>1,000 copies/mL confirmed over at least three measurements prior to enrollment)
3. CD4+ lymphocyte count > 100 cells/mm³ at screening
4. History of poor adherence to past antiretroviral regimens
5. Clinically normal ECG at screening  
6. Women of reproductive potential must have a negative pregnancy test.

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
1. Patients with CXCR4-tropic virus or dual/mixed tropic (R5X4) virus
2. Evidence of acute liver disease or other untreated co-morbidities  
3. Prior exposure to anyCCR5 inhibitors, including PRO 140  
4. Concurrent substance use compromising health status or adherence

**Study Methodology**  
Eligible subjects were randomized (1:1) to receive either PRO 140 or placebo as an adjunct to an OBR consisting of at least two active drugs. All subjects underwent intensive monitoring of viral load and adherence behavior through self-reported data and electronic adherence devices.

**Results**  
The analysis revealed limited efficacy of PRO 140 in this complex patient population. The following key issues emerged:

1. **Suboptimal Patient Population**: The inclusion criterion of non-adherent individuals likely contributed to overall poor treatment outcomes. Subjects demonstrated persistent adherence challenges throughout the trial, leading to failed viral suppression.
  
2. **Viral Load Variability**: The high variability in baseline viral load proved detrimental; many subjects exhibited fluctuations that rendered effective suppression challenging. Consequently, the overall percentage of subjects achieving sustained virologic suppression at week 24 was significantly lower than anticipated.

3. **Immunological Factors**: Participants with baseline CD4+ lymphocyte counts on the lower end of the inclusion criteria exhibited inadequate immune response to the treatment, resulting in suboptimal therapeutic benefit from PRO 140.

4. **Potential Drug Resistance**: Genetic analyses performed on the enrolled subjects revealed unaccounted drug resistance mutations, which hindered the activity of PRO 140 against resistant virus strains, diluting the efficacy outcomes significantly.

5. **Concomitant Health Complications**: A considerable proportion of participants had unmanaged co-morbidities exacerbated by substance use, further complicating their clinical status and response to therapy.

**Conclusion**  
The trial failed to demonstrate the anticipated efficacy of PRO 140 as an adjunct therapy in this cohort. Key elements identified include suboptimal selection of participants, high variability in viral load, and a pre-existing history of non-adherence, all of which undermined the reliability of the results. Further studies will benefit from refined inclusion criteria aimed at enhancing adherence and disease stability for optimal assessment of PRO 140's therapeutic potential.
