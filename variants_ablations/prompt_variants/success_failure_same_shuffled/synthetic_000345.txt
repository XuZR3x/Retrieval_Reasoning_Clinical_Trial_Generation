**Successful Clinical Trial of Telbivudine:**

**Telbivudine for the Management of HBeAg-Negative Chronic Hepatitis B: A Phase III, Multi-Center, Randomized, Double-Blind Trial Evaluating Efficacy and Safety over 72 Weeks**

**Objective:**  
This trial aims to rigorously assess the efficacy and safety of telbivudine in comparison to an active control, entecavir, in patients diagnosed with HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis B (CHB), thus expanding upon previous success factors of structured inclusion criteria and consistent endpoints. In this trial, we will implement a focus on stratified patient management throughout the treatment period.

**Methods:**  
This prospective, multi-center study will enroll a cohort of HBeAg-negative CHB patients over a duration of 72 weeks. The study adopts a randomized, double-blind design, ensuring data integrity through minimized biases. Patients will be randomly assigned to receive either telbivudine (600 mg/day) (Arm A) or entecavir (0.5 mg/day) (Arm B). Participants will be carefully monitored at regular intervals for virologic response and safety profile assessment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adults aged 18 years and above.
- Documented diagnosis of compensated HBeAg-negative CHB, characterized by:
  - Persistent serum HBsAg positivity.
  - HBeAg negativity and positive HBeAb at the screening visit.
  - Serum HBV DNA levels > 2000 IU/mL.
- ALT level > 1x ULN (<10x ULN) at screening, or normal ALT levels ≤1x ULN within the past 6 months, combined with signs of liver inflammation or fibrosis.
  
**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Co-infection with HCV, HDV, or HIV.
- History of nucleoside or nucleotide analog treatment.
- Recent use of interferon or immunomodulatory treatments within the last 6 months.
- Pregnant or nursing women.
- Presence of significant renal insufficiency or hepatic decompensation signs.
- History of myopathy or muscle weakness.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
The primary outcome measure will be the proportion of patients achieving HBV DNA < 300 copies/mL at Week 72 in the modified intention-to-treat (mITT) population. It is hypothesized that telbivudine will demonstrate non-inferiority to entecavir in achieving this virologic suppression. 

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
- Assessment of liver function through changes in ALT levels to within normal limits.
- Evaluation of clinical response based on Child-Turcotte-Pugh scores.
- Proportion of participants experiencing adverse events and serious adverse events, categorized by system organ class.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Baseline characteristics of participants will be compared using appropriate statistical methods. Efficacy comparisons will employ the Chi-squared test for categorical variables, with significance set at p < 0.05. Safety data will be summarized descriptively.

**Conclusion:**  
The study is anticipated to deliver robust insights into the long-term efficacy of telbivudine in treating HBeAg-negative CHB while utilizing strategic structures highlighted in previous successful trials, such as well-defined inclusion criteria and systematic assessments of clinical responses. The findings may contribute significantly to optimizing therapeutic protocols for chronic hepatitis B within diverse patient populations, ultimately enhancing patient adherence and clinical outcomes through advanced individualized treatment approaches.
