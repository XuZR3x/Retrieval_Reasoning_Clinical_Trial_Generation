**Successful Clinical Trial of Aspirin: Evaluation of Long-term Outcomes in Patients with Aspirin and Dual Antiplatelet Therapy Post-Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)**

**Title:** Evaluation of Long-term Outcomes in Patients with Aspirin and Dual Antiplatelet Therapy Post-Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)

**Objective:** The primary objective of this trial is to assess the efficacy of low-dose aspirin (20 mg twice daily) combined with either ticagrelor (90 mg twice daily) or clopidogrel (75 mg once daily) in reducing major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in patients with a history of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) who have undergone PCI.

**Background:** The WILLOW-ACS trial has demonstrated that low-dose aspirin in conjunction with ticagrelor can enhance antiplatelet efficacy without significantly increasing bleeding risks (reason 1). Additionally, investigations into dual antiplatelet strategies indicate a potential improvement in outcomes for patients post-surgery (reason 2). Leveraging insights from previous studies, this trial incorporates innovative dosage strategies and extends the monitoring period to evaluate long-term clinical benefits.

**Design:** This is a multicenter, randomized controlled trial involving 650 participants with previous ACS who have undergone PCI. Subjects will be randomly assigned to three arms:
- Arm A: Low-dose aspirin (20 mg twice daily) plus ticagrelor (90 mg twice daily).
- Arm B: Low-dose aspirin (20 mg twice daily) plus clopidogrel (75 mg once daily).
- Arm C: Placebo control group.

The treatment period will extend for 24 months, after which participants will be assessed for the incidence of MACE, including myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular death.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Provision of informed consent prior to any study-specific procedures.
2. Males and females aged 18 years and older.
3. Previous diagnosis of acute coronary syndrome greater than 30 days and less than 12 months prior to enrollment.
4. Undergoing PCI with drug-eluting or bare metal stents.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Active bleeding or a history of recurrent gastrointestinal ulcers within the last year.
2. Contraindications for the use of aspirin, ticagrelor, or clopidogrel.
3. Uncontrolled hypertension or severe renal insufficiency.
4. Participation in other investigational trials within 30 days prior to recruitment.

**Endpoints:** The primary endpoint is the composite of MACE at 24 months. Secondary endpoints include assessment of bleeding events, quality of life measurements using the Seattle Angina Questionnaire, and analysis of stent thrombosis.

**Rationale:** This trial aims to evaluate the synergistic effects of dual antiplatelet therapy in the post-PCI setting, taking into account aspirin's established safety profile (reason 5) and its role in mitigating thrombotic events. The decision to employ a lower dose of aspirin is supported by findings from the Durlaza™ trial, suggesting that extended-release formulations can provide sustained antiplatelet effects (reason 3).

**Statistical Analysis:** It is estimated that 650 patients will allow for an 80% power to detect significant differences in the primary outcome measures with a two-tailed alpha level set at 0.05. Data will be analyzed using intention-to-treat principles, and Cox proportional hazards models will be employed to assess the risk of MACE across treatment arms.

**Conclusion:** This trial seeks to provide rigorous evidence on the optimization of aspirin and dual antiplatelet therapies in the post-PCI population. By combining robust clinical design with insights gained from prior studies, including meticulous subject selection (reason 4), it aims to contribute to strengthened cardiovascular guidelines and improved patient outcomes in the management of ACS.
