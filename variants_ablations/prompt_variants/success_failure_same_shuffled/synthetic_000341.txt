**Failed clinical trial of donepezil: Cognitive Outcomes and Donepezil in Patients Undergoing Neurostimulation Therapy for Treatment-Resistant Depression**

**Title**: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Donepezil in Patients Undergoing Neurostimulation Therapy for Treatment-Resistant Depression: Evaluation of Cognitive Performance and Memory Preservation

**Abstract**: This clinical trial aimed to investigate the efficacy of donepezil in preserving cognitive function and autobiographical memory in patients undergoing novel neurostimulation therapies for treatment-resistant depression (TRD). Despite the inherent cognitive risks associated with TRD therapies, donepezil, an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, was hypothesized to enhance cognitive outcomes. However, the trial concluded that donepezil did not confer significant cognitive benefits when compared to placebo, highlighting inherent limitations in its mechanism of action, insufficient clinical efficacy, and adverse cognitive effects in the studied population.

**Introduction**: Treatment-resistant depression (TRD) poses substantial challenges in clinical management, particularly regarding cognitive dysfunction. Neurostimulation modalities, such as transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), are increasingly utilized for TRD but are often associated with memory impairments exacerbated by the underlying disease. Donepezil, while previously utilized for Alzheimer's disease, was posited to ameliorate cognitive deficits post-intervention. This trial aimed to elucidate the potential of donepezil to enhance cognitive performance and memory retention among TRD patients enrolled in a neurostimulation protocol.

**Methods**: This double-blind, placebo-controlled trial was conducted across multiple centers, enrolling 200 participants diagnosed with treatment-resistant major depressive disorder (MDD). Participants underwent TMS as the neurostimulation treatment, with concurrent randomization to receive either donepezil or placebo. Cognitive performance was measured using the Repeatable Battery for the Assessment of Neuropsychological Status (RBANS) and autobiographical memory was evaluated through the Autobiographical Memory Interview (AMI) at baseline, after 4 weeks, and 8 weeks of treatment. 

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
- Patients must have a diagnosis of treatment-resistant major depressive disorder as defined by current DSM-5 criteria.
- Aged 18-70 years, with a Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS) score ≥ 18.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
- History of alcohol or drug abuse within the past year.
- Neurological conditions (e.g., epilepsy, prior head trauma).
- Current use of medications that target neurotransmitter systems (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs) or significant medical comorbidities.

**Outcomes**: Primary endpoints focused on changes in cognitive performance (RBANS) and autobiographical memory (AMI). Secondary endpoints included safety and tolerability assessed through adverse event monitoring and the Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS).

**Results**: The study was terminated early due to lack of efficacy. Interim analyses revealed no significant differences in cognitive performance or memory retention between the donepezil and placebo groups. Specifically, cognitive assessments showed a mean difference of -0.5 (95% CI: -2.3 to 1.3; p=0.602), while autobiographical memory scores indicated similar results (mean difference: 0.3; 95% CI: -1.5 to 2.1; p=0.796). Additionally, donepezil was associated with increased reports of adverse cognitive effects, including confusion and interoperability errors during neurostimulation therapy.

**Discussion**: The results of this clinical trial underscore the ineffective mechanism of action of donepezil against the multifactorial cognitive impairments inherent in treatment-resistant depression following neurostimulation strategies. Insufficient clinical efficacy was further demonstrated by the lack of statistically significant cognitive enhancement when benchmarked against placebo. Adverse cognitive effects noted during the trial could contribute to exacerbated memory deficits, particularly in a vulnerable cohort already experiencing cognitive dysfunction due to TRD.

**Conclusion**: This trial did not support the hypothesis that donepezil is beneficial in enhancing cognitive outcomes in patients undergoing neurostimulation therapy for treatment-resistant depression. Future studies should explore novel pharmacological agents with mechanisms that more directly target the neurobiological underpinnings of cognitive deficits in TRD.
