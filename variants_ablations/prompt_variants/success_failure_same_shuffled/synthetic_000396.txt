**Failed Clinical Trial of Velcade: Velcade in Combination with Azacitidine for Intermediate to High-Risk Myelodysplastic Syndromes**

**EudraCT Number:** 2023-004567-01  
**Study Title:** Phase II Study Evaluating the Safety and Efficacy of Velcade in Combination with Azacitidine in Patients with Intermediate to High-Risk Myelodysplastic Syndromes

**Abstract:**  
This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of Velcade (bortezomib) in combination with azacitidine in patients diagnosed with intermediate to high-risk myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS). The primary endpoint focused on overall response rate (ORR) as defined by International Working Group criteria for MDS. Secondary objectives included assessment of progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and quality of life (QoL).

**Introduction:**  
Myelodysplastic syndromes encompass a heterogeneous array of hematological disorders characterized by ineffective hematopoiesis. Given the complexity of MDS subtypes and the aging population typically affected, a multi-modal approach integrating Velcade with azacitidine was hypothesized to enhance therapeutic outcomes. Prior studies demonstrated Velcade's partial efficacy; however, challenges in overarching patient selection and tolerability remain critical.

**Methodology:**  

**Study Design:**  
This multicenter, open-label, single-arm Phase II trial enrolled 50 eligible participants. Patients received Velcade (1.3 mg/m²) administered subcutaneously on days 1, 4, 8, and 11 in combination with azacitidine (75 mg/m²) on days 1 through 7 of each 28-day cycle.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18-80 with a diagnosis of intermediate-2 or high-risk MDS as per IPSS criteria.
- No prior treatment with nucleoside analogs or proteasome inhibitors.
- Voluntary written informed consent prior to any study-related procedures.
- WHO performance status of 0-2, with adequate organ function defined as: creatinine ≤ 2 x upper limit of normal, total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x upper limit of normal, and transaminases ≤ 2.5 x upper limit of normal.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with prior therapy involving azacitidine or Velcade within the last 6 months.
- Significant peripheral neuropathy (Grade 2 or higher).
- Patients with acute infections or uncontrolled medical conditions.
- Concurrent participation in other clinical trials.
- Pregnant or lactating women.

**Outcome Measures:**
- Primary: ORR as defined by CR, partial response (PR), and stable disease (SD).
- Secondary: PFS, OS, and QoL assessed via validated questionnaires.

**Results:**  
A total of 46 patients completed the screening process and began treatment. The trial encountered several adjuvant challenges that ultimately influenced its failure:

1. **Insufficient Efficacy Across Disease Variants:** Analysis revealed that only 18% of participants achieved an ORR, revealing Velcade's limited response in this diverse group of MDS patients suffering from varying subtypes and underlying comorbidities.

2. **Toxicity Profile and Poor Patient Compliance:** The combination exhibited an unacceptable safety profile, with a significant incidence of Grade 3 peripheral neuropathy affecting nearly 40% of patients. These adverse effects led to frequent dose reductions and contributed to discontinuation rates exceeding 30%.

3. **Incompatibility with Standardized Treatment Protocols:** The complicated dosing schedules of this trial did not align well with current practice for treating MDS, which often favors more conservative measures. Difficulty in recruitment was noted, as referring physicians expressed reservations about initiating protocols deviating from traditional management strategies.

4. **Limited Duration of Response and Need for Additional Interventions:** The small number of patients who demonstrated initial responses subsequently required concurrent therapies, indicative of Velcade's inability to maintain prolonged control of disease progression.

5. **Inadequate Patient Selection Criteria:** A significant proportion of subjects fell outside of the preferential treatment window due to comorbidities not initially considered, further limiting the generalizability of clinical outcomes.

**Conclusions:**  
The Phase II study of Velcade in combination with azacitidine for intermediate to high-risk MDS failed to meet its primary efficacy endpoints, primarily due to inadequate patient responses and significant adverse effects. This experience highlights the challenges within this patient population, necessitating further research into tailored treatment strategies and validation of more robust inclusion criteria to improve Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs) in MDS therapies.

**Study Termination:**  
The trial was officially terminated due to lack of efficacy and concerns regarding the safety profile of the treatment regimen, reinforcing the need for alternate approaches to optimize outcomes in MDS patients.
