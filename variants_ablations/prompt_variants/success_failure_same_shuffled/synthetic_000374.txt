**Failed Clinical Trial of Daclizumab: Investigation of Daclizumab for the Prevention of Post-Transplant Lymphoproliferative Disorder (PTLD) in Pediatric Liver Transplant Recipients**

**Study Title:** Efficacy of Daclizumab in the Prevention of Post-Transplant Lymphoproliferative Disorder in Pediatric Liver Transplant Recipients  
**Ind Number:** 20210  
**Study Protocol:** This clinical trial was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of daclizumab (Zenapax®) for the prevention of post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD) among children and adolescents undergoing liver transplantation. Building on hypotheses established in renal transplant populations, the study sought to investigate daclizumab's role in enhancing immunologic tolerance and preventing lymphocyte proliferation linked to Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) infection following transplantation. Despite promising preclinical data, the trial did not yield the expected outcomes.

**Background:** PTLD is a significant complication of solid organ transplantation, particularly in immunosuppressed pediatric populations. Daclizumab targets the interleukin-2 receptor (IL-2R) to modulate T cell responses, thereby aiming to improve overall immune tolerance. However, prior knowledge gaps regarding pediatric pharmacokinetics and complex immune responses to EBV in this population contributed to the trial's limitations.

**Objectives:**  
- Primary Objective: To evaluate the incidence of PTLD in pediatric liver transplant recipients treated with daclizumab compared to a control group receiving standard immunosuppressive therapy.
- Secondary Objectives: To assess overall survival rates, incidence of EBV reactivation, and adverse event profiles associated with daclizumab administration.

**Study Design:** The study was conceived as a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial. Patients were stratified based on pre-transplant EBV serology and previous immunosuppressive treatments.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients aged 1 to 18 years undergoing liver transplantation.
- No prior history of EBV-related post-transplant complications.
- Ability of patient/guardian to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- History of hypersensitivity to daclizumab or its components.
- Concomitant treatment with other investigational drugs or agents known to enhance the risk of PTLD.
- Pre-existing malignancies or active viral infections that could confound study results.

**Intervention Arms:**  
- **Arm I:** Patients received standard immunosuppressive therapy (including tacrolimus and mycophenolate mofetil) plus daclizumab administered preoperatively and at specified timepoints post-transplant (days 0, 14, and 28).  
- **Arm II:** Patients received standard immunosuppressive therapy plus placebo following the same administration schedule as daclizumab.

**Outcome Measures:**  
The primary outcome measure was the incidence of PTLD within the first year post-transplant. Secondary measures included rates of EBV reactivation, overall survival, and the incidence of adverse events related to daclizumab.

**Results:**  
The trial's findings indicated no statistically significant reduction in the incidence of PTLD between the daclizumab and placebo groups. 

1. **Limited Pediatric-Specific Pharmacokinetic Data:** Contributing to the trial's failure was the insufficient understanding of daclizumab pharmacokinetics in pediatric patients, leading to potential dosing inadequacies and subtherapeutic levels.

2. **Inadequate Efficacy in Targeting PTLD Mechanisms:** Daclizumab's mechanism, which appeared promising based on adult data, failed to show clinical relevance in the context of EBV-mediated PTLD in this patient population.

3. **Overestimation of Immune Tolerance in Pediatric Populations:** The trial did not account for the complexity of immune responses in children post-transplant, thereby undermining the therapeutic aim of establishing effective immunologic tolerance.

4. **High Rate of Adverse Events:** A notable incidence of adverse events attributed to daclizumab was recorded, including hypersensitivity reactions that necessitated discontinuation of the treatment in several patients, further complicating the study's outcomes.

5. **Lack of Optimal Study Population Representation:** Stringent exclusion criteria led to a study population that did not reflect the broader pediatric transplant demographic, impairing the external validity of the trial results.

**Conclusion:**  
The clinical trial investigating daclizumab for PTLD prevention in pediatric liver transplant recipients was unsuccessful. The findings underlined the necessity for thorough pharmacological understanding of pediatric populations and highlighted the multifaceted nature of post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorders. Further research is required to explore alternative strategies and therapies for managing this pressing clinical challenge, taking into account the unique immune landscape of pediatric patients.
