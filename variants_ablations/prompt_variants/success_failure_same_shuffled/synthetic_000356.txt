**Failed clinical trial of alfuzosin:** Alfuzosin in Patients With Recurrent Urinary Tract Infections Induced by Neurogenic Bladder Dysfunction

**Title:** A Multi-center, Double-blind, Randomized, placebo-controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Alfuzosin in Patients With Recurrent Urinary Tract Infections Secondary to Neurogenic Bladder Dysfunction

**Study Design:** This study is designed to assess the efficacy of alfuzosin in reducing the incidence of recurrent urinary tract infections (UTIs) in patients diagnosed with neurogenic bladder dysfunction over a treatment duration of 16 weeks. The primary objective is to determine whether alfuzosin can significantly decrease the recurrence rate of UTIs compared to placebo.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 18-65 years with a clinical diagnosis of neurogenic bladder dysfunction,
- History of recurrent urinary tract infections defined as ≥3 UTIs in the past 12 months,
- Urinary retention confirmed by urodynamic study, 
- Ability to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior use of alpha-1 antagonists within 30 days,
- Active urinary tract infection at screening,
- Any history of allergic reactions to alfuzosin or related medications,
- Significant renal impairment (eGFR < 60 mL/min),
- Pregnancy or lactation,
- Concurrent use of medications impacting urinary tract function such as anticholinergics,
- History of permanent urinary catheterization or bladder augmentation.

**Methods:** Participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either alfuzosin 10 mg once daily or matching placebo, with treatment duration lasting 16 weeks. Follow-up assessments were scheduled at weeks 4, 8, 12, and 16 to evaluate UTI recurrence based on clinical criteria and urine cultures.

**Primary Endpoint:** The rate of recurrent urinary tract infections (defined as symptomatic UTI episodes resulting in antibiotic treatment) within the treatment period.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Changes in urodynamic parameters, including bladder compliance and maximum bladder capacity,
- Incidence and severity of adverse events related to treatment,
- Patient-reported outcomes regarding urinary symptoms and quality of life.

**Outcome Measures:** UTI recurrence was tracked through clinical evaluation and confirmed by laboratory analysis of urine cultures. Safety and tolerability were assessed through adverse event reporting and monitoring vital signs at each follow-up visit.

**Results:** The trial enrolled 300 participants; however, the rate of recurrent UTIs did not differ significantly between the alfuzosin group (n=146) and placebo group (n=150). The primary endpoint of a statistically significant reduction in UTI recurrences was not met (p=0.65), demonstrating insufficient efficacy in addressing the treatment needs of this patient population. Furthermore, variability in patient responses was observed, with several subjects experiencing significant side effects such as orthostatic hypotension (reported in 12% of the alfuzosin group), leading to treatment discontinuation in 8% of cases. These adverse event profiles negatively influenced adherence.

**Discussion:** The failure of this trial may be attributed to several underlying factors. Firstly, insufficient efficacy was observed in the target population, indicating that alfuzosin's mechanisms may not adequately address the pathophysiology associated with neurogenic bladder dysfunction and recurrent UTIs. The trial also highlighted considerable interindividual variability in response, compounded by potential confounding conditions affecting bladder dynamics. Moreover, the adverse event profile of alfuzosin, particularly concerning orthostatic hypotension, impaired treatment adherence and overall data reliability.

**Conclusion:** In light of the evaluated endpoints, the primary hypothesis of enhanced UTI management with alfuzosin was not validated. The findings underscore the need for further investigation into alternative therapeutic strategies and the importance of optimizing dose schedules, durations, and a deeper understanding of the biological mechanisms at play in neurogenic bladder dysfunction affecting urinary infection patterns.
