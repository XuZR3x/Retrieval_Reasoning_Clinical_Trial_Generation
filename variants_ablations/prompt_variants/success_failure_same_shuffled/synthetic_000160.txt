**Failed Clinical Trial of Insulin Glulisine: Comparison of Insulin Glulisine and Insulin Aspart in a Fixed-Dose Combination with Metformin in Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus**

**Title**: A Randomized, Open-Label, Multicenter Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Insulin Glulisine vs. Insulin Aspart as Part of a Fixed-Dose Regimen Combined with Metformin in Adults With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus - The OPTION Study

**Primary Objective**: To assess the glycemic control represented by the change in HbA1c from baseline to week 52 in adult subjects with type 2 diabetes mellitus receiving either insulin glulisine or insulin aspart in combination with a fixed-dose regimen of metformin.

**Secondary Objectives**:
- To evaluate the incidence of symptomatic hypoglycemia in both treatment arms.
- To compare the weight change from baseline to study completion between groups.
- To analyze treatment satisfaction via the Diabetes Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire (DTSQ).

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Adults aged 18 to 75 years with a confirmed diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus for at least one year.
- HbA1c levels between 7.5% and 10% at screening.
- Treatment with basal insulin (Lantus or levemir) for at least 6 months prior to study initiation, combined with metformin.
- Ability and willingness to adhere to a regimen involving multiple daily injections.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- History of heart failure or clinically significant cardiovascular disease within the past year.
- Use of medications that could impact insulin sensitivity or confound study results, such as glucocorticoids or thiazolidinediones.
- Significant renal impairment (eGFR < 60 mL/min).
- PTSD or other psychological conditions impairing the ability to adhere to the treatment protocol.

**Trial Design**: 
The trial employed a randomized, open-label design with two parallel groups. An expected enrollment of 350 adults will be allocated in a 1:1 ratio to receive either insulin glulisine or insulin aspart, both administered at mealtimes alongside a stable dose of metformin during a 52-week treatment period.

**Endpoints**: 
The primary endpoint was the reduction in HbA1c at week 52 compared to baseline, with a non-inferiority margin set at 0.5%. Secondary endpoints included weight change measured as change from baseline to week 52, and the occurrence of adverse events, particularly symptomatic hypoglycemia.

**Results Overview**:
Despite enrolling the targeted number of participants, the trial encountered several critical issues leading to its failure. 

1. **Inadequate Glycemic Control**: Results indicated a less favorable reduction in HbA1c for insulin glulisine versus insulin aspart. The final evaluation demonstrated that a significantly higher proportion of participants on insulin glulisine failed to achieve the glycemic targets when compared to the insulin aspart cohort.

2. **Adverse Event Incidence**: Participants receiving insulin glulisine reported a higher incidence of symptomatic hypoglycemic episodes, leading to greater treatment discontinuation rates and subsequent safety concerns. This aspect notably undermined the perceived efficacy of insulin glulisine.

3. **Weight Gain Complications**: A substantial trend towards weight gain was documented among those treated with insulin glulisine, presenting a complication commonly associated with insulin regimens. Participants reported heightened dissatisfaction with treatment outcomes, which correlated with poorer glycemic control.

4. **Complexity of Regimen**: The requirement for multiple daily injections alongside oral agents led to issues with patient adherence, further contributing to inadequate glycemic management and overall study attrition.

5. **Patient Psychological Barriers**: Psychological assessments revealed a notable prevalence of diabetes-related distress among participants, which adversely impacted adherence to the complex injection regimen, thus complicating the study's intended outcomes.

**Conclusion**: This clinical trial ultimately demonstrated that the combination therapy of insulin glulisine with metformin was inadequate in providing superior glycemic control, with related safety issues impacting the trial's success. Consequently, insulin glulisine did not fulfill the expected efficacy benchmarks set before initiation, leading to the termination of the study prior to completion of the follow-up phase. The findings underscore the need for further investigation into the long-term efficacy and safety profiles of insulin glulisine in diverse diabetes treatment paradigms.
