### Successful Clinical Trial of Cilostazol: Cilostazol in Chronic Heart Failure Patients with Reduced Ejection Fraction (CILOSTEF)

**The Double-Blind, Randomized, Multi-Center, and Placebo-Controlled Trial for Efficacy and Safety of Cilostazol in Patients with Chronic Heart Failure and Reduced Ejection Fraction**

Chronic heart failure (CHF) continues to be a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, markedly impacting patient quality of life and healthcare resources. Emerging evidence suggests that cilostazol, a phosphodiesterase III inhibitor, may confer neuroprotective and cardioprotective benefits due to its vasodilatory and platelet-inhibitory properties. This study aims to evaluate the effects of cilostazol in patients diagnosed with CHF characterized by reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF).

**Study Objectives:**  
The primary objective of this study is to assess the impact of cilostazol on functional capacity, as measured by the 6-minute walk test (6MWT), and to evaluate its safety profile in a population suffering from CHF with LVEF less than 40%. Secondary objectives include determining the effects of cilostazol on biomarkers of heart failure, including N-terminal pro B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), and quality of life metrics quantified via the Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ).

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Patients aged 40 to 85 years.
2. Diagnosed with chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (LVEF < 40%) based on echocardiography.
3. NYHA functional class II to IV at baseline.
4. Patients able to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of myocardial infarction or coronary artery bypass graft surgery within the past 6 months.
2. Known arrhythmias requiring treatment or unstable cardiac conditions.
3. Severe hepatic dysfunction (ALT and AST > 3 times the upper limit of normal).
4. Concurrent use of contraindicated medications (including other PDE inhibitors, anticoagulants, and immunosuppressants).
5. Pregnant or lactating patients.

**Study Design:**  
This is a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial conducted at multiple centers. A total of 500 patients who meet the inclusion criteria will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either cilostazol (100 mg twice daily) or matching placebo for a period of 12 weeks. The study will utilize a robust methodology to ensure the reliability of the outcomes, including randomization stratified by NYHA class and institution.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
- Change in distance walked in the 6-minute walk test from baseline to 12 weeks.

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
- Change in NT-proBNP levels from baseline to 12 weeks.
- Change in MLHFQ scores from baseline to 12 weeks.
- Incidence of adverse events categorized as serious or non-serious during the treatment period.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
The primary analysis will be conducted using an intent-to-treat population. Changes from baseline to 12 weeks will be analyzed using mixed-effects models to account for repeated measurements. A p-value of less than 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Justification for Study Design and Population:**  
Previous clinical trials have demonstrated cilostazol's efficacy concerning vasodilation and inhibition of platelet aggregation, supporting its use in cardiovascular disease management. This trial's design incorporates rigorous methodologies to enhance the robustness of conclusions drawn, while the carefully defined inclusion criteria allow examination of cilostazol's potential benefits in a specifically vulnerable population at a higher risk of adverse cardiovascular events.

**Conclusion:**  
If cilostazol demonstrates significant improvement in exercise capacity and markers of heart failure alongside an acceptable safety profile in this high-risk population, it may establish a new therapeutic role for cilostazol in the management of chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. The results of this trial will have important implications for clinical practice and future research directions in cardiovascular therapeutics.
