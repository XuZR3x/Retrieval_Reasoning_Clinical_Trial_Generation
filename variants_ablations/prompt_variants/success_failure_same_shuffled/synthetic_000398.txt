**Failed Clinical Trial of S-1: Phase 2 Study of S-1 in Patients with Advanced Gastric Cancer**

**Title:** A Multicenter, Open-Label, Phase 2 Study of S-1 as a Monotherapy in Patients with Advanced Gastric Cancer Who Are Chemotherapy-Naïve or Have Failed First-Line Therapy

**Purpose:** The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of S-1 in terms of overall response rate (ORR) in patients with chemotherapy-naïve advanced gastric cancer or those who have experienced disease progression following first-line therapy. Secondary objectives include assessing safety and overall survival (OS) metrics.

**Background:** Gastric cancer is a prevalent malignancy associated with poor clinical outcomes, especially in advanced stages. Traditional chemotherapy approaches have shown limited effectiveness, particularly in patients expressing intrinsic resistance to standard cytotoxic regimens. In this context, S-1, an oral combination of tegafur, gimeracil, and oteracil, represents a potential therapeutic option with mechanistic advantages in enhancing systemic drug exposure while limiting gastrointestinal toxicity.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Provision of written informed consent.
2. Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced gastric adenocarcinoma.
3. Measurable disease as defined by RECIST criteria (lesions assessable in at least one dimension).
4. Age ≥ 18 years.
5. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status of 0-2.
6. Adequate organ function, confirmed by laboratory values within the normal range for AST, ALT, total bilirubin, platelet count, and creatinine clearance.
7. Life expectancy of ≥ 12 weeks.
8. Willing and able to comply with the study protocol.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior treatment with any anticancer therapy for advanced gastric cancer.
2. Previous adjuvant chemotherapy without evidence of disease progression.
3. Significant comorbidities or medical conditions that may interfere with treatment or assessments, including uncontrolled cardiac disease or active inflammatory bowel disease.
4. Known hypersensitivity to any component of S-1.
5. Pregnant or lactating women, or those of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.

**Study Design:** This Phase 2, open-label, multicenter study was conducted to determine the efficacy of S-1 as a single agent. Eligible subjects received S-1 orally, dosed as per body surface area, for a minimum of 8 weeks or until disease progression. Tumor responses were evaluated using RECIST criteria after each treatment cycle.

**Endpoints:** 
- **Primary Endpoint:** Overall response rate (ORR), defined as the percentage of subjects achieving a confirmed partial or complete response at any assessment time.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and the incidence and severity of adverse events, particularly gastrointestinal toxicities.

**Rationale for Failure:**
1. **Limited Efficacy in Chemotherapy-Naïve Population:** The overall response rate observed in this trial was significantly lower than pretrial expectations, demonstrating that advanced gastric cancer exhibits similar intrinsic resistance to S-1 as noted in other studies involving chemotherapeutics.
   
2. **Inadequate Impact on Survival Metrics:** The median OS and PFS observed did not show statistically significant improvements compared to historical data for standard treatments, raising concerns about S-1's potential benefits.

3. **Suboptimal Tolerability Profile:** Although designed to minimize gastrointestinal toxicities, a considerable number of participants reported severe side effects, including nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea, leading to early discontinuation and reduced dosing.

4. **High Rate of Patient Drop-outs:** The study experienced a notable drop-out rate attributed to strict eligibility criteria and adverse events, thus distorting the overall analysis of treatment response and efficacy due to non-representative populations.

5. **Complex Pharmacodynamics and Drug Interaction Issues:** Variability in patient responses, particularly in those on concomitant medications, complicated the assessments of efficacy and safety, ultimately confounding the interpretation of trial results and leading to a significant number of treatment failures.

**Conclusion:** The trial was terminated based on the observed lack of efficacy, significant adverse reactions, and issues related to participant retention, consequently failing to meet pre-established efficacy and safety endpoints. Further studies are warranted to explore alternative regimens or combinations that could enhance treatment outcomes in this patient population.
