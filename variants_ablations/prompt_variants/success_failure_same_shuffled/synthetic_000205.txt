**Successful Clinical Trial of Doxorubicin: Efficacy of Doxorubicin in Combination With Bevacizumab for Unresectable Metastatic Breast Cancer - A Multicenter Phase II Study**

**Abstract:**
This study investigates the efficacy of doxorubicin in combination with bevacizumab in patients with unresectable metastatic breast cancer. Previous trials demonstrated enhanced responses when doxorubicin is combined with targeted therapies, leveraging the cytotoxic effects while minimizing tumor burden, thus illustrating the rationale for this regimen. 

**Study Overview:**
The primary objective was to evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) as defined by RECIST criteria, alongside secondary endpoints including progression-free survival (PFS) and safety assessment. The trial emphasizes rigorous imaging assessment and patient monitoring protocols to ensure optimized treatment efficacy, safety profile, and timely intervention.

**Methodology:**
This is a multicenter, open-label, Phase II trial. Eligible patients were those with histologically confirmed, unresectable metastatic breast cancer, who had not received prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed breast cancer with unresectable metastatic disease.
- No prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease.
- >18 years of age with an ECOG score of 0-2.
- At least one measurable lesion based on RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Adequate hematological, hepatic, and renal function.
- Signed informed consent.
- Women of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and agree to use effective contraception.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Histopathological subtype other than invasive ductal carcinoma.
- Previous therapy with bevacizumab or doxorubicin.
- Active CNS metastases or significant concurrent medical conditions.
- Significant cardiovascular events or unstable conditions within the previous six months.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

**Intervention:**
Patients received intravenous doxorubicin at a standard dosage of 60 mg/m² every 21 days in combination with bevacizumab administered at 15 mg/kg every 21 days. Treatment efficacy was closely monitored using standardized imaging techniques, including CT scans at baseline, every two cycles, and at treatment completion.

**Outcomes:**
The primary endpoint assessed the ORR, calculated as the proportion of patients achieving a complete or partial response post-treatment. Secondary outcomes included assessment of PFS, safety profile, and quality of life, documented through validated questionnaires at baseline and follow-up.

**Results:**
The study enrolled 150 patients, with 75% achieving a confirmed response by RECIST criteria—25% complete response and 50% partial response. The median PFS was noted to be 9 months. Adverse events were monitored rigorously, with the majority being manageable (Grade 1-2), while severe adverse events (Grade 3-4) were seen in 20% of patients, primarily hematologic in nature.

**Conclusion:**
The combination of doxorubicin and bevacizumab in patients with unresectable metastatic breast cancer demonstrated a promising safety profile and significant antitumor activity, evidenced by a high ORR and acceptable management of treatment-related toxicities. This study underlines the importance of targeted combination therapies in improving therapeutic outcomes in high-risk populations, consistent with the paradigm shift toward personalized oncology approaches. Further trials are warranted to validate and potentially expand this treatment regimen in diverse patient cohorts.
