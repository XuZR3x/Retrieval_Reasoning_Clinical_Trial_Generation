**Failed Clinical Trial of Radiotherapy: Combining Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy (SBRT) with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Stage IV Melanoma**

**Phase II Study of Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy Combined with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Stage IV Metastatic Melanoma**

**RATIONALE:** Preclinical studies indicate potential enhancement of immune responses when radiation therapy is combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors. This trial aims to evaluate the effectiveness of stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) combined with programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors in patients with advanced melanoma. However, recent trials have suggested that insufficient immune modulation may limit the efficacy and diminish the expected synergistic outcomes.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial is designed to assess the clinical activity of SBRT combined with PD-1 inhibitors in patients with stage IV metastatic melanoma, specifically focusing on the systemic immune response and overall survival.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- Primary: To evaluate the objective response rate (ORR) as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria after 12 weeks of initiation of combination treatment.
- Secondary: To assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) at 6 and 12 months, as well as the incidence and severity of treatment-related adverse events (AEs).

**OUTLINE:**
Eligible patients will receive SBRT to a selected metastatic lesion targeting up to 5 lesions in total, with dosages delivering a total of 30-40 Gy in 3-5 fractions. Concurrently, patients will receive the PD-1 inhibitor administered intravenously every 2 weeks starting prior to SBRT until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Patients will be followed for disease progression monitoring at 3-month intervals for the first year and biannually thereafter.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
**Inclusion criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed stage IV melanoma.
- Measurable metastatic lesions as per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- ECOG performance status (PS) of 0-1.

**Exclusion criteria:**
- Prior treatment with radiotherapy to the target lesion.
- Prior or concurrent treatment with other systemic therapies including targeted therapies.
- Active, uncontrolled, or significant medical conditions that may interfere with study assessments.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
Inclusion criteria require adequate hematological and organ function as defined by:
- Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL,
- Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500 cells/mm³,
- Platelet count ≥ 100,000 cells/mm³.

**Exclusion criteria:**
- Known history of autoimmune disease necessitating systemic treatment.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

**EFFICACY ASSESSMENT:** 
The primary endpoint is the ORR evaluated by imaging obtained 12 weeks post initiation of therapy. A complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) is required as per RECIST 1.1 to qualify as a response. The study's null hypothesis (H0) posits that the combination therapy is ineffective (ORR ≤ 15%), while the alternative hypothesis (HA) suggests the combination treatment is effective (ORR ≥ 30%). With an intended sample size of 70 participants expecting 21 responses for adequate statistical power, the trial was prematurely terminated due to lack of sufficient response rates, indicative of the underlying issue identified in previous studies.

**REASON FOR FAILURE:** 
The primary reason for the failure of this clinical trial can be traced back to:
1. The absence of a significant objective response rate, impeding the anticipated immunomodulatory effect of combining SBRT with immune checkpoint inhibitors as previously predicted from preclinical data.
2. High attrition due to adverse events, likely attributing to an unexpected safety profile that was not adequately anticipant in the treatment design. Notably, the experienced high rates of immune-related adverse events led to numerous treatment interruptions and discontinuations.
3. The trial's sample size calculated through an optimistic estimation proved insufficient due to the lower-than-expected response in eligible participants, diminishing the statistical power, corroborating previous issues encountered in varying treatment protocols involving PD-1 inhibitors.

Further investigation and a reevaluation of patient selection criteria may foster improvements in the design of future studies aimed at elucidating the complex interplay between high-dose radiation and immune modulation in melanoma, a cancer type characterized by inherent heterogeneity in tumor biology.
