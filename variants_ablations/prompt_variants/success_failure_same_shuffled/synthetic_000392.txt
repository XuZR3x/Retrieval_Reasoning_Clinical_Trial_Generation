**Title**: Evaluating the Effect of Telbivudine on Long-term Virologic Response and Liver Function in Patients with Chronic Hepatitis B: A Multicenter, Randomized, Controlled Trial

**Abstract**: This clinical trial investigates the efficacy and safety of telbivudine in patients with chronic hepatitis B. It employs a rigorous design integrating a multicenter and multinational framework, ensuring a diverse patient population representative of varying disease severity. The aim is to determine the long-term virologic response to telbivudine and its impact on liver function over a 156-week treatment period.

**Study Design**: 
This is a double-blind, randomized, controlled trial comparing telbivudine (600 mg/day) to a placebo in patients diagnosed with chronic hepatitis B. The study will enroll participants across various countries to enhance the generalizability of findings. Enrollment will target adults aged 18 years and older with documented seropositivity for HBsAg and HBV DNA levels exceeding 2000 IU/mL. 

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Documented chronic hepatitis B infection (positive HBsAg for at least 6 months).
- Adult patients (≥18 years) with HBV DNA levels >2000 IU/mL.
- Baseline Child-Turcotte-Pugh (CTP) score of 5-9.
- Alanine transaminase (ALT) levels above the upper limit of normal (ULN).

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Coinfection with hepatitis C, D, or human immunodeficiency virus.
- Prior treatment with nucleoside or nucleotide analogs.
- History of hepatic decompensation as determined by clinical criteria.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding, alcohol abuse, or illicit drug use.

**Primary Endpoint**: 
The primary endpoint is the proportion of participants achieving undetectable serum HBV DNA (< 20 IU/mL) at Week 156, demonstrating sustained virologic response.

**Secondary Endpoints**:
- Improvement in CTP scores (≥2-point reduction from baseline).
- Safety assessment through the collection and reporting of adverse events, including serious adverse events (SAEs) attributed to telbivudine.
- Long-term tolerability and drug persistence rates.

**Study Procedures**: 
Following randomization, participants will receive either telbivudine or a placebo for 156 weeks. The pre-stratification of patients based on baseline ALT levels will ensure tailored analysis and delineation of treatment responses across monitored subgroups of chronic hepatitis B patients. Longitudinal follow-up visits will take place at 4-week intervals during the treatment and every 12 weeks post-treatment to ascertain safety and efficacy, aiming to provide insights into the longevity of treatment effects.

**Safety Monitoring**: 
A Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will oversee the study, ensuring that any reported adverse events, particularly related to hepatotoxicity or renal impairment, are promptly addressed. 

**Conclusion**: 
This trial aims to generate robust data assessing telbivudine's efficacy on long-term virologic response in a diverse population. The comprehensive design, inclusion of clinically relevant primary endpoints, and strict exclusion criteria combined will facilitate an accurate evaluation of telbivudine's benefits as a therapeutic agent for chronic hepatitis B while ensuring patient safety.

**Expected Outcomes**: 
The successful implementation of this trial should lead to the affirmation of telbivudine’s clinical relevance in managing chronic hepatitis B, potentially influencing treatment guidelines and enhancing patient health through improved liver function and virologic control.
