**Failed Clinical Trial of Azacitidine: A Phase II Study of Azacitidine Combined with Targeted Therapy in Patients with Advanced Ovarian Cancer**

**A Phase II Randomized Study of Azacitidine and Targeted Therapy in Patients with Advanced Ovarian Cancer Recurrence Following Platinum-Based Therapy**

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the percentage of patients with disease progression-free survival at 6 months post-treatment initiation.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To evaluate the overall survival (OS) among treated patients.
II. To determine the safety profile and toxicity associated with azacitidine administered in combination with targeted therapies.

**OUTLINE:**
Eligible participants will be randomized into two arms:
- **ARM A:** Patients receive azacitidine subcutaneously (SC) on days 1 to 5 and a targeted therapy agent (such as olaparib or niraparib) on a continuous basis. Treatment is repeated every 28 days for 4 cycles in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- **ARM B:** Patients receive the targeted therapy alone without azacitidine. Treatment is similarly repeated for a duration of 4 cycles.

Post-treatment, patients will be monitored for responses and will undergo follow-up evaluations every 3 months for 2 years.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced ovarian cancer with recurrence after at least one line of platinum-based chemotherapy.
- Measurable disease per RECIST criteria, with at least one lesion ≥ 10 mm in its longest diameter.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate hematological function: leukocytes ≥ 3,000/mcL, absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mcL, platelets ≥ 100,000/mcL.
- Creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min or serum creatinine levels within normal institutional limits.
- Informed consent obtained prior to study entry.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior exposure to azacitidine as part of treatment for ovarian cancer.
- Any concurrent use of investigational agents or differing interventions.
- Uncontrolled medical conditions, including active infections or severe psychiatric disorders that could limit adherence to the protocol.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- Major surgical procedures < 4 weeks prior to study drug initiation.

**PRIMARY ENDPOINT:**
The primary endpoint for this study is the percentage of patients progression-free at 6 months post-randomization. The statistical efficacy will be determined using a two-sided Fisher's Exact test, facilitating comparison between treatment arms. A sample size of approximately 60 patients per arm will render sufficient power to elucidate differences in treatment efficacy.

**RATIONALE FOR TRIAL FAILURE:**
1. **Patient Population Considerations:** Patients enrolled in this study presented with advanced stages of ovarian cancer and substantial prior treatment history. A majority had experienced a minimum of two lines of therapy prior to enrollment; this heavily pre-treated cohort may have exhibited diminished responsiveness to azacitidine, diminishing the likelihood of achieving clinically meaningful outcomes.

2. **Inefficacious Mechanism of Action:** The mechanism of action for azacitidine primarily involves hypomethylation of DNA, which may not sufficiently address the robust tumor heterogeneity found in advanced ovarian cancers, characterized by complex genetic landscapes. This inadequacy raises concerns regarding the effectiveness of azacitidine as a stand-alone agent within a combination approach.

3. **Combination Therapy Challenges:** The planned inclusion of targeted therapies with azacitidine led to increased polypharmacy, introducing the risk of unexpected drug interactions. Clinical outcomes may have been adversely affected by cumulative toxicities or differing pharmacokinetics between agents, thereby complicating the efficacy assessment of the intervention.

4. **Short Duration of Treatment and Follow-Up:** The treatment duration involving azacitidine was limited, potentially resulting in suboptimal exposure necessary to effectively exhibit biological activity, such as re-establishment of epigenetic mechanisms crucial to mediating therapeutic responses.

5. **Inadequate Biomarker-driven Patient Selection:** The lack of refined biomarker assessment to identify patients likely to benefit from azacitidine treatment hampered the stratification process. Failure to tailor patient selection based on epigenetic profiles may have diluted observed treatment benefits, obscuring potential signals of efficacy in sub-cohorts more amenable to hypomethylating therapies.

**TRIAL CONCLUSION:**
The trial ultimately fell short of its primary endpoint, with an insignificant proportion of patients demonstrating progression-free survival at the 6-month mark post-randomization. Notably, the combination strategy revealed tolerability issues alongside minimal clinical efficacy, reinforcing the premise that azacitidine may not serve a valuable role in the management of advanced ovarian cancer as proposed. Further investigations are warranted to delineate subpopulations that may exhibit a therapeutic response to epigenetic therapies tailored toward specific molecular profiles within ovarian malignancies.
