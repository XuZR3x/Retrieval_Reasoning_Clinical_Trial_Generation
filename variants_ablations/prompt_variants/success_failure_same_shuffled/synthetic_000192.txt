**Failed Clinical Trial Report of Sildenafil Citrate: Impact of Sildenafil on Dyspnea in Patients with Advanced Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension**

**Title**: A MULTICENTER, RANDOMIZED, DOUBLE-BLIND, PLACEBO-CONTROLLED TRIAL TO EVALUATE THE EFFICACY OF ORAL SILDENAFIL CITRATE ADMINISTERED AT 5 MG AND 20 MG TID IN IMPROVING DYSPNEA AND EXERCISE CAPACITY IN ADULTS WITH ADVANCED PULMONARY ARTERIAL HYPERTENSION

**Objective**:  
This study aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of sildenafil citrate in improving dyspnea and exercise capacity in patients with advanced pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) who exhibited persistent symptoms despite optimized background therapy.

**Design**:  
A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving a parallel-group design.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
- Adults aged ≥ 18 years with a clinical diagnosis of advanced PAH (idiopathic or due to connective tissue disease).
- Patients with a mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) ≥ 35 mmHg and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) ≤ 15 mmHg determined by right heart catheterization within 8 weeks of screening.
- A baseline six-minute walk distance (6MWD) of 50–350 meters.

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
- Previous treatment with any PDE-5 inhibitors or endothelium receptor antagonists within the last 3 months.
- Significant comorbidities limiting exercise capacity (e.g., severe cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled diabetes).
- Clinically relevant pulmonary disease other than PAH.
- Pregnancy or lactation.

**Intervention**:  
Participants were randomized to receive either sildenafil citrate at doses of 5 mg TID, 20 mg TID, or placebo for a period of 12 weeks. The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in 6MWD assessed at Week 12. Secondary endpoints included patient-reported dyspnea scores and quality of life measures.

**Statistical Analysis**:  
The trial was powered to detect a significant increase in 6MWD with a sample size estimated based on a target power of 0.80 to discern treatment effects across adequate groups. Statistical analyses were conducted using ANOVA and regression models to account for potential confounding factors such as baseline functional status and concurrent medication use.

**Results**:  
The trial enrolled a total of 180 participants. Following interim analysis, primary endpoint results indicated no statistically significant increase in the 6MWD for either sildenafil group when compared to placebo (p = 0.65 for 5 mg; p = 0.72 for 20 mg). Similarly, there was no notable difference in dyspnea scores or quality of life measurements between groups.

**Contributing Factors to Failure**:  
1. **Insufficient Sample Size**: The study faced high variability in patient responses which diluted potential efficacy signals, likely attributed to an inadequate sample size which was not sufficiently powered to detect real treatment differences.
   
2. **Inherent Pathophysiological Variability**: The variability within the PAH population, including differing etiologies and clinical profiles, may have masked the true effect of sildenafil, leading to diluted results across the treatment groups.
   
3. **Inconsistent Efficacy of Dosing**: The study evaluated only two doses, which could have limited the exploration of the dose-response relationship. It is plausible that the chosen dosing did not achieve an optimal balance necessary to augment pulmonary vasodilation.
   
4. **Potential Placebo Effect**: Several participants reported subjective improvements during the trial period, leading to concerns regarding a psychological placebo effect leading to inflated expectations and over-reporting of improvements.
   
5. **Interaction with Concurrent Medications**: Despite stringent exclusion criteria, prior inadequate washout periods and possible interactions with other PAH-targeted therapies could have compromised the effects of sildenafil on exercise capacity and symptomatic relief.

**Conclusion**:  
This clinical trial did not meet its primary endpoint of demonstrating an increase in exercise capacity via 6MWD or improvement in dyspnea in patients with advanced PAH treated with sildenafil citrate. The results underscore the complexities in treating PAH patients and highlight the need for further research to delineate effective management strategies for this challenging condition. Future trials should focus on more homogenous patient populations and explore varied dosing strategies to fully elucidate sildenafil’s therapeutic potential in PAH.
