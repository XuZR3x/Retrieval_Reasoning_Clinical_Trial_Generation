**Title:** A Phase II Trial of Arsenic Trioxide in Combination with Lenalidomide for Patients with Relapsed Multiple Myeloma 

**RATIONALE:** Arsenic trioxide has previously demonstrated cytotoxic effects against malignant hematologic cells. The combination of arsenic trioxide with lenalidomide, an immunomodulatory drug with established efficacy in multiple myeloma, has the potential for enhanced anti-myeloma activity. This study aims to assess the combination's effectiveness in achieving complete response and improving patient outcomes.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial seeks to evaluate the efficacy and safety of arsenic trioxide in conjunction with lenalidomide in individuals diagnosed with relapsed multiple myeloma who have had at least one previous line of therapy.

**OBJECTIVES:**  
*Primary:*  
- To assess the overall response rate (ORR) following treatment with arsenic trioxide and lenalidomide in patients with relapsed multiple myeloma.  

*Secondary:*  
- To evaluate the time to progression (TTP) and overall survival (OS) following treatment.  
- To determine the incidence and severity of treatment-related adverse events using the NCI CTCAE v5.0 criteria.  
- To explore biomarkers associated with clinical response through gene expression profiling collected during the study.  

**OUTLINE:**  
This is an open-label, multicenter, phase II trial. Patients will receive arsenic trioxide intravenously daily for 5 days in a 28-day cycle alongside lenalidomide orally on days 1-21 of each cycle. Treatment will continue for up to 6 cycles, followed by maintenance therapy with lenalidomide until disease progression.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of relapsed multiple myeloma.  
- Prior exposure to at least one line of therapy, including proteasome inhibitors or immunomodulatory agents.  
- Measurable disease as defined by at least one of the following: serum M-protein ≥ 1 g/dl or urine M-protein ≥ 200 mg/24 hours.  
- Age ≥ 18 years with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.  
- Adequate renal and hepatic function: creatinine ≤ 2.0 mg/dL and liver enzymes ≤ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal.  
- Informed consent obtained from the patient.  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Prior treatment with arsenic trioxide or lenalidomide.  
- Active central nervous system involvement by myeloma.  
- History of allergic reactions to arsenic trioxide, lenalidomide, or related compounds.  
- Concurrent serious medical conditions that may compromise study participation.  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.  

**OUTCOMES:**  
- Expected to accrue approximately 40 patients over a 12-month period.  
- The anticipated primary endpoint is assessed at 6 weeks post-treatment initiation with measures being updated every follow-up.  

**RESULTS:**  
Despite the initial promise of the intervention, key reasons for the trial's failure included:  

1. **Inadequate response rates:** The study ultimately revealed a disproportionately low overall response rate, with insufficient molecular remissions that called into question the anticipated synergy between arsenic trioxide and lenalidomide. The rates observed were significantly below the predefined threshold for efficacy, suggesting that the hoped-for interaction did not occur adequately within the tested population.  

2. **Toxicity profile issues:** Patient safety monitoring reported a concerning rate of severe hematological toxicities such as profound cytopenias and thromboembolic events, which necessitated treatment cessation in a considerable number of participants. The adverse effects were pronounced, directly influencing the tolerability of the treatment regimen.  

3. **Suboptimal patient selection criteria:** Many participants exhibited advanced disease with multiple cytogenetic abnormalities that may have predisposed them to poor outcomes. The inclusion of various risk stratifications without adequate stratification to exclude higher-risk patients likely exacerbated the treatment failure.  

4. **Lack of effective dosing optimization:** Early cycles indicated a lack of responsiveness at established dosage levels, yet adjustments to the dosing schedule were inadequately explored. The failure to adapt the regimen in response to emerging safety and efficacy data undermined the trial's potential.  

5. **Insufficient biomarker monitoring:** Essential early indicators, including specific cytogenetic markers known to predict poorer outcomes, were inadequately monitored, delaying critical decision-making regarding treatment modifications. This lack of timely biomarker analysis likely contributed to not being able to identify patients who could benefit or required intervention.  

In conclusion, the trial did not achieve its primary objective and was subsequently terminated due to the reasons outlined, reflecting the complexities involved in combining novel therapeutic agents in challenging clinical populations. The findings will inform future studies on combination therapies in relapsed multiple myeloma.  

**ACCUMULATION:** Patients will be followed for overall survival and safety until 12 months post-trial.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Diagnosed relapsed multiple myeloma consistent with established International Myeloma Working Group criteria.  
- Prior treatments that may include proteasome inhibitors and immunomodulatory agents.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Adequate organ function and performance status suitable for treatment protocol participation.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**  
- No investigational agent within 30 days prior to enrollment or regimen affecting myeloma therapy prior to initial study treatment.
