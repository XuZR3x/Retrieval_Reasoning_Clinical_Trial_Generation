**Clinical Trial Report: OSI-906 in Advanced Solid Tumors**

**Title:** A Phase I Study to Evaluate the Safety and Pharmacokinetics of OSI-906 with Tailored Dosing Strategies in Patients with Advanced Solid Tumors

**Study Design:** This multicenter, open-label, phase I study was conducted to assess the safety, pharmacokinetics, and preliminary efficacy of OSI-906 in patients with advanced solid tumors. Utilizing a stratified design, patients were allocated to varying dosing cohorts based on pre-existing comorbid conditions and tumor profiling, ensuring a comprehensive evaluation of the drug across diverse patient demographics.

**Objectives:**
- Primary Objective: To determine the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of OSI-906 in patients with advanced solid tumors.
- Secondary Objectives: To characterize the pharmacokinetics (PK) of OSI-906, assess its pharmacodynamic (PD) relationship with tumor response, and evaluate preliminary antitumor activity.

**Dosing Regimen:** The trial utilized a flexible dose-escalation model with three distinct dosing strategies tailored to the patient cohort:
1. **Dosing Schedule 1 (S1):** OSI-906 administered at a fixed dose three times weekly.
2. **Dosing Schedule 2 (S2):** OSI-906 administered at a two-week continuous dosing regimen followed by a week off. 
3. **Dosing Schedule 3 (S3):** OSI-906 administered continuously with dosage adjustments determined by individual patient tolerance and response.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with histologically confirmed advanced solid tumors, refractory to standard therapy.
- ECOG performance status ≤ 2.
- Life expectancy of at least 12 weeks.
- Measurable disease according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Documented history of diabetes mellitus.
- Active brain metastases or significant cardiovascular disease.
- Concurrent anticancer therapies, except for certain tolerated hormones.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Results:**

The trial successfully enrolled 150 patients with various solid tumors, predominantly colorectal, lung, and pancreatic cancers. The phased dose escalation allowed for the identification of the MTD of OSI-906, which was determined to be 100 mg three times weekly. This innovative approach resulted in a low incidence of adverse events, with only 8% reporting grade 3 or higher toxicities, predominantly manageable gastrointestinal disturbances.

Pharmacokinetic analysis demonstrated a dose-proportional exposure at the determined MTD, with a half-life supporting the administered dosing schedules. PD outcomes were promising; tumor response assessments indicated a clinical benefit rate of 54%, with 12% of patients achieving partial responses, notably in the colorectal cohort where tumor resistance to previous therapies was established.

**Conclusion:**
The trial concluded that OSI-906, administered via a tailored dosing regimen, is both safe and tolerable in patients with advanced solid tumors, producing encouraging preliminary anti-tumor activity. The study's design, which included strict inclusion criteria and robust patient monitoring strategies, played a pivotal role in its success. Future studies are recommended to further explore the efficacy of OSI-906, particularly in combination with other targeted agents in refractory malignancies. The data collected herein will inform subsequent Phase II trials, aimed at establishing OSI-906’s role in oncologic therapeutics.
