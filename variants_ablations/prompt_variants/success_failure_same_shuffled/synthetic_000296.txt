**Clinical Trial Title**: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Phase III Study of Alemtuzumab for the Prevention of Acute Rejection in Recipients of Living Donor Kidney Transplants

**RATIONALE**: Alemtuzumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting CD52, plays a pivotal role in depleting T-cells, thus offering a targeted approach to immunosuppression. The specific mechanism of action enhances graft preservation while minimizing adverse effects associated with more traditional immunosuppressive regimens. This study evaluates alemtuzumab's efficacy and safety in preventing acute rejection in living donor kidney transplant recipients.

**PURPOSE**: To assess the efficacy of alemtuzumab in reducing episodes of acute rejection in recipients of living donor kidney transplants compared to placebo, focusing on both short-term and long-term outcomes.

**OBJECTIVES**:
- Primary Objective: Evaluate the incidence of biopsy-proven acute rejection episodes within the first year post-transplant in recipients treated with alemtuzumab versus placebo.
- Secondary Objectives: 
  - Assess graft survival rates at 1 year and 3 years post-transplantation.
  - Evaluate the safety profile of alemtuzumab in this patient population, including rates of infection, malignancy, and other adverse events.
  - Analyze the feasibility of withdrawal of auxiliary immunosuppressive agents without compromising graft integrity.

**OUTLINE**: 
Patients will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either alemtuzumab or placebo intravenously at a dose of 30 mg on Day -1, with the standard immunosuppressive regimen comprising tacrolimus and mycophenolate mofetil initiated concurrently post-transplant. Treatment continues for up to 12 months, with follow-up assessments every 3 months.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL**: A total of 150 patients will be enrolled over 3 years.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Histologically confirmed end-stage renal disease necessitating transplantation
- Suitable candidates for living donor kidney transplantation 
- No prior organ transplant or significant comorbidities that would preclude participation

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Age: 18 to 60 years 
- Performance status: ECOG 0-1
- Life expectancy: > 1 year
- Hematopoietic: WBC > 3,000/mm^3
- Renal: No chronic kidney disease other than that requiring transplant 
- Female patients must be non-pregnant and utilize effective contraception.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA**:
- History of T-cell depletion therapy within 6 months prior to enrollment
- Positive T-cell crossmatch 
- Active malignancy or previous history of malignancy requiring treatment, except for non-melanoma skin cancers
- Serious systemic infection at time of enrollment or active autoimmune disease

**CONCURRENT THERAPY**:
Patients must not receive other investigational agents or additional immunosuppressive therapies beyond those indicated.

**PRIMARY OUTCOMES**:
- Incidence of biopsy-proven acute rejection episodes (Banff criteria) in all enrolled participants from Day 0 through Year 1 post-transplant.

**SECONDARY OUTCOMES**:
- Graft survival rates at Year 1 and Year 3
- Adverse event rate, specifically monitored for opportunistic infections and malignancy rates
- Change in immunosuppressive regimen and withdrawal success rates 

**FOLLOW-UP**:
Patients will be monitored with laboratory tests and clinical assessments at 1 week, 1 month, and quarterly up to 3 years post-transplant. Special emphasis will be placed on the assessment of infection markers during follow-ups due to alemtuzumab's favorable toxicity profile.

The study hypothesis anticipates that alemtuzumab will provide superior protection against acute rejection while maintaining an excellent safety profile, thereby encouraging minimal long-term reliance on immunosuppressive therapy. This aligns with previously documented evidence demonstrating improved outcomes with alemtuzumab and provides a novel perspective on best practices in kidney transplantation protocol design.
