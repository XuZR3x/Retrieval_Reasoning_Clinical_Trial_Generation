**Failed Clinical Trial of Raltegravir: Efficacy of Raltegravir in Reducing Cerebral Inflammation and Neurocognitive Impairment in HIV-Infected Individuals with Asymptomatic Neurocognitive Disorder**

**Objective:** This study aims to investigate the efficacy of the integrase inhibitor raltegravir in mitigating cerebral inflammation and improving neurocognitive function in HIV-infected patients diagnosed with asymptomatic neurocognitive disorder (ANI). The trial hypothesizes that raltegravir’s central nervous system penetration will result in significant reductions in neuroinflammation markers, as well as improvements in cognitive performance, compared to a control group receiving standard ART without integrase inhibitors.

**Background:** Neurocognitive disorders remain prevalent among patients living with HIV, even when viral loads are suppressed to undetectable levels. The interplay between persistent HIV infection and inflammatory responses in the CNS may hinder neurocognitive recovery. Raltegravir, known for its therapeutic penetration in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), presents a unique opportunity to target CNS reservoirs of HIV and potentially reduce neuroinflammation and restore neurocognitive function.

**Study Design:** A multi-center, double-blind, randomized controlled trial comparing 60 HIV-infected individuals diagnosed with ANI who will receive either raltegravir combined with a backbone of two NRTIs (tenofovir/emtricitabine) versus a control group receiving a standard NNRTI regimen (efavirenz) for 24 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults aged 18-65 years.
2. Documented HIV infection with persistent plasma viral loads <50 copies/mL.
3. Clinical diagnosis of ANI based on the Frascati criteria.
4. Baseline detectable HIV-1 RNA levels in CSF.
5. Agreement to undergo serial lumbar punctures for CSF sampling.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Cognitive or psychiatric disorders that significantly impair judgement or consent.
2. Previous or current treatment with any integrase inhibitors.
3. Contraindications to lumbar puncture.
4. History of major opportunistic infections within the past 90 days.
5. Co-infection with HCV or active hepatitis B.
6. Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

**Primary Endpoints:**
1. Change from baseline in HIV RNA levels in CSF.
2. Improvement in neurocognitive assessment scores (using the Mini-Mental State Examination).

**Secondary Endpoints:**
1. Levels of inflammatory markers in CSF (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α).
2. Neuropsychological performance assessed using comprehensive cognitive battery tests.
3. Quality of life measured by the HIV-specific Quality of Life Questionnaire (HIV-QOL).

**Results:**
Upon statistical analysis, no significant differences were observed between the raltegravir treatment group and the standard NNRTI control group in terms of reductions in viral load in CSF or improvements in cognitive function and inflammatory markers. Despite the adequate CNS drug levels demonstrated post-treatment, the expected reduction in neuroinflammatory response did not materialize. Furthermore, assessment of cognitive performance revealed no statistically significant change from baseline, suggesting limited efficacy of raltegravir in addressing cognitive decline associated with ongoing neuroinflammation.

**Conclusion:**
The findings of this trial indicate that while raltegravir achieves therapeutic concentrations in the CNS, it does not demonstrate sufficient efficacy in alleviating neurocognitive impairment or reducing cerebral inflammation markers in individuals with ANI compared to standard NNRTI regimens. Potential reasons for these suboptimal outcomes may include inadequate drug efficacy against persistent CNS reservoirs, the impact of ongoing inflammatory states in HIV-infected individuals, and insufficient statistical power due to an inadequate sample size. These results highlight the need for further research focused on specific HIV-related neurocognitive dysfunctions with greater attention to CNS-targeted therapies and their mechanisms of action within the neuroinflammatory context.

**Anticipated Clinical Impact:** The failure to demonstrate significant benefits with raltegravir emphasizes the complexity of HIV-associated neurocognitive disorders, suggesting a reevaluation of therapeutic strategies aimed at CNS involvement and further exploring inflammatory pathways that may underlie cognitive dysfunction in this patient population.
