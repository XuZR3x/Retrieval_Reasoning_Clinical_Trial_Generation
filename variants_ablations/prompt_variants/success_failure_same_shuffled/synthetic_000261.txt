**Study Protocol: Dabigatran for the Acute Management of Progressive Cerebral Ischemia**

**Study Title**: Efficacy and Safety of Dabigatran in Patients with Progressive Cerebral Ischemia: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Rationale**: Progressive cerebral ischemia, characterized by the gradual worsening of neurological deficits due to cerebral ischemia, poses a significant therapeutic challenge. Standard anticoagulant therapies often lead to increased hemorrhagic events. Dabigatran, a direct thrombin inhibitor, has demonstrated a favorable hemorrhagic safety profile while providing effective anticoagulation. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of dabigatran in patients experiencing progressive cerebral ischemia, ensuring timely intervention through structured enrollment within 48 hours of symptom onset.

**Study Design**: This study is a multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled trial involving 400 participants across five (5) specialized stroke centers. Patients with progressive cerebral ischemia, defined by an NIHSS score increase of ≥2 points over a 24-hour period, will be enrolled following central neuroimaging verification of ischemic changes. Participants will be randomized 1:1 to receive either dabigatran (150 mg BID) or standard therapy with aspirin (ASA 81 mg daily) for 60 days.

**Objectives**:
1. To assess the safety of dabigatran compared to standard antiplatelet therapy in patients with progressive cerebral ischemia.
2. To evaluate the rate of symptomatic and asymptomatic hemorrhagic transformation (HT) associated with dabigatran therapy.
3. To analyze functional outcomes measured by the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) at 90 days post-treatment initiation.

**Hypothesis**: It is hypothesized that dabigatran will have a comparable or lower rate of symptomatic HT compared to ASA, while providing superior functional outcomes at 90 days.

**Outcomes**:
- **Primary Endpoint**: The rate of symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation (HT), defined as parenchymal hematoma exceeding 30% of the infarcted area on diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), accompanied by clinical deterioration (≥4 point increase in NIHSS) within 60 days of treatment initiation.
- **Secondary Endpoints**: 
  - Rate of asymptomatic HT evaluated at the 30-day follow-up MRI.
  - Change in functional status assessed via the mRS at 90 days post-enrollment.
  - Incidence of ischemic stroke events within the treatment duration.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Males and females aged 18 years and older.
2. Diagnosis of progressive cerebral ischemia as indicated by an NIHSS score increase of ≥2 points within a 24-hour period and confirmed by neuroimaging.
3. Symptom onset within 48 hours prior to randomization.
4. Informed consent from the patient or a legally acceptable representative.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Major hemorrhagic or ischemic stroke within the past 30 days.
2. Contraindications to MRI imaging.
3. Renal dysfunction defined as eGFR <30 ml/min.
4. Known hypersensitivity to dabigatran.
5. Patients with significant ongoing bleeding risks or contraindications to anticoagulation therapy.

**Administrative Structure**: The study will be conducted in collaboration with the Canadian Stroke Consortium, with the University of British Columbia serving as the coordinating center. Data collection will be overseen through an electronic data capture system, ensuring centralized monitoring of imaging studies and treatment protocols.

**Results Analysis**: Statistical analyses will be conducted using the intention-to-treat approach. The comparison of HT rates between groups will utilize Chi-square tests, while mRS scores will be analyzed using logistic regression models adjusted for baseline characteristics.

This innovative study aims to solidify the role of dabigatran in the management of patients with progressive cerebral ischemia, utilizing timely intervention protocols and comprehensive safety monitoring to substantiate its efficacy in routine clinical practice.
