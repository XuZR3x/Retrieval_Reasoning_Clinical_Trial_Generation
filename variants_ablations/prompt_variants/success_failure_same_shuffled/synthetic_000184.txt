<clinicalTrial>
   <trialID>CT-LIRA-9876</trialID>
   <title>The Impact of Liraglutide on Psoriasis Severity and Associated Comorbidities: A Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Study</title>
   <objective>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of liraglutide in the reduction of psoriasis severity as measured by the Psoriasis Area Severity Index (PASI) and to explore its impact on metabolic parameters in patients with plaque psoriasis. The secondary objective is to assess the safety profile of liraglutide in this patient population.</objective>
   <design>Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial</design>
   <duration>24 weeks</duration>
   <inclusionCriteria>
       <criterion>Caucasian participants aged 18 years and above</criterion>
       <criterion>Diagnosis of plaque psoriasis with a PASI score greater than 12</criterion>
       <criterion>Body mass index (BMI) between 27 and 40 kg/m²</criterion>
       <criterion>Stable treatment regimen for psoriasis for a minimum of 3 months prior to enrollment</criterion>
       <criterion>Normal blood pressure and metabolic parameters</criterion>
       <criterion>Informed consent obtained and willingness to comply with study protocol</criterion>
   </inclusionCriteria>
   <exclusionCriteria>
       <criterion>Psoriatic arthritis or history of inflammatory joint disease</criterion>
       <criterion>History of diabetes mellitus (type 1 and type 2)</criterion>
       <criterion>Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance <30 mL/min)</criterion>
       <criterion>Any active malignancy or a history of cancer (excluding non-melanoma skin cancers)</criterion>
       <criterion>History of significant cardiovascular disease (NYHA class III-IV)</criterion>
       <criterion>Current use of other biologic therapies or systemic medications that influence Psoriasis outcomes</criterion>
       <criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding women</criterion>
   </exclusionCriteria>
   <primaryEndpoints>
       <endpoint>Change in PASI scores at week 24</endpoint>
       <endpoint>Adverse events reported and treatment discontinuation rate due to side effects</endpoint>
   </primaryEndpoints>
   <secondaryEndpoints>
       <endpoint>Change in Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) scores at week 24</endpoint>
       <endpoint>Metabolic parameters including fasting glucose and lipid profiles assessed at week 24</endpoint>
   </secondaryEndpoints>
   <rationale>The study was initiated based on emerging evidence suggesting potential benefits of liraglutide in metabolic modulation; however, it faced several unforeseen challenges leading to termination. Key reasons included:</rationale>
   <reasons>
       <reason>Insufficient therapeutic target engagement, given that GLP-1 pathways may not effectively align with the multifactorial etiology of psoriasis, ultimately yielding inconclusive results.</reason>
       <reason>Patient population demonstrated substantial heterogeneity along with multiple comorbid conditions (especially cardiovascular and renal diseases) that convoluted data interpretation, increasing variability in outcomes.</reason>
       <reason>Inadequate outcome measures potentially failed to encapsulate the clinical breadth of improvement or deterioration in quality-of-life metrics among participants.</reason>
       <reason>Participants already maintained a stable treatment plan, which may have inhibited their responsiveness to liraglutide due to treatment saturation effects.</reason>
       <reason>Many subjects reported adverse effects predominantly gastrointestinal, resulting in substantial discontinuation rates and non-compliance, ultimately confounding the integrity of the study outcomes.</reason>
   </reasons>
</clinicalTrial>
