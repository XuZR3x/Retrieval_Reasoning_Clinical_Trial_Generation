**Failed Clinical Trial of Efavirenz: Impact of Psychosocial Interventions on Adherence to Antiretroviral Therapy in HIV-Infected Patients**  
*Phase II, Randomized, Open-label Study to Evaluate the Effect of Psychosocial Support on Adherence and Treatment Outcomes in HIV-Infected Patients Initiating Efavirenz-Based Antiretroviral Therapy*  

This trial aims to investigate whether the integration of structured psychosocial support into the management of HIV patients receiving efavirenz can improve medication adherence and overall treatment outcomes compared to standard care alone. Given the documented correlation between psychological comorbidities, such as depression, and non-adherence, this study will assess the efficacy of tailored interventions aimed at enhancing adherence in patients initiating efavirenz treatment. 

**Study Design:**  
Enrolled participants will be randomized into two arms.  
- **Intervention Group:** Participants will receive efavirenz along with a structured psychosocial support program, including counseling and support groups, in conjunction with routine clinical care.  
- **Control Group:** Participants will receive efavirenz with standard clinical care without any additional psychosocial interventions.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Documented HIV-1 infection diagnosed via ELISA.  
- ART-naïve status (excluding any mono-therapy for mother-to-child transmission).  
- Age ≥18 years and ≤65 years.  
- A score indicating moderate to severe depressive symptomatology on the PHQ-9.  
- Ability and willingness to provide informed consent.  
- No contraindications to efavirenz as per standard treatment guidelines.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Prior history of non-adherence to any form of medical therapy.  
- Active substance abuse or severe psychiatric illness that could interfere with participation.  
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh score > 6).  
- Concurrent use of medications with known pharmacokinetic interactions with efavirenz without appropriate monitoring.  
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.  

**Outcomes:**  
The primary outcome measure is the rate of adherence to efavirenz therapy at 48 weeks, assessed via pill counts and self-reported adherence data. Secondary outcome measures will include:  
- Virologic suppression (HIV RNA < 50 copies/mL) at 48 weeks.  
- Change in CD4+ T-cell counts.  
- Safety and tolerability of the efavirenz regimen in this population.  

**Statistical Analysis:**  
An intention-to-treat analysis will be conducted to compare adherence rates and treatment outcomes between groups. Statistical significance will be set at p < 0.05, accounting for potential confounders.

**Results:**  
Following 48 weeks of intervention, a total of 250 participants were enrolled, with analysis revealing significantly low adherence rates in both the intervention and control groups, largely attributable to under-addressed psychological barriers and uncertainties surrounding efavirenz therapy. Participants consistently expressed concerns regarding adverse effects, particularly neuropsychiatric symptoms, which exacerbated feelings of anxiety and depression, thus undermining adherence efforts. Statistical evaluations demonstrated no significant difference in virologic suppression rates (p=0.45) between the two groups at the 48-week mark.

**Conclusion:**  
This trial exemplifies the complexities surrounding the management of HIV-infected individuals initiating efavirenz-based therapy. Failure to adequately incorporate comprehensive psychological assessments into routine care, coupled with unrealistic expectations for adherence without addressing underlying mental health issues, ultimately resulted in suboptimal treatment outcomes. Future trials must adopt a more holistic approach, recognizing the interplay of psychological health and medication adherence in optimizing clinical results for this vulnerable population.
