**Failed Clinical Trial of Pioglitazone: Evaluation of Pioglitazone on Cognitive Function in Subjects with Mild Cognitive Impairment Due to Vascular Contributions to Cognitive Impairment and Dementia**

**Study Design:**
A Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Study to Assess the Safety and Efficacy of Pioglitazone in Slowing Cognitive Decline in Subjects with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) Due to Vascular Contributions to Cognitive Impairment and Dementia (VCID).

**Purpose:**
The purpose of this clinical trial is to evaluate the efficacy of pioglitazone in improving cognitive function as measured by a standardized cognitive battery over 24 months in patients diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment attributed to vascular factors. This study seeks to determine whether pioglitazone can mitigate the cognitive decline associated with VCID.

**Background:**
While pioglitazone has been shown to enhance insulin sensitivity and exert pleiotropic effects on inflammation, its specific effects on cognitive decline due to vascular factors remain inadequately documented. Prior studies have highlighted failures regarding efficacy in cognitive function and safety due to adverse cardiovascular profiles associated with the intervention.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Males and females, aged 65 years and older, diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment due to VCID.
2. Documented cognitive impairment defined as a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score between 18 and 26.
3. Able to provide informed consent and comply with study procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease or other neurodegenerative disorders.
2. History of cardiovascular event within the last six months.
3. Uncontrolled hypertension (systolic blood pressure >160 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure >100 mmHg).
4. Current treatment with high-dose thiazolidinediones or other agents affecting insulin sensitivity.

**Intervention:**
Participants will receive either pioglitazone (0.8 mg daily) or placebo for 24 months. 

**Study Procedures:**
All participants will undergo baseline cognitive assessments, followed by continuous monitoring of cognitive changes through a battery of cognitive tests at regular intervals (6, 12, 18, and 24 months). Safety assessments will include monitoring for potential adverse events related to cardiovascular and renal health, given previous indications that the adverse event profile associated with pioglitazone may outweigh potential benefits.

**Endpoints:**
- Change in the Composite Score of Cognitive Function (measured by a wide array of neuropsychological tests assessing multiple cognitive domains).
- Incidence of adverse events, particularly cardiovascular events and new diagnoses of heart failure.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Comparative analysis between the pioglitazone and placebo groups will be performed using an intention-to-treat approach. A primary focus will be on the change in composite cognitive test scores from baseline to 24 months, with secondary outcomes addressing safety profiles and quality of life measures. 

**Trial Duration:**
The expected duration of participation is 24 months, with assessments scheduled at baseline, 6 months, 12 months, 18 months, and 24 months.

**Results:**
The trial ultimately failed due to multiple factors: 
1. Lack of demonstrable efficacy in slowing cognitive decline, as evidenced by composite score measurements that did not yield significant differences from placebo at 24 months, confirming previous findings indicated in the AD-4833/TOMM40_303 study.
2. Insufficient patient adherence to study protocols observed, as a number of participants reported issues with daily dosing requirements, potentially compromising validity due to underpowered analysis resulting from significant attrition.
3. An adverse event profile indicating increased incidences of peripheral edema and complications associated with renal function, highlighting the limitations of using pioglitazone in populations where vascular health is already compromised.

This failure reaffirms the findings of previous studies, indicating that the specific mechanisms targeted by pioglitazone may not align with the underlying pathophysiological mechanisms of VCID, thus questioning its role as a viable therapeutic option within this cohort. Enhanced understanding of the dose-response relationship is necessitated, as undetermined efficacious doses may have contributed to the ineffectiveness observed. 

**Conclusion:**
Given these outcomes, the trial concluded that pioglitazone does not confer the anticipated cognitive benefits in patients with mild cognitive impairment due to vascular factors over a specified period and brings forth critical insights into the drug's overall profile for this application. Further research is warranted to explore alternative interventions that could more effectively address cognitive impairment associated with vascular contributions.
