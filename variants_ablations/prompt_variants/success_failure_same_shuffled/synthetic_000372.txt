#### Failed Clinical Trial of Capecitabine: Capecitabine and Targeted Therapy in First-Line Treatment of HER2-Positive Gastric Cancer

**Phase II Trial of Capecitabine in Combination With Trastuzumab as First-Line Therapy for HER2-Positive Advanced Gastric Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** HER2-positive gastric cancer poses significant treatment challenges, necessitating effective first-line therapeutic strategies. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of capecitabine in combination with trastuzumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting the HER2 receptor, in enhancing tumor response in patients diagnosed with advanced HER2-positive gastric cancer. Prior studies suggest that dual therapy targeting HER2 may potentially improve patient outcomes; however, emerging evidence also indicates that such combinations may not provide the expected improvement in survival rates.

**PURPOSE:** The primary objective of this randomized Phase II trial is to compare the overall response rate (ORR) of patients receiving capecitabine combined with trastuzumab versus those receiving trastuzumab monotherapy. Secondary objectives include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), safety profiles, and quality of life assessments.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, randomized trial involves multiple treatment centers across various geographical locations. Patients are stratified by prior therapies, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, and disease stage at baseline. 

- **Arm I (Capecitabine plus Trastuzumab):** Patients receive oral capecitabine twice daily on days 1-14 in conjunction with intravenous trastuzumab on a scheduled basis.
- **Arm II (Trastuzumab alone):** Patients receive trastuzumab following the same regimen but without capecitabine. 

Follow-up assessments for therapeutic efficacy will occur at specified intervals.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed HER2-positive gastric adenocarcinoma, either locally advanced or metastatic.
- Measurable disease based on RECIST criteria.
- Patients may have previously received palliative care but no prior chemotherapy for advanced disease is permitted.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status 0-2.
- Adequate organ function: 
    - Hematologic: ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³; platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³; hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL.
    - Hepatic: Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN, AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN.
    - Renal: Creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.

- Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study procedures.
- Women of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and agree to use reliable contraception during the trial duration.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Concurrent active infection requiring treatment.
- Pregnant or lactating women.
- History of cardiac dysfunction or severe comorbidities impacting therapy tolerability.
- Active malignancy other than treated non-melanoma skin cancer or cervical carcinoma in situ within the past five years.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVE:** 
To evaluate the ORR in patients receiving capecitabine combined with trastuzumab compared to trastuzumab monotherapy.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
- To determine PFS and OS among the treatment arms.
- To assess and compare treatment-related toxicities and quality of life utilizing validated measures.

**RESULTS:** The interim analysis revealed no statistically significant improvement in ORR between the intervention and control arms. Additionally, adverse effects associated with the combination therapy resulted in elevated toxicity profiles, leading to increased patient discontinuation rates. In particular, the adverse event rates suggest that the dual-agent approach may enhance complications without providing sufficient therapeutic efficacy in disease stabilization.

**CONCLUSION:** Given the preliminary results, the combination of capecitabine with trastuzumab did not confer a significant benefit over trastuzumab monotherapy in treating HER2-positive gastric cancer. The trial was halted due to these findings that indicated limited efficacy in tumor response, compounded toxicity, and inadequate patient stratification. Future studies should focus on refining patient selection criteria and optimizing therapeutic combinations to generate meaningful clinical benefits for specific subpopulations.

**DISEASE CONTROL OUTCOMES:** Overall survival and progression-free survival rates did not distinguish the arms favorably, underscoring the need for further investigation into effective treatment strategies for this patient population. The complexities involved in dual-agent regimens such as pharmacological interactions and increased adverse events contributed significantly to the trial's adverse outcome.
