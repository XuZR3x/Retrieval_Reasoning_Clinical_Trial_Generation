**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy of Paroxetine in Treating Menopausal Symptoms and Comorbid Depression in Women Over 50**

**Study Design**: A Double-Blind, Randomized Controlled Trial

**Study Title**: Efficacy of Paroxetine in Improving Menopausal Symptoms and Comorbid Depression in Women Aged Over 50: A 12-Week Randomized Clinical Trial

**Objective**: This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of paroxetine in alleviating symptomatic menopause-related disturbances, specifically targeting vasomotor symptoms, depressive symptoms, and overall quality of life in women aged 50 and above.

**Background**: Menopausal women often experience debilitating symptoms due to hormonal changes, which can also contribute to the development of mood disorders, notably depression. The interrelationship between menopause-related symptoms and mood disorders may heighten vulnerability, necessitating effective pharmacological alternatives, especially for those reluctant to use hormone replacement therapy due to associated risks. Paroxetine, as a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor, has demonstrated significant potential in addressing the dual facets of psychological and physiological disturbances experienced by this demographic.

**Methodology**: 
This 12-week double-blind randomized controlled trial will include 200 participants aged 50 and over who meet the following inclusion criteria:

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Women aged 50 years or older, perimenopausal or postmenopausal status defined by the cessation of menstruation for 12 months.
2. Presence of menopausal symptoms as evidenced by a total Green Climacteric Scale (GCS) score > 25, characterized by severe hot flashes and night sweats.
3. Diagnosis of mild to moderate depressive symptoms scored via the Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS score 10-19).
4. Participants must consent to the study protocol and verification of compliance.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. History of severe depressive disorders or any Axis I psychiatric disorders.
2. Current hormonal therapy or other antidepressant use within the past month.
3. Unstable medical conditions including seizure disorders, substance abuse history, or contraindications to SSRIs.
4. Pregnant or lactating women.

**Outcome Measures**:
Primary outcome measures will include changes in mean GCS scores from baseline to the 12-week endpoint and the mean MADRS scores, analyzed for treatment group differences to assess both physiological and psychological improvements. Secondary outcomes will involve assessment of quality of life via the Short Form Health Survey (SF-12) at both baseline and study conclusion.

**Statistical Analysis**:
The data will be analyzed using ANOVA for repeated measures to determine within-group and between-group differences over time. A p-value of less than 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Ethical Considerations**:
All participants will provide informed consent prior to enrollment. The study will be conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the protocol will be approved by the institutional review board.

**Expected Outcomes**:
This study anticipates a significant reduction in menopausal symptoms (hot flashes and night sweats) and depressive symptoms with paroxetine treatment, asserting its potential as an effective intervention in managing overlapping menopausal and psychological distress. By utilizing standardized measurement tools like GCS and MADRS, we aim to produce reliable results that can reinforce awareness and formulation of treatment pathways for non-hormonal therapeutic strategies, echoing prior findings regarding paroxetine’s efficacy in dual modalities.
