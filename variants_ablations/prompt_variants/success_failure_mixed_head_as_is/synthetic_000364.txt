<clinicalTrial>
    <title>A Phase II Trial of Cetuximab and Nivolumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer</title>
    <rationale>
        The combination of cetuximab, a monoclonal antibody targeting the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), and nivolumab, a programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitor, has been hypothesized to enhance antitumor effects through improved immunogenicity and synergistic therapeutic mechanisms. Cetuximab’s ability to inhibit EGFR-mediated signaling can potentiate nivolumab's immunologic efficacy, thereby leading to improved clinical outcomes in advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) that expresses EGFR.
    </rationale>
    <objectives>
        <primary>
            To evaluate overall survival in patients with advanced NSCLC treated with cetuximab and nivolumab versus nivolumab alone.
        </primary>
        <secondary>
            <item>
                To assess progression-free survival in both treatment arms.
            </item>
            <item>
                To correlate EGFR expression levels with treatment response and overall survival.
            </item>
            <item>
                To evaluate the safety and tolerability of the combination therapy.
            </item>
        </secondary>
    </objectives>
    <design>
        <type>Multicenter, randomized controlled study</type>
        <groups>
            <group>
                <name>Arm A</name>
                <description>Patients receive nivolumab intravenously (IV) every two weeks while also receiving cetuximab IV weekly.</description>
            </group>
            <group>
                <name>Arm B</name>
                <description>Patients receive nivolumab IV every two weeks alone.</description>
            </group>
        </groups>
        <randomization>
            <description>Patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either the combination of cetuximab and nivolumab or nivolumab monotherapy.</description>
        </randomization>
    </design>
    <population>
        <criteria>
            <item>
                Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced non-small cell lung cancer with documented EGFR expression.
            </item>
            <item>
                No prior therapy targeting EGFR or immune checkpoint inhibitors.
            </item>
            <item>
                Performance status (ECOG) of 0-1.
            </item>
            <item>
                Adequate hematologic and organ function: Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm<sup>3</sup>, platelets ≥ 100,000/mm<sup>3</sup>, creatinine clearance ≥ 45 mL/min.
            </item>
            <item>
                Age ≥ 18 years.
            </item>
            <item>
                No significant comorbidities that would compromise participation.
            </item>
        </criteria>
    </population>
    <outcomes>
        <primary>
            Overall survival at 12 months, assessed using Kaplan-Meier survival curves.
        </primary>
        <secondary>
            Progression-free survival at 6 months, response rates in accordance with RECIST criteria, and assessment of treatment-related adverse events.
        </secondary>
    </outcomes>
    <timeline>
        <accrualPeriod>
            <duration>12 months</duration>
            <targetPatients>200</targetPatients>
        </accrualPeriod>
        <followUp>
            <duration>24 months post-treatment initiation</duration>
        </followUp>
    </timeline>
    <results>
        <description>The combination of cetuximab and nivolumab resulted in a statistically significant improvement in overall survival compared to nivolumab alone (18-month survival rate: 65% vs. 50%). Patients treated with the combination demonstrated enhanced progression-free survival and an increased overall response rate, correlating positively with higher EGFR expression levels.</description>
        <tolerability>
            <description>The combination therapy exhibited a favorable safety profile, with manageable adverse effects including fatigue, rash, and immune-related events, reflecting the low incidence of severe toxicities.</description>
        </tolerability>
    </results>
</clinicalTrial>
