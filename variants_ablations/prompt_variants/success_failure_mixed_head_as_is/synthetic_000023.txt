**Title: Phase II CABO-LUNG: Evaluation of Cabozantinib in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) Post-Progression on EGFR Inhibitors**

**Study Overview:**
This multicenter, open-label, single-arm Phase II clinical trial assesses the efficacy and safety of cabozantinib in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have experienced disease progression following treatment with EGFR inhibitors. Cabozantinib, a potent dual inhibitor of MET and VEGFR pathways, is theorized to facilitate tumor control through its ability to mitigate resistance mechanisms associated with EGFR-targeted therapies.

**Background Rationale:**
1. **Pharmacodynamics:** Cabozantinib's dual inhibition of c-MET and VEGFR2 pathways addresses crucial pathways involved in both tumor growth and angiogenesis, providing a robust mechanism of action in relapsed disease states, particularly following resistance to EGFR inhibitors in NSCLC.

2. **Adaptive Design:** This trial utilizes an adaptive design which includes a preliminary assessment of treatment response every 8 weeks. This approach permits timely modifications of the therapeutic regimen based on emerging data concerning efficacy and tolerability, thus enhancing the chances of identified optimal doses while minimizing adverse events.

3. **Patient Selection:** Rigorous eligibility criteria have been established to recruit a well-defined patient population. Eligible participants exhibit histologically confirmed, advanced NSCLC with a documented progression after at least one line of EGFR TKI therapy. This enhances the specificity of treatment efficacy assessments.

4. **Dynamic Monitoring:** Consistent imaging evaluations utilizing CT scans for tumor measurement will occur bi-monthly throughout the treatment course, facilitating prompt recognition of disease response or progression. Such structured monitoring will allow for timely adjustments in treatment strategies and support the extension of therapy duration for patients achieving clinical benefit.

5. **International Multi-Center Collaboration:** The diverse demographic nature of this international trial, spanning various clinical sites worldwide, promotes the representation of different ethnicities and genetic backgrounds, thereby increasing the external validity of the findings and fostering a more comprehensive understanding of cabozantinib's performance across populations.

**Trial Design:**
The study will enroll approximately 60 patients who meet the predetermined inclusion criteria. Participants will receive cabozantinib daily at a dose of 60 mg for 28-day treatment cycles. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed NSCLC.
- Documented progression of disease despite prior EGFR inhibition.
- At least one measurable lesion per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Age ≥ 18 years with a life expectancy of ≥ 3 months.
- ECOG performance status of ≤ 1.
- Adequate organ and marrow function, including:
  - ANC > 1,500/mcL,
  - Platelets > 100,000/mcL,
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL, and others per institutional parameters.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment with cabozantinib or other MET/VEGFR inhibitors.
- Symptomatic CNS metastases.
- Non-recovered adverse effects from prior therapies within the specified washout periods.
- History of significant cardiovascular conditions or active infections requiring systemic treatment.

**End Points:**
The primary endpoint is to evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) to cabozantinib as observed by imaging assessments at scheduled intervals. Secondary endpoints will include median progression-free survival (PFS) rates and overall survival (OS) metrics, alongside safety evaluations per CTCAE v4.0 criteria.

**Follow-Up:**
Patients will enter follow-up assessments every 30 days during treatment and for up to 12 months post-treatment completion to evaluate long-term safety and progression status.

**Conclusion:**
The Phase II CABO-LUNG clinical trial seeks to extend the existing knowledge around the utilization of cabozantinib in advanced NSCLC populations with prior EGFR inhibition, anticipating that the pharmacological profile of cabozantinib combined with methodical adaptive trial design will yield significant advancements in treatment outcomes for this challenging cancer subtype.
