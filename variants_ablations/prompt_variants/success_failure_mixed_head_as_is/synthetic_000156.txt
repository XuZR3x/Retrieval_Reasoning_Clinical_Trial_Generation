**Title**: The Efficacy and Safety of Metformin in Weight Management Among Obese Patients with Pre-Diabetes: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial

**Abstract**: The study aims to evaluate the effects of metformin on weight reduction and metabolic parameters in obese individuals diagnosed with pre-diabetes. This multicenter trial will employ rigorous inclusion criteria to minimize confounding variables, utilize a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design, and focus on precise quantifiable endpoints. 

**Objectives**:
- Primary: To assess the change in body weight from baseline to 24 weeks of intervention between the metformin and placebo groups.
- Secondary: To evaluate changes in fasting glucose levels, insulin sensitivity, and lipid profiles.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Adults aged 18-65 years.
- Body Mass Index (BMI) ≥ 30 kg/m² and ≤ 40 kg/m².
- Fasting glucose levels between 100 mg/dL and 125 mg/dL indicating pre-diabetes.
- Willingness to adhere to protocol requirements and sign an informed consent form.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- History of diabetes mellitus (Type 1 or Type 2).
- Current use of medications affecting glucose metabolism or appetite, including insulin.
- Significant renal impairment (Creatinine > 1.5 mg/dL).
- Presence of gastrointestinal disorders affecting drug absorption.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding individuals.

**Design**: This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial will be conducted across multiple centers to enhance the generalizability of the findings. Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either metformin (1000 mg/day) or placebo for a duration of 24 weeks. 

**Methodology**:
After a 4-week run-in period during which participants will receive lifestyle intervention guidance, randomization will occur. The study will assess body weight changes as the primary outcome, along with secondary measures including HgbA1c, fasting glucose, HOMA-IR (Homeostasis Model Assessment for Insulin Resistance), and lipid profiles (total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, and triglycerides) at baseline, 12 weeks, and 24 weeks. 

**Statistical Analysis**: Sample size was calculated assuming a minimum clinically significant weight loss of 5 kg with a power of 80% and a significance level of 0.05, resulting in an estimated enrollment of 150 participants to account for a 15% dropout rate. Changes from baseline in continuous variables will be assessed using mixed-effects models, with treatment effects evaluated through analysis of covariance (ANCOVA). 

**Results**: The primary outcome measure, change in body weight, will be assessed via two-sample t-tests, while secondary endpoints will undergo analysis of variance for repeated measures (ANOVA) to determine group differences over time.

**Safety Monitoring**: Continuous monitoring for any adverse effects related to metformin therapy, particularly gastrointestinal symptoms, will be implemented throughout the trial, in accordance with strict protocol adherence to ensure participant safety.

**Conclusion**: This clinical trial aspires to determine the efficacy and safety of metformin as a therapeutic agent for weight loss and metabolic improvement in individuals with pre-diabetes, utilizing strong methodological frameworks to guarantee high-quality results.

**Significance of the Study**: Establishing the role of metformin in promoting weight loss and improving metabolic health in a preventative setting may contribute to the broader management strategies of obesity and pre-diabetes, addressing an urgent need for effective interventions in this population.
