**Failed Clinical Trial of Enfuvirtide: Study of Enfuvirtide in HIV-1 Infected Patients with Late Stage Disease**

**Title**: A Randomized, Open-label Trial Assessing the Efficacy of Enfuvirtide Addition to Conventional Antiretroviral Therapy in Adults with Late Stage HIV-1 Infection.

**Phase**: IIb

**Objective**: The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the immunological and virological impact of adding enfuvirtide to a standard antiretroviral therapy (ART) regimen in HIV-1 infected patients presenting with advanced disease (CD4 count <150 cells/mm³).

**Study Design**: This multicenter, randomized, open-label study was designed to assess the efficacy of enfuvirtide administered in conjunction with a conventional ART regimen versus the ART regimen alone in enhancing CD4 cell recovery and achieving sustained viral suppression.

**Eligibility Criteria**:
- **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Adults aged ≥ 18 years, diagnosed with HIV-1 infection.
  - CD4 T-cell count <150 cells/mm³.
  - HIV-1 viral load ≥ 10,000 copies/mL.
  - Prior history of opportunistic infections or AIDS-defining conditions.
  - Patients naïve to enfuvirtide.
  
- **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Patients currently on enfuvirtide or with a documented history of enfuvirtide use.
  - Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
  - Presence of severe psychological conditions that may impede adherence to study protocols.
  - Coinfection with other viruses (e.g., HIV-2, Hepatitis B/C) or untreated opportunistic infections.

**Randomization and Treatment**:
Participants meeting the eligibility criteria were randomized in a 1:1 ratio:
- Control Group (CG): Standard ART regimen consisting of tenofovir and emtricitabine with either efavirenz or lopinavir/ritonavir.
- Experimental Group (EG): Same ART regimen plus enfuvirtide administered at a dose of 90 mg subcutaneously twice daily for 24 weeks.

**Endpoints**:
- The primary endpoint was defined as the achievement of a CD4 cell count >200 cells/mm³ at week 24.
- Secondary endpoints included the proportion of participants reaching undetectable HIV-1 RNA (<50 copies/mL) and monitoring adverse effects, treatment adherence, and the emergence of resistance mutations.

**Results**:
The trial enrolled 200 participants, with 100 individuals in each arm. The expected immunological benefit of achieving a CD4 count of >200 cells/mm³ after 24 weeks of treatment was not met in the experimental group, with only 30% of participants in the enfuvirtide arm attaining this immunological benchmark compared to 40% in the control group, indicating no added benefit from the addition of enfuvirtide. Furthermore, virological suppression rates also fell significantly short, with only 25% achieving HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL in the EG versus 35% in the CG.

**Discussion**: The underperformance of enfuvirtide can be attributed to several pivotal factors that align with reasons encountered in previous studies. Most notably, the severely compromised immune systems of patients recruited into the study hindered the expected outcomes, as indicated by poor immune recovery observed in participants with very low CD4 counts. Additionally, the pharmacokinetic interactions, coupled with the parenteral route of enfuvirtide administration, likely affected patient adherence, leading to suboptimal therapeutic exposure and subsequent failure to achieve significant therapeutic endpoints. 

Moreover, the potential emergence of resistance mutations presents a concern, as a considerable number of participants experienced virological failure, although they were previously untreated. This raises questions about the robustness of enfuvirtide as a therapeutic strategy in late-stage HIV-1 patients.

**Conclusion**: This study reinforces findings from previous trials that suggest the ineffectiveness of enfuvirtide in enhancing outcomes in patients with severe immunosuppression and points to the necessity for alternative treatment strategies beyond traditional intensive ART approaches in this patient population. The results call for a re-evaluation of enfuvirtide's role in late-stage HIV-1 treatment paradigms given the adverse therapeutic implications observed.
