**Title:** Assessment of Hydroxyurea in Reducing Vaso-Occlusive Events in Adults with Sickle Cell Disease (ADULT-SCD-HU)

**Objective:** To evaluate the efficacy and safety of hydroxyurea in reducing the frequency of vaso-occlusive crises (VOCs) and improving the overall quality of life in adult patients with sickle cell disease (SCD).

**Study Design:** A multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial aimed at assessing the short-term and long-term effects of hydroxyurea compared to placebo. This study leverages a robust statistical framework to enhance reliability and generalizability of results.

**Background:** Previous studies have highlighted the effectiveness of hydroxyurea in pediatric populations with SCD, such as the REACH trial, which employed rigorous protocols to evaluate dosing and safety. This trial intends to extend those findings to an adult population and will focus on individualized patient care through clinical and pharmacogenomic assessments, addressing the variability of treatment response seen in different genetic backgrounds. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults aged 18-65 years with confirmed diagnosis of SCD (HbSS or HbSC).
2. History of at least three vaso-occlusive crises in the past year.
3. No current use of hydroxyurea or other disease-modifying therapy for SCD within the past six months.
4. Capable of providing written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Presence of significant comorbid conditions (e.g., malignancy, kidney or liver failure).
2. Female patients who are pregnant or nursing.
3. Occurrence of acute infections or other unstable medical conditions.
4. Known hypersensitivity to hydroxyurea.

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
1. Frequency of vaso-occlusive crises during the 12-month intervention period.
2. Changes in hemoglobin levels and fetal hemoglobin (HbF) percentages from baseline.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
1. Assessment of quality of life using validated questionnaires (e.g., SF-36) at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months.
2. Evaluation of safety, including monitoring for myelosuppression and infection rates.

**Methods:**
Participants will be randomly assigned to receive hydroxyurea or placebo. Dose escalation protocols will be implemented to ensure safety monitoring and dose optimization based on hematologic parameters. Comprehensive data will be collected to assess the pharmacogenomic factors influencing treatment response, enhancing personalized medicine approaches.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Sample size calculations will be performed to ensure adequate power to detect significant differences between the hydroxyurea and placebo groups. Intention-to-treat analysis will be employed to maintain the integrity of statistical findings.

**Expected Outcomes:**
It is hypothesized that hydroxyurea will significantly reduce the frequency of vaso-occlusive crises in adults with SCD, paralleling the successes established in pediatric studies. The integration of clinical monitoring and risk stratification, as well as an emphasis on adherence and patient engagement, are expected to facilitate improved health outcomes.

**Conclusion:**
This clinical trial aims to affirm hydroxyurea’s utility in adult sickle cell populations, establishing a foundation for wider implementation and optimizing treatment strategies in diverse healthcare settings. By ensuring stringent protocols and focusing on practical intervention aspects, this study seeks to build confidence in the efficacy of hydroxyurea, thereby enhancing real-world applicability and accessibility of the treatment for adult patients with SCD.
