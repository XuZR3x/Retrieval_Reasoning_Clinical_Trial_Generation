**Title**: Phase II Study of Cabozantinib S-Malate in Patients With Advanced Cholangiocarcinoma

**Objective**: This randomized Phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of cabozantinib-s-malate compared to best supportive care (BSC) in patients with advanced cholangiocarcinoma who have progressed following first-line chemotherapy. The study will focus on progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety of the intervention.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES**:  
I. To determine the median progression-free survival (PFS) in patients treated with cabozantinib-s-malate compared to best supportive care.  
II. To evaluate the overall survival (OS) rates at 6 and 12 months between the two treatment groups.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES**:  
I. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) based on RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
II. To analyze the safety and tolerability profile of cabozantinib-s-malate.  
III. To explore associations between MET pathway alterations and clinical outcomes.

**OUTLINE**:  
Patients will be randomized to one of two treatment arms:  

**ARM I**: Patients receive cabozantinib-s-malate orally once daily for 28 days, followed by a 14-day treatment-free period, repeating every 42 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.  

**ARM II**: Patients will receive best supportive care without cabozantinib-s-malate.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
- Histologically confirmed advanced cholangiocarcinoma (intrahepatic or extrahepatic) that is unresectable or metastatic.  
- Disease progression following at least one line of systemic chemotherapy.  
- Measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.  
- Adequate organ and marrow function.  
- Patients must provide written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
- Prior treatment with cabozantinib or other MET inhibitors.  
- Clinically significant cardiovascular disease, including uncontrolled hypertension.  
- Significant active bleeding or gastrointestinal perforation within 12 weeks prior to enrollment.  
- History of other malignancies except for non-invasive cancers.  
- Active infection requiring systemic therapy.

**Expected Outcomes**:  
The primary endpoint of this study is to determine if cabozantinib-s-malate can prolong PFS compared to best supportive care in patients with refractory cholangiocarcinoma. 

**Statistical Considerations**:  
The primary analysis will utilize Kaplan-Meier methods to estimate PFS, with hazard ratios calculated via Cox regression models. Comparisons of ORR will be performed using Fisher's exact test.

**Anticipated Challenges and Rationale for Failure**:  
The previously established reasons suggest a high likelihood of failure for this trial, notably:

1. **Tumor Microenvironment Adaptation**: The tumor microenvironment might exhibit adaptability that leads to drug resistance mechanisms via upregulation of alternative growth pathways, diminishing cabozantinib’s effectiveness.  
2. **Intratumoral Heterogeneity**: The complexity of cholangiocarcinomas, including varied MET expression levels among tumor cells, may result in differential responsiveness to cabozantinib-targeted therapy.  
3. **Inadequate Patient Selection Criteria**: Diverse tumor biology and varying previous treatment histories among participants may result in an overall population skewed towards poor prognosis, complicating interpretation of results.  
4. **Pharmacokinetic Variability**: Inconsistent drug metabolism could be observed, particularly in patients with liver impairment due to underlying disease, potentially leading to suboptimal dosing profiles.  
5. **Insufficient Mechanistic Understanding**: Inadequate knowledge of the specific signaling pathways driving cholangiocarcinoma progression limits the ability to predict responses to cabozantinib, impacting the design and possible success of the trial.

**Follow-Up**:  
Following completion of study treatment, patients will be monitored for safety and disease status every 3 months for 2 years.

**Conclusion**:  
Given the complexity of cholangiocarcinoma and the multifaceted mechanisms of therapeutic resistance, this trial will illuminate potential reasons for the observed failures, shaping future studies and treatment approaches for advanced biliary tract cancers.
