**Study Title:** A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of Risperidone in Patients with Treatment-Resistant Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

**Objective:** The primary objective of this clinical trial is to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of risperidone in adults diagnosed with Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) who demonstrate inadequate response to existing pharmacotherapy. 

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial spanning 12 months, wherein 320 adults aged 18-65 diagnosed with treatment-resistant OCD will be enrolled. Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either risperidone (starting dose of 0.5 mg/day) or placebo, in addition to their current, stable regimen of pharmacotherapy. The primary endpoint will focus on the reduction of OCD symptoms as measured by the Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) at the conclusion of the 12-week treatment phase.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 to 65 years
- Diagnosed with OCD, as validated by DSM-5 criteria
- Current stable pharmacotherapy with SSRIs or Clomipramine for a minimum of 8 weeks prior to participation
- Global Assessment of Functioning (GAF) score < 60
- Written informed consent from participants

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Comorbid psychiatric disorders necessitating antipsychotic treatment 
- Current substance use disorder 
- Significant cardiovascular, hepatic, or renal impairment
- History of hypersensitivity to risperidone

**Methods:** Following a two-week screening period, eligible participants will be randomized to receive flexible-dose risperidone (0.5 mg to 3 mg) or matching placebo. Dosing adjustments will occur every two weeks based on clinical response and tolerability. Safety will be monitored via routine assessments of adverse events, metabolic indices, and vital signs. 

**Primary Outcome Measures:** The primary outcome measure will be the change in total Y-BOCS score from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints will include the assessment of depressive symptoms using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D) and evaluation of quality of life through the WHO Quality of Life-BREF instrument.

**Statistical Analysis:** An intent-to-treat approach will be employed for data analysis, with statistical significance defined as p < 0.05. Change scores will be analyzed using mixed-model repeated measures (MMRM) and the primary hypothesis will test the superiority of risperidone over placebo.

**Safety Assessments:** A comprehensive safety monitoring protocol will be deployed throughout the study, with regular assessments of adverse events, cardiovascular parameters, weight, fasting glucose, and lipid profiles to ensure the intervention's safety profile is adequately characterized.

**Expected Outcomes:** Based on prior successful trials of risperidone for diverse psychological symptomatology, we anticipate that participants receiving risperidone will exhibit statistically significant reductions in OCD symptoms compared with placebo. This expected outcome is predicated on optimal study design and rigorous safety measures adopted, such as careful monitoring of metabolic side effects, which contribute significantly to patient compliance and overall trial success.

**Conclusion:** This study aims to extend the current understanding of risperidone's efficacy in treating complex OCD symptoms by targeting unique symptom profiles and employing a rigorous trial design, while ensuring thorough safety evaluations are executed throughout the study trajectory. If successful, this trial could significantly enhance the therapeutic armamentarium available for managing treatment-resistant OCD.
