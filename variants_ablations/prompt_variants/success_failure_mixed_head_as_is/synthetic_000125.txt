<Study>
    <Title>Assessment of a Rivaroxaban-Based Antithrombotic Regimen Compared to Standard Antiplatelet Therapy in Patients Post-Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement: A Multicenter, Randomized, Open-Label Trial</Title>
    <Investigator>Dr. [REDACTED]</Investigator>
    <Sponsorship>Pharmaceutical Research Partnership</Sponsorship>
    
    <Objective>This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a rivaroxaban-based anticoagulation strategy versus standard antiplatelet therapy in reducing the composite of death or thromboembolic events (DTE) in a cohort of patients undergoing Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR), while also assessing the incidence of primary bleeding events (PBE).</Objective>
    
    <Design>This was a global, multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial involving a minimum of [REDACTED] centers, designed to dichotomize patients following TAVR into rivaroxaban and control arms without double-blinding. Randomization occurred at the post-procedure period, with participants assigned to receive either rivaroxaban or standard antiplatelet therapy for a minimum follow-up of 12 months.</Design>
    
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>- Adults aged ≥ 65 years undergoing TAVR procedures, assessed by a cardiovascular surgeon.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>- Documented severe aortic stenosis with a determined indication for TAVR.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>- No contraindications to anticoagulation or antiplatelet therapy as per the study Exclusion Criteria below.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>- History of intracranial hemorrhage or other conditions that may increase the risk of bleeding.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>- Atrial fibrillation or other indications requiring concurrent anticoagulant therapy.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>- Major surgery or planned interventions requiring the discontinuation of anticoagulation or antiplatelet therapy.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>- Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) or moderate to severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B or C).</Criterion>
        <Criterion>- Patients with significant comorbidities that contributed to frailty or advanced multi-organ dysfunction, which would bias the assessment of clinical outcomes.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    
    <Endpoints>
        <PrimaryEndpoints>
            <Endpoint>- Composite of death and first thromboembolic event (DTE) at 12 months post-TAVR.</Endpoint>
            <Endpoint>- Incidence of primary bleeding events (PBE), defined as major and clinically relevant non-major bleeding events, assessed as time-to-event outcomes.</Endpoint>
        </PrimaryEndpoints>
        <SecondaryEndpoints>
            <Endpoint>- Secondary safety endpoints focusing on adverse events related to rivaroxaban in the post-TAVR population.</Endpoint>
            <Endpoint>- Quality of life assessments via EQ-5D at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months post-randomization.</Endpoint>
        </SecondaryEndpoints>
    </Endpoints>
    
    <Results>The trial was prematurely terminated after a pre-defined interim analysis disclosed a significant imbalance in thromboembolic risk inherent to the study population. Notably, the predominantly older cohort exhibited complex comorbidities, resulting in an escalation of both thromboembolic complications and adverse bleeding events that exceeded threshold safety parameters. Furthermore, the open-label nature of the trial introduced substantial reporting biases which undermined the reliability of outcomes. The concomitant use of antiplatelet therapies in a large subset of participants diluted the efficacy conclusions regarding rivaroxaban, making it difficult to ascertain its comparative benefit over standard antiplatelet management.</Results>
    
    <Conclusion>This study exemplifies the complex interplay of patient comorbidities and pharmacological interactions in anticoagulation strategies following TAVR. The failure to stratify for baseline risk factors and optimally assess renal and hepatic function across the cohort critically hampered the endorsement of rivaroxaban as an effective post-TAVR intervention, echoing issues seen in prior clinical trials where outcome biases and patient characteristics impacted drug efficacy and safety comprehensively.</Conclusion>
</Study>
