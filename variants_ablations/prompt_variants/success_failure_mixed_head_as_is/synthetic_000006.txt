<clinical_trial>
    <title>A Phase II Evaluation of Laboratory Biomarker Analysis in Patients with Advanced Colorectal Cancer</title>
    <intervention>Laboratory Biomarker Analysis</intervention>
    <study_type>Phase II</study_type>
    <reason_for_failure>
        <reason>
            Insufficient Biomarker Sensitivity and Specificity: The evaluation of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) as a biomarker for treatment response in advanced colorectal cancer did not achieve sufficient sensitivity and specificity as per the predefined metrics for clinical outcome correlation.
        </reason>
        <reason>
            Patient Population Heterogeneity: The significant variability in genetic profiles, previous treatment regimens, and corresponding tumor characteristics led to inconsistent biomarker performance, complicating the analysis of efficacy and clinical applicability across the enrolled cohort.
        </reason>
        <reason>
            Methodological Limitations: The quantitative PCR assays utilized for ctDNA analysis exhibited inter-assay variability and insufficient validation against clinical endpoints, rendering a substantial portion of the data unreliable for the intended analysis.
        </reason>
        <reason>
            Insufficient Follow-Up Duration: The follow-up period was inadequate to ascertain the long-term benefits of the identified biomarkers, which may have resulted in an underappreciation of their potential implications on overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS).
        </reason>
        <reason>
            Resistance Mechanisms within Tumor Biology: Underlying mechanisms of resistance inherent in colorectal cancer, including alterations in key signaling pathways and the tumor microenvironment, obscured the interpretability of biomarker analyses, thereby limiting their association with patient outcomes.
        </reason>
    </reason_for_failure>
    <primary_objectives>
        <objective>
            To estimate the proportion of patients who exhibit a measurable decrease in tumor burden as assessed by RECIST criteria within 12 weeks of initiating therapy.
        </objective>
        <objective>
            To determine the correlation between ctDNA levels and treatment response, as evidenced by imaging studies and biomarker assays throughout the patient follow-up period.
        </objective>
    </primary_objectives>
    <secondary_objectives>
        <objective>
            To evaluate the toxicities associated with the combination of standard chemotherapy and the incorporation of biomarker-guided therapy.
        </objective>
        <objective>
            To assess the effects of baseline biomarker expression on treatment outcomes, including overall survival and progression-free survival as evaluated at six-month intervals.
        </objective>
        <objective>
            To explore the association between ctDNA profiles and established molecular markers of colorectal cancer.
        </objective>
    </secondary_objectives>
    <outline>
        Patients receive standard chemotherapy (FOLFOX regimen) combined with biomarker-guided treatment based on pre-treatment ctDNA analysis. Treatment continues for up to 6 cycles or until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Following treatment cessation, patients are monitored every 2 months for the duration of 24 months, with biomarker analyses scheduled at baseline, mid-treatment, and completion.
    </outline>
    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Patients aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed advanced colorectal cancer.</criterion>
        <criterion>Patients with measurable disease according to RECIST version 1.1 criteria.</criterion>
        <criterion>Prior therapy must include at least one standard regimen for colorectal cancer.</criterion>
        <criterion>Available tumor tissue for molecular profile analysis.</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>History of other malignancies, except adequately treated basal cell or squamous cell skin cancer.</criterion>
        <criterion>Uncontrolled comorbidities that could confound the clinical outcomes of treatment.</criterion>
        <criterion>Known hypersensitivity to components of the chemotherapy regimen.</criterion>
        <criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding women.</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <status>Failed</status>
    <conclusion>The phase II evaluation of laboratory biomarker analysis in patients with advanced colorectal cancer did not demonstrate efficacy due to insufficient biomarker performance and methodological limitations, warranting a reevaluation of the study design and biomarker selection in future studies.</conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
