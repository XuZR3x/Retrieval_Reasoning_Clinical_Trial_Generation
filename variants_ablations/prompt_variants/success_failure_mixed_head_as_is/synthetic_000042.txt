**A Phase I/II Open-Label Multicenter Study Evaluating LEE011 in Combination with Atezolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Background:** LEE011, a selective inhibitor of cyclin-dependent kinase 4 and 6 (CDK4/6), aims to halt the proliferation of cancerous cells by enforcing G1 phase arrest in the cell cycle. Prior studies have indicated varying responses to CDK4/6 inhibition in conjunction with immune checkpoint inhibitors, primarily due to the heterogeneity of tumor microenvironments. This trial investigates the potential synergistic effects of combining LEE011 with atezolizumab (an anti-PD-L1 monoclonal antibody) in patients with advanced NSCLC.

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to ascertain the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) and safety profile of LEE011 when administered concurrently with atezolizumab. Secondary objectives encompass the efficacy of the combination therapy evaluated by objective response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) rates through standardized response criteria.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients aged ≥ 18 years with histologically confirmed metastatic or unresectable NSCLC, not amenable to standard treatment approaches.
2. Patients must exhibit measurable disease defined per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
3. An ECOG performance status of 0-1, with adequate organ and bone marrow function corroborated by laboratory assessments: 
   - Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L
   - Platelets ≥ 100 x 10^9/L
   - Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL
4. Prior therapy inclusive of platinum-based chemotherapy does not exceed two lines of treatment.
5. Patients should possess available tumor tissue for biomarker evaluation (i.e., PD-L1 expression).
6. Signed informed consent prior to any protocol-specific procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients with primary CNS tumors or known brain metastases, unless adequately treated and stable for ≥ 3 months.
2. Evidence of significant cardiopulmonary comorbidities that might predispose to cardiac events during the study (e.g., history of myocardial infarction ≤ 6 months).
3. Prior use of CDK4/6 inhibitors or therapeutic agents directed at checkpoint pathways, such as PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, within 6 months preceding enrollment.
4. Concurrent severe and/or uncontrolled medical conditions that may affect compliance or compromise patient safety.
5. Patients with active infections requiring systemic treatment.

**Methodology:** This open-label study will leverage a dose-escalation scheme to identify the optimal dosing regimen of LEE011 in parallel with the standard dosing of atezolizumab, employed every three weeks. Each cohort's safety and tolerability will be evaluated through continuous monitoring of adverse events and dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) assessed via CTCAE Version 5.0.

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
- MTD of LEE011 in combination with atezolizumab across patient cohorts, assessed by the frequency of DLTs.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
- ORR, PFS, and OS quantified utilizing appropriate statistical analysis, based on RECIST criteria.

**Rationale:** 
1. The specified patient population represents a well-defined cohort specifically vulnerable to novel interventions, increasing the probability of observing meaningful pharmacodynamic responses.
2. A dose-escalation strategy permits not only the identification of the MTD but also monitors DLTs and safety parameters underpinning this treatment combination.
3. Combining LEE011 with atezolizumab provides a solid mechanistic justification, addressing inherent tumor resistance mechanisms while potentially improving immunogenicity within the tumor milieu due to resultant enhanced T-cell activation.
4. Strong preclinical backing, highlighting the positive interplay observed between CDK4/6 inhibition and immune modulation, lends credence to pursuing this clinical pathway.
5. The systematic assessment and standardized criteria for monitoring treatment outcomes will support robust conclusions regarding the therapeutic potential of this novel combination.

This trial's intent rests on potentially transforming treatment paradigms for NSCLC through innovative combinatorial strategies, thus advancing therapeutic options for patients with limited remaining viable treatment pathways.
