**Title**: Efficacy of Imatinib in Patients with Сhronic Hypereosinophilia: A Phase II Study

**Abstract**:  
The objective of the present study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of imatinib in patients diagnosed with chronic hypereosinophilia (CHE) characterized by persistent eosinophil counts exceeding 1,500 cells/mm3 without identifiable secondary causes. This trial focuses on determining the clinical and hematological responses contingent upon imatinib treatment for a specified duration, with an emphasis on patient selection criteria that align closely with imatinib's pharmacodynamic properties.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Adult patients aged ≥ 18 years with a diagnosis of chronic hypereosinophilia defined by persistent eosinophil counts exceeding 1,500 cells/mm3.
- Documented evidence of organ involvement secondary to eosinophilia, including but not limited to cardiovascular, pulmonary, and skin complications.
- Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study requirements.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2.
- No prior treatment with imatinib or any targeted therapy.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Patients with secondary causes for eosinophilia (e.g., parasitic infections, autoimmune disorders).
- Presence of any other malignancy, with the exception of completely resected skin carcinoma.
- Severe hepatic or renal insufficiency as evidenced by laboratory values: bilirubin > 1.5 x ULN or creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL.
- Known hypersensitivity to imatinib or its components.
- Concurrent participation in another clinical trial.

**Primary Outcomes**:
- The primary endpoint is the rate of complete hematological response (CHR), defined as eosinophil counts ≤ 0.5 x 10^9/L after 24 weeks of treatment with imatinib.

**Secondary Outcomes**:
- Rate of symptomatic improvement measured via a validated Eosinophilia Symptom Questionnaire (ESQ).
- Quality of Life assessment using the EQ-5D-5L scale pre-and post-treatment.
- Pharmacokinetic analysis to assess drug levels after the first week of administration.

**Statistical Analysis**:
- The sample size was calculated to achieve 80% power to detect an improvement in response rates with a one-sided significance level of 0.05. Rates of CHR will be analyzed using the Clopper-Pearson method to determine exact confidence intervals. Secondary endpoints will be analyzed with appropriate parametric or non-parametric tests based on distribution characteristics.

**Monitoring and Reporting**:
- Regular monitoring of laboratory parameters will be conducted bi-weekly to evaluate eosinophil counts and liver enzymes post-initiation of treatment. An independent safety monitoring board will oversee the trial to ensure patient safety, with provisions for dose adjustment in cases of adverse events, aligned with prior successful studies in similar indications where tolerability was determined essential for therapeutic effectiveness.

**Conclusion**:
This trial anticipates that the select patient population demonstrating distinct chronic hypereosinophilia and suitable inclusion parameters will benefit from imatinib, consistent with its mechanism of action targeting the PDGF receptor pathways that are implicated in eosinophil proliferation and function. The incorporation of robust patient monitoring protocols alongside stringent eligibility criteria aims to elucidate the therapeutic potential of imatinib in a previously understudied indication, potentially enhancing the management landscape for chronic hypereosinophilia patients.
