**Failed Clinical Trial of Aprepitant: Efficacy of Aprepitant in Patients with Inadequate Response to First-line Antiemetics in Chemotherapy**

**RATIONALE:** While aprepitant is recognized for its efficacy in preventing chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV), patient variability in response may limit its effectiveness when administered as a subsequent choice after inadequate response to first-line antiemetic therapies. 

**PURPOSE:** This randomized clinical trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy of aprepitant in patients with recurrent nausea and vomiting despite treatment with standard antiemetic regimens, predominantly consisting of ondansetron and dexamethasone.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** To assess the difference in control of nausea and vomiting as defined by the number of complete response days (no vomiting or requirement of rescue antiemetics) in patients treated with aprepitant compared to a placebo over the course of their chemotherapy cycle.
- **Secondary:** To investigate the impact of aprepitant on overall patient quality of life as evaluated by the Functional Living Index-Emesis (FLIE), alongside any changes in appetite and nutritional intake.

**OUTLINE:** This is a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial. Patients (n=120) were stratified based on their prior response to antiemetic treatment. 

- **Arm I:** Patients receive aprepitant 125 mg orally on day 1 and 80 mg orally on days 2 and 3 of their chemotherapy cycle, in addition to their standard antiemetic regimen. 
- **Arm II:** Patients receive a placebo for the same dosing schedule alongside the same standard antiemetics as in Arm I. 

The study commenced with the administration of treatment concomitant with the initiation of the chemotherapy regimen and continued until 120 hours post-chemotherapy.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 120 patients (60 per treatment arm) were planned for this study.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Adults aged 18 years or older.
- Patients scheduled to receive a chemotherapy regimen known to induce nausea and vomiting.
- Documented history of inadequate control of CINV based on previous cycles of chemotherapy despite standard prophylactic antiemetic treatment.
- ECOG performance status ≤ 2.
- Signed informed consent.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Known hypersensitivity to aprepitant, ondansetron, or dexamethasone.
- Recent administration (within 30 days) of any experimental medication.
- Pregnancy or lactation status.
- Significant hepatic impairment or renal insufficiency (creatinine clearance < 60 mL/min).
- Concurrent intake of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers.

**PRIMARY OUTCOME MEASUREMENT:** To measure the percentage of patients experiencing complete response (no vomiting and no use of rescue medication) during the 120-hour observation period post-administration of the first cycle of chemotherapy.

**RESULTS:** The trial experienced multiple challenges, resulting in failure to meet its primary outcome:
1. **Inherent Patient Variability:** Patients included exhibited significant heterogeneity in genetic polymorphisms affecting drug metabolism associated with aprepitant, leading to inconsistent therapeutic responses.
   
2. **Insufficient Sample Size:** Despite the accrual goal of 120 patients, low enrollment numbers due to reluctance from patients with previous negative experiences with antiemetic regimens limited the robustness of the statistical analysis.
   
3. **Pharmacokinetic Interference:** Pharmacokinetic analyses indicated that previously administered antiemetics influenced the metabolism of aprepitant, thereby resulting in inadequate plasma concentrations and diminished efficacy.

4. **Symptom-based Inclusion Criteria:** The strict pre-defined exclusion of patients exhibiting atypical chemotherapy-related nausea and vomiting syndromes further reduced the generalizability of trial outcomes.

5. **Adverse Effects on Appetite and Nutrition:** Patient-reported outcomes indicated a significant prevalence of appetite suppression following aprepitant therapy, thus adversely impacting overall quality of life assessments and creating confounding variables in interpreting treatment response.

**CONCLUSION:** The trial was unable to demonstrate a significant benefit of aprepitant in controlling CINV in a population with previous treatment failures. These findings underscore the importance of considering patient variability and pharmacokinetic interactions when designing intervention strategies using aprepitant in complex chemotherapy regimens. Future studies should focus on more comprehensive inclusion criteria and stratified enrollment based on genetic polymorphism profiles to enhance treatment precision.
