**Failed Clinical Trial of Insulin Glargine: Efficacy and Safety of Insulin Glargine in Mixed Therapy for Type 2 Diabetes Management (INSIGHT Study)**

A Multicenter, Randomized, Open-label Trial to Evaluate the Safety, Efficacy, and Patient-Centered Outcomes with Insulin Glargine in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Not Controlled on Dual Oral Antidiabetic Agents

**Primary Objective:**  
To compare the efficacy of insulin glargine (Lantus) versus continued dual oral antidiabetic therapy regarding glycemic control in a population of Type 2 diabetes mellitus patients experiencing inadequate control.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
1. To evaluate the incidence of hypoglycemic events (both symptomatic and severe).
2. To assess changes in body weight and quality of life metrics associated with treatment.
3. To examine the adherence rates to therapy and barriers in treatment transition.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients with established Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus inadequately controlled on a regimen of two oral antidiabetic agents (OADs) for a minimum of three months.  
- HbA1c levels between 7.5% and 10%.  
- BMI between 22 kg/m² and 36 kg/m².  
- Patient age 30 years or older with a clinical diagnosis of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus for at least 12 months.  
- Ability and willingness to provide informed consent, perform self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG), and participate in quality of life assessments.

**Exclusion Criteria:** 
- Patients with Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus or with a clear diagnosis of autoimmune diabetes.  
- History of significant cardiovascular events within the past year.  
- Presence of clinically significant renal impairment (serum creatinine > 1.5 mg/dl for males and > 1.4 mg/dl for females) or active liver disease (serum alanine aminotransferase levels exceeding three times the upper limit of normal).  
- Current participation in other clinical trials or use of investigational agents within the preceding three months before enrollment.  
- Pregnancy or nursing mothers, and women of childbearing potential not using effective contraceptive measures.  
- Known hypersensitivity to insulin glargine or its excipients.

**Outcome Measures:**  
1. **Efficacy**: Mean change in HbA1c from baseline to 24 weeks in both groups.  
2. **Safety**: Incidence of hypoglycemic events (minor and severe) during the trial period.
3. **Patient Adherence**: Rates of adherence to the prescribed regimen, assessed through pill counts and patient self-reports.  
4. **Quality of Life**: Change in scores on standardized quality of life assessment tools.

**Results and Conclusions:**  
Despite intentions to assess the therapeutic benefits of adding insulin glargine to a background of dual oral therapy, this trial ultimately faced limitations that led to its failure:

1. **Patient Population Differences**: The inclusion of patients with advanced stages of Type 2 diabetes already burdened by significant complications reduced the overall efficacy of insulin glargine. Many individuals demonstrated diminished beta-cell function and higher expectations of medication benefits.

2. **Complexity of Treatment Paradigms**: Transitioning patients from dual therapy to a basal insulin regimen proved difficult, resulting in inconsistent medication adherence and dissatisfaction with treatment. The complicated conversion protocols contributed to adverse reactions and challenges in achieving optimized dosing.

3. **Risks of Hypoglycemia**: A notable concern arose regarding the heightened risk of hypoglycemia, particularly as patients transitioned from combination OAD therapies. Instances of severe hypoglycemic events negatively impacted adherence and treatment satisfaction, confounding efficacy assessments.

4. **Quality of Life Assessments**: Initial reports from participating patients highlighted a misalignment with treatment expectations, significantly affecting perceived quality of life indicators. Prioritization by participants of weight-related and hypoglycemic event risks over glycemic control measures skewed results.

5. **Inconsistent Protocol Adherence**: Variations among healthcare professionals in implementing the insulin titration protocol further aggravated the complications faced in achieving pragmatic, real-world applicability of the treatment regimen.

In conclusion, the INSIGHT Study illustrates the multifaceted challenges posed in transitioning to insulin glargine in a complex treatment landscape characterized by advanced disease stages and inadequate management strategies, suggesting a re-evaluation of patient selection criteria and therapeutic approaches in future trials.
