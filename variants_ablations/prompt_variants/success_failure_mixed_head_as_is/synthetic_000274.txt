**A Phase II Trial of Aldesleukin Combined with Ziv-Aflibercept and Nivolumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** Aldesleukin stimulates the immune response, potentially enhancing anti-tumor activity, while ziv-aflibercept inhibits angiogenesis. Nivolumab, an immune checkpoint inhibitor, has demonstrated effectiveness in NSCLC. The combination therapy may provide synergistic effects resulting in improved clinical outcomes through enhanced immune modulation and tumor microenvironment alteration.

**PURPOSE:** Phase II trial to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of the combination of aldesleukin, ziv-aflibercept, and nivolumab in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) that is not amenable to surgical resection.

**OBJECTIVES:**
I. Primary Objective: Assess the progression-free survival (PFS) in patients receiving the combination therapy of aldesleukin, ziv-aflibercept, and nivolumab compared to historical controls receiving nivolumab alone.
II. Secondary Objectives: 
   1. Evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) as defined by RECIST criteria, comparing to historical control data for nivolumab alone.
   2. Assess the safety profile and tolerability of the combination therapy, focusing on adverse events and discontinuation rates.
   3. Correlate baseline biomarkers and immune activation profiles with treatment responses.

**OUTLINE:** This is a randomized, open-label, multicenter Phase II study involving two treatment arms.

**ARM I:** Patients receive aldesleukin intravenously (IV) every 8 hours for 5 days and nivolumab IV on day 1 of each 28-day cycle, combined with ziv-aflibercept IV over at least 1 hour on days 1 and 15 of the first three treatment cycles.

**ARM II:** Patients receive nivolumab IV on day 1 of each cycle for up to 12 cycles, as the control arm.

**Projected Accrual:** A total of 120 patients will be accrued for this study, with 60 patients in each arm.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:** Histologically confirmed NSCLC, Stage IIIB or IV, not amenable to surgical resection. Patients must have at least one measurable lesion as per RECIST version 1.1 criteria, without lymphomas or active central nervous system metastases.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: 18 years and older
- Performance status: Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) 0-1
- Estimated life expectancy: At least 3 months
- Adequate organ function as defined by specific laboratory thresholds including leukocytes, hemoglobin, renal, and hepatic parameters.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- No previous systemic therapy for metastatic disease, including prior use of ziv-aflibercept or interleukin therapies.
- Limited prior radiotherapy to one site is permitted.

**RESEARCH DESIGN:**
- Patients will undergo rigorous eligibility assessments, including biomarker studies for immunological checkpoint expression and immune cell activity.
- A structured dose-escalation component will facilitate the identification of optimal dosing schedules for both aldesleukin and ziv-aflibercept administered concurrently with nivolumab.
- Longitudinal follow-up will enable comprehensive evaluation of survival metrics, tumor response rates, and assessment of adverse events.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Patients must provide written informed consent and are willing to comply with the study protocol.
2. Measurable disease per RECIST v1.1 definitions.
3. No active, uncontrolled medical conditions or concurrent malignancies except for adequately treated in situ cancers or basal cell carcinoma.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Uncontrolled hypertension or significant cardiovascular disease.
2. Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy.
3. Pregnant or lactating females.

**OUTCOMES MEASUREMENTS:**
- Progression-free survival will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method.
- Response evaluations will be performed every 8 weeks, assessing complete and partial responses as per RECIST. Subsequent analyses will stratify outcomes by treatment naïveté and biomarker profiles.

By incorporating these diverse therapeutic agents, this clinical trial aims to harness their complementary mechanisms, potentially resulting in improved outcomes for patients with advanced NSCLC and bolstering the role of immunotherapy in oncological treatment paradigms.
