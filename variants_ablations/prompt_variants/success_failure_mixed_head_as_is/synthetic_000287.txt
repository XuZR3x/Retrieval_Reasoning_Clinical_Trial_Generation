**Trial Title:** Evaluation of Raltegravir for Cognitive and Inflammatory Outcomes in HIV-infected Patients: A Randomized Controlled Clinical Trial

**Study Design:** Multi-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized clinical trial.

**Objective:** To evaluate the effects of raltegravir compared to placebo on cognitive function and inflammatory markers in HIV-infected individuals with mild cognitive impairment (MCI), recognizing the potential inadequacy of efficacy demonstrated in previous studies targeting complex co-morbidities and the need for a robust methodological design.

**Study Population:** 160 HIV-infected patients aged 40-65 years with documented MCI (defined by robust neuropsychological assessments) will be recruited from specialized HIV clinics. These individuals will have maintained viral suppression (<50 copies/mL) on stable HAART regimens for at least six months.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. HIV-1 positive males or females aged 40 to 65.
2. MCI diagnosed as per established neuropsychological protocols.
3. Documented viral load suppression (<50 copies/mL) for at least 6 months.
4. Ability to provide informed consent.
5. No previous exposure to integrase inhibitors, including raltegravir.
6. Baseline cognitive assessments indicating impairment.
7. Adequate renal function (eGFR ≥ 60 mL/min).

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Presence of other neurodegenerative conditions (e.g., Alzheimer’s disease, multiple sclerosis).
2. Recent (within 6 months) history of substance abuse or psychiatric disorders.
3. Clinical evidence of acute opportunistic infections.
4. Current use of medications that could interfere with cognitive assessments.
5. Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

**Intervention:**
Participants will be randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either:
- Raltegravir 400 mg twice daily, or 
- Placebo matching the raltegravir capsule.

Treatment duration will be 6 months, with follow-up assessments to occur at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months post-randomization. Careful monitoring of adherence using pharmacy refill records will be undertaken.

**Endpoints:**
*Primary Endpoint:*
- Change in cognitive function (global z-score derived from neuropsychological assessments) from baseline to 6 months.

*Secondary Endpoints:*
- Changes in inflammatory markers (e.g., IL-6, TNF-alpha) from baseline to 6 months as assessed via standard cytokine assays.
- Changes in measures of quality of life (WHOQOL-HIV BREF).

**Statistical Considerations:**
Data will be analyzed using an intention-to-treat approach. Sample size calculations based on expected treatment effects, with an anticipated dropout rate of 15%, will provide sufficient statistical power to detect meaningful differences between the intervention and control groups. Multivariable analyses will control for potential confounding variables in cognitive assessments.

**Rationale:**
This clinical trial addresses previous study limitations regarding inadequate sample sizes and insufficient efficacy observed in patients with MCI. Increasing the participant cohort to 160 enhances statistical power, while dual monitoring of cognitive and inflammatory markers tackles overlapping variables that may obscure the specific impacts of raltegravir. Comprehensive evaluations over the 6-month duration aim to capture potential long-term benefits of the regimen while remaining vigilant for any adverse impacts the treatment may theoretically introduce.

**Anticipated Challenges:**
The trial may encounter obstacles such as participant retention and reliable adherence to the study regimen. Additionally, the varying baseline levels of cognitive impairment among participants may present considerable variability in treatment response, necessitating robust statistical methodologies to account for these factors.

**Conclusion:**
Outcomes from this trial will significantly contribute to the understanding of raltegravir’s dual action on cognitive function and inflammation in mid-life HIV-infected patients, especially considering the previously noted failures where insufficient population focus and cognitive assessments were implicated. Ultimately, findings from this rigorous investigation will inform future therapeutic strategies and enhance the clinical management of HIV-associated cognitive impairments.
