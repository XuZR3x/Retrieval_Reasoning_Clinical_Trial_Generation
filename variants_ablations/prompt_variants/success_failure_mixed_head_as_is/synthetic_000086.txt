<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Ramipril Efficacy in Reducing Cardiovascular Events in Patients with Stage 1 Hypertension: A Double-Blind, Randomized Controlled Trial</Title>
    <Phase>Phase 3</Phase>
    <Objective>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of ramipril in reducing the incidence of major cardiovascular events in patients diagnosed with stage 1 hypertension.</Objective>
    <Design>A double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial design will be employed to assess the effectiveness of ramipril compared to placebo in a cohort of stage 1 hypertensive patients.</Design>
    
    <HealthcareSetting>Multicenter, outpatient settings across multiple hospitals</HealthcareSetting>
    
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Patients aged 40 years to 70 years with a diagnosis of stage 1 hypertension (systolic blood pressure 130-139 mmHg and diastolic blood pressure 85-89 mmHg)</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Not currently undergoing antihypertensive therapy</Criterion>
        <Criterion>At least one other cardiovascular risk factor (e.g., dyslipidemia, obesity, family history of cardiovascular disease)</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>History of myocardial infarction, stroke, or transient ischemic attack</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Chronic kidney disease stage 3 or higher</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Severe hepatic impairment or active liver disease</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Current use of medications that affect the renin-angiotensin system</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding women</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>

    <PrimaryEndpoints>
        <Endpoint>Incidence of composite cardiovascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular death) after a follow-up period of 5 years</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Mean change in systolic and diastolic blood pressure from the baseline at 1 year</Endpoint>
    </PrimaryEndpoints>
    
    <SecondaryEndpoints>
        <Endpoint>Quality of life assessment utilizing the EQ-5D scale at baseline and at 1, 3, and 5 years</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Incidence of new-onset diabetes mellitus at 3 and 5 years</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Changes in lipid profile measured annually</Endpoint>
    </SecondaryEndpoints>

    <Methodology>
        <Detail>Approximately 1,000 participants will be randomly assigned to receive either ramipril (10 mg daily) or matching placebo. The randomization process will use computer-generated random numbers to ensure the unbiased allocation of participants.</Detail>
        <Detail>The statistical analysis will be conducted using intention-to-treat principles, with Cox proportional hazards models used to calculate hazard ratios for the primary endpoint.</Detail>
    </Methodology>

    <Rationale>
        <Detail>This study is designed with a well-defined target population of stage 1 hypertensive patients, allowing for the possibility of achieving significant and measurable outcomes as demonstrated in previous successful studies. Rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria will minimize confounding variables that could compromise the effectiveness of ramipril. The primary endpoints focus on quantifiable cardiovascular outcomes, enhancing the reliability of the efficacy data. A placebo-controlled, double-blind design strengthens validity, ensuring biases are mitigated. Extended follow-up will provide insights into the long-term effects of ramipril on cardiovascular health.</Detail>
    </Rationale>
    
    <ExpectedResults>
        <Result>It is hypothesized that participants receiving ramipril will demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in the incidence of major cardiovascular events compared to the placebo group, as well as improved control of blood pressure over time.</Result>
    </ExpectedResults>
</ClinicalTrial>
