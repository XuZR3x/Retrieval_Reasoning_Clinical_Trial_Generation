**Title:** A Phase III Study of Capecitabine in Combination With Pembrolizumab Versus Capecitabine Alone in Patients With Locally Advanced or Metastatic Triple-Negative Breast Cancer

**Rationale:** Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a subtype characterized by the absence of estrogen and progesterone receptors and limited expression of HER2. This cohort demonstrates significant challenges in effective treatment, necessitating exploration for novel therapeutic strategies. The administration of capecitabine as a single agent has exhibited antitumor activity; however, improvement in response rates and survival outcomes may be achieved through the addition of immune checkpoint inhibitors. Pembrolizumab targets the programmed death-1 (PD-1) pathway, potentially enhancing the immune response against tumor cells.

**Purpose:** This multicenter, randomized, open-label Phase III trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of capecitabine in conjunction with pembrolizumab compared to capecitabine monotherapy in patients diagnosed with locally advanced or metastatic TNBC.

**Objectives:**
   
- **Primary Objective:** To determine the overall survival (OS) among patients receiving capecitabine combined with pembrolizumab compared to those receiving capecitabine alone.
  
- **Secondary Objectives:** 
    - To assess progression-free survival (PFS) between the two treatment arms.
    - To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) as per RECIST v1.1 criteria.
    - To characterize the safety and tolerability of both regimens through monitoring of adverse events (AEs).
    - To explore biomarkers predictive of response to pembrolizumab in the context of TNBC.

**Study Design:** This is a randomized, controlled, multicenter study. Patients will be randomly assigned (1:1) to one of two treatment arms:

- **Arm A:** Patients will receive capecitabine (1250 mg/m² orally twice daily for 14 days) in conjunction with pembrolizumab (200 mg intravenously every 3 weeks).
  
- **Arm B:** Patients will receive capecitabine (1250 mg/m² orally twice daily for 14 days) alone.

**Projected Accrual:** A total of 800 patients will be accrued for this study.

**Disease Characteristics:**
- Histologically confirmed locally advanced or metastatic triple-negative breast cancer.
- Patients must have measurable disease defined as at least one lesion ≥10 mm by CT or MRI.
- No prior chemotherapy or targeted therapy for metastatic disease is permitted.

**Patient Characteristics:**
- Age: 18 years or older.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function: 
  - Hematopoietic: WBC ≥ 3,000/mm³, Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³, Hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL.
  - Hepatic: Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 × ULN, AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 × ULN (or ≤ 5 × ULN with liver metastases).
  - Renal: Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 × ULN or creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.

**Prior Concurrent Therapy:**
- No prior immune checkpoint inhibitors or investigational therapies for advanced disease.
- Prior endocrine therapy and chemotherapy in the adjuvant setting is allowable if completed over 12 months prior to enrollment.

**Outcome Measures:**
- **Primary Measure:** Overall survival (defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause).
- **Secondary Measures:** 
    - Progression-free survival (time from randomization to disease progression or death)
    - Overall response rate (calculated as the percentage of patients with a complete or partial response to therapy)
    - Adverse events of any grade recorded according to NCI CTCAE version 5.0.

**Follow-up Phase:** Patients will be monitored for safety until disease progression or withdrawal of consent, with follow-up assessments at least every 12 weeks for 2 years post-randomization.

**Statistical Analysis:** Survival curves will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and comparison between groups will be performed using the log-rank test. A two-sided significance level of 0.05 will be set for primary analyses.

This study's design asserts a structured approach towards targeted patient selection and optimal treatment scheduling, alongside comprehensive monitoring, which align with previously identified success factors for capecitabine's efficacy in clinical trials. Given the integration of combination therapy targeting immune mechanisms, this trial is positioned to potentially yield significant advancements in the management of locally advanced or metastatic triple-negative breast cancer, thereby enhancing clinical outcomes in this vulnerable patient population.
