**Failed Clinical Trial of Dasatinib: Efficacy of Dasatinib in Patients with Recurrent or Metastatic Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma**

**A Phase II Study of Dasatinib in Recurrent or Metastatic Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma**

**RATIONALE:** Dasatinib is hypothesized to exert antitumor effects through the inhibition of Src family kinases, aiming to disrupt cellular proliferation and survival pathways in various malignancies, including cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC). However, the complexity of tumor biology, particularly the impact of tumor heterogeneity, may critically undermine its efficacy in this patient population.

**PURPOSE:** The primary purpose of this phase II trial is to assess the objective response rate (ORR) of dasatinib in patients diagnosed with recurrent or metastatic cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma, alongside evaluating tolerability and progression-free survival (PFS).

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To determine the objective response rate (ORR) per RECIST criteria in patients treated with dasatinib.
II. To evaluate the safety and tolerability of dasatinib in this specific cohort.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess 12-week progression-free survival (PFS) rates among participants receiving dasatinib.
II. To measure duration of response (DoR) and overall survival (OS).
III. To analyze pharmacokinetic profiles and correlate them with the efficacy of treatment.
IV. To evaluate the expression of phosphorylated Src (pSrc) and other relevant biomarkers in tumor tissues pre- and post-treatment.

**OUTLINE:** Participants receive oral dasatinib at a dosage of 100 mg daily in 28-day cycles, continuing until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or study termination. Tumor assessments are conducted at baseline, 12 weeks, and every 8 weeks thereafter.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically confirmed cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma with measurable disease defined by RECIST criteria.
- Recurrent or metastatic disease after prior surgical intervention and/or adjuvant radiotherapy.
- Patients aged ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG Performance Status 0-1.
- Adequate organ function as defined by specified laboratory criteria.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- History of concurrent malignancies except localized non-melanoma skin cancers.
- Prior treatment involving Src inhibitors or targeted therapies directed against c-KIT.
- Significant cardiovascular diseases, including QTc prolongation > 480 msec.
- Clinically significant pneumonia, interstitial lung disease, or oxygen supplementation at study entry.

**OUTCOMES MEASURED:**
- Objective response is defined as complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) according to RECIST criteria.
- Adverse events and toxicity are monitored using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0.

**RESULTS:**
The trial accrued 80 participants over an enrollment period spanning 18 months. Despite initial hope regarding dasatinib's mechanistic action, outcomes revealed a disappointing ORR of only 10%, far below the anticipated threshold of clinical significance. The primary reasons for the suboptimal results include:

1. **Tumor Heterogeneity:** Significant variability in the molecular and phenotypic characteristics of cSCC was reported, leading to differential responses to dasatinib treatment. Enhanced expression of alternative pro-survival pathways counteracted the sporadic inhibition of Src kinases.

2. **Pharmacodynamic Factors:** Investigators noted frequent upregulation of compensatory signaling pathways such as Akt and ERK, diminishing the expected efficacy of dasatinib.

3. **Limited Patient Populations:** The majority of enrolled patients were heavily pre-treated with prior systemic therapies, resulting in acquired resistance mechanisms that likely precluded meaningful response to subsequent dasatinib administration.

4. **Suboptimal Therapeutic Window:** The administered dosing regimen failed to achieve adequate plasma levels of dasatinib, as evidenced by pharmacokinetic assessments, further detracting from its therapeutic potential.

5. **Inadequate Objective Response Measurement:** The reliance on RECIST may have overlooked subtle therapeutic effects, as many patients displayed disease stabilization without meeting strict criteria for response.

**CONCLUSION:** The overall findings from this trial indicate that dasatinib, in its evaluated dosing and context, does not achieve meaningful therapeutic efficacy in patients with recurrent or metastatic cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma. Further investigation is warranted to explore alternative treatment strategies that may provide benefit to this patient population, emphasizing the necessity for improved predictive biomarker identification and patient stratification methodologies in future studies. Participants will continue monitoring every 3 months post-treatment completion to gather long-term follow-up data on survival and secondary outcomes.
