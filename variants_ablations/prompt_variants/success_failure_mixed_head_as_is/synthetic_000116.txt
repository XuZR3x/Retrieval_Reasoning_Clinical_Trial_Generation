**Title**: Phase II Study Assessing the Efficacy of Mifepristone in Patients with Hypercortisolism Associated with Ectopic ACTH Syndrome

**Background**:  
Ectopic adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) syndrome is characterized by excessive production of cortisol, primarily due to malignant neoplasms secreting ACTH. This condition results in severe hypercortisolism leading to substantial morbidity, including type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and decreased quality of life. Mifepristone, a glucocorticoid receptor antagonist, presents a targeted mechanism of action advantageous for reducing cortisol levels and associated metabolic dysregulation. Prior evidence indicates mifepristone’s role in effectively managing glucocorticoid receptor-related conditions. 

**Objectives**:
- To evaluate the efficacy of mifepristone in decreasing plasma cortisol levels in patients with confirmed ectopic ACTH syndrome.
- To assess the safety and overall tolerability of mifepristone therapy in this cohort.
- To investigate the impact of mifepristone on metabolic parameters, including glucose levels and body mass index (BMI).

**Methods**:
This phase II, multi-center, open-label trial involved a cohort of adult patients aged 18 to 75 with biochemically confirmed ectopic ACTH syndrome. 

**Eligibility Criteria**:
- **Inclusion Criteria**:
  1. Men and women aged 18 to 75 years.
  2. Documented diagnosis of ectopic ACTH syndrome.
  3. Informed consent capability.
  
- **Exclusion Criteria**:
  1. Significant comorbidities requiring immediate intervention (e.g., severe uncontrolled diabetes).
  2. History of hypersensitivity to mifepristone.
  3. Concurrent systemic glucocorticoid therapy within the preceding month.

**Intervention**:
Participants received mifepristone orally at a dose of 300 mg daily for a total duration of 12 weeks. 

**Endpoints**:
- **Primary Endpoint**: Change in serum cortisol levels from baseline to week 12, measured at 8 AM.
- **Secondary Endpoints**: 
  1. Change in body weight and BMI over the treatment period.
  2. Change in fasting plasma glucose and hemoglobin A1c levels after 12 weeks.

**Statistical Considerations**:
The change in cortisol levels was analyzed using a paired t-test for pre- and post-treatment comparisons. Secondary endpoints were subjected to repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) to identify significant differences.

**Results**:
A total of 50 patients were enrolled; 47 completed the 12-week treatment. The primary endpoint revealed a significant reduction in serum cortisol levels (mean decrease of 50% from baseline, p < 0.001). Secondary analyses demonstrated an average weight reduction of 4.2 kg (p < 0.005) and improved fasting plasma glucose levels (mean reduction of 10 mg/dL, p < 0.01). Safety assessments indicated mild to moderate adverse events, primarily gastrointestinal disturbances, which were manageable without treatment interruption.

**Conclusions**:
This clinical trial supports the efficacy of mifepristone in managing hypercortisolism in patients with ectopic ACTH syndrome. The significant reduction in cortisol levels correlates with improved metabolic parameters and weight management. Safety monitoring interventions proved effective in minimizing adverse effects. Future investigations should focus on long-term outcomes and the comparative effectiveness of mifepristone versus conventional treatments in this population.
