**Title: Efficacy and Safety of Nitazoxanide in Patients with Acute Gastroenteritis Due to Rotavirus Infection: A Multi-center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial**

This clinical trial is designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of nitazoxanide in patients diagnosed with acute gastroenteritis caused by rotavirus. The study hypothesizes that nitazoxanide will significantly shorten the duration of diarrhea and gastrointestinal symptoms compared to placebo, thereby improving clinical outcomes in affected individuals.

**Primary Objectives**: 
- To assess the efficacy of nitazoxanide in reducing the duration of diarrhea in patients with confirmed rotavirus infection.
- To evaluate the safety profile and tolerability of nitazoxanide compared to placebo.

**Study Design**: 
This is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving multiple centers. Participants will be allocated to receive either nitazoxanide or placebo in a 2:1 ratio.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
- Age ≥ 6 months and ≤ 65 years.
- Diagnosis of acute gastroenteritis confirmed by stool assay positive for rotavirus.
- Presence of diarrhea defined as the passage of three or more loose or liquid stools within a 24-hour period.
- Written informed consent from the caregivers or valid assent from the patient.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
- Severe dehydration requiring hospitalization.
- History of hypersensitivity to nitazoxanide or any components of the formulation.
- Concurrent gastrointestinal disorders (e.g., inflammatory bowel disease).
- Recent (within 30 days) treatment with any antiviral medications, especially those targeting rotavirus.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding females, or females of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.
- Immunocompromised patients or those receiving immunosuppressive therapy.

**Primary Endpoint**:
- Duration of diarrhea, measured from the first recorded stool to the cessation of diarrhea, confirmed by two consecutive days of normal stool consistency.

**Secondary Endpoints**: 
- Change in severity of gastrointestinal symptoms assessed via a validated scoring system (i.e., the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale).
- Incidence of adverse effects reported during the trial.
- Time to complete recovery defined by resolution of all gastrointestinal symptoms and return to baseline bowel function.

**Eligibility Assessment**: 
The initial visit will encompass a thorough medical evaluation to confirm the diagnosis of rotavirus-induced acute gastroenteritis, including stool sample analysis and vital signs assessment. 

**Statistical Analysis**: 
The statistical analysis will be performed using intention-to-treat methodology. Efficacy will be evaluated using Kaplan-Meier survival curves for time to resolution of diarrhea, analyzed using log-rank tests. Safety data will be summarized descriptively.

**Hypotheses**: 
The study posits that nitazoxanide's broad-spectrum antiviral activity and optimized dosing regimen will enhance clinical outcomes in rotavirus gastroenteritis, thereby providing a clear therapeutic alternative for patients. Successful trials in similar domains demonstrate that nitazoxanide’s unique mechanism on viral replication and its favorable safety profile leads to superior patient adherence, fostering positive clinical results.

**Safety Monitoring**: 
A Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will conduct periodic reviews of interim analyses to ensure patient safety throughout the duration of the trial.

**Conclusion**: 
This study aims to robustly establish the efficacy of nitazoxanide in managing acute gastroenteritis due to rotavirus while maintaining a favorable safety profile. The clear primary endpoints and meticulous patient selection process promise to yield interpretable and impactful results in a population in urgent need of effective therapeutic options.
