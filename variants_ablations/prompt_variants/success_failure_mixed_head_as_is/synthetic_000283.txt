**Tranexamic Acid Administration for Reduction of Perioperative Blood Loss in Elective Hip Replacement Surgery: A Multicentric, Randomized Controlled Trial**

**Objective:**  
The primary aim of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of tranexamic acid in reducing perioperative blood loss in patients undergoing elective hip replacement surgery. This trial seeks to ascertain whether tranexamic acid, administered intravenously preoperatively and postoperatively, is effective in mitigating blood loss compared to a placebo in this surgical context. The use of tranexamic acid has been supported by recent literature, demonstrating its capacity to effectively manage surgical bleeding and enhance patient safety.

**Design:**  
This is a multicentric, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving multiple surgical centers specializing in orthopedic procedures. The study will recruit patients requiring elective hip replacement surgery, ensuring a robust inclusion of participants to enhance the generalizability of the findings.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adults aged 18 years and older
- Scheduled for elective hip replacement surgery
- Signed informed consent prior to participation
- Hemoglobin levels within the normal range preoperatively

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Known allergy to tranexamic acid
- History of thromboembolic events, including deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism
- Concurrently on anticoagulation therapy
- Severe renal or liver dysfunction
- Any condition requiring urgent surgical intervention rather than elective surgery

**Intervention:**  
Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either tranexamic acid (1g IV) or a placebo prior to incision and again 24 hours postoperative. The administration protocol aligns with evidence supporting the use of tranexamic acid in various surgical settings to optimize hemostatic management. The control of perioperative strategies will be standardized across all sites, including blood loss measurement via drainage tubes.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
The primary outcome measure will be the total blood loss quantified within a 72-hour period post-surgery, collected through an established drainage system. This evaluation will allow for an accurate assessment of the intervention's impact, consistent with past successful trials demonstrating significant reductions in bleeding when tranexamic acid was utilized.

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
1. The incidence of blood transfusions within 72 hours postoperatively.
2. The frequency of postoperative complications, including infection and thromboembolic events.
3. Patient recovery metrics, including postoperative mobility and analgesia requirements.

**Sample Size Calculation:**  
Based on prior studies indicating a reduction in blood loss, a total of 300 participants (150 in each arm) will be required to achieve sufficient statistical power, allowing for the detection of significant differences with a p-value <0.05.

**Ethical Considerations:**  
The trial will adhere to the ethical standards of clinical research, with oversight by an independent ethics committee to ensure patient safety and compliance with relevant regulations.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Data will be analyzed using an intention-to-treat approach. Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline characteristics, while inferential statistics (e.g., t-tests, chi-squared tests) will be utilized to evaluate differences in primary and secondary outcomes between groups.

**Significance:**  
This trial is poised to contribute valuable insights regarding the utilization of tranexamic acid in orthopedic surgery, particularly in the context of elective hip replacement procedures. By employing rigorous methodological standards and a comprehensive intervention strategy, the study aims to address critical gaps in the existing literature regarding perioperative bleeding management, thereby refining clinical protocols and enhancing patient safety outcomes.
