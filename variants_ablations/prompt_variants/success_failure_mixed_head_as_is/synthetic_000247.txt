**Title**: Investigating the Impact of Insulin Management on Glycemic Control and Hospital Outcomes in Critically Ill Patients: A Multi-center, Randomized Trial 

**Abstract**: The aim of this clinical trial is to evaluate the effectiveness of a standardized insulin administration protocol versus usual care in managing hyperglycemia in critically ill patients. Prior trials have failed to demonstrate a clear benefit from intensive insulin therapy, with factors such as varying target glycemic ranges, recruitment challenges, and a lack of uniform protocol potentially confounding outcomes. This trial seeks to mitigate these issues and assess the effects on patient recovery and mortality.

**Background**: Critically ill patients frequently present with stress hyperglycemia, leading to potentially adverse outcomes if glycemic levels remain unregulated. Previous trials demonstrated inconclusive results regarding the benefit of strict glycemic control, suggesting that factors such as aggressive treatment targets (80-110 mg/dL), recruitment biases, and heterogeneous medical conditions may have contributed to discrepancies in findings. This study aims to address these complexities by employing a uniform insulin protocol.

**Study Design**: This is a multi-center, double-blind, randomized controlled trial involving ICU patients diagnosed with critical illness due to various etiologies (including but not limited to, infection, trauma, or post-operative recovery). A total of 300 participants will be enrolled across 5 participating hospitals. All patients will be randomized into two arms: one receiving a target glucose range of 100-140 mg/dL (standardized intervention group) and the other receiving conventional management with a target range of 150-180 mg/dL.

**Methods**: 
1. **Inclusion Criteria**: 
   - Adults aged 18-75 years 
   - Admitted to the ICU with a blood glucose level exceeding 150 mg/dL within the first 24 hours 
   - No previous history of diabetes prior to hospitalization 
   - Capable of giving informed consent or with legal representative consent available

2. **Exclusion Criteria**: 
   - Patients with chronic medical conditions that alter glucose metabolism (e.g., acute pancreatitis)
   - Requirements for emergency surgery within the first 24 hours of admission 
   - Pre-existing severe hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia unawareness

3. **Protocol**: Patients will be randomized to receive insulin via an explicit titration protocol stratified by the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) to ensure consistent treatment adherence. Blood glucose levels will be monitored every hour, and insulin administration shall be adjusted according to protocol-driven algorithms. In cases of unanticipated hypoglycemia, patients will be managed according to established medical protocols.

4. **Outcome Measures**: The primary endpoint will be a composite of hospital mortality and length of ICU stay, assessed at 30 days post-enrollment. Secondary endpoints will include the occurrence of adverse events, frequency of hypoglycemia (defined as blood glucose <70 mg/dL), and functional neurological outcomes assessed via the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) at hospital discharge and at 3 and 6 months post-discharge.

**Statistical Analysis**: Descriptive statistics will characterize the study population, and Student's t-test or Mann-Whitney U test will be utilized for continuous variables, while Fisher’s exact test will assess categorical variables. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Potential Limitations**: Challenges regarding patient recruitment, specifically the extended time required for enrollment due to initial diagnostic assessments, may introduce selection bias. Moreover, a potential variance in clinician adherence to the insulin titration protocol could hinder intention-to-treat analysis.

**Conclusion**: This trial seeks to elucidate the potential clinical benefits of a standardized insulin administration strategy in critically ill patients, addressing the reasons for past trial failures to determine the true efficacy of insulin management in glycemic control. Results from this study may provide implications for future guidelines regarding insulin therapy in critical care settings.
