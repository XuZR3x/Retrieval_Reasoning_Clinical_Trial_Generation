**Failed Clinical Trial of Eplerenone: Evaluating the Efficacy of Eplerenone in Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease and Hypertension**

**Clinical Protocol for a Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Study of the Antihypertensive Effects of Eplerenone in Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease and Hypertension**

**Abstract:** The objective of this trial was to assess the efficacy of eplerenone in reducing blood pressure and improving renal function in patients with stage 3 chronic kidney disease (CKD) associated with hypertension. Despite prior evidence suggesting a therapeutic role for mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists in the context of CKD, this study was ultimately terminated due to insufficient patient enrollment, highlighting the necessity for adequate recruitment strategies in clinical research.

**Study Design:** This was a Phase 2, multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial designed to evaluate the effects of eplerenone on 24-hour mean arterial pressure and renal parameters in adults aged 40-75 years with stage 3 CKD and systolic blood pressure between 130 and 160 mmHg. Participants were randomized to receive either eplerenone or placebo for 24 weeks, with the primary endpoint being the change in mean arterial pressure from baseline.

**Inclusion Criteria:** 
- Adults aged 40 to 75 years.
- Diagnosed with stage 3 CKD, defined by an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of 30-59 mL/min/1.73 m².
- Systolic blood pressure (SBP) between 130 mmHg and 160 mmHg.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Secondary hypertension due to renal artery stenosis or other identifiable causes. 
- Life-threatening arrhythmias or significant cardiovascular disease.
- Current use of other antihypertensive agents, diuretics, or corticosteroids.
- Serum potassium levels outside the range of 3.5 to 5.0 mmol/L.
- Recent acute kidney injury or major surgery within 3 months prior to enrollment.

**Methodology:** Participants underwent baseline assessments that included blood pressure monitoring, serum biochemistry, and renal function tests. Following baseline evaluations, subjects were randomized to receive either eplerenone at a dose of 50 mg daily or matched placebo. Blood pressure measurements and renal function assessments using serum creatinine and eGFR were conducted at 12 and 24 weeks.

**Outcome Measures:** The primary outcome was the percentage change in mean arterial pressure from baseline to 24 weeks. Secondary outcomes included changes in renal function parameters (serum creatinine and eGFR), frequency of adverse events, and changes in quality of life assessed by a validated questionnaire.

**Results:** The study was terminated prematurely because of inadequate recruitment, with only 45 participants enrolled out of the targeted 150. Statistical analyses were not performed due to insufficient sample size, preventing the extrapolation of meaningful data regarding the efficacy of eplerenone in this population. Notably, participant variability and the potential for competing physiological mechanisms in the pathophysiology of CKD significantly contributed to recruitment challenges, as many candidates presented with complex comorbidities rendering them ineligible based on strict exclusion criteria.

**Conclusion:** The early termination of the trial underscores the critical role of robust participant recruitment and the definition of clear inclusion/exclusion criteria in clinical studies. The inability to secure sufficient enrollment limited the ability to ascertain the efficacy of eplerenone in the treatment of hypertension in the CKD population, illustrating that multi-faceted recruitment strategies need to be adopted in studies addressing complex chronic conditions. Further research is warranted to explore optimal therapeutic strategies in this patient demographic to address hypertension and protect renal function effectively.

**Study Identifier:** EPLA-0321-074 (Eplerenone in Chronic Kidney Disease and Hypertension).
