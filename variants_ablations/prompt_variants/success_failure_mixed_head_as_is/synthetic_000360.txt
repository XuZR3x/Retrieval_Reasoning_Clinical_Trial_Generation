**Phase 3 Multicenter Trial Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Daptomycin in Patients with Infected Acute Diabetic Foot Ulcers**

**Study Title**: A Phase 3, Multicenter, Randomized, Open-label Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Daptomycin versus Standard of Care in Patients with Infected Acute Diabetic Foot Ulcers

**Study Overview**:

This Phase 3 clinical trial aims to compare the efficacy and safety of daptomycin administered at a dose of 8 mg/kg daily for 7 days against standard care comprising of optimized intravenous vancomycin (1 g every 12 hours) in patients presenting with acute diabetic foot ulcers infected predominantly by Gram-positive pathogens. This study will assess the clinical response rate and microbiological eradication at the end of the treatment period.

**Objectives**:

1. To determine if daptomycin demonstrates non-inferiority to standard care, measured by the clinical response at Day 21 post-therapy.
2. To evaluate the safety profile of daptomycin in relation to the conventional treatment regimen.
3. To assess the pharmacokinetic profile of daptomycin in the target population.

**Inclusion Criteria**:

- Adults aged 18 to 85 years, diagnosed with Type 1 or Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.
- Presence of infected foot ulcers classified as Wagner grades 2 or 3, necessitating antibiotic therapy without surgical intervention at enrollment.
- Documented Gram-positive infection confirmed by microbiological culture.
- Ability and willingness to provide written informed consent and comply with the protocol.
- Adequate renal function defined as creatinine clearance ≥ 50 mL/min.

**Exclusion Criteria**:

- Presence of osteomyelitis as determined by clinical consensus guidelines.
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min).
- History of hypersensitivity or significant adverse reaction to daptomycin or vancomycin.
- Major surgeries planned within the study period.
- Pregnant or lactating women not adhering to effective contraceptive measures.

**Methodology**:

Participants will be randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either daptomycin or standard therapy. The trial structure employs a multi-center approach, focusing on facilities with seasoned experience in managing diabetic infections.

**Clinical Endpoints**:

The primary endpoint will include the clinical response rate at the test-of-cure (TOC) visit on Day 21, defined as:
- Cure: Complete resolution of signs of infection and wound healing.
- Improvement: Significant reduction in clinical signs, with residual symptoms not warranting ongoing treatment.
- Treatment Failure: Persistence or exacerbation of infection necessitating additional intervention.

Secondary endpoints will assess microbiological (culture-confirmed eradication), safety (evaluated through adverse event reports), quality of life (using a standardized questionnaire), and pharmacokinetics profiles of daptomycin.

**Safety Assessment**:

Safety monitoring will include the rigorous evaluation of any treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) throughout the study duration. Adverse events will be classified according to severity and attentiveness to any documented correlations with medication received.

**Statistical Analysis**:

The primary analysis will utilize an intention-to-treat framework with descriptive statistics to detail demographic characteristics, and inferential statistics to gauge efficacy and safety outcomes between treatment arms, including the estimated proportion of clinical success.

**Conclusion**:

This clinical trial protocol endeavors to substantiate the therapeutic role of daptomycin in the management of infected diabetic foot ulcers, leveraging its enhanced pharmacokinetic profile and robust safety assessment, thereby potentially positioning daptomycin as a viable alternative to traditional antibiotics in this patient demographic. The trial’s structure reflects stringent patient selection criteria and anticipates robust endpoint evaluation, ensuring high fidelity in outcomes.

**Keywords**: Daptomycin, diabetic foot ulcer, clinical trial, Gram-positive infection, safety, efficacy, randomized control trial.
