**Clinical Trial Title:** A 12-Month, Multicenter, Open-Label Study to Evaluate the Long-Term Efficacy, Safety, and Patient-Reported Outcomes of Fingolimod in Patients with Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis Following Inadequate Response to Prior Disease Modifying Therapies

**Study Identifier:** FTY-2024-RRMS

**Objectives of the Study:**
The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of fingolimod in reducing the annualized relapse rate (ARR) in patients diagnosed with Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis (RRMS) who exhibited an inadequate response to at least one prior Disease Modifying Therapy (DMT). The secondary objectives include assessment of safety, tolerability, and patient-reported outcomes related to treatment satisfaction and quality of life.

**Study Design:**
This study is designed as a 12-month, multicenter, open-label, non-comparative prospective trial. Patients diagnosed with RRMS will be enrolled based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria mirroring successful previous trials while allowing tailored approaches to engage patients with high disease activity. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults aged 18 to 65 years with a confirmed diagnosis of RRMS per the revised McDonald criteria.
2. Documented inadequate response to at least one prior DMT as evidenced by clinical or MRI findings indicative of disease activity (i.e., relapses, new MRI lesions).
3. Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) score between 0 and 5.5 at baseline.
4. Patients must provide written informed consent prior to any study-related procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of secondary progressive MS or other chronic autoimmune conditions.
2. Prior exposure to fingolimod or other sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor modulators.
3. Known contraindications to fingolimod treatment including significant cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled systemic infections.
4. Pregnant or lactating women, or women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.

**Sample Size:**
A total of 600 patients will be enrolled across multiple sites based on the assumption of a significant ARR reduction and improvement in patient-reported outcomes compared to baseline response to previous therapies.

**Primary Endpoint:**
- Annualized Relapse Rate (ARR) after 12 months of treatment with fingolimod.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Frequency and severity of adverse events, including potential cardiac events monitored through 6-hour observation post-initial dosing.
- Change in EDSS score from baseline to 12 months.
- Patient-reported treatment satisfaction evaluated through the Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire for Medication (TSQM-9).
- Health-related quality of life measures assessed using the Multiple Sclerosis Quality of Life-54 (MSQoL-54).

**Study Procedures:**
Patients will initially undergo screening assessment to confirm eligibility followed by an open-label treatment phase with fingolimod administered orally at 0.5 mg once daily. Safety assessments will occur bi-weekly for the initial month and monthly thereafter, with regular follow-ups to assess relapses, adverse effects, and patient-reported outcomes.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Data will be analyzed using appropriate statistical methods for continuous and categorical variables. Efficacy measures will include comparisons of ARR before and after treatment initiation. Safety analysis will involve monitoring adverse events and changes in vital signs across the treatment timeline.

**Outcome Measures:**
The study is anticipated to validate the therapeutic role of fingolimod in a highly active MS population, corroborated by patient retention and satisfaction metrics. Efficacy outcomes will be critically appraised in relation to adherence and compliance facilitated by the open-label study design that emphasizes patient engagement and transparency.

**Potential Impact:**
This trial aims to contribute to improving management strategies in RRMS, especially among patients exhibiting suboptimal responses to existing therapies, thereby enhancing treatment paradigms and patient outcomes in this challenging patient population. The integration of robust patient-reported outcomes is critical for a comprehensive evaluation of fingolimod’s efficacy and quality of life impacts in an aligned fashion with previously successful trials, paving the way for possible repositioning of fingolimod in the clinical management of RRMS.
