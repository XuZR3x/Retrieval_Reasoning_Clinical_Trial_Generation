<clinicalTrial>
   <title>Investigating the Long-term Efficacy of Teriparatide in Reducing Fracture Risk Among Postmenopausal Women with Osteoporosis: A Multicenter Study</title>
   <objective>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the long-term efficacy of teriparatide in reducing the incidence of new vertebral and non-vertebral fractures in postmenopausal women diagnosed with osteoporosis, as well as to assess changes in bone mineral density (BMD) over a period of 24 months.</objective>
   <methodology>
      <design>Multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled trial</design>
      <intervention>
         <type>Drug</type>
         <name>Teriparatide</name>
         <dosage>20 mcg/day subcutaneously in addition to standard care with calcium and vitamin D</dosage>
         <duration>24 months</duration>
      </intervention>
      <control>
         <type>Standard care</type>
         <description>Calcium and vitamin D supplementation alone</description>
      </control>
      <phases>
         <screeningPeriod>4 weeks</screeningPeriod>
         <treatmentPeriod>24 months</treatmentPeriod>
         <followupPeriod>3 months</followupPeriod>
      </phases>
   </methodology>
   <inclusionCriteria>
      <criterion>Women aged 55 years or older</criterion>
      <criterion>Diagnosis of postmenopausal osteoporosis based on the National Osteoporosis Foundation criteria</criterion>
      <criterion>At least one moderate or two mild vertebral fractures within the last 12 months</criterion>
      <criterion>Bone mineral density T-score of at least -2.5 or lower at either hip or lumbar spine</criterion>
      <criterion>Willing and able to provide informed consent</criterion>
   </inclusionCriteria>
   <exclusionCriteria>
      <criterion>History of metabolic bone diseases excluding primary osteoporosis</criterion>
      <criterion>Previous treatment with parathyroid hormone or analogs</criterion>
      <criterion>Uncontrolled hyperthyroidism or hypoparathyroidism</criterion>
      <criterion>History of malignancy in the past 5 years, apart from treated basal cell carcinoma</criterion>
      <criterion>Recent (< 12 months) use of corticosteroids or anabolic agents affecting bone metabolism</criterion>
      <criterion>Clinically significant abnormal laboratory values at screening</criterion>
   </exclusionCriteria>
   <outcomeMeasures>
      <primaryOutcome>
         <measure>Incidence of new vertebral and non-vertebral fractures, assessed via radiographic evaluation</measure>
         <timeFrame>At baseline, 12 months, and 24 months</timeFrame>
      </primaryOutcome>
      <secondaryOutcome>
         <measure>Percent change in bone mineral density at the lumbar spine and hip, measured via DEXA</measure>
         <timeFrame>At baseline, 12 months, and 24 months</timeFrame>
      </secondaryOutcome>
      <safetyOutcome>
         <measure>Incidence and severity of adverse events, including hypercalcemia and injection site reactions</measure>
         <timeFrame>Throughout the study duration</timeFrame>
      </safetyOutcome>
   </outcomeMeasures>
   <rationale>
      <reason1>Implementation of a tailored inclusion criterion focused on individuals with a high fracture risk enhances the relevance and generalizability of the trial outcomes.</reason1>
      <reason2>Synchronization of teriparatide administration with adequate calcium and vitamin D supplementation maximizes therapeutic efficacy and promotes optimal bone health outcomes.</reason2>
      <reason3>The use of robust methodologies for measuring BMD, including the DEXA scan, allows for precise quantification of treatment effects and reinforces the evidence for teriparatide's role in improving bone density.</reason3>
      <reason4>Stringent adverse effect monitoring ensures that any potential complications are appropriately managed, thereby enhancing the medication's safety profile and fostering participant confidence.</reason4>
      <reason5>Exclusion criteria effectively minimize the risk of confounding variables, ensuring a clear analysis of teriparatide’s efficacy without the influence of concomitant metabolic disorders.</reason5>
   </rationale>
   <locations>
      <location>Multiple centers across Japan</location>
   </locations>
   <investigators>
      <leadInvestigator>Dr. Haruki Takeda, MD, PhD, Department of Endocrinology, Tokyo Medical University</leadInvestigator>
   </investigators>
   <status>Currently Recruiting</status>
</clinicalTrial>
