**A Phase II Study of Brentuximab Vedotin in Combination with Nivolumab for Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma**  

A Randomized, Open-Label, Phase II Trial of Brentuximab Vedotin and Nivolumab in Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma  

**Background:** The combination of brentuximab vedotin, a monoclonal antibody-drug conjugate targeting CD30, with nivolumab, an immune checkpoint inhibitor, is hypothesized to augment the therapeutic efficacy in patients with relapsed or refractory classical Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL). Previous successful trials demonstrated the tolerability and efficacy of brentuximab vedotin alone or combined with traditional chemotherapy. This study aims to further enhance therapeutic outcomes through immunologic modulation while maintaining an excellent safety profile.  

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, randomized, open-label Phase II trial assessing the efficacy and safety of brentuximab vedotin in combination with nivolumab versus nivolumab alone in participants with relapsed or refractory cHL. The primary objective is to evaluate the overall response rate (ORR). Secondary endpoints include duration of response, progression-free survival, overall survival, and safety metrics.

**Participants:** Patients with histologically confirmed relapsed or refractory cHL, who have received at least one prior line of therapy, will be enrolled. Key inclusion criteria include:  
- Age ≥ 18 years  
- ECOG performance status 0-2  
- Measurable disease per the Cheson criteria  
- Adequate hepatic, renal, and hematologic function  
- Signed informed consent  

**Exclusion Criteria:** Key exclusions encompass:  
- Prior treatment with brentuximab vedotin or nivolumab  
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment  
- Concurrent malignancy, with exceptions for non-melanoma skin cancers    
- Uncontrolled infections, including HIV, hepatitis B, and hepatitis C  
- History of hypersensitivity reactions to either drug  

**Treatment Regimen:** Participants will be randomized to receive either:  
1. **Brentuximab Vedotin** (1.8 mg/kg IV) on day 1 and **Nivolumab** (3 mg/kg IV) on days 1 and 15 of a 28-day cycle for up to 6 cycles, followed by maintenance nivolumab (3 mg/kg) every 28 days for 12 months, provided there is no disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
  
2. **Nivolumab** (3 mg/kg IV) alone on days 1 and 15 for the same duration.  

**Efficacy Assessment:** The primary endpoint, ORR, will be evaluated using independent radiological review, assessing responses as per Cheson criteria. Secondary endpoints will include survival analyses, quality of life assessments, and safety evaluations documented through adverse events and clinical laboratory tests.

**Results:** Initial data indicate an increased ORR in the brentuximab vedotin in combination with nivolumab arm compared to the nivolumab alone group, demonstrating the potential of this regimen to heighten treatment response. Detailed safety analyses revealed manageable adverse events, largely consistent with known profiles of both agents without significant unexpected toxicities.

The successful integration of both agents supports the hypothesis of their synergistic activity and provides a compelling rationale to expand treatment options for patients with relapsed or refractory classical Hodgkin lymphoma. 

**Conclusion:** This study demonstrated that combining brentuximab vedotin with nivolumab may significantly improve outcomes in patients with relapsed cHL. Such findings highlight the importance of immunotherapeutic strategies in the treatment landscape of Hodgkin lymphoma and establish a foundation for further research in larger trials. 

**Follow-Up:** Continuous assessment post-treatment will evaluate long-term outcomes, including potential durability of response and improvement in quality of life, reinforcing an adaptive management approach to patient care in this population. 

**Keywords:** Brentuximab vedotin, Nivolumab, Relapsed classical Hodgkin lymphoma, Immunotherapy, Combination therapy, Phase II Trial.
