**A Phase III Study of Liposomal Doxorubicin Combined with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Triple-Negative Breast Cancer**

**Objective**: This randomized, multicenter Phase III trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of liposomal doxorubicin in combination with pembrolizumab as a first-line treatment for patients diagnosed with advanced triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). The study hypothesizes that the addition of pembrolizumab will significantly enhance the objective response rate (ORR) and overall survival (OS) compared to liposomal doxorubicin alone.

**Primary Objectives**:
I. To assess the ORR according to RECIST v1.1 criteria in patients receiving liposomal doxorubicin combined with pembrolizumab.
II. To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) of the combination therapy.

**Secondary Objectives**:
I. To monitor overall survival rates after 12 months of treatment.
II. To assess the safety and tolerability profile of the liposomal doxorubicin and pembrolizumab regimen.
III. To explore biomarker correlations with treatment response, including PD-L1 expression levels.

**Design**: This active-controlled trial will consist of an estimated 120 participants randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either liposomal doxorubicin (30 mg/m² IV on day 1 every 21 days) in conjunction with pembrolizumab (200 mg IV on day 1 every 21 days) or liposomal doxorubicin monotherapy. 

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced triple-negative breast cancer (stage IIIB or IV).
- Patients with one measurable lesion as defined by RECIST v1.1.
- Age ≥ 18 years and ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function: LVEF ≥ 50% as verified by echocardiography, AST and ALT ≤ 1.5 x ULN, total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL.
- Patients must provide written informed consent prior to any study procedures.
- Patients should be naïve to chemotherapy for metastatic disease.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Prior systemic therapy for advanced disease.
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the study treatment.
- Concurrent participation in another clinical study that may interfere with outcomes.
- A history of autoimmune disease or severe immunosuppression.

**Endpoints**:
The primary endpoint will be the ORR, defined as the proportion of patients with a confirmed complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) at 24 weeks assessed through imaging techniques. The PFS will be measured from the date of randomization to the date of documented disease progression or death.

**Statistical Considerations**: Sample size was calculated based on achieving a minimum ORR of 55% in the combination group vs. 30% in the control (liposomal doxorubicin only), considering a two-tailed alpha of 0.05 and a power of 80%. The effectiveness will be assessed using a modified Simon's two-stage design.

**Safety Monitoring**: Adverse events will be recorded in accordance with CTCAE v5.0 criteria during treatment and follow-up. Cardiac function will be monitored through regular echocardiographic assessments, with particular focus on changes in LVEF.

**Supporting Rationale**: Combining liposomal doxorubicin with pembrolizumab targets both the tumor-specific and immune-mediated pathways. The rationale stems from previous trials demonstrating enhanced response rates with immunotherapy in HER2-negative malignancies, particularly in mutated forms of breast cancer. As noted in prior studies, the strategic targeting of TNBC, coupled with a robust trial design, enhances the statistical validity of the outcome measures. Furthermore, precise patient stratification minimizes confounding factors, fostering clearer interpretations of efficacy results.

**Conclusion**: This Phase III clinical trial proposes an innovative approach to treating advanced TNBC through a synergistic application of liposomal doxorubicin and pembrolizumab. With a design that emphasizes robust efficacy endpoints, comprehensive safety monitoring, and a well-defined patient population, the potential for establishing a new standard of care in clinical applications could materialize, advancing therapeutic paradigms in breast cancer management.
