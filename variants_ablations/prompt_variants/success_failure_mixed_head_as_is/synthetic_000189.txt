<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Exploratory Trial of Cilostazol in Patients with Peripheral Artery Disease: A Dual Evaluation of Efficacy and Safety</Title>
    <StudyType>Phase III, Multi-center, Randomized, Double-blind</StudyType>
    <Objective>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of cilostazol in improving functional capacity and reducing cardiovascular events in patients with intermittent claudication related to peripheral artery disease (PAD), compared with a placebo control. The secondary objective is to assess the safety profile of cilostazol in this patient population.</Objective>
    
    <Intervention>Cilostazol oral tablets at a dosage of 100 mg twice daily for 24 weeks.</Intervention>

    <Control>Placebo oral tablets identical in appearance administered twice daily for 24 weeks.</Control>
    
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Patients aged 40 to 85 years with a documented diagnosis of PAD defined by an ankle-brachial index (ABI) less than 0.9.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients experiencing intermittent claudication with a pain score of at least 3 on the Walking Impairment Questionnaire.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients with stable medical conditions and no significant cardiovascular events within the last 6 months.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients who have provided informed consent to participate in the study.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to cilostazol or any components of its formulation.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Current use of other antiplatelet agents, anticoagulants, or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients with any significant renal, hepatic, or hematologic disorder.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Pregnant or nursing females and women of childbearing potential not using reliable contraception.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>History of severe hemorrhagic conditions or myocardial infarction within the last 3 months.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    
    <Endpoints>
        <PrimaryEndpoint>Change in walking distance to onset of claudication from baseline to 24 weeks, measured through a standardized treadmill exercise test.</PrimaryEndpoint>
        <SecondaryEndpoints>
            <Endpoint>Incidence of major cardiovascular events including myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular-related death over the period of the study.</Endpoint>
            <Endpoint>Composite measure of safety including frequency and severity of adverse events reported during treatment.</Endpoint>
        </SecondaryEndpoints>
    </Endpoints>
    
    <StudyDesign>
        <Design>Patients will be randomly assigned to receive either cilostazol or placebo in a 1:1 ratio using a computer-generated randomization scheme. The study will be conducted over 24 weeks at multiple centers, with assessments occurring at baseline, 12 weeks, and 24 weeks.</Design>
        <MethodologicalRigor>Employing a double-blind design minimizes bias, ensuring that effects of treatment can be distinguished from placebo effects. The choice of a well-defined patient population enhances the applicability and relevance of outcomes.</MethodologicalRigor>
    </StudyDesign>
    
    <Rationale>
        <Reason>The successful precedents established by prior studies, such as the Cilostazol Stroke Prevention Study 2, affirm cilostazol’s therapeutic efficacy in reducing serious vascular events. Mechanistic insights reveal cilostazol's inhibition of smooth muscle cell proliferation, pivotal in the management of vascular conditions.</Reason>
        <Reason>This trial taps into cilostazol’s favorable pharmacodynamics via optimized dosing and safety protocols, addressing both efficacy and tolerability, thus directly enhancing its clinical applicability.</Reason>
        <Reason>The robust assessment of functional outcomes aligns closely with patient-centered goals in PAD management, rendering the study design methodologically sound and promising for patient impact.</Reason>
    </Rationale>
    
    <Outcomes>
        <Outcome>The study anticipates demonstrating a statistically significant improvement in both functional capacity and reduction in cardiovascular events in the cilostazol group as compared to placebo, based on thorough statistical analysis performed by independent evaluators.</Outcome>
    </Outcomes>
    
    <Conclusion>This clinical trial is poised to provide vital insights into the efficacy and safety of cilostazol in patients with peripheral artery disease, potentially positioning cilostazol as a dual-action therapeutic in this demographic, combining symptomatic relief with protective cardiovascular benefits.</Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
