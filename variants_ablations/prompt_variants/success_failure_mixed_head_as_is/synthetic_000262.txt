**Title:** Efficacy of Bosentan on Quality of Life and Functional Exercise Capacity in Patients with Symptomatic Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension: A Multi-Center, Double-blind, Randomized, Controlled Trial

**Study Overview:**  
This Phase 4 trial aims to assess the efficacy and safety of bosentan compared to placebo in improving quality of life and exercise capacity in symptomatic patients with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). By integrating a comprehensive patient-centric approach and utilizing established clinical endpoints, this study seeks to substantiate bosentan's therapeutic potential in this patient population.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Signed informed consent prior to initiation of any study-mandated procedures.  
2. Adults (≥ 18 years of age) with a confirmed diagnosis of symptomatic PAH (WHO Group I), as corroborated by right heart catheterization within the last three months.  
3. Mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) ≥ 25 mmHg and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) ≤ 15 mmHg.  
4. Documented 6-minute walk test (6MWT) distance between 150 meters and 450 meters at screening.  
5. Patients must exhibit a positive response to acute vasodilator testing during the right heart catheterization, defined as a decrease in mPAP of at least 10 mmHg.  
6. Patients who have not undergone prior treatment with endothelin receptor antagonists (ERAs) or other advanced therapies for PAH within the last 12 weeks.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. PAH due to significant other causes (WHO Groups II-V), including but not limited to connective tissue diseases or portal hypertension.  
2. Patients with severe uncorrected hypoxia, systemic hypertension, or other significant comorbidities that may interfere with study participation.  
3. Acutely decompensated heart failure or unstable angina in the three months preceding enrollment.  
4. Pregnant or nursing women, or those not using reliable contraception.  
5. History of hypersensitivity reactions related to bosentan or its components.  
6. Patients with hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B or C) or elevated liver enzymes (AST or ALT > 1.5 times the upper limit of normal).  

**Study Design:**  
This multicenter, double-blind, randomized, controlled trial will involve approximately 250 patients, randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either bosentan (62.5 mg b.i.d. for 4 weeks, followed by 125 mg b.i.d.) or placebo for a total treatment duration of 24 weeks. The study outlines specific parameters allowing for real-time assessment of patient adherence and therapy outcomes, akin to the COMPASS 3 study design. Patients will be monitored closely for adherence to ensure robust engagement, echoing previous successful trials that highlighted the importance of adherence in achieving clinical outcomes.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
The primary endpoint will be the change in quality of life, as measured by the World Health Organization Quality of Life-BREF (WHOQOL-BREF) scale, from baseline to 24 weeks. Secondary endpoints include the change in exercise capacity assessed by the 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) at baseline and at 24 weeks, along with safety and tolerability metrics via adverse event reporting.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
The data will be analyzed using an intention-to-treat approach. Changes from baseline to endpoint will be evaluated with mixed-effects models to accommodate repeated measures. A p-value < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Survival analysis using Kaplan-Meier methods will be engaged to assess time to first morbidity or mortality event, reinforcing evaluation criteria established in preceding studies.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
It is anticipated that bosentan will significantly improve quality of life and exercise capacity, thus corroborating its role as a viable therapy for symptomatic PAH. Enhanced patient-selection methodologies paired with a structured monitoring strategy are projected to yield higher response rates, supported by mechanistic insights into bosentan's effects on endothelial function, which have been previously confirmed in successful trials.

**Conclusions:**  
This trial will provide pivotal insights into the therapeutic benefits of bosentan in symptomatic PAH, enhancing stratification of treatment responses, and potentially establishing new efficacy benchmarks within advanced PAH therapies. Enhancing patient adherence and utilizing subjective and objective endpoints are critical components of this study design, reflecting the operational modifications in successful prior interventions involving bosentan.
