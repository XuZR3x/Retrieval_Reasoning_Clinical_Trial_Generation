**Study Title**: A Phase II, Multicenter, Open-Label Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Pomalidomide Combined with Dexamethasone and Anti-CD38 Monoclonal Antibody in Patients with Relapsed/Refractory Multiple Myeloma with High-Risk Cytogenetic Features

**Background**: Multiple myeloma (MM) remains a challenge due to its heterogeneity and propensity for relapse. High-risk cytogenetic features such as del(17p) or t(4;14) are linked to poor prognosis and highlight the need for more effective therapeutic approaches. Pomalidomide, a next-generation immunomodulatory drug, has shown promise in enhancing therapeutic efficacy when combined with other anti-myeloma agents. By leveraging combination therapy synergy, this study investigates the efficacy and safety of pomalidomide, dexamethasone, and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody (daratumumab).

**Study Design**: This Phase II, multicenter, open-label study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a triplet regimen of pomalidomide (4 mg/day), dexamethasone (40 mg weekly), and daratumumab (16 mg/kg) administered intravenously once per week for the first two cycles, followed by every 2 weeks until progression in patients with relapsed or refractory MM exhibiting high-risk cytogenetic features.

**Primary Objective**: To determine the overall response rate (ORR) of the triplet regimen in relapsed/refractory MM patients with high-risk cytogenetic features after 12 treatment cycles.

**Secondary Objectives**:
1. To assess progression-free survival (PFS) from the start of treatment.
2. To evaluate the safety profile of the combination regimen, focusing on adverse event incidence and severity.
3. To analyze patient-reported outcomes and quality of life through validated questionnaires.
4. To investigate predictive biomarkers associated with response using gene expression profiling pre- and post-treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
1. Adults (≥ 18 years) with a confirmed diagnosis of active relapsed/refractory MM.
2. Presence of high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities (del(17p) or t(4;14)).
3. Measurable disease defined as serum M-protein ≥ 0.5 g/dL or urine M-protein ≥ 200 mg/24 hours.
4. Patients must be refractory to at least one prior line of therapy, including lenalidomide.
5. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
6. Adequate organ function, including liver, renal, and hematopoietic reserve.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Prior exposure to pomalidomide or daratumumab.
2. Active infection requiring systemic therapy.
3. Uncontrolled medical conditions, including active cardiovascular disease.
4. Concurrent malignancies except for basal cell carcinoma or localized cancers.
5. Significant peripheral neuropathy ≥ Grade 2.

**Treatment Regimen**:
- Pomalidomide: 4 mg orally on days 1-21 of a 28-day cycle.
- Dexamethasone: 40 mg orally weekly.
- Daratumumab: 16 mg/kg intravenously once per week for the first two cycles, followed by every two weeks for up to 12 cycles.

**Efficacy Assessment**: 
Tumor response will be assessed using the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) criteria, including complete response (CR), very good partial response (VGPR), and partial response (PR) rates determined at baseline and at the end of each treatment cycle.

**Safety Monitoring**: 
Adverse events will be recorded and graded according to National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Laboratory testing will include monitoring of blood counts, liver enzymes, and renal function at baseline and throughout the study.

**Outcome Measures**:
- Primary Outcome: Overall response rate (CR+VGPR+PR) after 12 cycles of treatment.
- Secondary Outcomes: Progression-free survival, overall survival, adverse events, and quality of life scores at baseline and post-treatment.

**Statistical Analysis**: The proportion of subjects achieving an ORR will be evaluated using descriptive statistics. PFS will be estimated by the Kaplan-Meier method, with comparisons made via log-rank tests as appropriate.

**Ethics and Compliance**: All participants will provide written informed consent prior to enrollment, and the trial will adhere to Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki.

**Conclusion**: This study is designed to maximize the therapeutic index through strategic patient selection and combination therapy with pomalidomide, dexamethasone, and anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody. The anticipated results will contribute significant insights towards establishing an effective treatment regimen for patients with high-risk relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, potentially improving patient outcomes in this challenging disease subset.
