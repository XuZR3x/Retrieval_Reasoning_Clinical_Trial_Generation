**Title**: Aspirin and Cognitive Function in Older Adults with Mild Cognitive Impairment: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial

**Objective**: This clinical trial aims to investigate the efficacy of aspirin in improving cognitive function among older adults diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment (MCI). The primary objective is to assess whether aspirin administration can slow cognitive decline as measured by standardized neuropsychological assessments.

**Design**: This study is designed as a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial comprising two parallel groups. A total of 200 participants aged 65 years and older, diagnosed with MCI based on the National Institute on Aging-Alzheimer's Association criteria, will be enrolled. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either a daily dose of 100 mg of aspirin or matching placebo for a duration of 12 months.

**Background**: Emerging evidence suggests that neuroinflammation plays a critical role in the progression of cognitive decline in aging populations. Aspirin, with its known anti-inflammatory properties, may mitigate neuroinflammatory processes and thereby offer neuroprotective effects. The trial hypothesizes that regular aspirin intake will significantly enhance cognitive performance while preserving functional independence.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
1. Age ≥ 65 years.
2. Clinical diagnosis of mild cognitive impairment as per established criteria.
3. Ability to provide informed consent.
4. Established ensurement of stability in comorbid conditions.
5. No history of major neurological disorders or acute psychiatric illnesses.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
1. Contraindications for aspirin use (e.g., active peptic ulcer disease, bleeding disorders).
2. Use of other anti-inflammatory drugs or anticoagulants within 30 days prior to enrollment.
3. Major depressive disorder or other significant psychiatric conditions.
4. Cognitive impairment secondary to other diseases.
5. Pregnant or nursing women.

**Primary Outcome Measures**: Cognitive function will be assessed using the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive Subscale (ADAS-Cog) at baseline, 6, and 12 months post-randomization. Secondary outcomes will include assessments of activities of daily living and the quality of life measured through the EQ-5D and the Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADL) scale.

**Statistical Analysis**: The data will be analyzed using intention-to-treat principles. Comparisons between the two groups will employ repeated measures ANOVA to evaluate changes over time with respect to cognitive outcomes, taking into account baseline performance as a covariate.

**Safety Monitoring**: Regular safety assessments will be conducted at each follow-up visit, with an emphasis on monitoring for gastrointestinal side effects and any signs of bleeding. Periodic blood tests will assess platelet counts and coagulation profiles.

**Justification for Success**: The success of this trial is predicated on prior studies demonstrating the importance of trial rigor in mitigation of biases and enhancing the reliability of results. The rigorous double-blind methodology will ensure that participant expectations cannot influence outcomes, fulfilling the requirement for robust clinical evidence. As seen in previous successful trials of aspirin’s role in modulating inflammatory markers and physiological parameters, this investigation aims to bridge gaps in current understanding regarding aspirin’s neuroprotective potential.

In summary, the trial aims to expand on existing literature by delineating aspirin's impact within the context of cognitive health, ultimately aiming to establish a straightforward, widely accessible intervention for older adults at risk of cognitive decline. The implications for public health could be substantial, given the looming increase in the elderly population worldwide and the associated burden of cognitive impairments. 

**Expected Outcomes**: The successful completion of this trial may contribute valuable insights into adjunctive therapies in MCI management, potentially advocating for aspirin's use as a preventive measure in the trajectory of cognitive decline amongst older adults in various clinical settings.
