**Title**: Efficacy of Varenicline Combined with Digital Therapeutics for Smoking Cessation: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Rationale**: With growing evidence for the need for integrated solutions in smoking cessation, this trial aims to evaluate the effectiveness of varenicline combined with a digital behavioral support application in enhancing smoking cessation rates among adults with a history of unsuccessful quit attempts. 

**Primary Objective**: To investigate the efficacy of varenicline in combination with a digital therapeutic intervention (DTI) as compared to varenicline alone in achieving continuous abstinence from smoking over a 24-week period.

**Study Design**: This is a parallel-group, double-blind, randomized controlled trial.

**Participants**: Smokers aged 18 years or older, with a documented smoking history of 10 or more cigarettes per day over at least the past year, will be recruited. 

**Methods**: Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either varenicline plus DTI (Group A) or varenicline plus a control digital application providing generic health information (Group B). Both groups will initiate a 12-week treatment of varenicline, beginning with a 1 mg/day dosage for the first week, followed by a 2 mg/day dosage for the subsequent 11 weeks. The DTI will provide personalized behavioral interventions, including goal setting, motivational messaging, and continuous feedback. Study visits are scheduled at baseline, Week 2, Week 6, Week 12, and Week 24, during which continuous support and monitoring will be provided.

**Outcomes**: The primary outcome is defined as continuous abstinence from smoking, assessed by self-reports and confirmed by expired carbon monoxide (CO) levels <10 ppm, measured at Week 12 and Week 24. Secondary outcomes will include withdrawal symptom severity, quality of life assessments using the St. George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ), and adherence to the treatment protocol as measured by pill counts and app engagement metrics.

**Sample Size**: A total of 300 participants (150 per group) will be needed to detect a significant difference in quitting rates at a power of 80% and an alpha level of 0.05, based on expected continuous abstinence rates of 30% in the DTI group versus 15% in the control group.

**Statistical Analysis**: Continuous variables will be analyzed using t-tests, while categorical variables will be examined using chi-squared tests. A logistic regression analysis will be performed to evaluate the association between treatment groups and the primary outcome, adjusting for baseline characteristics.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
- Adult smokers aged ≥18 years. 
- Smoked an average of at least 10 cigarettes per day over the past year. 
- Motivated to quit smoking and willing to engage with both the pharmacological and digital intervention.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Contraindications for varenicline use (e.g., history of seizures, active psychiatric disorders).
- Recent participation in a smoking cessation clinical trial within the last 3 months.
- Current use of nicotine replacement therapies or other smoking cessation medications.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Monitoring and Support**: Comprehensive follow-up will include bi-weekly check-ins via digital platforms, as well as in-person consultations at designated study visits. This continuous interaction fosters a supportive environment, reinforcing motivational strategies that enhance treatment adherence.

**Expected Benefits**: The integration of digital therapeutics into smoking cessation treatment protocols may improve engagement and treatment adherence, aiming to optimize outcomes for smokers who are unable to quit using pharmacological methods alone. 

This study aims to elucidate the multifactorial aspects of smoking addiction and the potential for a combined approach to address both psychological and physiological components of smoking cessation.
