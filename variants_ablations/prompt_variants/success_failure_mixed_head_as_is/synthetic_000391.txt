**Study to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of Sildenafil Citrate in Combination with Digitalis Glycosides in the Management of Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension**

A Multi-Center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Parallel Group Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Sildenafil Citrate (20 mg TID) When Used in Combination with Digitalis Glycosides in the Treatment of Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension in Subjects Aged 18 Years and Over.

**Objectives:**  
To determine the therapeutic efficacy and safety of sildenafil citrate in conjunction with digitalis glycosides among patients diagnosed with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH).

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Males and females aged 18 years and older diagnosed with PAH (idiopathic or secondary to connective tissue disease).
- A baseline six-minute walk test (6MWT) showing a distance between 100 to 450 meters.
- Patients must exhibit a mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) ≥ 25 mmHg and a pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) ≤ 15 mmHg as confirmed by right heart catheterization performed within 12 weeks prior to randomization.
- Patients must be naïve to treatment or have undergone a washout from other PAH therapies for at least 30 days prior to screening.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients with significant co-morbid conditions limiting their ability to engage in the 6MWT (e.g., severe musculoskeletal disorders).
- Current use of other medications for PAH management, including other phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors, endothelin receptor antagonists, or prostacyclin therapy.
- Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to sildenafil citrate, digitalis glycosides, or other investigational drugs.

**Endpoints:**  
The primary endpoint is the change from baseline in the total distance walked during the 6-minute walk test (6MWT) at Week 24. Secondary endpoints include the assessment of hemodynamic measurements, clinical safety parameters, and quality of life assessments via standardized patient questionnaires.

**Study Design:**  
This clinical trial is designed as a multi-center effort involving multiple sites internationally to ensure a diverse patient population. A total of 300 participants will be enrolled and randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either sildenafil citrate in combination with digitalis or placebo in conjunction with digitalis, ensuring a rigorous double-blind methodology to mitigate bias.

**Rationale for Study Design:**  
Prior successful studies demonstrated that sildenafil citrate significantly improves hemodynamic parameters and exercise capacity. The integration of digitalis glycosides in a regimen is hypothesized to enhance myocardial contractility and improve clinical outcomes in PAH patients while simultaneously benefitting from the synergistic vasodilatory action of sildenafil. Through carefully defined inclusion criteria, this trial seeks to target the specific PAH patient population most likely to respond to combination therapy. Moreover, rigorous randomization and a double-blind study design have been implemented to produce reliable, generalizable data while reducing bias—a crucial aspect highlighted in previous successful sildenafil trials.

**Assessment Strategies:**  
The efficacy of treatment will be evaluated using the 6MWT as the primary measure, alongside other clinically relevant assessments such as echocardiographic parameters and patient-reported outcomes. The implementation of standardized assessment tools will ensure that observed treatment effects are quantifiable and clinically meaningful, paralleling methodologies employed in prior successful trials using sildenafil for PAH.

**Conclusion:**  
This trial aims to establish sildenafil citrate's efficacy and safety when used with digitalis glycosides in the targeted management of pulmonary arterial hypertension. The outcomes of this study could significantly enhance therapeutic strategies for PAH, establishing concrete evidence for the utility of combination therapy in this patient population.
