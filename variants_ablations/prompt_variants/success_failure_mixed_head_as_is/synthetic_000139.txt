**Title:** A Phase II Clinical Trial Evaluating the Efficacy of Low-Dose Interleukin-2 in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Advanced Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Head and Neck

**Abstract:** This Phase II study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of low-dose interleukin-2 (IL-2) in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors in patients with unresectable advanced squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck (SCCHN). Despite previous evidence suggesting that IL-2 may enhance anti-tumor immunity, this trial hypothesized that the combined approach would improve overall survival and reduce local recurrence rates. 

**Background:** Immune checkpoint inhibitors have demonstrated significant efficacy in SCCHN; however, the high rates of disease relapse underscore the need for novel adjunctive therapies. IL-2, a critical regulator of T-cell activation and proliferation, was posited to enhance the therapeutic efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors through potentiation of systemic immune responses. Therefore, this trial was designed to assess the impact of low-dose IL-2 on treatment outcomes in this patient cohort.

**Study Design:** This multicenter, randomized, controlled Phase II trial enrolled 240 patients with unresectable advanced SCCHN after progressive disease while on Standards of Care, including at least one regimen of immune checkpoint inhibition. Patients were allocated to receive either low-dose IL-2 subcutaneously (1 million IU/m²) five times a week combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors (nivolumab or pembrolizumab) or the control group receiving only immune checkpoint inhibitors. The treatment duration was set for 6 cycles.

**Primary Outcome Measures:** The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS) at 12 months from the start of therapy. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), overall response rate (ORR), and assessment of immune-related adverse events.

**Eligibility Criteria:**
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  - Histologically confirmed SCCHN at advanced unresectable stages (III/IV)
  - Prior treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors
  - Measurable disease per RECIST criteria
  - Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal functions
  - Age ≥ 18 years
  - No significant autoimmune disorders or intercurrent illnesses

- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Active infections requiring systemic treatment
  - Recent steroid use or any immunosuppressive therapies
  - Prior exposure to IL-2 within 6 months of trial initiation
  - Secondary malignancies, other than skin cancers
  - Female patients who are pregnant or lactating
  
**Results:** The trial faced significant challenges leading to its early termination due to adverse outcomes that contributed to its failure:
1. **Insufficient Therapeutic Window:** Despite initial hypotheses, the low-dosing of IL-2 did not stimulate an adequate T regulatory and natural killer cell response. Consequently, this led to ineffective control of tumor progression.
  
2. **Adverse Immune Reconstitution:** Combo therapy with immune checkpoint inhibitors demonstrated adverse interactions, diminishing the anticipated immune-boosting effects of IL-2, leading to deterioration in patient immune profiles and subsequently lower response rates than anticipated.

3. **Limited Systemic Absorption:** Subcutaneous administration did not result in sufficient systemic distribution of IL-2, failing to evoke a robust anti-tumor response against non-injected lesions, which ultimately affected both local and distant control of disease.

4. **Tumor Microenvironment Adaptations:** Tumors employed adaptive resistance mechanisms that were not counteracted by the combination therapy, resulting in unmanageable disease progression despite treatment adherence.

5. **Patient-Related Factors:** Significant variability in baseline immune status among patients contributed to heterogeneous therapeutic responses, highlighting the importance of understanding individual patient profiles in immunotherapy management.

**Conclusion:** The trial results exhibited no significant difference in overall survival rates between the intervention and control groups, leading to the conclusion that the combination of low-dose IL-2 with immune checkpoint inhibitors was not effective in improving clinical outcomes for patients with unresectable SCCHN. Future studies should consider optimizing IL-2 dosing and timing, alongside more comprehensive patient stratification based on immune status prior to therapeutic intervention.
