**Hydrocortisone in the Treatment of Postoperative Delirium in Elderly Patients: A Phase II Randomized Controlled Trial**

**Background:** Postoperative delirium (POD) is a common and serious complication in elderly surgical patients characterized by acute confusion and cognitive dysfunction, which can lead to prolonged hospitalization and increased morbidity. The inflammatory response to surgery may play a role in POD, suggesting that hydrocortisone, with its anti-inflammatory properties, could mitigate this response and subsequently lower the incidence of delirium in this vulnerable population.

**Objective:** To evaluate the efficacy of hydrocortisone in reducing the incidence of postoperative delirium in elderly patients undergoing elective orthopedic surgery.

**Design:** This study is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Patients will be stratified by age and comorbidities to ensure a balanced representation across treatment groups, aligning with the reasoned approach to cohort specificity that improves external validity.

**Intervention:** A total of 200 participants aged 65 years and older will be enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either intravenous hydrocortisone (200 mg over 24 hours) or placebo starting 24 hours prior to surgery until 48 hours postoperative. This dosing regimen is justified by the pharmacokinetic profile of hydrocortisone, supporting its targeted action to modulate inflammatory response without significant systemic side effects.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged ≥ 65 years.
- Scheduled for elective orthopedic surgery (hip or knee arthroplasty).
- Ability to provide informed consent either directly or through a legally authorized representative.
- Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score ≥ 24.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of or current cognitive impairment (e.g., dementia, Parkinson's disease).
- Use of corticosteroids in the past three months.
- Acute infection requiring hospitalization.
- Presence of significant uncontrolled comorbid conditions (e.g., severe cardiac, hepatic, or renal disease).
- Known allergy to hydrocortisone or its components.

**Primary Endpoint:** Incidence of postoperative delirium as assessed by the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) within 72 hours post-surgery.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Duration of delirium (if present).
- Length of hospital stay.
- Postoperative cognitive assessment using the MMSE at baseline and at discharge.
- Patient-reported outcomes on quality of life assessed by the EQ-5D scale at baseline, 1 month, and 3 months after surgery.

**Statistical Analysis:** The primary analysis will utilize intention-to-treat methodology, with comparisons made using chi-square tests for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous variables. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Ethical Considerations:** The study protocol has been reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board at participating sites, following the Declaration of Helsinki guidelines. Informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

This clinical trial is designed to rigorously assess the hypothesis that hydrocortisone can effectively reduce the incidence of postoperative delirium in elderly patients, leveraging the pharmacological understanding of its effects on inflammation, and utilizing a robust methodological framework to ensure reliability and validity of the findings. The expected outcomes are anticipated to provide valuable insights into therapeutic strategies that enhance recovery and cognitive outcomes in vulnerable surgical populations.
