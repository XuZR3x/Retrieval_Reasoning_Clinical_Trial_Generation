**Study Title**: A Phase III Multicenter, Randomized Trial of BIBW 2992 (Afatinib) Versus Docetaxel in Patients with EGFR-Positive Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer After Failure of First-Line Platinum-Based Chemotherapy

**Study Identifier**: BIBW-2992-NSCLC-003

**Study Design**: This is a Phase III, open-label, multicenter, randomized trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of BIBW 2992 compared to docetaxel in patients with metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring EGFR activating mutations who have progressed following first-line platinum-based chemotherapy.

**Primary Objective**: To determine the objective response rate (ORR) of BIBW 2992 as compared to docetaxel in patients with EGFR-positive metastatic NSCLC, as assessed by independent radiological review according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**Secondary Objectives**: 
1. To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) between the two treatment groups.
2. To assess the safety profile and adverse events associated with BIBW 2992 compared to docetaxel.
3. To evaluate the impact of specific EGFR gene mutations on treatment response and outcomes.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of metastatic NSCLC.
2. Presence of documented activating mutations in the EGFR gene (exon 19 deletions or exon 21 L858R substitution) verified by molecular analysis.
3. Must have progressed after a standard first-line platinum-based chemotherapy regimen.
4. At least one measurable lesion according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.
5. Patients aged 18 years or older with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1.
6. Provision of written informed consent in accordance with ICH-GCP guidelines.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. More than one prior chemotherapy regimen for metastatic disease.
2. Prior treatment with any EGFR-targeted agents other than those included in first-line therapy.
3. Documented history of interstitial lung disease or severe pulmonary fibrosis.
4. Active brain metastases or symptomatic central nervous system involvement.
5. Clinically significant cardiovascular disease as determined by the investigator.
6. Any concurrent malignancies, except for adequately treated non-melanomatous skin cancer.

**Randomization**: Patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either:
- Arm A: BIBW 2992 orally at a dose of 40 mg once daily.
- Arm B: Docetaxel administered intravenously at a dose of 75 mg/m² every three weeks.

**Endpoints**: 
- **Primary Endpoint**: Objective response rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients with a confirmed complete response (CR) or partial response (PR).
- **Secondary Endpoints**: PFS measured from randomization to disease progression or death, overall survival from randomization to death from any cause, and safety assessed through the frequency and severity of adverse events as per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0.

**Statistical Considerations**: Efficacy analyses will be conducted using intention-to-treat methods. A total sample size of 300 patients (150 per arm) will provide adequate power to detect a statistically significant difference in ORR, assuming a two-sided alpha of 0.05.

**Rationale for Design**: Based on the successful outcomes observed in clinical trials LUX Lung 2 and LUX Head & Neck, the trial capitalizes on the specific selection criteria to ensure that only those patients with documented EGFR mutations that respond well to BIBW 2992 are included. Furthermore, the robust methodologies regarding objective response assessment will parallel those previously validated in successful trials, thus enhancing the relevance and applicability of findings. By utilizing an open-label design, the study aims to foster a comprehensive understanding of treatment efficacy and safety, while also extending treatment options to a population with a significant unmet clinical need.

**Monitoring**: The trial will include ongoing safety monitoring by an independent data safety monitoring board (DSMB) to ensure patient safety and adherence to clinical endpoints.

**Conclusion**: This trial is designed to validate the therapeutic impact of BIBW 2992 on EGFR-positive NSCLC, exploring its potential as a superior alternative to existing standard therapies post-first-line chemotherapy. The integration of molecular profiling to inform treatment decisions thickens the prospects for personalized medicine in oncology, ultimately aiming to optimize patient outcomes and enhance therapeutic benefits within this demographic.
