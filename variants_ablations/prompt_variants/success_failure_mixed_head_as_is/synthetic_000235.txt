**Failed Clinical Trial of Romidepsin: Romidepsin in Combination with Targeted Therapies for Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Title:** A Phase II Study Evaluating Romidepsin in Combination with Targeted Therapies in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**RATIONALE:** Romidepsin, a histone deacetylase inhibitor, is hypothesized to enhance the therapeutic activity of targeted agents in advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). This phase II trial aimed to assess the therapeutic efficacy and safety of romidepsin in combination with targeted treatments such as epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) inhibitors. 

**OBJECTIVES:**
- To determine the confirmed objective response rate (complete and partial responses) in patients with advanced NSCLC treated with romidepsin in combination with targeted therapies.
- To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) among participants receiving the combination therapy.
- To assess the qualitative and quantitative toxicities of romidepsin when administered alongside targeted agents.

**STUDY DESIGN:** A multicenter randomized phase II trial in which patients were allocated to receive either romidepsin plus targeted therapy or the targeted therapy alone as a control.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced or metastatic NSCLC.
- Patients must have received at least one prior line of therapy including platinum-based chemotherapy.
- Presence of measurable disease as defined by RECIST criteria.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score of 0 or 1.
- Written informed consent obtained.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior treatment with romidepsin or any histone deacetylase inhibitors.
- Diagnosed with brain metastases, as patients with central nervous system involvement have a poor prognosis.
- Uncontrolled intercurrent illness including, but not limited to, active infections, symptomatic congestive heart failure, or cardiac arrhythmias.
- Concurrent use of immunosuppressive therapies or investigational agents that may confound the results.

**OUTLINE:** Eligible patients received intravenous romidepsin on days 1, 8, and 15 in a 28-day cycle, combined with the designated targeted therapy (e.g., erlotinib) based on randomized assignment. Treatment continued until disease progression or the emergence of unacceptable side effects. Follow-up evaluations were conducted every 6 weeks for the first year and then every 3 months.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** The study sought to enroll 100 patients within a 12-month period across multiple centers.

**RESULTS:** The clinical trial ultimately failed to achieve its primary endpoint of confirmed response rate. The anticipated enhanced efficacy of the combination therapy was not observed, primarily attributable to several key factors:

1. **Immunosuppressive Properties:** The immunosuppressive effects of romidepsin appeared to negate the expected benefits from simultaneous use of targeted therapies. The overall immune response was inadequate, leading to suboptimal tumor control.

2. **Aggressive Disease Characteristics:** The trial population exhibited a significant degree of heterogeneity, with several patients experiencing rapid disease progression characteristic of advanced NSCLC, thereby limiting the effectiveness of histone deacetylase inhibition.

3. **Dose-Related Toxicities:** High rates of adverse events were documented, as illustrated in CTCAE assessments. Dose reductions and discontinuations due to severe toxicities such as fatigue, thrombocytopenia, and dysgeusia negatively impacted treatment continuity and patient compliance.

4. **Lack of Biomarker Stratification:** The absence of comprehensive pharmacodynamic and biomarker assessments hindered the stratification of patients. This deficiency precluded the identification of subgroups more likely to benefit from romidepsin co-administration.

5. **Restrictive Patient Selection:** The stringent inclusion/exclusion criteria resulted in a limited and potentially unrepresentative cohort, failing to encompass patients with varying disease presentations, thus reducing external validity and generalizability of results.

**CONCLUSION:** The phase II trial failed to demonstrate a benefit from the combination of romidepsin with targeted therapies in advanced NSCLC. The identified reasons for therapeutic failure underscore the complexities associated with the clinical utilization of romidepsin in the context of advanced malignancies, necessitating a critical reevaluation of treatment strategies within this patient population.
