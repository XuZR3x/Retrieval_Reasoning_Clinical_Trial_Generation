**Title:** A Phase 3, Randomized, Double-Blind Study of Maraviroc in Combination With Dolutegravir and Etravirine in Treatment-Experienced Adult HIV-1 Patients: IMPACT Study

**Study Design:** This comprehensive Phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of maraviroc administered in combination with dolutegravir and etravirine in treatment-experienced adult patients living with HIV-1 infection exhibiting resistance to multiple antiretroviral classes.

**Purpose:** The primary purpose of the IMPACT study is to investigate the antiviral efficacy, safety profile, and immunological response of the combination therapy compared to a standard of care regimen consisting of two comparator regimens: dolutegravir/lamivudine and etravirine/ritonavir-boosted darunavir over a treatment period of 48 weeks.

**Participants:** The study will enroll 200 adult subjects aged 21 years or older with confirmed HIV-1 infection and documented evidence of treatment failure or resistance to at least two classes of antiretrovirals (i.e., NRTIs, NNRTIs, PIs). The participants must be naïve to maraviroc and be identified as harboring R5-tropic virus based on Trofile testing. Randomization will ensure balanced treatment groups while stratification based on baseline CD4+ counts (≥200 cells/mm³ vs. <200 cells/mm³) will allow for targeted analysis of treatment outcomes in clinically significant populations.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Male or female subjects aged ≥21 years at the time of screening.
2. Documented HIV-1 infection verified through serological testing.
3. Evidence of resistance to at least two classes of antiretrovirals and documented treatment failure defined as persistent plasma viral load above 1000 copies/ml despite an optimized regimen.
4. Subjects must possess R5-tropic HIV-1 as determined by a validated Trofile assay.
5. Signed informed consent form prior to any study-specific procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Current or past history of hypersensitivity or allergic reactions to maraviroc, dolutegravir, or etravirine.
2. Active opportunistic infections or AIDS-defining illnesses within 30 days prior to screening.
3. Treatment with any investigational drugs in the 30 days preceding study entry.
4. Pregnancy or breastfeeding, or lack of contraception in women of child-bearing potential.
5. Clinically significant hepatic, renal or hematological abnormalities at screening.

**Intervention:** Eligible participants will be randomly assigned in a 2:1 ratio to receive maraviroc (300 mg once daily), dolutegravir (50 mg once daily), and etravirine (200 mg twice daily) or the comparator regimens as outlined above. Study medications will be administered continuously for 48 weeks, with primary efficacy evaluations occurring at 24 and 48 weeks.

**Outcomes:** The primary efficacy endpoint is the proportion of participants achieving viral suppression (plasma HIV RNA <50 copies/ml) at week 48, assessed in an intent-to-treat analysis. Secondary endpoints include changes in CD4+ cell count, rates of treatment-emergent adverse events, and a comprehensive assessment of participant quality of life through validated questionnaires over the trial duration.

**Follow-up:** A detailed follow-up protocol will be enforced, comprising safety assessments at weeks 4, 12, 24, and 48, with an additional 12-week post-treatment safety assessment for late-onset adverse events and immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS).

**Data Analysis:** Data will be analyzed using appropriate statistical methods for efficacy and safety endpoints with interim analyses conducted at designated time points to allow for timely adjustments in trial management to enhance patient retention and engagement.

**Ethics and Safety:** This study will adhere to ethical standards as defined by the Declaration of Helsinki, with oversight from Institutional Review Boards (IRBs). Comprehensive safety monitoring will involve frequent evaluations of laboratory parameters and clinical symptoms to ensure participant welfare throughout the trial duration.

In conclusion, this phase 3 clinical trial aims to elucidate the role of maraviroc in a combination regimen tailored for treatment-experienced individuals with complex resistance patterns, leveraging careful patient selection and adaptive trial methodologies to bolster treatment success in a historically underserved population.
