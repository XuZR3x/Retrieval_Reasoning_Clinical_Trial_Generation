<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>A Phase III Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Interferon Beta-1a in Patients with Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis</Title>
    <Objective>This study aims to determine the efficacy and safety of interferon beta-1a compared to placebo in patients diagnosed with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS). The primary outcome is measured by the reduction in the annualized relapse rate (ARR) at 52 weeks.</Objective>
    
    <Design>
        <StudyType>Phase III</StudyType>
        <StudyDesign>Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled</StudyDesign>
        <Duration>52 weeks</Duration>
        <Centers>Multi-center, international</Centers>
    </Design>
    
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Patients aged 18-55 years</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Confirmed diagnosis of relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis, aligned with the McDonald criteria (2010)</Criterion>
        <Criterion>History of at least two clinical relapses within the last three years</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) score between 0 and 5.0</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients must be treatment-naïve regarding any immunomodulatory therapy</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Primary progressive or secondary progressive multiple sclerosis</Criterion>
        <Criterion>History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to interferon beta-1a or any component of the formulation</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Pregnancy or lactation status</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Comorbidities such as active infections or significant cardiovascular, hepatic, or renal disease</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Prohibited concurrent therapies including systemic corticosteroids and immunosuppressants during the study</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    
    <Methods>
        <Randomization>Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either interferon beta-1a (30 mcg subcutaneously three times weekly) or matching placebo, with both study groups blind to treatment.</Randomization>
        <Endpoints>
            <PrimaryEndpoint>
                <Measure>Annualized relapse rate (ARR) at 52 weeks</Measure>
            </PrimaryEndpoint>
            <SecondaryEndpoints>
                <Measure>Change from baseline in EDSS score</Measure>
                <Measure>Quality of life measures assessed using the MS Quality of Life-54 instrument</Measure>
                <Measure>Frequency of adverse events related to treatment</Measure>
            </SecondaryEndpoints>
        </Endpoints>
    </Methods>
    
    <Outcomes>
        <ExpectedOutcome>Interferon beta-1a is expected to demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in the ARR compared to placebo, thus confirming its efficacy in reducing relapses in RRMS patients.</ExpectedOutcome>
        <SafetyMonitoring>The safety profile will be closely monitored, with specific attention given to adverse events and laboratory abnormalities throughout the trial duration.</SafetyMonitoring>
    </Outcomes>
    
    <Rationale>
        The meticulous definition of inclusion criteria focusing on patients with a confirmed diagnosis of RRMS will help ensure that the trial investigates a population likely to benefit from the intervention. Utilizing a double-blind randomized design is expected to minimize bias, thereby reinforcing the credibility of the efficacy data. The multi-center approach is anticipated to enhance the generalizability of the findings across diverse patient backgrounds, while well-defined primary and secondary endpoints are set to ensure the assessment of clinically relevant outcomes pertinent to the quality of life of patients treated with interferon beta-1a.
    </Rationale>
</ClinicalTrial>
