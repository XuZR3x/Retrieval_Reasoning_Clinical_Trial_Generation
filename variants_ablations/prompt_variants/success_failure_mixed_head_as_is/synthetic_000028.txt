<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Multi-Center, Randomized Study Evaluating the Effect of Cilostazol on Cognitive Outcomes in Post-Stroke Patients</Title>
    
    <Objective>
        The primary goal of this study is to evaluate the effect of cilostazol on cognitive function improvement, as measured by the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), in patients with a history of ischemic stroke, in comparison to placebo.
    </Objective>
    
    <Background>
        Previous studies, including the Cilostazol Stroke Prevention Study 2, demonstrate cilostazol's efficacy in preventing recurrent cerebral infarction, suggesting it may also benefit cognitive outcomes through vasodilation and inhibition of platelet aggregation. This study aims to expand on its therapeutic applications by exploring cognitive outcomes in post-stroke patients, an area that has not been fully explored in prior research.
    </Background>
    
    <TrialDesign>
        This is a double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized, multi-center trial with parallel group design. Participants will be assigned to receive cilostazol 200 mg/day or placebo for a duration of 6 months.
    </TrialDesign>
    
    <Participants>
        Participants will consist of 400 ischemic stroke patients aged 45 to 85 years, who meet the inclusion criteria and provide written informed consent.
    </Participants>
    
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criteria>Stable medical condition for at least 3 months post-stroke, confirmed diagnosis of ischemic stroke via MRI or CT</Criteria>
        <Criteria>Age between 45 and 85 years</Criteria>
        <Criteria>No prior history of dementia or severe cognitive impairment</Criteria>
        <Criteria>No chronic liver or renal disease</Criteria>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criteria>Participants who had hemorrhagic stroke or are currently on anticoagulant therapy</Criteria>
        <Criteria>Participants with any contraindications for cilostazol</Criteria>
        <Criteria>Patients with uncontrolled hypertension or metabolic disorders</Criteria>
        <Criteria>Pregnant or lactating women</Criteria>
    </ExclusionCriteria>

    <Intervention>
        <InvestigationalProduct>Cilostazol 200 mg (100 mg twice a day)</InvestigationalProduct>
        <ConcomitantMedication>Placebo for the placebo group</ConcomitantMedication>
        <Duration>6 months</Duration>
    </Intervention>
    
    <OutcomeVariables>
        <PrimaryOutcomeVariable>
            <Description>Change in MoCA scores from baseline to post-treatment</Description>
        </PrimaryOutcomeVariable>
        <SecondaryOutcomeVariables>
            <Description>Changes in plasma levels of adipocytokines and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP)</Description>
            <Description>Quality of life assessment using the EQ-5D scale</Description>
            <Description>Incidence of recurrent strokes or major cardiovascular events</Description>
        </SecondaryOutcomeVariables>
    </OutcomeVariables>
    
    <StatisticalAnalysis>
        <Description>Intention-to-treat analysis will be employed. Comparisons between groups for primary and secondary outcome measures will be performed using mixed-effects models, accounting for potential confounders.</Description>
    </StatisticalAnalysis>
    
    <ExpectedOutcomes>
        This study anticipates that cilostazol will lead to significant improvements in cognitive outcomes post-stroke, as well as provide additional insights into its effects on inflammatory markers and overall cardiovascular health, ultimately demonstrating the broader therapeutic potential of cilostazol beyond stroke recurrence prevention.
    </ExpectedOutcomes>
</ClinicalTrial>
