**Failed clinical trial of qva149: Efficacy and Safety of QVA149 in Patients with Moderate to Severe COPD.**

*A Multi-center, Randomized, Parallel-group, Active-controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of QVA149 Compared to Standard Therapy in Patients with Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease*

The objective of this clinical trial is to evaluate the comparative efficacy and safety profile of the fixed-dose combination QVA149 against standard care therapies (tiotropium) in managing patients with moderate to severe COPD. This multicenter study is designed to measure the efficacy endpoints primarily focused on lung function parameter changes and the incidence of adverse events.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male and female adult patients aged ≥40 years.
- Established diagnosis of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) per the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease guidelines (GOLD 2021).
- Patients with a post-bronchodilator Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 Second (FEV1) of ≥ 30% and <80% of the predicted normal, with a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio <0.70.
- Documented smoking history of at least 10 pack years.
- Patients scoring ≥ grade 2 on the modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) dyspnea scale.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients diagnosed with asthma or other comorbid pulmonary diseases (e.g., interstitial lung disease).
- Recent exacerbation of COPD necessitating systemic corticosteroids or antibiotics within 6 weeks preceding the study.
- Any history of significant cardiovascular disease that may interfere with the assessment of efficacy and safety endpoints.
- Prior use of investigational drugs or participation in another clinical trial within the previous three months.
- Known history of long QT syndrome or other significant arrhythmias.

**Efficacy Endpoints:**
The primary efficacy endpoint is the change from baseline in Forced Expiratory Volume (FEV1) Area Under the Curve (AUC) over 24 hours (FEV1 AUC0-24h) following 12 weeks of treatment. Secondary endpoints will include improvement in patient-reported outcomes as measured by the COPD Assessment Test (CAT) score, as well as hospitalizations due to exacerbations of COPD.

**Safety Assessments:**
Safety will be monitored via the frequency of serious adverse events reported from enrollment up to 30 days post-treatment. Additionally, laboratory tests and regular vital sign checks will be performed to assess the medication's safety profile.

**Study Design and Statistical Analysis:**
This is a randomized, parallel-group study comparing QVA149 versus tiotropium over a 12-week treatment period with an anticipated enrollment of 400 subjects. The statistical analysis will employ an intention-to-treat approach to evaluate the primary efficacy endpoint, with significance set at p<0.05.

**Rationale for Anticipated Failure:**
1. **Study Design**: The simplistic parallel-group approach might lack the robustness required in assessing efficacy, which fails to capture variability in treatment response seen in chronic diseases like COPD.
2. **Competing Active Control**: With tiotropium being a well-established standard of care for COPD management, demonstrating significant differences in efficacy between QVA149 and this active comparator may prove challenging.
3. **Homogeneity in Population**: The narrow inclusion criteria may significantly limit generalizability, thus constraining the trial’s ability to prove treatment effects across diverse COPD phenotypes.
4. **Inflammatory Biomarker Monitoring**: Absence of pretreatment biomarker assessments such as hs-CRP or Eosinophil counts could mainly restrict insights into QVA149’s therapeutic mechanisms, resulting in inconclusive efficacy results.
5. **High Exclusion Rates**: The stringent exclusion criteria may result in an underrepresentation of diverse COPD phenotypes, potentially biasing efficacy results and diminishing external validity.

The combination of these factors, particularly the influence of established therapies, strict controls, and exclusion of a potentially representative population, may result in QVA149 failing to demonstrate significant efficacy over its comparative treatment. 

**Change From Baseline**: The primary analysis will assess differences in mean FEV1 AUC0-24h pre- and post-treatment, with a hypothesis that QVA149 will not outperform tiotropium, leading to an anticipated failure in meeting the primary endpoint for regulatory assessment.
