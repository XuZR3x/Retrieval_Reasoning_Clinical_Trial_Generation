**Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Telbivudine in Chronic Hepatitis B Patients with Compensated Cirrhosis: A Randomized, Controlled Trial Evaluating Long-term Outcomes

**Abstract:**  
This clinical trial aims to evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety of telbivudine as an antiviral therapy in patients with chronic hepatitis B (CHB) and compensated cirrhosis. This study is designed to provide insights into sustained virologic response and liver function improvement over a 156-week period.

**Study Design:**  
A multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled trial comparing telbivudine (600 mg/day) with a placebo in patients diagnosed with compensated chronic hepatitis B and cirrhosis. The trial seeks to assess the impact of telbivudine on HBV viral load, liver enzyme normalization, and quality of life. Secondary endpoints include the evaluation of safety profiles, incidence of serious adverse events, and patient adherence to treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18–75 years with compensated chronic hepatitis B
- Biopsy or imaging-confirmed evidence of compensated cirrhosis
- Serum HBV DNA level ≥ 5 log10 copies/mL
- ALT levels > 1.5 times the upper limit of normal
- Ability and willingness to provide informed consent

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnant or breastfeeding
- Coinfection with HCV, HDV, or HIV
- Previous treatment with any anti-HBV nucleoside or nucleotide analog
- History of liver transplantation
- Presence of unstable medical conditions unrelated to hepatitis B

**Primary Endpoint:**
- Proportion of participants achieving HBV DNA levels < 2 log10 copies/mL and normalization of ALT levels at 156 weeks.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Improvement in quality of life assessed by the Chronic Liver Disease Questionnaire (CLDQ)
- Safety analysis including the proportion of patients experiencing treatment-related adverse events
- Monitoring of liver function through Child-Turcotte-Pugh scores over the study duration

**Study Population:**  
A total of 700 patients were included from 20 sites across multiple countries, ensuring diverse demographic representation, thus enhancing the generalizability of findings. Patients were pre-stratified based on demographic and clinical characteristics to maintain a well-defined cohort representative of those experiencing decompensated chronic hepatitis B.

**Results:**  
In the final analysis after 156 weeks, telbivudine demonstrated a statistically significant advantage over placebo concerning the primary endpoint, with 63% of patients in the telbivudine group achieving the targeted HBV DNA suppression compared to 10% in the placebo group. Additionally, normalization of ALT levels was observed in 55% of patients receiving telbivudine versus 12% in the control cohort.

The trial further elucidated the safety profile of telbivudine, revealing a low incidence of serious adverse events, consistent with previous trials; most reported adverse events were mild and transient and manageable within a clinical setting. Efficacy in long-term outcomes was supported by sustained virologic response rates at 48 weeks post-therapy completion illustrating telbivudine's potential in chronic hepatitis B management, underpinning its role in clinical practice.

**Conclusion:**  
This trial corroborates the findings from previous studies of telbivudine targeting similar populations, validating its role as an effective antiviral agent in managing chronic hepatitis B with compensated cirrhosis. By demonstrating superior efficacy in HBV suppression and favorable safety, telbivudine is well-positioned as a first-line therapy, promoting adherence and improving long-term patient outcomes in clinical settings. Further long-term follow-up is warranted to assess the durability of these results and ongoing safety surveillance.
