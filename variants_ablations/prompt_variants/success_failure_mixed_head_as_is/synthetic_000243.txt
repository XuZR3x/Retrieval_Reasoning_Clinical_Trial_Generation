**Title: A Phase II Clinical Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of High-Dose Ibrutinib Combined with Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Relapsed/ Refractory Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL)**

**Objective:**  
The primary aim of this study is to determine the safety and efficacy of high-dose ibrutinib (up to 840 mg daily) in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in treating patients with relapsed or refractory CLL. Given the emergence of treatment resistance and the noted immunological compromise in a population previously treated with ibrutinib, this trial seeks to investigate the potential utility of combining high-dose ibrutinib and ICIs to overcome treatment failure.

**Study Design:**  
This is a Phase II, non-randomized, open-label clinical trial. Patients diagnosed with relapsed or refractory CLL will receive twice-weekly infusions of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor (ICI) in conjunction with high-dose ibrutinib administered orally. 

**Patient Population:**  
Approximately 120 patients will be enrolled, all of whom must have confirmed diagnosis of CLL or SLL and show evidence of relapse or treatment resistance defined by the 2008 International Workshop on Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia criteria. All subjects will have received at least one prior therapy including ibrutinib alone.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Age ≥ 18 years  
- Documented CLL or SLL, as per the 2008 IWCLL criteria  
- Relapsed or refractory disease after at least one prior therapy, including ibrutinib  
- ECOG Performance Status of 0-2  
- Adequate organ function: renal (creatinine clearance > 30 mL/min), hepatic (ALT and AST ≤ 2.5 x ULN)  
- Agree to protocol-defined use of effective contraception  
- Ability to provide informed consent

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- History of grade III or IV Graft-Versus-Host Disease (GVHD)  
- Known CNS involvement or Richter's transformation  
- History of autoimmune conditions, except for manageable conditions that do not require immunosuppressive therapies  
- Requirement for concomitant medications that are strong CYP3A inhibitors or inducers  
- Pregnant or lactating females  
- Chronic infections (e.g., active hepatitis B or C, HIV)

**Treatment Regimen:**  
Patients will concurrently receive high-dose ibrutinib (escalated to a maximum of 840 mg daily) along with an ICI (nivolumab or pembrolizumab) administered biweekly for six cycles. Following the ICI regimen, Ibrutinib will be continued until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**Outcome Measures:**  
The primary endpoint is the overall response rate (ORR) at 6 months. Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), incidence of adverse events categorized by CTCAE v5.0, and assessment of treatment-related toxicities. The PFS will be defined from the time of treatment initiation until disease progression or death, while OS will be tracked until the end of the study, or death from any cause.

**Rationale for Potential Failure:**  
1. **Treatment Resistance:** Due to the known high rates of acquired resistance observed in patients previously treated with single-agent ibrutinib and potential cross-resistance to the combination regimen, it stands to undermine any therapeutic benefit expected from the combined approach.
  
2. **Dose-Related Toxicities:** Previous trials have indicated that high doses of ibrutinib may lead to significant adverse effects, causing heightened toxicity rates and potentially leading to reduced patient compliance or early termination of treatment.

3. **Suboptimal Patient Selection:** Given the heterogeneous nature of the patient population and prior treatment histories, there may be a variable response, complicating the interpretability of outcomes. This variation might diminish the apparent efficacy of the combined therapeutic approach.

4. **Immunological Compromise Post-Transplant:** With many enrolled patients having undergone previous therapies or transplants, residual immunological deficits (such as GVHD or chronic immune suppression) may diminish potential benefits from the ICI/ibrutinib combination.

5. **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Interactions stemming from concomitant medications, especially those that affect the metabolism of ibrutinib via CYP3A4 or other pathways, may yield subtherapeutic levels of drug exposure or exacerbated side effects contributing to treatment failure. 

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Efficacy evaluations will employ the intention-to-treat approach, with comparisons made through Kaplan-Meier survival analyses for PFS and OS, alongside the Fisher’s exact test for ORR. A predetermined stopping rule will be in place for safety, evaluating excess toxicity and lack of efficacy based on interim analysis.

**Study End:**  
The study will be concluded once a defined number of patients achieve disease progression, excess toxicity is encountered beyond acceptable margins, or upon the predetermined completion date of three years post last patient enrollment.

**Ethics:**  
The study will adhere to the ethical standards outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and will be submitted for approval to the Institutional Review Board (IRB) at participating institutions. All patients will provide written informed consent prior to participation.
