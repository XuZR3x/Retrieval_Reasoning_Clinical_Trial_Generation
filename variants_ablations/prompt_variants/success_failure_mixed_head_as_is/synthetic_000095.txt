**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy of Ferric Carboxymaltose in the Management of Iron Deficiency Anemia in Patients Undergoing Hemodialysis**

**Trial Title:**  
Efficacy of Intravenous Ferric Carboxymaltose in Correcting Iron Deficiency Anemia in Patients Undergoing Hemodialysis: A Randomized, Open-label, Multicenter Study

**Background:**  
Hemodialysis (HD) patients frequently develop iron deficiency anemia (IDA) due to dialysis-related blood loss, the reduced erythropoietic response to erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs), and the chronic inflammatory state associated with chronic kidney disease (CKD). This study investigates the efficacy of intravenous ferric carboxymaltose (FCM) as a corrective and supportive treatment for IDA in hemodyalisis patients compared to the standard care of oral iron supplementation.

**Objectives:**  
The primary objective is to assess the efficacy of intravenous FCM in improving hemoglobin (Hb) levels from baseline to week 12 in patients with IDA undergoing hemodialysis. Secondary objectives include evaluating the safety profile of FCM and its impact on iron status markers (serum ferritin and transferrin saturation).

**Design:**  
A multicenter, randomized, open-label trial will be conducted across multiple dialysis centers. Eligible participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either FCM or oral ferrous sulfate (the control).

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adult patients aged ≥ 18 years receiving maintenance hemodialysis for at least 3 months.  
- Documented persistent IDA, defined by Hb levels < 11 g/dL and serum ferritin < 100 µg/L or transferrin saturation (TSAT) < 20%.  
- Ability to provide informed consent.  
- Stable comorbid conditions (if any) for at least 4 weeks prior to the study initiation.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Known hypersensitivity to ferric carboxymaltose or iron overload disorders.  
- Chronic inflammatory diseases, malignancies, or active infections as determined by clinical judgment.  
- Recent blood transfusion (within 3 months), ESA therapy, or parenteral iron therapy.  
- Liver dysfunction or significantly elevated liver enzymes (ALT/AST > 3x ULN).  
- Pregnant or lactating women.  

**Intervention:**  
Participants will receive a single dose of FCM (1000 mg) via slow intravenous infusion, delivered within 24 hours post dialysis session, compared to daily oral ferrous sulfate (200 mg) administered for the same duration (12 weeks).

**Endpoints:**  
- **Primary Efficacy Endpoint:** Change in hemoglobin concentration from baseline to week 12.  
- **Secondary Efficacy Endpoints:** Changes in serum ferritin, TSAT, quality of life (assessed via the Short Form Health Survey), and the number of participants requiring blood transfusions.  
- **Safety Endpoints:** Adverse events and laboratory assessments will be monitored throughout the trial.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline characteristics. The primary endpoint will be analyzed using ANCOVA to compare changes in hemoglobin concentration between groups, adjusting for baseline values. A p-value < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Conclusions:**  
This study aims to establish that the administration of intravenous ferric carboxymaltose will exhibit superior efficacy over oral iron in correcting IDA in hemodialysis patients. Previous studies have shown the advantages of intravenous FCM due to its rapid action and improved bioavailability, particularly beneficial in patients undergoing significant physiological stress. The design ensures a focused patient population, rigorous safety assessments, and standardized outcome measures, mitigating confounding variables and strengthening the validity of the findings.

Anticipated outcomes may lead to improved anemia management protocols and enhanced quality of life, confirming the clinical utility of FCM in this patient population.
