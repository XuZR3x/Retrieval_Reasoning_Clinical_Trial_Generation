Study Title: A Multi-Center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Risperidone for the Treatment of Acute Mania in Adolescents with Co-Morbid Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)

Objective: This clinical study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of risperidone in adolescents diagnosed with Bipolar I disorder who concurrently exhibit symptoms of Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD). Given the critical developmental phase of adolescents and the complexities of co-morbid conditions, this trial seeks to establish the therapeutic potential of risperidone in improving manic and hyperactive symptoms.

Methods:
Design: This is a multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study, anticipated to recruit approximately 120 adolescents aged 12 to 18 years diagnosed with Bipolar I disorder experiencing a manic episode with co-morbid ADHD. Subjects will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either risperidone or placebo.

Treatment Duration: Participants will undergo a treatment phase of 12 weeks. Risperidone will be initiated at a dose of 0.5 mg/day, titrated as needed to a maximum of 4 mg/day based on clinical response and tolerability assessed weekly. Plausible adjustment steps will consider both improvement in manic symptoms (as evaluated by the Young Mania Rating Scale [YMRS]) and the severity of ADHD symptoms measured by the ADHD Rating Scale (ADHD-RS).

Inclusion Criteria:
1. Adolescents aged 12 to 18 years.
2. Primary diagnosis of Bipolar I disorder, manic episode established by DSM-5 criteria.
3. Comorbid diagnosis of ADHD as per DSM-5 criteria.
4. YMRS score ≥ 20 indicating acute manic symptoms.
5. Parent/guardian consent and participant agreement to adhere to protocol assessments.

Exclusion Criteria:
1. Current treatment with other antipsychotic medications or mood stabilizers within 4 weeks prior to study entry.
2. History of severe adverse reactions to antipsychotic medications.
3. Presence of significant medical conditions that may interfere with the study outcomes.
4. Substance use disorder in the past 6 months.
5. Active suicidal ideation or behavior.

Primary Outcome Measures: The primary endpoint of this study is the change in the YMRS total score from baseline to 12 weeks in the risperidone-treated group compared to placebo, with a minimum clinically meaningful difference pre-defined as a reduction of ≥ 30% in YMRS scores.

Secondary Outcome Measures: Secondary endpoints will include changes in ADHD-RS total scores and the Clinical Global Impression Scale for Bipolar Disorder (CGI-BP). Safety and tolerability will be monitored throughout the study, with particular attention to treatment-emergent adverse events and metabolic parameters.

Assessment Schedule: Subjects will attend weekly visits for monitoring and assessment, where psychiatric evaluations will be conducted, and safety measures reviewed. Regular blood tests, EKGs, and weight assessments will be performed at baseline and every four weeks thereafter.

Statistical Analysis: Descriptive statistics will summarize demographic and baseline characteristics. Between-group differences will be analyzed using appropriate statistical methods (e.g., ANCOVA). Efficacy analyses will be conducted using the intention-to-treat approach.

Rationale for Study Design: This trial incorporates structured dosing regimens to minimize variability and enhance therapeutic efficacy, paralleling findings from prior successful trials that advocated for careful dose titration. The clear delineation of ADHD and manic symptoms through robust standardized assessment scales (YMRS and ADHD-RS) permits precise evaluation of treatment efficacy, reinforcing targeted interventions during a critical age window for behavioral development.

Conclusion: The integration of risperidone as a potential therapeutic intervention for adolescents with bipolar disorder and co-morbid ADHD thereby addresses an important clinical gap, facilitating understanding of treatment interactions in this unique patient population. Through rigorous methodology and objective assessment, this study aims to elucidate the role of risperidone in the management of complicated psychiatric presentations among adolescents, inspiring future therapeutic strategies in related domains.
