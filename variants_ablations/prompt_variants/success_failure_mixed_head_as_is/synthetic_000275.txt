**Title:** Apixaban for Prophylaxis of Venous Thromboembolism in Inpatients Undergoing Major Abdominal Surgery: A Phase III, Randomized, Double-Blind, Parallel-Group Study

**Objective:** This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of apixaban in preventing venous thromboembolism (VTE) in patients undergoing major abdominal surgery relative to standard prophylactic treatment with enoxaparin.

**Summary:** Major abdominal surgery poses a significant risk for the development of venous thromboembolism, necessitating effective prophylactic strategies. This randomized controlled trial will investigate the hypothesis that apixaban is superior to enoxaparin in the prevention of postoperative VTE while maintaining an acceptable safety profile.

**Study Design:** 
- **Type:** Phase III, Multi-center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Parallel-Group
- **Duration:** 6 months post-randomization
- **Sample Size:** 1,200 participants

**Primary Outcome:** The primary endpoint is the incidence of VTE, defined as symptomatic deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE) occurring within 30 days following surgery.

**Secondary Outcomes:** 
1. The rate of major bleeding episodes within the same time frame, monitored via stringent assessment protocols.
2. Mortality rates due to VTE-related complications or bleeding.
3. Quality of life measurements at baseline, 30 days, and 6 months post-surgery.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18-80 years undergoing major abdominal surgery at participating centers
- Anticipated hospital stay of at least three days 
- No evidence of active bleeding or high bleeding risk (e.g., prior history of major bleeding events)
- Informed consent obtained prior to randomization

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of venous thromboembolism (VTE) within the past three months
- Ongoing treatment with anticoagulants or antiplatelet agents (excluding standard-dose aspirin)
- Renal impairment with a calculated creatinine clearance of <30 mL/min
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding

**Randomization:** Participants will be assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either apixaban 2.5 mg orally twice daily or enoxaparin 40 mg subcutaneously once daily starting 12 hours prior to surgery and continuing for 14 days post-surgery.

**Monitoring and Data Collection:** A comprehensive framework for monitoring adverse events will be instituted. Adverse events will be classified according to International Society on Thrombosis and Hemostasis standards. 

**Follow-Up:** Patients will be monitored for VTE and bleeding complications at 30 days and 6 months post-surgery. The follow-up will also include quality of life assessments using validated questionnaires.

**Hypothesis:** The hypothesis is that apixaban will reduce the incidence of postoperative VTE compared to enoxaparin with a similar or lower rate of major bleeding complications, thereby enhancing the clinical effectiveness and safety in the surgical population.

**Significance:** This trial is designed with meticulous patient selection criteria based on high-risk populations, aligning with prior successful clinical trials of apixaban. The expected robust data from a comprehensive follow-up period will inform clinical practice guidelines for VTE prophylaxis in surgical patients. Employing a multi-disciplinary approach involving surgeons, hematologists, and clinical pharmacists will ensure a thorough management strategy alongside trial implementation.

**Conclusion:** Through the meticulous design and execution of this clinical trial, we aim to substantiate the therapeutic utility of apixaban in a high-need surgical population, potentially altering preventative strategies for venous thromboembolism in major abdominal surgery.
