**Failed Clinical Trial of Bevacizumab: Bevacizumab and Temozolomide in Patients with Recurrent or Progressive Glioblastoma Multiforme (GBM)**

**RATIONALE**: Bevacizumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), has been hypothesized to augment the efficacy of temozolomide, an alkylating agent, in the treatment of recurrent glioblastoma multiforme. However, preliminary clinical observations suggest that GBM's inherent resistance mechanisms significantly impair the therapeutic potential of this combination, potentially mitigating the anticipated survival benefits.

**PURPOSE**: The primary objective of this Phase III trial was to assess whether the addition of bevacizumab to a regimen of temozolomide leads to improved overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with recurrent or progressive GBM versus temozolomide monotherapy.

**OBJECTIVES**: 
- To investigate the overall survival and progression-free survival in patients receiving temozolomide in combination with bevacizumab compared to those receiving temozolomide alone.
- To evaluate the incidence of treatment-related adverse events, particularly focusing on grade 3+ non-hematologic toxicities.
- To analyze pharmacokinetic interactions between bevacizumab and temozolomide.

**OUTLINE**: This study was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind trial. Participating patients were stratified based on prior treatment history, Karnofsky performance status (KPS), and steroid use. Patients were randomized at a 1:1 ratio to receive either:
- Arm 1: Temozolomide 150-200 mg/m² orally for 5 days in a 28-day cycle combined with bevacizumab 10 mg/kg intravenously every two weeks.
- Arm 2 (control arm): Temozolomide single agent 200 mg/m² orally for 5 days in a 28-day cycle.

**Population**:
- Inclusion Criteria:
  - Histologically confirmed recurrent or progressive glioblastoma multiforme.
  - Measurable disease by MRI with a minimum lesion size of 10 mm in the longest axis.
  - KPS of 70 or greater.
  - Adequate hematologic and organ function as defined by protocol-specific criteria.

- Exclusion Criteria:
  - Prior treatment with avastin or anti-VEGF agents.
  - History of significant cardiovascular disease, including but not limited to uncontrolled hypertension (systolic BP > 150 mmHg) and myocardial infarction within the past 6 months.
  - Major surgery or trauma within 28 days prior to randomization.
  - Evidence of significant renal impairment or proteinuria (>2+ on dipstick) at screening.

**ASSESSMENTS**: Patients were monitored biweekly for adverse events including hypertension, proteinuria, and other treatment-related toxicities. Efficacy endpoints included OS and PFS, assessed using imaging studies at 8-week intervals.

**RESULTS**: 
- The trial was prematurely terminated due to high rates of progression among participants in both treatment arms. The overall median OS was noted to be statistically insignificant between groups, with no observable survival advantage for the bevacizumab combination.
- Treatment-related grade 3+ adverse events, predominantly hypertension and renal complications, were reported in 45% of patients receiving the combination therapy, which necessitated dose adjustments and treatment discontinuation in many cases.
- Pharmacokinetic analyses suggested potential alterations in temozolomide's clearance profiles among patients receiving concurrent bevacizumab, leading to decreased drug availability at the tumor site.

**CONCLUSION**: This Phase III trial did not demonstrate a survival benefit for the addition of bevacizumab to temozolomide in patients with recurrent glioblastoma multiforme. The lack of efficacy could be attributed to inherent tumor resistance, failure of the vascular normalization hypothesis, and adverse pharmacokinetic interactions leading to compromised treatment delivery. Furthermore, high treatment-related toxicities adversely impacted patient compliance and treatment continuity. Future investigations might explore alternative regimens or combinations tailored to GBM's biological characteristics for more favorable outcomes.
