**Title:** Multicenter Randomized Trial of Telbivudine Versus Placebo in Adults with Chronic Hepatitis B: Efficacy in Clinical Outcomes and Viral Suppression

**Study Design:** This is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted across multiple sites to evaluate the efficacy of telbivudine in adults diagnosed with chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection. The trial duration is planned for 52 weeks with an additional 24-week follow-up period to assess sustained virologic response (SVR).

**Background:** Despite the advancements in antiviral therapy for chronic hepatitis B, there remains a significant proportion of patients who do not achieve adequate viral suppression. Previous studies on telbivudine have demonstrated favorable outcomes in specific populations. This trial aims to broaden the understanding of telbivudine's efficacy within a wider adult population diagnosed with chronic HBV, including those with variances in liver function and disease severity.

**Objectives:** The primary objective is to compare the proportion of patients achieving undetectable HBV DNA levels (< 263 copies/mL) at week 52 between the telbivudine and placebo groups. Secondary objectives include assessing the changes in alanine transaminase (ALT) levels, improvement in liver function as measured by Child-Turcotte-Pugh (CTP) scores, and the safety profile of telbivudine.

**Participants:** A total of 800 adult participants aged 18-65 years with chronic hepatitis B infection are to be enrolled. Inclusion criteria are as follows: confirmed HBsAg positivity for at least 6 months, HBV DNA levels > 10^6 copies/mL, and ALT levels > 1.5 times the upper limit of normal. Exclusion criteria include pregnancy, co-infection with HIV or HCV, prior use of other antiviral therapy for HBV, and presence of severe concomitant illnesses.

**Intervention:** Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either telbivudine (600 mg/day) or a matching placebo for a duration of 52 weeks. Randomization will be stratified by baseline Child-Turcotte-Pugh score (CTP score < 8 or ≥ 8) and HBV genotype to ensure balanced groups regarding disease severity.

**Outcome Measurements:**
- **Primary Outcome:** Proportion of participants achieving HBV DNA < 263 copies/mL at week 52.
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Changes in ALT levels, improvement or stabilization of CTP scores, incidence of adverse events, and the overall health-related quality of life assessed through standard questionnaires.

**Statistical Analysis:** Efficacy will be measured using chi-square tests for proportions, and continuous variables will be analyzed using t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests as appropriate. Multivariate logistic regression will adjust for potential confounders to ascertain the independent effect of telbivudine on virologic outcomes.

**Subject Follow-up:** Patients will be monitored for a total of 76 weeks post-enrollment, with assessments occurring at baseline, 12 weeks, 24 weeks, 52 weeks, and 76 weeks. Data collection will include laboratory tests for HBV DNA, ALT levels, and CTP scores alongside a detailed evaluation of adverse events.

**Potential Implications:** This trial is anticipated to provide robust evidence on the effectiveness of telbivudine in a broader population of patients with chronic HBV infection, contributing to the evolving landscape of antiviral therapy and offering enhanced treatment protocols for managing HBV.

**Conclusion:** By properly stratifying patients according to baseline liver function and employing rigorous statistical methodologies, this study endeavors to clarify the role of telbivudine in achieving viral suppression and improving overall liver health in those affected by chronic hepatitis B.
