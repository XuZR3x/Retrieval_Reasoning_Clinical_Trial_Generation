Study of Plitidepsin in Combination with Monoclonal Antibodies for the Treatment of Relapsed/Refractory B-Cell Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma: A Multicenter, Phase II Clinical Trial

Objective: This Phase II multicenter clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of plitidepsin in combination with monoclonal antibodies (rituximab and obinutuzumab) in patients with relapsed or refractory B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL). The study hypothesizes that plitidepsin, which acts as a potent pro-apoptotic agent through the induction of apoptosis in neoplastic cells reliant on pro-survival signaling pathways, will demonstrate synergistic effects when administered alongside established monoclonal antibody therapies.

Design: This open-label, multicenter, sequential cohort trial utilizes a dose-escalation design to identify the optimal dosing schedule for the plitidepsin-monoclonal antibody combination. The primary endpoint will include the overall response rate (ORR), assessed by computed tomography (CT) scans at baseline and after every two cycles of treatment, in line with the Revised Response Criteria for Malignant Lymphoma.

Inclusion Criteria: 
1. Age ≥ 18 years.
2. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of relapsed or refractory B-cell NHL, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, or mantle cell lymphoma, based on local pathology review.
3. Previous treatment with at least two systemic therapies, including monoclonal antibody-based regimens.
4. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2.
5. Measurable disease according to clinical and radiological assessments as per International Workshop Criteria (IWC).
6. Sufficient organ function as evidenced by laboratory tests (hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL, absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1.0 x 10^9/L, creatinine clearance ≥ 40 mL/min, and transaminases ≤ 2.5 × upper limit of normal).

Exclusion Criteria: 
1. Prior administration of plitidepsin.
2. Active or uncontrolled infections.
3. Participation in another clinical trial within 30 days of enrollment.
4. Serious cardiac or pulmonary conditions impairing safety.
5. Known hypersensitivity to plitidepsin or any involved study drugs.

Results: The primary objective evaluating ORR will be compared against historical controls of patients receiving rituximab and/or obinutuzumab monotherapy. Secondary endpoints will include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), duration of response (DOR), and evaluation of safety based on the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) in accordance with the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE, version 5.0).

Rationale: The mechanistic rationale for incorporating plitidepsin in the treatment of B-cell NHL is well-supported by in vitro data showing enhanced apoptosis in lymphoma cells exhibiting hyperactivation of key pro-survival pathways. The combinatorial approach with monoclonal antibodies is anticipated to enhance therapeutic efficacy, utilizing complementary mechanisms of action that target malignant cells effectively. 

Conclusion: This clinical trial seeks to provide robust clinical evidence regarding the therapeutic complementarity and safety profile of plitidepsin when used in combination with established monoclonal antibodies in relapsed or refractory B-cell NHL. By identifying an innovative therapeutic strategy that leverages plitidepsin's apoptosis-inducing capacity alongside the specific targeting of B-cell malignancies, this study aims to address a critical unmet need in the treatment landscape for this patient population. Results will inform potential pathways for regulatory approval and clinical practice modifications based on demonstrated clinical outcomes, thereby translating scientific insights into tangible benefits for patients.
