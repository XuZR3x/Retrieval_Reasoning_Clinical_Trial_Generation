<clinicalTrial>
    <title>Randomized Phase II Study of Pemetrexed in Combination with Docetaxel in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Exhibiting Molecular Heterogeneity</title>
    <purpose>The aim of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of pemetrexed combined with docetaxel in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) characterized by diverse molecular profiles. This trial will analyze the correlation between tumor heterogeneity and response to treatment among participants.</purpose>
    
    <inclusionCriteria>
        <criterion>Histologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) at clinical stages IIIB or IV.</criterion>
        <criterion>Presence of at least one measurable tumor lesion as per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) 1.1.</criterion>
        <criterion>Aged 18 years or older, with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.</criterion>
        <criterion>Prior chemotherapy with a taxane is mandatory.</criterion>
        <criterion>Patients must provide written informed consent prior to study enrollment.</criterion>
    </inclusionCriteria>
    
    <exclusionCriteria>
        <criterion>Patients with previous treatment using pemetrexed or docetaxel.</criterion>
        <criterion>Known brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease.</criterion>
        <criterion>Severe comorbidities that may impair immune response or treatment tolerance, as determined by the investigator.</criterion>
        <criterion>Patients pregnant or breastfeeding.</criterion>
        <criterion>History of other malignancies within the last 5 years, except in situ carcinoma of the skin.</criterion>
    </exclusionCriteria>
    
    <primaryEndpoint>
        <endpoint>Overall survival rate at 12 months post-treatment initiation.</endpoint>
    </primaryEndpoint>
    
    <secondaryEndpoints>
        <endpoint>Objective response rate (ORR) assessed through serial imaging studies every 6 weeks for the first 6 months.</endpoint>
        <endpoint>Assessment of treatment-related adverse events, graded using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.</endpoint>
        <endpoint>Molecular profiling of tumor samples to identify associations between genetic markers and treatment response.</endpoint>
    </secondaryEndpoints>
    
    <observations>
        <observation>Enrollment faced significant challenges due to the inadequate sample size resulting in underrepresentation of heterogeneous subgroups, hindering sufficient statistical analysis.</observation>
        <observation>Subsequent analyses revealed substantial molecular diversity within the enrolled participants, leading to confounding interpretations regarding treatment efficacy.</observation>
        <observation>High rates of treatment-related adverse events were documented, particularly among patients with pre-existing comorbid conditions, further complicating the evaluation of pemetrexed and docetaxel's safety profile.</observation>
        <observation>Incomplete genetic characterization limited the ability to ascertain potential predictive biomarkers that may have influenced therapeutic outcomes.</observation>
        <observation>Timing of treatment relative to disease stage raised questions about the appropriateness of neoadjuvant rather than maintenance therapy, potentially affecting response rates and overall treatment effectiveness.</observation>
    </observations>
    
    <results>
        <result>Despite initial optimism for the combination regimen, the trial was terminated prematurely due to failure to achieve statistically significant improvements in overall survival and objective response rates.</result>
        <result>The analysis indicated that molecular heterogeneity and sample size limitations were definitive factors in the inability to demonstrate treatment efficacy.</result>
        <result>Overall, this trial reinforces the necessity for comprehensive molecular characterization and larger cohort studies in identifying suitable candidate populations who may benefit from pemetrexed therapy.</result>
    </results>
</clinicalTrial>
