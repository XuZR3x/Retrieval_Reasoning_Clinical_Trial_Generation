**Bevacizumab in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors for Treatment of Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: A Multicenter, Open-Label, Randomized Phase III Trial**

**RATIONALE:** Immune checkpoint inhibitors have revolutionized the therapeutic approach towards advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Previous studies indicate that the combination of anti-angiogenic agents like bevacizumab may potentiate the effects of immunotherapy by altering the tumor microenvironment, enhancing immune surveillance, and counteracting tumor-induced immunosuppression. This trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of bevacizumab coupled with immune checkpoint inhibitors, specifically pembrolizumab or nivolumab, in patients with advanced NSCLC.

**PURPOSE:** This randomized phase III trial will compare the progression-free survival (PFS) of patients receiving bevacizumab in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors to patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors alone.

**OBJECTIVES:** 
**Primary:** To determine if the addition of bevacizumab to immune checkpoint inhibitors increases the percentage of participants who are progression-free at 6 months.
**Secondary:** To evaluate overall survival (OS), response rates, and safety profiles for both treatment regimens. Additionally, to assess changes in tumor-associated immune markers and their correlation with treatment response.

**OUTLINE:** This trial will include participants stratified by histology (adenocarcinoma vs squamous cell carcinoma) and prior treatment history (naïve vs previously treated). Participants will be randomized into two arms:
- **Arm I:** Patients will receive investigational combination therapy consisting of intravenous bevacizumab once every 21 days along with pembrolizumab or nivolumab on day 1 of each cycle.
- **Arm II:** Patients will receive pembrolizumab or nivolumab alone, following the same schedule.

**METHODOLOGY:** 
- Screening will include assessment of tumor histology, immunohistochemical profiling to determine PD-L1 expression, and availability of tissue for biomarker analysis.
- Eligibility criteria will ensure a diverse cohort, minimizing heterogeneity that may compromise outcomes:
  - Histologically confirmed stage IIIB/IV NSCLC
  - Measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria
  - ECOG performance status of 0-2
  - Adequate organ function and resolution of prior therapy-related toxicities

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Participants must be aged ≥18 years.
2. Confirmed diagnosis of advanced NSCLC, with operable tumors not eligible.
3. No prior treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors or anti-angiogenic agents.
4. Availability of tumor samples for biomarker analysis pre-initiation of treatment.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. History of uncontrolled hypertension (≥150/100 mmHg on antihypertensive treatment) or cardiovascular events within 6 months prior to randomization.
2. Significant comorbidities that could interfere with treatment adherence or response.
3. Presence of brain metastases or uncontrolled malignancies other than NSCLC.
4. History of severe hypersensitivity to bevacizumab or its components.

**EFFICACY MEASUREMENT:** 
- Primary endpoint will assess the PFS at 6 months using radiographic evaluation according to RECIST criteria.
- Secondary endpoints will include overall survival, objective response rates, assessment of tumor microenvironment biomarkers (e.g., VEGF levels, PD-L1 expression), and safety profile elucidation through adverse event monitoring.

**FOLLOW-UP:** 
Participants will be monitored for clinical outcomes every 6 weeks for the first 6 months, then every 3 months until disease progression. Adverse events will be documented according to NCI CTCAE version 5.0.

**DATA ANALYSIS:** Randomized group comparisons will be conducted utilizing intention-to-treat analysis. Progression-free survival curves will be plotted using Kaplan-Meier methods, while median survival times will be compared using log-rank tests.

This innovative study design integrates the synergistic potential of bevacizumab with the promising realm of immunotherapy, aligning with the rationale that targeting both tumor vasculature and enhancing immune response may significantly improve clinical outcomes for patients afflicted with advanced NSCLC.
