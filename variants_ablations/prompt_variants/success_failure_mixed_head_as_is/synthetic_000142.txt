**Study of Selinexor in Patients With Relapsed High-Grade Urethelial Carcinoma**  
**An Investigator-Sponsored Phase 2 Study of Selinexor in Patients With Relapsed High-Grade Urethelial Carcinoma**

This Phase II trial investigates the efficacy and safety of selinexor in patients with high-grade urethelial carcinoma that has relapsed following standard therapy. The underlying rationale for the study hinges upon the mechanism by which selinexor inhibits the nuclear export protein XPO1, thereby reactivating tumor suppressor proteins critical for cancer cell survival. Despite promising preclinical findings, previous investigations have encountered difficulties attributable to several factors.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To evaluate the efficacy of selinexor, as measured by progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with relapsed high-grade urethelial carcinoma.  
II. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR) as defined by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To evaluate safety and tolerability of selinexor, with a focus on dose-limiting toxicities (DLT) and overall adverse event profile.  
II. To investigate clinical correlatives through biomarker analysis, including XPO1 expression quantification pre- and post-treatment.

**STUDY DESIGN:**  
The study comprises a single-arm design, enrolling participants with high-grade urethelial carcinoma who have experienced disease recurrence after at least one prior line of systemic therapy. All eligible subjects will receive oral selinexor at a standard dose of 60 mg twice weekly, delivered in 21-day cycles. Response assessments will occur every 8 weeks.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Histologically confirmed high-grade urethelial carcinoma with documented recurrence.  
- Age ≥18 years with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.  
- Adequate hematologic function:  
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1.5*10^9/L  
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm^3  
- Adequate hepatic function:  
  - Total bilirubin < 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN)  
  - Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) < 2.5 x ULN  
- Measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 at baseline.  
- Written informed consent in accordance with institutional regulations.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Presence of symptomatic visceral metastases or serious concurrent medical conditions that would interfere with study participation.  
- Active gastrointestinal conditions leading to malabsorption or significant diarrhea.  
- History of progressive disease on prior platinum-based chemotherapy or other relevant systemic therapies within 90 days of enrollment.  
- Active infection requiring treatment within one week prior to enrollment.  
- Known hypersensitivity to selinexor or any component of the formulation.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**  
The primary outcome measure is PFS determined by central radiologic review at pre-defined intervals, while secondary outcomes will include ORR, DCR at 12-weeks, as well as characterizing the safety profile associated with selinexor treatment.

**RESULTS AND DISCUSSION:**  
Preliminary analyses revealed a suboptimal efficacy rate when evaluated against the expected response rates based on preclinical and early-phase studies. Notably, one prominent hurdle was the **biological heterogeneity of high-grade urethelial carcinomas**, which significantly affected the therapeutic responsiveness to selinexor, resulting in inadequate PFS measurements amongst the cohort.

Additionally, variability in **drug target expression** was evident, as many subjects exhibited low baseline levels of XPO1, minimizing the therapeutic potential of selinexor. The selection criteria inadvertently favored a population with extensive histories of chemotherapy resistance, reflecting the **limited prior treatment options** and further exacerbating potential treatment resistance.

Patient adherence and overall tolerability issues also emerged, with a high incidence of adverse events, particularly hematologic toxicities leading to dose modification and non-compliance. This corresponds with earlier findings that **tolerability and adverse event profiles** could markedly influence the course of therapy, ultimately contributing to trial cessation.

In conclusion, despite the initial rationale for targeting high-grade urethelial carcinoma with selinexor, compounded biological variabilities, insufficient drug target expression, and adverse toxicity profiles resulted in a clinical trial that did not meet its primary endpoint. The insights gleaned from this investigation will inform future studies aiming to strategically approach the treatment of this challenging malignancy. Furthermore, refining patient selection criteria and identifying biomarker-driven approaches to enhance therapeutic response remain essential next steps in overcoming the limitations observed in this trial.
