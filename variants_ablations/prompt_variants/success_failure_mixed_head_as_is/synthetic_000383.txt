A Study of Sativex® for the Management of Intractable Cancer-related Pain: A Multicenter, Double-blind, Randomized, Placebo-controlled Study

This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Sativex® compared to placebo in alleviating intractable cancer-related pain in patients who have not achieved adequate pain relief from their current analgesic regimen. The study addresses the unique pain characteristics associated with advanced cancer, which often leads to significant distress and reduced quality of life. The robust design of this trial incorporates stringent methodological principles and well-defined parameters to ensure the reliability of the outcomes assessed.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Male or female, aged 18 years or older, diagnosed with any type of cancer.
- Patients must demonstrate a history of cancer-related pain, evidenced by a Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) pain score ≥ 4 out of 10 on at least 3 out of 7 days during a 1-week baseline period.
- Patients experiencing intractable pain that is poorly controlled despite the use of stable doses of opioids or other analgesics for at least 2 weeks prior to the study.
- Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study requirements.
- Patients must be willing to maintain stable doses of existing analgesics throughout the study duration.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Patients with pain of a non-cancerous etiology or whose pain is adequately controlled with current pharmacotherapy.
- Current or past history of substance abuse or significant psychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia or severe mood disorders.
- Significant cardiovascular, renal, or hepatic impairment as determined by the investigator.
- Uncontrolled comorbid conditions that may confound the assessment of treatment outcomes.
- Pregnant or lactating women, or those of childbearing potential not willing to employ effective contraception during the study.

Study Design:
This study is designed as a multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled parallel group trial. Patients will undergo a 2-week baseline phase followed by a 12-week treatment phase during which they will receive either Sativex® or placebo. Randomization will ensure balanced distribution of participant characteristics across treatment arms, mitigating potential biases that could impact the validity of findings.

Efficacy Assessments:
The primary endpoint will be the change from baseline in average daily pain scores measured using the NRS over the treatment period. Secondary endpoints will include assessments of quality of life quantified through the Brief Pain Inventory (BPI) and the Karnofsky Performance Status Scale (KPS). Safety and tolerability will also be monitored through regular assessments of adverse events and vital signs during the 12-week period.

Follow-up:
Patients will be scheduled for follow-up visits at weeks 2, 6, 12, and 14 post-treatment initiation. Additional evaluations of treatment satisfaction and quality of life will be conducted at each visit. A final visit will occur 28 days after the end of the treatment phase for the assessment of any delayed adverse effects and long-term outcomes.

The comprehensive assessment of patients, combined with the application of objective and validated measurement tools, will facilitate the thorough evaluation of Sativex® in a challenging cohort. By addressing well-defined pain characteristics associated with advanced cancer and incorporating robust methodology, the trial aims to substantiate claims of Sativex's analgesic efficacy, thus contributing valuable insights into therapeutic options for patients suffering from inadequate pain relief.
