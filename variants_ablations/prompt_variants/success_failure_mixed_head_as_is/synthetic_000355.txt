**Failed Clinical Trial of Cisplatin: Evaluation of Cisplatin in Combination with Novel Immune Modulators for Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

A Phase II Study of Cisplatin in Combination With Immune Modulators in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**RATIONALE:** Cisplatin is a cornerstone chemotherapy agent in the treatment of various malignancies, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Emerging research has indicated potential benefits of combining traditional cytotoxic agents with immune modulators to enhance antitumor immune responses. However, due to the limitations of drug synergy and resistance mechanisms, its efficacy when utilized with immune modulators in advanced NSCLC is under investigation.

**PURPOSE:** This Phase II trial aims to study the efficacy and safety of cisplatin in combination with novel immune modulators in patients with advanced-stage NSCLC.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) to the combination of cisplatin and immune modulators in patients with advanced NSCLC.
- To assess the progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) of patients treated with this regimen.
- To identify the incidence of adverse events and toxicity profiles associated with the combination treatment.
- To investigate potential resistance mechanisms by analyzing tumor biopsies for biomarkers prior to and post-treatment.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter randomized study compares the efficacy of cisplatin administered in conjunction with two immune modulators. Patients will be stratified based on prior treatment history and baseline disease characteristics.

- **Arm A:** Patients receive cisplatin IV over 60 minutes on day 1, alongside Immune Modulator A per approved dosing regimen. Treatment cycles repeat every 21 days for a maximum of 6 cycles.
- **Arm B:** Patients receive cisplatin IV over 60 minutes on day 1, alongside Immune Modulator B per approved dosing regimen. Treatment cycles repeat every 21 days for a maximum of 6 cycles.

- Patients with stable disease after initial treatment who are tolerating therapy will continue on the assigned treatment until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity develops.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 120 patients (60 per treatment arm) will be accrued for this study within 2 years.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed stage IIIB or IV NSCLC.
- Must demonstrate at least 1 measurable lesion per RECIST v1.1.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age ≥ 18 years
- Zubrod performance status 0-1
- Adequate organ function: 
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL
  - Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior treatment with more than one line of chemotherapy or immunotherapy for advanced NSCLC.
- Active or uncontrolled CNS metastasis.
- Any significant comorbidities that would limit the ability to tolerate study treatment.
- Pregnant or nursing patients.

**PRIMARY ENDPOINTS:**
- To evaluate the ORR as defined by RECIST v1.1 at the conclusion of the trial.

**SECONDARY ENDPOINTS:**
- Assessment of PFS and OS.
- Safety profile determined by the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).
- Biomarker analysis of tumor samples for expression of immune checkpoint proteins and resistance pathways.

**REASONS FOR TRIAL FAILURE:**
This clinical study encountered multiple challenges that contributed to its failure. 

1. **Drug Synergy Limitations:** The lack of evidence supporting the synergistic efficacy of cisplatin with both immune modulators limited response rates, notably due to potential antagonistic pharmacodynamics which impeded the immune response required to elicit favorable cytotoxic effects.

2. **Compromised Patient Eligibility:** High rates of enrollment among patients with extensive disease burden led to insufficient baseline health status across the study population, which undermined treatment efficacy and constrained the ability to achieve measurable responses.

3. **Toxicity and Tolerability Issues:** The combination regimen exhibited significant dose-limiting toxicities, including nephrotoxicity and immune-related adverse effects, resulting in increased treatment discontinuation rates and poor patient adherence, indicating a need for better dose adjustment strategies.

4. **Resistance Mechanisms:** Tumor profiling revealed a substantial prevalence of resistant markers within enrolled patients, which rendered cisplatin less effective when combined with immune modulators, demonstrating acquired resistance that precluded favorable outcomes.

5. **Inadequate Treatment Duration:** The fixed treatment duration did not allow for sufficient assessment of the potential delayed response associated with immune modulation, limiting the opportunity to observe long-term benefits from cisplatin in this patient cohort.

This multi-facet evaluation highlights the complexity and challenges associated with integrating cisplatin into novel combination therapies for advanced NSCLC, reinforcing the need for continued research into optimal therapeutic strategies.
