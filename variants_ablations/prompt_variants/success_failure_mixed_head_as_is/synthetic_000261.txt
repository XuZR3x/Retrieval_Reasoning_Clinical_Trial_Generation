**Successful Clinical Trial of Doxorubicin Hydrochloride: Doxorubicin Hydrochloride Combined With Targeted Radioimmunotherapy for Patients With Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma**

**RATIONALE:** The combination of doxorubicin hydrochloride with targeted radioimmunotherapy (RIT) is hypothesized to enhance therapeutic efficacy in patients with non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) by maximizing tumor cell ablation while sparing healthy tissues. The use of RIT may augment the cytotoxic effects of doxorubicin by facilitating targeted delivery of radiation directly to malignant cells, thus potentially improving the overall response rates.

**PURPOSE:** This randomized phase III trial aims to assess the efficacy and safety of doxorubicin hydrochloride administered in conjunction with targeted radioimmunotherapy compared to doxorubicin alone in patients diagnosed with aggressive non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

**OBJECTIVES:**  
**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. Evaluate the overall survival and progression-free survival rates in NHL patients treated with doxorubicin hydrochloride combined with targeted radioimmunotherapy versus those receiving doxorubicin monotherapy.  
II. Assess the safety profile and treatment-related adverse events associated with the combination therapy.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. Examine the clinical response rates, including complete response and partial response, between the treatment groups.  
II. Investigate the quality of life parameters in relation to treatment outcomes.  
III. Monitor the incidence of treatment-related toxicities to establish a comprehensive safety profile.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, randomized, controlled trial. Participants will be stratified by age, histologic subtype, and baseline performance status as per ECOG criteria. Patients will be sequentially assigned to one of two treatment arms.

- **ARM I:** Patients will receive doxorubicin hydrochloride intravenously (IV) at a standard dosing schedule for six cycles. Concurrently, targeted radioimmunotherapy will be administered in a single dose at the end of cycle three, with dosimetry calculated to optimize tumor targeting.

- **ARM II:** Patients will receive doxorubicin hydrochloride IV alone for the same duration and will serve as the control group.

Patients will be followed for a minimum of two years after treatment completion, with assessment intervals every three months for adverse event reporting and clinical response evaluation.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 400 patients will be enrolled to achieve adequate statistical power for comparison of primary endpoints.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of aggressive non-Hodgkin lymphoma (including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma or mantle cell lymphoma)  
- Staging will be established according to the Ann Arbor classification.  
- Measurable lesions as determined by computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- **Age:** 18 years and older  
- **Performance status:** ECOG 0-2  
- **Hematologic parameters:**  
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³  
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³  
- **Renal:** Creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min  
- **Cardiovascular:** No symptomatic heart disease or arrhythmias  
- Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- History of significant sensitivity to anthracyclines or radioimmunotherapeutic agents.  
- Previous treatment with radioimmunotherapy or any investigational agent within four weeks prior to enrollment.  
- Presence of CNS involvement or other significant comorbidities that may preclude study participation.  
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding at the time of enrollment.

This trial is anticipated to address several clinical questions regarding the efficacy of integrated therapies for NHL, with an emphasis on enhancing the therapeutic horizon through synergistic mechanisms of action. The careful patient selection and stratification protocols aim to minimize the risk of treatment-related toxicities while optimizing clinical outcomes. Results from this study may significantly influence treatment paradigms for aggressive non-Hodgkin lymphoma and broaden the understanding of combined modality therapies.
