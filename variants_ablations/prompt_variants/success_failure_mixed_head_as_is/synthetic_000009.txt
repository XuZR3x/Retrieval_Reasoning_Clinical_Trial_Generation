**Failed Clinical Trial of Pemetrexed: Phase II Study of Pemetrexed with Radiotherapy for Patients with Locally Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Title:** A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Pemetrexed with Radiotherapy in Patients with Locally Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

**Purpose:** The primary objective of this study was to assess the overall survival and progression-free survival in patients with unresectable, locally advanced stage IIIB NSCLC treated with a combination of pemetrexed and radiotherapy. Additionally, the study aimed to investigate the relationship between tumor heterogeneity and treatment response, as well as to evaluate the frequency and severity of adverse events.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Diagnosis of unresectable stage IIIB NSCLC with measurable disease as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Patients aged 18 years or older, with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1.
- Adequate hematological, renal, and hepatic function as determined by laboratory tests.
- No prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy for current disease. 
- Written informed consent provided.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Histological diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma, given distinct biological behaviors.
- Concurrent treatment with investigational agents or systemic anti-cancer therapies within 30 days prior to enrollment.
- History of significant cardiac events, uncontrolled infections, or other concurrent medical conditions that could interfere with treatment or follow-up.
- Prior allergic reactions to pemetrexed, folic acid, or vitamin B12.
- Inability to comply with the recommended supplementation of folic acid and vitamin B12 for mitigating pemetrexed toxicity.

**Study Design:** This was a single-center, open-label, phase II trial. After eligibility screening, eligible patients received pemetrexed at a dose of 500 mg/m² every 21 days combined with radiotherapy delivered over 6 weeks (60 Gy total). Patients were monitored for treatment response and adverse events at regular intervals.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Overall survival (OS) from the date of treatment initiation to death from any cause.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Progression-free survival (PFS), objective response rate (ORR), and assessment of adverse events according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

**Results:**
A total of 65 patients were enrolled, however, the trial was terminated prematurely due to insufficient efficacy evidenced by progression-free survival rates that did not meet the pre-defined thresholds after 6 months of treatment. Moreover, significant treatment-related adverse events were reported, including severe pneumonitis and grade 3 hematological toxicities. The heterogeneity of tumor responses observed underscored variations in sensitivity to pemetrexed treatment, likely diminished due to inherent resistance mechanisms previously established in literature. 

**Conclusions:** The combination regimen of pemetrexed with radiotherapy in this cohort of patients with locally advanced NSCLC demonstrated inadequate efficacy, linked with serious toxicity profiles that compromised patient safety. The failure of this trial reinforced the necessity for molecular stratification and understanding of resistance mechanisms prior to combining pemetrexed with other modalities. Future investigations should focus on more homogenous patient populations and optimization of supportive care to enhance treatment tolerability and efficacy.

**Overall Survival Data:** The overall survival was reported, with a median OS of 8 months and a PFS of 3 months, which fell significantly short of the expected outcomes based on preliminary preclinical data and prior successful trials in different cancer types.

Policy implications arising from the study highlight the importance of precise patient selection and robust preclinical modeling before initiation of combination therapy in NSCLC populations, particularly underpinned by insights into pharmacodynamic interactions and resistance pathways.
