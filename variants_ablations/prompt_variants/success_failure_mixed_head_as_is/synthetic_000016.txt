**Title:** Evaluation of Tivozanib in Patients with Platinum-Resistant Epithelial Ovarian Cancer: A Multi-Center Phase II Study

**Purpose:** To assess the clinical efficacy and safety of tivozanib in patients diagnosed with platinum-resistant epithelial ovarian cancer, with a secondary focus on identifying potential biomarkers related to treatment response.

**Study Design:** This Phase II, open-label, non-randomized, multi-center study plans to enroll approximately 150 patients with platinum-resistant ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal carcinoma. The study will include biomarker analysis and will monitor the pharmacodynamic effects of tivozanib in this specific patient population over a treatment duration of 12 months.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
1. To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) to tivozanib, defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete or partial response as per RECIST v1.1 criteria.
2. To analyze progression-free survival (PFS) rates at 6 and 12 months post-treatment initiation.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
1. To determine the safety and tolerability profile of tivozanib in this cohort.
2. To explore biomarker variability and its correlation with clinical outcomes and treatment-related toxicity.

**OUTLINE:**
Patients receive tivozanib administration orally once daily, in continuous 28-day cycles. The initial treatment phase will span 12 months, followed by a 6-month observational period for tracking long-term survival and adverse events.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of recurrent, platinum-resistant epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal carcinoma.
- Patients must demonstrate a recurrence of disease within 6 months following the completion of platinum-based chemotherapy.
- Measurable disease per RECIST criteria.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior treatment with any anti-angiogenic therapies or agents targeting the VEGF pathway, including prior therapies with tivozanib.
- Significant underlying comorbidities adversely impacting treatment safety and tolerability.
- Patients manifesting severe cardiovascular issues or significant renal dysfunction.
- Evidence of inherent resistance mechanisms to anti-angiogenic treatments, including previously identified biomarkers predictive of ineffective responses to tivozanib.

**ADVERSE EVENTS MEASUREMENT:**
Adverse events will be monitored continuously throughout the study using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0 standards. Special attention will be given to severe events, particularly those affecting cardiovascular function, liver metabolism, and renal health. 

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- **Primary Outcome:** ORR, calculated as the sum of patients achieving a complete response (CR) and those with a partial response (PR).
- **Secondary Outcomes:** PFS at 6 months and 12 months, incidence of treatment-related adverse events, and correlation of clinical outcomes with biological markers evaluated via archived tissue and serum samples.

**ANTICIPATED CHALLENGES:**
1. **Biomarker Variability:** Due to the observed heterogeneity in tumor histologies among participants, a critical challenge will be to ascertain the uniform efficacy of tivozanib across the varied molecular profiles present in the patient cohort.
2. **Patient Selection Constraints:** The requirement for demonstration of platinum-resistant disease may inadvertently limit enrollment diversity, reducing the generalizability of the trial results.
3. **Resistance Mechanisms:** The anticipated failure to account for both inherent and acquired resistance mechanisms could undermine the therapeutic effectiveness of tivozanib, leading to suboptimal response rates.
4. **Safety Complications:** Given the history of significant adverse events associated with previous studies of tivozanib, monitoring for cardiovascular complications and ensuring patient safety will be paramount, given the necessity to exclude individuals with existing conditions.

**Following Completion:** 
Patients will be followed up bi-monthly for a total of 12 months, and subsequently every 3 months for 2 years post-therapy to ascertain long-term safety and efficacy.

**CONCLUSION:**
This trial aims to elucidate the role of tivozanib in treating platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. However, various potential risk factors, including patient population limitations, biomarker variability, and inherent resistance mechanisms may lead to challenges that could contribute to the anticipated trial failure. An interim analysis will be conducted after the first 75 patients complete the initial treatment phase to determine if modifications to the trial protocols are required based on the preliminary response and safety data.
