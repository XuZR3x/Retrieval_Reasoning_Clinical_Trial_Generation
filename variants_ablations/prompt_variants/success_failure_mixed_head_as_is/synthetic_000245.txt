**Cabozantinib S-Malate in Recurrent or Persistent Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors (GIST)**  
A Phase II Study of Cabozantinib S-Malate in Patients With Recurrent or Persistent Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors 

**Study Summary**  
This phase II clinical trial investigates the efficacy and safety of cabozantinib s-malate in patients diagnosed with recurrent or persistent gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST). GISTs represent a group of tumors characterized by variable biological behavior, with imatinib resistance seen in a significant proportion of cases, which forms the rationale for exploring cabozantinib s-malate as an alternative treatment option.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To evaluate the antitumor activity of cabozantinib s-malate in patients with recurrent or persistent GIST as measured by progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months per the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST v1.1).  
II. To assess the objective response rate (ORR) of cabozantinib s-malate defined as complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) through the same criteria.  

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To evaluate the overall survival (OS) at 1 and 2 years.  
II. To characterize the toxicity profile of cabozantinib s-malate using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).  
III. To explore the pharmacokinetics of cabozantinib s-malate in this patient population.  
IV. To assess blood-based biomarkers (e.g., circulating tumor DNA [ctDNA] levels) for potential correlation with clinical outcomes.

**OUTLINE:**  
Eligible patients will receive cabozantinib s-malate orally (PO) once daily on a continuous 28-day treatment cycle. Treatment will continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Patients will undergo clinical assessments every 2 cycles, with follow-ups extending every 6 months post-treatment for survival outcomes.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of recurrent or persistent GIST.  
- Patients must have measurable disease defined as one or more target lesions per RECIST v1.1.  
- Patients must have documented progression following at least one line of standard systemic therapy.  
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0-2.  
- Sufficient hematologic, hepatic, and renal function as defined by pre-treatment laboratory criteria.  
- Informed consent obtained from the patient or legally authorized representative.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Previous treatment with cabozantinib s-malate or other MET/VEGFR inhibitors.  
- Concurrent treatment with strong cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) inducers or inhibitors.  
- History of cardiac arrhythmias or uncontrolled hypertension.  
- Significant gastrointestinal disease that could interfere with drug absorption.  
- Patients with a history of progressive disease within the gastrointestinal tract or any evidence of active bleeding.  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.  

**TRIAL STATUS:**  
The trial was initiated with the intention to generate pivotal data regarding cabozantinib's activity against GISTs. However, the study was prematurely halted due to observed trends indicating lack of efficacy across multiple endpoints assessed, despite adequate dosing and adherence to the protocol. Key reasons for failure included:

1. **Inadequate Dose Response:** The trial failed to establish a significant dose-response relationship, reflecting the challenges in achieving therapeutic concentrations sufficient to elicit antitumor effects in GISTs, many of which exhibited resistance profiles against the intended pathways of inhibition.
  
2. **Biological Heterogeneity of Tumors:** The histological and molecular diversity inherent within GISTs proved to complicate therapeutic targeting. Patient tumors presented with varying levels of c-KIT mutations, resulting in heterogeneous responses to cabozantinib leading to suboptimal overall treatment results.

3. **Patient Population Variability:** The inclusion of patients with diverse treatment histories resulted in variable levels of tumor burden and prior resistance mechanisms. This heterogeneity may have contributed significantly to diminished efficacy, particularly as many patients had substantial prior exposure to imatinib and sunitinib.

4. **Concurrent Medication Interference:** Multiple participants reported concomitant therapies including NSAIDs and other antineoplastic agents, likely influencing cabozantinib’s pharmacokinetics, resulting in altered plasma concentrations and subsequent therapeutic implications.

5. **Limited Engagement of Target Pathways:** Evidence suggested a lack of significant engagement of the MET and VEGFR pathways in the tumor microenvironment of the enrolled patients, leading to insufficient induction of apoptosis or growth inhibition in GISTs.

**CONCLUSION:**  
Despite promising preclinical data and theoretical foundations for the utilization of cabozantinib s-malate in GIST, the trial outcomes demonstrate the critical limitations posed by intra-tumoral diversity and pharmacological interactions, necessitating further exploration into tailored treatment strategies aimed at overcoming resistant tumor subtypes. Comprehensive profiling of tumor genomic landscape prior to intervention is recommended in future studies to guide therapeutic choices effectively.
