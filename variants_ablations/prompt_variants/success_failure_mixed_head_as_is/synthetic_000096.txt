**Escitalopram in the Management of Chronic Pain in Adult Patients with Fibromyalgia: A Randomized Double-Blind Placebo-Controlled Trial**

**Objective:** To evaluate the efficacy and safety of escitalopram in reducing chronic pain and improving overall quality of life in adult patients diagnosed with fibromyalgia.

**Methods:** This study will enroll 300 adult patients aged 18-65, diagnosed with fibromyalgia based on the American College of Rheumatology criteria. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either escitalopram (10-20 mg daily) or placebo over a period of 12 weeks. The primary outcome measure will be the change in the Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire (FIQ) score, which evaluates pain severity, sleep quality, and overall functioning. Secondary outcomes will include assessment of anxiety and depression using the Hamilton Depression Scale (HAM-D) and Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAM-A) as well as sleep quality indices using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI).

**Discussion:** The unique serotonergic mechanism of escitalopram is well-established in the management of anxiety and depressive symptoms, which are frequently comorbid with fibromyalgia. Recent literature suggests that serotonin reuptake inhibition can positively modulate pain perception, potentially offering symptomatic relief in fibromyalgia, a condition often resistant to conventional pain therapies. The rigorous methodology applied, including randomization, double-blinding, and strict adherence to inclusion and exclusion criteria, enhances the study's internal validity. Furthermore, objective psychometric assessments, capturing multidimensional aspects of chronic pain, will facilitate robust data collection and analysis, thereby accurately characterizing the intervention's efficacy. 

**Inclusion Criteria:** 
- Adults aged 18-65 years
- Diagnosed fibromyalgia for at least 6 months
- FIQ score ≥ 30
- Capable of providing informed consent

**Exclusion Criteria:** 
- Pregnant or lactating women
- Current or past major psychiatric disorders (excluding generalized anxiety or depressive disorders)
- Previous intolerability to escitalopram
- Neuropathic pain from other etiologies
- Significant cardiovascular or renal disease
- Concurrent use of other psychotropic medications within 4 weeks prior to enrollment, excluding as-needed anxiolytics

**Conclusion:** This study will serve to elucidate the effectiveness of escitalopram as a pharmacotherapeutic intervention in chronic pain management for fibromyalgia patients, contributing vital information regarding its role in enhancing quality of life and treatment adaptability in this challenging population. Through the adoption of explicit and comprehensive assessment tools, this trial aims to provide conclusive evidence supporting escitalopram as an alternative treatment approach not only for associated depressive symptoms but also for pain management in fibromyalgia, addressing an urgent clinical need.
