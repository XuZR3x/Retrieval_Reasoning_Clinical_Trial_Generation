**Title:** A Phase II Study of Selinexor Monotherapy in Patients with Advanced Refractory Endometrial Carcinoma

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm Phase II clinical trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of selinexor in patients with advanced refractory endometrial carcinoma who have failed at least one line of prior systemic therapy.

**Objective:** The primary objective is to assess the overall response rate (ORR) of selinexor in patients with advanced endometrial carcinoma, defined as the proportion of participants with a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) according to RECIST v1.1 criteria. Secondary objectives include assessing progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety profile of the drug.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of endometrial carcinoma.
2. Patients must have experienced progression or recurrence after at least one prior line of systemic therapy.
3. Measurable disease as defined by RECIST v1.1, with at least one tumor lesion that can be accurately measured.
4. Age ≥18 years and able to provide informed consent.
5. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
6. Adequate organ function defined as: platelet count ≥100 x 10^9/L, hemoglobin ≥9 g/dL, total bilirubin ≤1.5 x upper limit of normal (ULN), and AST/ALT ≤2.5 x ULN.
7. Life expectancy of at least 12 weeks.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior treatment with selinexor or other selective inhibitors of nuclear export.
2. Concurrent malignancies with the exception of basal cell carcinoma of the skin that has been treated.
3. Significant uncontrolled medical comorbidities, including but not limited to active infections or cardiac conditions.
4. Patients who are pregnant or breastfeeding.
5. Evidence of central nervous system metastases.
6. Unresolved acute toxicity from prior anti-cancer therapy (NCI CTCAE grade >2).

**Treatment Regimen:** Participants will receive selinexor at a dose of 60 mg orally twice weekly. Treatment will continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Regular assessments for response will be conducted every 8 weeks.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Overall Response Rate (ORR) as defined by RECIST v1.1 criteria (CR + PR) after 16 weeks of treatment.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Progression-Free Survival (PFS), Overall Survival (OS), and the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) based on NCI CTCAE v5.0.

**Data Analysis:** The ORR will be reported with a 95% confidence interval. PFS and OS will be analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method. Safety data will be summarized descriptively, including the incidence of grade 3 or higher toxicities.

**Rationale for Success:**
1. **Effective Mechanism of Action:** As a Selective Inhibitor of Nuclear Export (SINE), selinexor facilitates the retention of pro-apoptotic proteins, rendering it particularly effective in addressing the apoptosis resistance frequently observed in endometrial carcinoma.
2. **Patient Population:** Strict inclusion criteria ensure enrollment of patients with endometrial carcinoma who exhibit measurable disease and have shown resistance to prior therapies, thereby optimizing the clinical response data interpretation.
3. **Monotherapy Approach:** An emphasis on single-agent selinexor treatment avoids potential confounding results associated with combination therapies, clarifying the drug's efficacy and safety profile.
4. **Robust Pharmacokinetics:** The favorable pharmacokinetic properties of selinexor enable predictable absorption and adequate bioavailability, which enhances treatment consistency across the population.
5. **Well-Monitored Protocol:** Close monitoring for adverse events in a well-defined treatment plan fosters patient safety and ensures accurate recording of dosage effects, which contributes to a high-quality evidentiary basis for assessing drug efficacy.

**Expected Outcome:** This trial aims to establish selinexor as a compelling monotherapy option in a population severely lacking effective treatment avenues, ultimately enhancing the therapeutic landscape of advanced endometrial carcinoma.
