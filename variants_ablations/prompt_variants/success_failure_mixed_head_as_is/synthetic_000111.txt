**Title**: Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Lacosamide in Patients with Lived Experience of Post-Traumatic Neuralgia

**Objective**: This clinical trial aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of lacosamide for the management of post-traumatic neuralgia in a targeted patient population exhibiting neuralgia secondary to traumatic injury. The primary hypothesis posits that lacosamide administration will result in a statistically significant reduction in pain intensity compared to placebo.

**1. Background**  
Post-traumatic neuralgia often arises following nerve injury or trauma, leading to chronic pain characterized by neuropathic features. The enhancement of synaptic activity following such injuries induces hyperexcitability within nociceptive pathways, exacerbating experiences of pain. Lacosamide, a functionalized amino acid, is known to enhance the slow inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels, particularly Nav1.7, thus providing a rationale for its potential effectiveness in managing neuropathic pain post-trauma. Prior successful trials with lacosamide in patients holding homogeneous genetic profiles have established its favorable safety profile; this study will similarly leverage a targeted subject group to further delineate its efficacy.

**2. Study Design**  
This is a Phase III, multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. The study will enroll adult patients aged 18-65 years with clinically diagnosed post-traumatic neuralgia, confirmed via quantitative sensory testing. Eligible participants will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either lacosamide at a dosing level of 300 mg/day or placebo, for a duration of 12 weeks. An open-label extension phase will follow for subjects completing the initial study without significant adverse events.

**3. Inclusion Criteria**  
- Males and females aged 18 to 65 years.
- Clinically diagnosed post-traumatic neuralgia lasting for at least 3 months.
- Baseline pain intensity score of at least 4 on a numerical rating scale (NRS 0-10) for pain.
- Ability to provide written informed consent.

**4. Exclusion Criteria**  
- History of significant psychiatric disorders that could interfere with participation.
- History of substance abuse within the past year.
- Current use of other sodium channel modulators such as carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine.
- Documented conditions affecting the lungs or kidneys which may confound results or safety.

**5. Primary Endpoint**  
- Change in pain intensity, measured using the Numerical Rating Scale (NRS), from baseline to week 12.

**6. Secondary Endpoints**  
- Change in Quality of Life as measured by the Brief Pain Inventory (BPI) and the Pain Disability Index (PDI).
- Assessment of mood disturbances using the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ-9).
- Safety profile evaluated through adverse events reported by participants and routine laboratory assessments.

**7. Study Procedures**  
Patients will undergo the following assessments:

1. **Screening Visit**: Verify eligibility through clinical assessment, documentation, and consent acquisition.
2. **Baseline Visit**: Evaluate NRS scores, pain history, and administer pre-treatment questionnaires.
3. **Randomization**: Employ a computer-generated randomization method to allocate subjects to treatments.
4. **Follow-Up Visits**: Participants will return at weeks 4, 8, and 12 for efficacy and safety assessments, including NRS scores, laboratory tests, and adverse event reports.

**8. Justification of Design**  
Success in past clinical trials for lacosamide leveraged mechanisms such as its selective action on Nav1.7 and homogeneous patient grouping. This trial aims to build upon these insights, regarding both efficacy and safety observed through rigorous exclusion criteria and objective outcome measures—critical components for minimizing confounding variables and solidifying the recommendations for lacosamide in clinical practice for post-traumatic neuralgia.

**9. Conclusion**  
This study endeavors to provide robust evidence regarding the efficacy of lacosamide in managing post-traumatic neuralgia, affirming its potential role in pain management strategies through a precise mechanistic understanding and a methodologically sound clinical framework. Engaging a homogenous population with defined inclusion and exclusion criteria elucidates the potential therapeutic value of lacosamide, which has been a foundational principle in ensuring clarity in safety and efficacy data analysis.

**10. Trial Registration**  
This trial will be registered on ClinicalTrials.gov, outlining all study parameters, eligibility, and contact information for participant inquiries, ensuring compliance with established guidelines for clinical research.
