<clinicalTrial>
    <title>Multinational, Longitudinal Evaluation of Insulin Detemir in Adults with Type 2 Diabetes: A Study on Efficacy, Safety, and Patient-Centered Outcomes</title>
    <studyType>Observational, Non-Interventional Study</studyType>
    <setting>Conducted across multiple healthcare institutions in North America, Asia, and Europe</setting>
    <objectives>The primary objective is to evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety of insulin detemir in patients with type 2 diabetes who have had inadequate glycaemic control on previous regimens. Secondary objectives include assessing patient-reported outcomes related to quality of life and treatment satisfaction.</objectives>
    <inclusionCriteria>
        - Adults aged 18 years or older with a diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus
        - Participants demonstrating inadequate control on prior antidiabetic therapies (HbA1c ≥ 7.5%)
        - Willingness to adhere to study follow-ups and provide informed consent
    </inclusionCriteria>
    <exclusionCriteria>
        - Individuals with a hypersensitivity to insulin detemir or its excipients
        - Patients with serious co-morbidities that may affect study outcomes, including severe cardiovascular disease or renal impairment
        - Prior treatment with insulin detemir within the past six months
    </exclusionCriteria>
    <duration>Participants will be followed for a duration of 12 months, with evaluations conducted at baseline, 3, 6, and 12 months.</duration>
    <outcomes>
        <primaryOutcome>
            - Change in HbA1c levels from baseline to 12 months.
        </primaryOutcome>
        <secondaryOutcomes>
            - Assessment of serious adverse reactions (SARs), including any major hypoglycaemic episodes.
            - Changes in body weight from baseline to 12 months.
            - Patient-reported outcomes measured using validated questionnaires to evaluate treatment satisfaction and quality of life.
        </secondaryOutcomes>
    </outcomes>
    <rationale>This trial's success is attributed to the inclusion of a diverse patient population from multiple geographical regions, which enhances the generalizability of findings. The open-label observational design allows for collecting relevant data reflective of real-world clinical scenarios, thereby improving ecological validity. Rigorous monitoring of SARs, especially concerning major hypoglycaemic events, contributes to a comprehensive safety profile of insulin detemir. Targeting poorly controlled patients directly addresses a crucial clinical gap, facilitating effective outcome measures that resonate with patient needs.</rationale>
</clinicalTrial>
