**Title**: Efficacy of Imatinib in the Consolidation Phase for Patients with Philadelphia Chromosome-Positive Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia: A Multicenter Phase II Study

**Purpose**: This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of imatinib as a consolidation therapy in Philadelphia chromosome-positive (Ph+) acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) patients who have achieved complete remission following induction therapy. Given the critical role of precise molecular targeting for improving patient outcomes, we hypothesize that the addition of imatinib during the consolidation phase will increase rates of minimal residual disease (MRD) negativity, thereby enhancing overall survival (OS) and event-free survival (EFS).

**Study Design**: This phase II, multicenter, open-label trial will enroll adult and pediatric patients diagnosed with Ph+ ALL who have attained complete hematological remission after initial chemotherapy. Participants will be recruited from five leading oncology centers with expertise in treating Ph+ leukemia.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Histologically or cytogenetically confirmed diagnosis of Ph+ ALL.
2. Age ≥ 1 year and ≤ 65 years.
3. Achievement of complete remission as defined by the European LeukemiaNet (ELN) criteria after induction chemotherapy.
4. Presence of the Philadelphia chromosome confirmed by cytogenetic analysis or BCR-ABL fusion transcript positive via PCR.
5. Adequate organ function: 
   - Cardiac function with an ejection fraction ≥ 50%.
   - Liver function tests ≤ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).
   - Renal function with creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.
6. Signed informed consent form.
7. Willingness to comply with study visits and procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Prior treatment with tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKI) prior to study entry.
2. Concurrent severe comorbidities or other malignancies.
3. Known hypersensitivity to imatinib or any component of the formulation.
4. Pregnant or breastfeeding women; females of childbearing potential must use effective contraception.
5. Presence of central nervous system leukemia at diagnosis.

**Intervention**: Eligible patients will receive oral imatinib at a dose of 400 mg daily for a duration of 12 months, during the consolidation phase post-induction chemotherapy. Regular monitoring of MLPA (multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification) for MRD will be performed at baseline, at 3 months, 6 months, and at the end of treatment.

**Efficacy Assessments**: The primary endpoint will be the rate of MRD negativity (defined as BCR-ABL transcript levels <0.01%) at 6 months post-treatment initiation. Secondary endpoints include:
- Event-free survival (assessed at 12 and 24 months).
- Overall survival rates at 12 and 24 months.
- Safety profile assessed by the incidence and severity of adverse events as per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

**Monitoring Strategies**: Comprehensive monitoring protocols will be established, utilizing molecular assessments to evaluate therapeutic response and tolerance. Patients will be stratified based on MRD status after 3 months, allowing for potential dose adjustments or therapeutic modifications if necessary.

**Statistical Analysis**: Efficacy will be evaluated using intent-to-treat principles. Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline and outcome measures. The primary endpoint will be analyzed using a Chi-square test for proportions. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis will be applied to assess time-to-event outcomes.

**Expected Outcomes**: Given the specificity of imatinib in targeting BCR-ABL fusion protein associated with Ph+ ALL, combined with rigorous patient selection and monitoring protocols, we anticipate a significant increase in rates of MRD negativity and an improvement in overall and event-free survival. This trial will provide crucial data to inform future treatment strategies and optimize therapy for Ph+ ALL patients. 

**Conclusion**: By integrating imatinib into the consolidation phase for patients with Ph+ ALL, guided by comprehensive molecular monitoring and patient stratification, this study aims to validate the therapeutic efficacy in prolonging remission rates and improving clinical outcomes, contributing to the growing body of evidence on targeted therapies in hematologic malignancies.
