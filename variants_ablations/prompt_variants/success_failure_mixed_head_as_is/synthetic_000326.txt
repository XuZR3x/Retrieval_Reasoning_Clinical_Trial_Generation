<clinical_trial>
    <title>Evaluation of Ranibizumab Alone Versus Corticosteroid Combination Therapy in Patients With Persistent Diabetic Macular Edema: A 12-Month Phase III Multicenter Randomized Trial</title>
    <objective>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ranibizumab monotherapy compared to a combination therapy of ranibizumab and corticosteroids in patients diagnosed with persistent diabetic macular edema over a 12-month period.</objective>
    <design>A randomized, double-masked, multicenter, controlled Phase III trial with two arms. Participants will be stratified based on baseline visual acuity and diabetic retinopathy severity to ensure a balanced representation across both study groups, promoting high internal validity. The assay employs robust inclusion criteria to minimize confounding variables and ensure a homogenous study population.</design>
    <population>The trial will enroll a total of 300 patients aged 18 years or older, diagnosed with persistent diffuse diabetic macular edema in at least one eye, confirmed through retinal imaging. Participants will be sourced from multiple centers, thus facilitating the collection of varied demographic and clinical data.</population>
    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Age 18 years or older</criterion>
        <criterion>Persistent diabetic macular edema with central subfield thickness (CST) ≥ 300 µm</criterion>
        <criterion>Documented diabetic retinopathy as per fundus examination</criterion>
        <criterion>Visual acuity between 20/40 and 20/200</criterion>
        <criterion>Stable diabetes management for at least three months prior to enrollment</criterion>
        <criterion>Written informed consent</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Previous treatment for diabetic macular edema within the last three months</criterion>
        <criterion>History of intraocular surgery within the past six months</criterion>
        <criterion>Uncontrolled systemic conditions affecting ocular health</criterion>
        <criterion>Any ocular or systemic contraindications to the use of ranibizumab or corticosteroids</criterion>
        <criterion>Pregnant or lactating women not using adequate contraception</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <intervention>Patients in the ranibizumab monotherapy arm will receive 0.5 mg of ranibizumab via intravitreal injection monthly for six months, followed by treatment on an as-needed basis. The combination therapy arm will receive the same ranibizumab regimen with an additional 1 mg intravitreal injection of a corticosteroid every three months.</intervention>
    <outcomes>
        <primary_outcome>
            <measure>Change in central subfield thickness, assessed by Spectral Domain Optical Coherence Tomography (SD-OCT) at the end of 12 months</measure>
        </primary_outcome>
        <secondary_outcomes>
            <measure>Change in visual acuity measured by the Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) chart</measure>
            <measure>Incidence of ocular and systemic adverse events during the study period</measure>
        </secondary_outcomes>
    </outcomes>
    <statistical_analysis>The primary analysis will utilize an ANCOVA model to assess the differences in CST between the two treatment groups while adjusting for baseline CST and visual acuity. Secondary outcomes will be evaluated using repeated measures analysis to analyze trends over time. A p-value < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.</statistical_analysis>
    <follow_up>Follow-up visits will be conducted monthly for the first six months, and at month 12 to evaluate treatment response and safety profiles, including monitoring for any intercurrent adverse events.</follow_up>
    <conclusion>This proposed trial leverages strengths inherent in rigorous multi-center participation and strict inclusion/exclusion criteria. The use of standardized methodologies ensures accurate measurement of therapeutic efficacy while providing robust data needed for compliance with regulatory assessments. This trial aspires to establish ranibizumab as a standard of care in managing persistent diabetic macular edema, offering a clearer pathway towards optimizing patient outcomes.</conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
