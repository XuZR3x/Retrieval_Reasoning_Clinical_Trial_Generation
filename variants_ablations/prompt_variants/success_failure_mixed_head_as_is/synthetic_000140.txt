<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Evaluation of Atorvastatin in Combination with Lifestyle Intervention versus Lifestyle Intervention Alone for Lipid Management in Overweight Patients with Dyslipidemia: A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind Trial</Title>
    <Objective>The study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of atorvastatin in conjunction with lifestyle modifications in reducing LDL-C levels in overweight patients diagnosed with dyslipidemia.</Objective>
    
    <Background>Despite existing treatments for dyslipidemia, a significant proportion of patients do not achieve recommended LDL-C targets. Integrating pharmacotherapy with lifestyle intervention is hypothesized to optimize therapeutic outcomes. This trial is structured to ensure the inclusion of a clearly defined patient population, engaging individuals at high cardiovascular risk due to obesity-related dyslipidemia.</Background>
    
    <Design>Multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial assessing 300 overweight participants (BMI ≥ 25 kg/m²) with HDL < 40 mg/dL and LDL-C > 130 mg/dL. Participants will receive atorvastatin 20 mg daily combined with an individualized lifestyle intervention (diet and exercise) or placebo alongside the lifestyle intervention over 12 weeks.</Design>
    
    <Endpoints>Primary endpoint will be the percent change from baseline in LDL-C at the end of treatment. Secondary endpoints will include weight change, percent change in triglycerides, and assessment of compliance based on pill count. Chronic inflammatory biomarkers (hs-CRP) will also be measured, providing insight into atorvastatin's anti-inflammatory effects in conjunction with lifestyle modification.</Endpoints>
    
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Age ≥ 18 years</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Overweight individuals with LDL-C > 130 mg/dL</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Willingness to adhere to recommended lifestyle modifications</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>History of statin intolerance or hypersensitivity</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Pregnancy or lactation</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Presence of liver disease with elevated liver enzymes (ALT/AST > 150 U/L)</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Current use of other lipid-lowering therapies</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Cardiovascular event within the last 6 months</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    
    <Methods>The trial includes standardized assessments at screening, baseline, and post-treatment. LDL-C will be measured via fasting blood samples, and lifestyle compliance will be tracked using validated questionnaires and self-reported logs. Data analysis will focus on comparing the efficacy of topical changes in LDL-C, triglyceride levels, and inflammatory markers across groups using an intention-to-treat approach.</Methods>
    
    <AnticipatedOutcomes>It is anticipated that patients receiving atorvastatin will demonstrate a significant percent reduction in LDL-C levels compared to the placebo group, further suggesting the role of atorvastatin as an effective pharmacological agent in managing dyslipidemia when combined with lifestyle changes. Improved patient compliance and satisfaction are expected through individualized care tailored to the high-risk patient population.</AnticipatedOutcomes>
    
    <Conclusion>Results from this study have the potential to reinforce the importance of a multifaceted approach in cholesterol management, highlighting the synergistic effects of atorvastatin and lifestyle modifications on lipid profiles and cardiovascular outcomes in at-risk populations.</Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
