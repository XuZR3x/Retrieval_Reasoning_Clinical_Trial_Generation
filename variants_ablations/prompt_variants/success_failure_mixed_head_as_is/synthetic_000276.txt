**Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Raltegravir in HIV-1 Infected Patients with Persistently Low CD4+ T Cell Counts: A Double-Blind, Multicenter Study

**Study Design:** This is a phase II, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial aimed at evaluating the efficacy and safety of raltegravir in HIV-1 infected patients exhibiting persistent low CD4+ T cell counts despite long-term antiretroviral therapy. The primary objective is to assess changes in CD4+ T cell counts from baseline to week 24. 

**Participants:** A total of 100 HIV-1 infected adults aged between 18 and 65 years will be recruited, all displaying a CD4+ T cell count < 300 cells/mm3 despite at least 12 months of stable antiretroviral therapy including at least three different classes of antiviral agents, with undetectable plasma HIV RNA (<50 copies/mL) for the last 6 months. 

**Intervention:** Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either raltegravir 400 mg twice daily or identical placebo for a duration of 24 weeks. Participants will be monitored for safety and therapeutic response.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. HIV-1 infection as confirmed by standard serological tests.
2. Age 18 to 65 years.
3. Documentation of stable antiretroviral therapy for at least 12 months.
4. CD4+ T cell count of < 300 cells/mm3 at screening, with all available historical counts falling below this threshold.
5. Plasma HIV RNA < 50 copies/mL for at least 6 months prior to screening.
6. Ability to provide informed consent.
7. No prior exposure to raltegravir or other integrase inhibitors.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Significant co-morbidities impacting immune function (e.g., active tuberculosis, ongoing opportunistic infections).
2. Use of immunomodulatory drugs within the past 16 weeks.
3. History of HIV resistance mutations against commonly used antiretroviral drugs.
4. Severe laboratory abnormalities such as absolute neutrophil count < 1,000 cells/mm3 or platelet count < 70,000 cells/mm3.
5. Current substance abuse or psychiatric illness that may interfere with adherence.
6. Intent to modify antiretroviral therapy during the study period.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Change in CD4+ T cell counts from baseline to week 24, evaluated through flow cytometry.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Change in plasma HIV RNA levels, incidence of adverse events, changes in quality of life measured via the WHOQOL-BREF scale, and pharmacokinetic analysis of raltegravir levels in plasma at baseline and week 24.

**Assessment Schedule:**
Participants will undergo regular assessments at baseline, weeks 12, and 24. Patients will be followed for an additional 4 weeks post-treatment to evaluate the persistence of therapeutic effects and to monitor any delayed adverse reactions.

**Rationale for Study Design:** The inclusion of subjects with persistently low CD4+ T cell counts corresponds to findings suggesting insufficient immune reconstitution, as observed in previous trials where CD4+ T cell counts remained suboptimal despite antiretroviral therapy. A 24-week duration for response evaluation aligns with prior studies that underscored a delayed therapeutic response, prompting the necessity for extended follow-up in assessing the comprehensive efficacy of raltegravir. This design intends to mitigate patient selection bias through broad inclusion of eligible subjects reflective of varied demographic and treatment backgrounds.

**Expected Outcomes:** It is hypothesized that despite challenges in immune reconstitution and potential individual pharmacodynamic variability, the introduction of raltegravir could yield statistically significant improvements in CD4+ T cell counts and overall health status in this underrepresented cohort.

**Conclusion:** This trial aims to elucidate the role of raltegravir as a potential adjunctive therapy in patients exhibiting inadequate immunological recovery, with the understanding that the intervention may face challenges related to pharmacokinetics and drug-drug interactions within the context of existing therapeutic regimens. Given previous trial failures, careful monitoring of response rates and safety parameters will be critical in gauging the validity of this therapeutic strategy. 

**Ethics Statement:** This study will adhere to ethical principles and regulatory guidelines, ensuring safety and confidentiality, with the appropriate institutional review board approval and informed consent obtained from all participants before enrollment.
