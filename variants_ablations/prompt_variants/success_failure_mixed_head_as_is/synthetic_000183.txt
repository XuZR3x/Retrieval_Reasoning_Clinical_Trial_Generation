<ClinicalTrial>
  <Title>A Phase III Study Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Sorafenib (Nexavar, Bay43-9006) in Combination With Nivolumab in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Who Have Failed First-Line Chemotherapy</Title>
  
  <Objectives>
    <Objective>
      To evaluate the overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) treated with the combination of sorafenib and nivolumab versus nivolumab plus placebo.
    </Objective>
    <Objective>
      To assess the safety profile and tolerability of sorafenib when administered in combination with nivolumab.
    </Objective>
  </Objectives>

  <InclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>
      Ages eligible for study: 18 years and older.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced NSCLC (stage IIIB or IV) with measurable disease as defined by RECIST criteria.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      Patients must have received at least one prior treatment regimen for advanced NSCLC and exhibit disease progression following last treatment.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      Patients must have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status of 0-2.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      Adequate organ function as determined by baseline laboratory values defined in the protocol.
    </Criterion>
  </InclusionCriteria>

  <ExclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>
      History of central nervous system metastases or active brain lesions requiring treatment.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      Presence of uncontrolled intercurrent illness that could interfere with the trial, such as severe infections or cardiovascular disease.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      Patients who have previously received treatment with any tyrosine kinase inhibitors.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      Patients with a documented history of hypersensitivity to sorafenib or any component of the formulation.
    </Criterion>
    <Criterion>
      Patients with serious concurrent illness that could compromise safety or interfere with the study.
    </Criterion>
  </ExclusionCriteria>

  <OutcomeMeasures>
    <Outcome>
      OS defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause.
    </Outcome>
    <Outcome>
      PFS defined as the time from randomization to the first observation of progressive disease or death from any cause.
    </Outcome>
    <Outcome>
      Assessment of adverse events (AEs) using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.
    </Outcome>
  </OutcomeMeasures>

  <StudyDesign>
    <Design>
      Phase III, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial with two arms: sorafenib in combination with nivolumab versus nivolumab plus placebo.
    </Design>
    <Design>
      Subjects will receive treatment in 21-day cycles until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.
    </Design>
  </StudyDesign>

  <JustificationForFailure>
    <Reason>
      The inherent variability in tumor biology and genetic mutations among the patient population may have resulted in diminished responses to sorafenib and thus contributed to suboptimal clinical outcomes.
    </Reason>
    <Reason>
      Patients with advanced NSCLC have a diverse history of prior treatments, creating a risk of built-up resistance mechanisms that limit the effectiveness of subsequent therapies, including sorafenib.
    </Reason>
    <Reason>
      The combination of sorafenib with nivolumab may yield pharmacodynamic interactions that decrease the efficacy of sorafenib, a concern particularly noted during the interim analysis.
    </Reason>
    <Reason>
      The potential for inadequate patient follow-up and challenges in recording adverse events could result in incomplete data, thereby hindering the evaluation of sorafenib's true efficacy.
    </Reason>
    <Reason>
      The complex nature of lung cancer treatment regimens may introduce confounding variables that could impair the accurate assessment of treatment outcomes, especially in a real-world patient population.
    </Reason>
  </JustificationForFailure>
</ClinicalTrial>
