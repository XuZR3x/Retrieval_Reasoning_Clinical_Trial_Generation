**Title**: A Phase II Multicenter Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Sequential Combination Therapy with Abiraterone Acetate and Enzalutamide in Advanced Metastatic Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer

**Objective**: This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of a sequential combination regimen utilizing abiraterone acetate followed by enzalutamide in patients with advanced metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC). This approach seeks to exploit the distinct pharmacological mechanisms of abiraterone acetate and enzalutamide, thereby maximizing therapeutic responses and minimizing the development of cross-resistance.

**Design**: This is a Phase II, multi-center, open-label, randomized controlled trial. Eligible participants will be allocated to two arms: one receiving abiraterone acetate followed by enzalutamide, and a control arm receiving enzalutamide followed by abiraterone acetate.

**Participants**: The study will enroll male patients aged 18 years or older, diagnosed with histologically confirmed mCRPC, exhibiting disease progression on prior androgen deprivation therapy. 

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Histologically confirmed adenocarcinoma of the prostate with evidence of mCRPC.
2. Documented progression of disease, defined as at least two increasing PSA levels over a minimum span of two weeks.
3. Prior therapy with a luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) analog or orchiectomy, with serum testosterone levels consistently maintained at ≤50 ng/dL.
4. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
5. Adequate hematological and organ function as defined by protocol parameters.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Prior therapy with abiraterone acetate or enzalutamide.
2. Active brain metastases.
3. Significant cardiac conditions including recent myocardial infarction.
4. Known hypersensitivity to abiraterone acetate or enzalutamide.
5. Use of herbal remedies known to influence PSA levels.

**Intervention and Assessment**: Participants assigned to the experimental arm will receive abiraterone acetate (1000 mg daily) in conjunction with prednisone (5 mg twice daily) for a minimum of 12 weeks. Upon demonstrated disease progression, these patients will then transition to treatment with enzalutamide (160 mg daily). For patients randomized to the control arm, enzalutamide will be administered first for the same duration before switching to abiraterone acetate upon progression.

Throughout the study, comprehensive pharmacokinetic assessments will be conducted to evaluate drug absorption and metabolism profiles, thereby ensuring optimal dosing and enhanced patient safety. Monitoring of PSA levels will occur every four weeks, with a principal endpoint of assessing the proportion of patients demonstrating a ≥30% reduction in PSA from baseline after 12 weeks of sequential therapy.

**Biomarker Exploration**: The trial will incorporate biomarker analyses of circulating tumor DNA to elucidate potential markers of treatment efficacy and resistance. This will involve comprehensive evaluations of alterations in androgen receptor expression, various mutations, and non-coding RNAs related to the treatment interventions, thereby providing insights into personalized medicine approaches.

**Outcome Measures**: 
1. Primary endpoint: Response rate defined as ≥30% decline in PSA levels post-treatment.
2. Secondary endpoints include time to progression, overall survival rates, and analysis of adverse events.
3. Longitudinal assessments of cognitive function will be evaluated given the differential central nervous system penetration profiles of the therapies involved.

**Analysis**: Efficacy will be appraised using a statistical analysis plan incorporating Kaplan-Meier survival curves and logistic regression models to ascertain factors influencing treatment outcomes. 

**Conclusion**: This innovative trial seeks to systematically evaluate the additive benefits of sequencing abiraterone acetate and enzalutamide, integrating pharmacokinetic profiling and biomarker analysis, with the aim of improving treatment outcomes for patients with mCRPC while minimizing resistance. This combinatorial strategy could provide a new therapeutic avenue in the rapidly evolving landscape of prostate cancer management.
