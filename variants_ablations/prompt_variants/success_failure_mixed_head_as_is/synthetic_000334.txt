**Failed Clinical Trial of Temozolomide: Evaluation of Temozolomide in Patients with Newly Diagnosed Hematological Malignancies and Concurrent Genetic Risk Assessment**

**RATIONALE:** Despite the established role of temozolomide in the management of malignant gliomas, the translational application of this agent in hematological malignancies remains inadequately explored. Thus, understanding the pharmacogenomic landscape of patients undergoing treatment with temozolomide may elucidate the drug's potential hematotoxicity and contribute to personalized treatment paradigms.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aims to assess the efficacy and toxicity profile of temozolomide in patients with newly diagnosed hematological malignancies while simultaneously evaluating genetic variants' influence on drug pharmacokinetics and adverse effects, particularly focusing on the incidence of severe thrombocytopenia.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- Primary: Investigate the rate of severe thrombocytopenia (grade 3 or 4) in patients receiving temozolomide as part of their induction therapy for hematological malignancies.
- Secondary: Define the pharmacokinetic profile of temozolomide in context with polymorphisms in the O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase gene and evaluate how these genetic variations impact treatment outcome and tolerability.

**OUTLINE:** This is an open-label, multicenter, prospective study. Eligible participants will receive oral temozolomide at a standard dosage daily for 5 days, followed by a 23-day rest period in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Blood specimens will be collected at baseline and at regular intervals throughout the treatment cycle for both pharmacokinetic assessments and genotyping of pharmacogenomic markers associated with drug metabolism.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** The trial was projected to enroll 100 patients, with accrual based on patient availability and interest in participation.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) or other high-risk hematological malignancies requiring aggressive chemotherapeutic management.
- Not previously treated with chemotherapy for the current diagnosis.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age > 18 years.
- Karnofsky performance status ≥ 70%.
- Adequate hematological parameters: Neutrophils ≥ 1,000/mm³, platelets ≥ 50,000/mm³.
- No active infections requiring systemic antibiotics, and no contraindications to oral medication.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:** 
- No prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy for hematological malignancies.
- Concurrent use of hematopoietic growth factors allowed after confirmation of thrombocytopenia.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:** The primary endpoint is the occurrence of severe thrombocytopenia, defined as a platelet count of < 50,000/mm³, evaluated through clinical laboratory assessment. Secondary outcomes include overall patient survival, complete response rate, and correlations between genetic markers and thrombocytopenic incidence.

**RESULTS:** The trial was terminated prematurely due to insufficient patient accrual, primarily attributed to inadequate awareness of the study among potential participants and corroborating clinicians, as highlighted in the failures of prior trials of temozolomide. Moreover, the tight inclusion criteria inadvertently excluded a broader patient population, undermining generalizability. Furthermore, the inherent genetic variability within this cohort was not fully characterized prior to study commencement, which complicated prospective pharmacogenomic analyses and increased the likelihood of unexpected adverse events.

**CONCLUSION:** The investigation into the application of temozolomide for hematological malignancies revealed critical barriers to recruitment and an inadequate understanding of pharmacogenomic interactions, leading to trial cessation. These factors are paramount for future studies that aim to explore temozolomide in other malignancies, emphasizing the need for enhanced patient engagement and tailored study designs that account for population genetics.
