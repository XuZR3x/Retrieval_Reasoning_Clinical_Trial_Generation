**Clinical Trial Report: Evaluation of Gabapentin in the Management of Postoperative Pain Following Total Hip Arthroplasty**

**Study Identifier:** GABA-TOTALHIP-2023

**Lay Title:** Investigating the Role of Gabapentin in Reducing Postoperative Pain After Total Hip Surgery

**Overview:**  
This double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter clinical trial aims to assess the efficacy of gabapentin in reducing postoperative pain and opioid consumption in patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty (THA). The study hypothesizes that the administration of gabapentin will lead to a statistically significant reduction in postoperative pain scores compared to placebo. Previous literature supports gabapentin's effectiveness in pain modulation through its mechanism of action, which includes binding to voltage-gated calcium channels and decreasing the release of excitatory neurotransmitters, thus justifying its investigational use in this postoperative context.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Male or female patients aged 40-80 years undergoing elective total hip arthroplasty.
2. American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) Physical Status Classification scores of I or II.
3. Undergoing surgery for primary osteoarthritis of the hip with no prior hip surgery.
4. Informed consent by the patient for participation in the study.
5. Ability to understand and comply with study procedures, including the completion of pain rating scales.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Pregnant or lactating women.
2. History of severe adverse reactions to gabapentin or related compounds.
3. Chronic pain conditions unrelated to the arthritic hips.
4. Pre-operative opioid use exceeding prescribed limits or use of gabapentin within 6 weeks prior to surgery.
5. Documented neurological disorders that may affect pain perception or pain assessment.
6. Severe renal impairment (glomerular filtration rate < 30 mL/min).
7. Any condition or concurrent medication deemed unsuitable by the investigator, including mental health disorders.

**Trial Design:**  
Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either oral gabapentin (300 mg preoperatively followed by 300 mg thrice daily for 72 hours post-surgery) or identical placebo. The study will utilize a randomized, double-blind design to mitigate bias and ascertain the true effect of gabapentin on postoperative pain levels. 

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
- Pain scores will be evaluated using the Numeric Rating Scale (NRS), which ranges from 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst possible pain), at 12, 24, 48, and 72 hours post-surgery.
- The total opioid consumption will be recorded for each participant in milligrams of morphine equivalents during the first 72 hours after THA.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
- Incidence of adverse effects related to gabapentin, including dizziness, somnolence, and gastrointestinal disturbances.
- Patient satisfaction scores evaluated through a structured questionnaire at discharge and at the 2-week follow-up visit.

**Rationale for Study Design:**
Incorporating gabapentin as a multimodal analgesic reflects the current evidence supporting the integration of various analgesic modalities to improve postoperative outcomes and minimize reliance on opioids. The successful trials focusing on preemptive analgesia, which highlighted gabapentin's ability to reduce postoperative pain and requirements for additional analgesics, strongly support implementing gabapentin within this surgical population.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Statistical comparisons between the treatment and placebo groups will employ the Mann-Whitney U test for pain scores due to likely non-normal distribution, while opioid consumption will be analyzed using paired t-tests. A p-value of < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant for all analyses, using a two-tailed approach.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
It is anticipated that patients receiving gabapentin will demonstrate significantly lower pain scores and reduced opioid requirements, implying enhanced postoperative pain management. Furthermore, the findings could substantiate gabapentin's role in multimodal analgesia protocols, contributing to opioid-sparing strategies in orthopedic surgery.

**Conclusion:**  
This trial aspires to expand the existing body of literature on gabapentin's utility in postoperative pain management, particularly following total hip arthroplasty. Enhanced pain control strategies have the potential to improve patient satisfaction, accelerate recovery times, and reduce postoperative complications associated with opioid analgesics.
