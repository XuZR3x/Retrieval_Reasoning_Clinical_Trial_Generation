**A Phase III Multicenter Study of Gemcitabine Hydrochloride Combined with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** Gemcitabine hydrochloride, a nucleoside analog, has demonstrated efficacy in various malignancies. The combination of gemcitabine with pembrolizumab, a programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitor, aims to augment the immune response against non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Prior studies suggest that combining cytotoxic agents with immunotherapy may improve antitumor activity through complementary mechanisms, potentially enhancing patient outcomes compared to monotherapy.

**PURPOSE:** This randomized phase III trial evaluates the clinical efficacy and safety of gemcitabine hydrochloride in combination with pembrolizumab versus gemcitabine alone in patients with advanced NSCLC who have received one or more lines of prior treatment.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** To compare the overall survival (OS) of patients receiving gemcitabine hydrochloride combined with pembrolizumab to those receiving gemcitabine monotherapy.
- **Secondary:** 
  - To assess progression-free survival (PFS) in both treatment arms.
  - To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR) in both groups.
  - To analyze the safety and tolerability of the combination therapy.
  - To explore biomarkers associated with treatment response and immune activation.

**OUTLINE:** This is a randomized, double-blind, multicenter trial. Eligible patients are stratified by histological subtype (squamous vs. non-squamous NSCLC) and prior treatment regimens (1 vs. ≥2). Participants will be randomized to one of two arms:
- **Arm I:** Patients receive gemcitabine hydrochloride intravenously over 30 minutes on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle combined with pembrolizumab administered on day 1 of each cycle.
- **Arm II:** Patients receive gemcitabine hydrochloride intravenously over 30 minutes on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle with placebo matching pembrolizumab administered on day 1 of each cycle.

After completion of treatment, patients will be monitored every 6 weeks for the first year and every 12 weeks thereafter for progression and subsequent therapy.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 800 participants will be recruited.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:** 
- Histologically confirmed advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), Stage IIIB or IV.
- No prior treatment with PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors.
- Measurable disease defined according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 criteria.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- An Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
- Adequate organ function as defined by standard laboratory tests including:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥1,500/μL
  - Platelets ≥100,000/μL
  - Corrected serum calcium, creatinine, and liver enzymes within acceptable limits.
- Female patients of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and agree to use effective contraception during and for 6 months after treatment conclusion.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior treatment with gemcitabine or chemotherapeutic agents targeting the same pathway within 6 months.
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy.
- History of malignancy other than NSCLC within the past 5 years (with certain exceptions).

**IMMEDIATE OBJECTIVES:** 
Define the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of the combined regimen, operating under the hypothesis that the synergy of gemcitabine's cytotoxic properties with pembrolizumab's immunomodulatory effects will lead to significant improvements in OS and PFS compared to gemcitabine alone, supported by a rigorous statistical design ensuring adequate power to detect meaningful differences.

**SAMPLE SIZE JUSTIFICATION:** 
The sample size calculation is based on the estimated hazard rates derived from prior studies, with the intention to achieve a power of 80% to detect a minimum survival benefit of 6 months with an alpha level of 0.05.

**FOLLOW-UP:** Patients will be followed post-treatment for adverse events, subsequent therapies, and long-term survival outcomes for a duration of 5 years.

By implementing a strong statistical framework, a precise selection of eligible candidates, and a combination therapy approach exploiting synergistic mechanisms, the study aims to contribute essential data regarding the efficacy of gemcitabine hydrochloride in the advanced NSCLC therapeutic arena. This trial is anticipated to significantly enhance our understanding of treatment modalities in this challenging cancer subtype.
