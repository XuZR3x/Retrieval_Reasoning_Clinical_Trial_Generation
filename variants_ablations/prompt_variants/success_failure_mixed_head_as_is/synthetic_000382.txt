**Phase II Study of OGX-427 in Combination With Pembrolizumab in Patients With Advanced Urothelial Carcinoma: The Phoenix Clinical Trial**

**Background:** Recent advancements in immunotherapy have opened new avenues for treatment in advanced urothelial carcinoma (UC). OGX-427, an antisense oligonucleotide targeting heat shock protein 27 (Hsp27), has demonstrated potential antitumor activity when added to standard therapies. The Phoenix Clinical Trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of combining OGX-427 with pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 immune checkpoint inhibitor, in patients with advanced UC.

**Design:** This open-label, multicenter, randomized Phase II trial will enroll patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma who have received at least one prior platinum-based chemotherapy regimen and exhibit disease progression.

**Objectives:**
- Primary: To determine the overall response rate (ORR) defined by the proportion of patients achieving a complete or partial response according to RECIST v1.1 criteria within 24 weeks of initiating treatment.
- Secondary: To assess progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), safety, and quality of life metrics.

**Study Arms:**
**Investigational Arm (Arm A):** Patients will receive OGX-427 (600 mg IV weekly), starting with three loading doses within the first week, in conjunction with pembrolizumab (200 mg IV every three weeks).

**Control Arm (Arm B):** Patients will receive pembrolizumab (200 mg IV every three weeks) alone without OGX-427.

**Eligibility Criteria:** 
Inclusion:
- Histologically confirmed advanced Urothelial Carcinoma (UC) - T4b, N2, N3, or M1 disease.
- Patients must have measurable disease defined by RECIST v1.1.
- Prior chemotherapy must have been a platinum-containing regimen; must have relapsed or demonstrated progressive disease.
- ECOG performance status of 0-1 and life expectancy ≥ 12 weeks.
- Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function as defined by specified lab parameters.

Exclusion:
- Active autoimmune disease or history of severe allergies to immunotherapy agents.
- Known brain metastases or uncontrolled CNS disease.
- Significant cardiac events (NYHA Class III or IV).
- Prior treatment with OGX-427 or other investigational agents within the past 30 days.

**Patient Monitoring and Assessments:**
Comprehensive monitoring will include imaging assessments at 8-week intervals post-treatment initiation until disease progression. Blood tests and quality-of-life questionnaires will be conducted at each treatment cycle.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Proportion of patients achieving ORR at 24 weeks.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Assessment of median PFS and OS, incidence of adverse events, and patient-reported outcomes related to health-related quality of life.

**Rationale for Success:**
Multiple reasons warrant the potential success of the Phoenix Clinical Trial comprising OGX-427 and pembrolizumab:
1. **Enhanced Antitumor Activity:** The synergistic effects of immunotherapy and Hsp27 inhibition may effectively enhance the antitumor immune response, potentially leading to greater tumor regression.
2. **Appropriate Patient Population:** The inclusion of patients with relapsed or refractory advanced UC who have a desperate unmet medical need and previously demonstrated poor outcomes offers a robust platform to observe treatment efficacy.
3. **Combination of Mechanisms:** Combining the immune responsiveness of pembrolizumab with the tumor-suppressive effects of OGX-427 may counteract tumor resilience mechanisms, improving patient outcomes.
4. **Rigorous Treatment Protocol:** The structured regimen allows for strategic administration schedules ensuring maximal therapeutic exposure while managing potential toxicities.
5. **Ongoing Monitoring:** Intensive tracking of treatment effectiveness through imaging and lab assessments ensures prompt interventions based on clinical responses.

**Conclusion:**
The Phoenix Clinical Trial presents a rigorous investigation of OGX-427's synergistic potential with immunotherapy in a high-need patient population. If successful, this may lead to significant advancements in treatment options for patients with advanced urothelial carcinoma.
