**Failed Clinical Trial of Maraviroc: Evaluation of Maraviroc for Management of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)**

**Maraviroc for Management of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease in Adults**

The purpose of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of maraviroc as an adjunct therapy in adults with moderate to severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). The trial aims to assess whether maraviroc, as a CCR5 antagonist, can provide beneficial outcomes in respiratory function and quality of life in the context of COPD, despite the limited biological rationale for its application outside HIV-related contexts.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 40 years and older with a documented diagnosis of moderate to severe COPD (GOLD stage II-IV).
- Patients must have a history of smoking (≥10 pack-years) combined with a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio of <0.7 and an FEV1 ≤ 70% of the predicted normal.
- Patients must be stable on their current treatment regimen, including bronchodilators and corticosteroids, with no exacerbations in the preceding 6 weeks.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Any prior treatment with CCR5 antagonists, including maraviroc.
- Patients experiencing acute exacerbations or requiring hospitalization for COPD within the last 12 weeks.
- Significant comorbid conditions that could interfere with study participation, including cardiac abnormalities with ejection fraction <40% or classified as Child-Pugh class B or C for hepatic impairment.

**Methodology:**
This multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled trial aimed to enroll a sufficient number of participants to achieve robust statistical power. The primary endpoint is the change from baseline in FEV1 at 12 weeks, along with secondary endpoints measuring exacerbation frequency and quality of life assessments via validated instruments such as the St. George's Respiratory Questionnaire.

**Challenges Encountered:**
The trial faced significant obstacles led by limited participant enrollment owing to strict eligibility criteria that inadvertently narrowed the patient demographic. Many potential participants were excluded based on prior CCR5 antagonist use or insufficient disease severity, leading to an underpowered study poised to yield inconclusive results.

Compounding factors included the ongoing use of multiple respiratory treatments alongside maraviroc, raising concerns regarding potential pharmacokinetic interactions and making it difficult to isolate the direct effects of maraviroc on respiratory parameters. This complexity in analysis was exacerbated by the lack of clearly defined clinical endpoints that could adequately capture the compound’s effects in the context of COPD, ultimately reflecting insufficient capability to demonstrate therapeutic benefit.

Moreover, the reliance on FEV1 as a primary endpoint, and the absence of broader clinical markers reflecting patient quality of life improvements, limited the comprehensiveness of the study's efficacy assessment.

In conclusion, the trial encountered multiple synergistic failures, suggesting that exploring maraviroc as a therapeutic modality in chronic inflammatory respiratory diseases could be premature without a stronger biological rationale. Consequently, the study was terminated prior to the completion of the recruitment phase due to anticipated difficulties in achieving sufficient participant numbers to support credible efficacy conclusions.
