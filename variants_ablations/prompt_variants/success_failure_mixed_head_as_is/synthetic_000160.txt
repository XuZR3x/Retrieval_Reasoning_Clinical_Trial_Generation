**Liraglutide and Cardiovascular Risk Reduction in Type 2 Diabetes (LIRRCD)**  
A Randomized Controlled Study Assessing the Impact of Liraglutide on Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus: The Liraglutide and Cardiovascular Risk Reduction in Type 2 Diabetes (LIRRCD) Study

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is associated with increased cardiovascular morbidity and mortality, necessitating urgent therapeutic strategies that not only manage glycemia but also mitigate cardiovascular risk. This double-blind, randomized controlled trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of liraglutide, a GLP-1 receptor agonist, in reducing cardiovascular events in individuals with T2DM at high cardiovascular risk. The study's hypothesis posits that liraglutide administration will significantly decrease the occurrence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) compared to placebo.

Inclusion Criteria:  
- Male and female individuals aged 40-75 years.  
- Diagnosis of T2DM for ≤ 10 years, with HbA1c between 7.0% and 9.5%.  
- Documented history of cardiovascular disease, including coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, or peripheral arterial disease.  
- At least two cardiovascular risk factors, such as hypertension, dyslipidemia, or smoking history.

Exclusion Criteria:  
- Prior history of severe hypersensitivity to liraglutide or any exogenous GLP-1 receptor agonist.  
- Current use of any SGLT2 inhibitors or DPP-4 inhibitors.  
- History of pancreatitis or severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²).  
- Recent hospitalization for heart failure or any major cardiovascular event in the past year.

The primary endpoint will consist of the composite of MACE defined as cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, and non-fatal stroke. This outcome is particularly relevant as it not only addresses the primary concern in high-risk T2DM patients but also aligns with observed beta-cell protective effects previously reported in the LIBRA study, thereby supporting liraglutide’s multifaceted therapeutic role.

Secondary endpoints will include changes in HbA1c, body weight, and quality of life assessments using the EQ-5D scale. Glycemic control will be measured as a function of changes in fasting plasma glucose and glycated hemoglobin. The study design ensures a homogeneous cohort, thus isolating the effect of liraglutide from confounding variables often prevalent in diverse diabetic populations.

A rigorous monitoring plan will be instituted to assess adverse events, with particular attention to gastrointestinal disturbances often associated with GLP-1 therapy. Participants will be randomized 1:1 to receive either liraglutide or placebo for a duration of 104 weeks, followed by an active follow-up for an additional year to evaluate long-term cardiovascular safety and efficacy.

This study seeks to extend the current understanding of liraglutide beyond traditional glycemic management by exploring its potential cardiovascular protective effects in a population exhibiting elevated cardiovascular risk, utilizing the robust endpoints and duration highlighted in previous successful trials. Further, the involvement of multiple centers will foster a diverse population base while adhering to rigorous criteria established to ensure participant safety and treatment efficacy.
