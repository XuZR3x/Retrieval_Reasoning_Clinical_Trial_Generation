**Title**: A Phase II Study of 5-Fluorouracil, Leucovorin, and Oxaliplatin Combined with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Gastric Cancer  

**Study Overview**:  
This multicenter, open-label Phase II clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a novel combination regimen of 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU), Leucovorin, Oxaliplatin, and Pembrolizumab in patients with advanced gastric adenocarcinoma. The study will assess key clinical endpoints, including objective response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS).

**Objectives**:  
1. **Primary Objective**: To estimate the ORR following treatment with the combination of 5-FU, Leucovorin, Oxaliplatin, and Pembrolizumab.  
2. **Secondary Objectives**:
   - To determine the median PFS and OS in the treated population.
   - To evaluate the safety profile and tolerability of the regimen through adverse event monitoring.
   - To assess the relationship between PD-L1 expression in tumor samples and treatment efficacy.
   
**RATIONALE**:  
In advanced gastric cancer, the incorporation of immune checkpoint inhibitors like Pembrolizumab alongside established cytotoxic agents such as 5-FU, Leucovorin, and Oxaliplatin could lead to enhanced therapeutic responses through dual mechanisms of action. This trial will capitalize on the synergistic effects of 5-FU and Oxaliplatin in chemotherapy with the immunomodulatory effects of Pembrolizumab to effectively target both the tumor DNA repair mechanisms and the immune evasion pathways.

**Trial Design**:  
This will be a single-arm study with an estimated enrollment of 80 patients with confirmed unresectable, locally advanced, or metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma.  

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
1. Histologically confirmed advanced gastric adenocarcinoma, not amenable to curative treatment.  
2. Measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
3. Age ≥ 18 years with Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.  
4. No prior systemic chemotherapy or immunotherapy for metastatic disease.  
5. Adequate organ function as defined by standard laboratory parameters.  

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
1. Prior treatment with any PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors.  
2. Active autoimmune diseases requiring systemic treatment.  
3. History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to any component of the regimen.  
4. Concurrent malignancies or serious uncontrolled medical conditions.  
5. Pregnant or lactating women.  

**Treatment Protocol**:  
Patients will receive the combination therapy every two weeks for up to six cycles. Treatment regimen:  
- 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) 400 mg/m² IV bolus on Day 1.  
- Leucovorin 200 mg/m² IV over 2 hours on Day 1.  
- Oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² IV over 2 hours on Day 1.  
- Pembrolizumab 200 mg IV on Day 1.  

Subsequent cycles will consist of the same dosing schedule every 14 days. Following completion of six cycles, patients will enter a follow-up phase for OS assessment, undergoing monitoring for relapse and overall health for a minimum of 2 years. 

**Endpoints**:  
- **Efficacy endpoints**: ORR, median PFS, and OS duration as measured by Kaplan-Meier survival analysis.  
- **Safety endpoints**: Incidence of adverse events (AEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs) based on clinical assessments and laboratory tests. 

**Comprehensive Monitoring and Biomarker Assessment**:  
Rigorous post-treatment follow-up and imaging assessments will be conducted every two months for the first year and subsequently every six months. The study will also incorporate biomarker-driven stratification based on PD-L1 expression via immunohistochemistry to explore the potential predictive value of this marker on treatment response.

**Anticipated Outcomes**:  
This trial is anticipated to demonstrate a meaningful improvement in ORR and survival outcomes compared to historical controls utilizing cytotoxic chemotherapy alone, supporting further development of this 5-FU-based regimen with Pembrolizumab in the control of advanced gastric cancer. The biomarker analyses will further elucidate the predictive mechanisms underlying responsiveness to the treatment combination.

**Conclusion**:  
With a well-established combination of chemotherapeutic agents and a targeted immunotherapy approach, this trial anticipates enhancing patient outcomes in a challenging treatment landscape, thus potentially leading to successful management strategies for advanced gastric cancer.
