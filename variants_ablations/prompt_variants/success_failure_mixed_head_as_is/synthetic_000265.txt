**A Study of Pertuzumab in Combination With Trastuzumab and a Novel Selective Estrogen Receptor Degrader (SERD) for Patients With Advanced HER2-Positive and Estrogen Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer**

**Study Overview:**
This phase II, multicenter, open-label trial investigates the efficacy and safety of pertuzumab combined with trastuzumab and a novel selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) in patients diagnosed with advanced HER2-positive and estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer. The objective is to elucidate whether this therapeutic approach, focusing on dual HER2 blockade and targeted estrogen receptor degradation, effectively improves progression-free survival (PFS) compared to historical controls.

**Primary Endpoints:**
- Progression-Free Survival (PFS) defined as the duration from randomization until disease progression as per RECIST v1.1 or death from any cause.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Objective response rate (ORR)
- Clinical benefit rate (CBR)
- Safety and tolerability profile, including adverse events (AEs)
- Quality of life assessment via FACT-B questionnaire

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of HER2-positive, estrogen receptor-positive advanced breast cancer
- Measurable disease per RECIST v1.1
- HER2 overexpression or amplification established via local testing, defined as either: IHC staining of 3+ in ≥10% of invasive tumor cells or a FISH ratio ≥2.0
- Adequate organ function: 
  - LVEF ≥55% 
  - ANC ≥1500/uL 
  - Platelets ≥100,000/uL 
  - Hemoglobin ≥10 g/dL
  - Creatinine ≤1.5 times ULN, liver function tests ≤2.0 times ULN
- Performance status of 0-1 as per ECOG

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment with any anti-HER2 therapy or SERDs
- Evidence of symptomatic brain metastases
- Concurrent malignancies (excluding non-melanoma skin cancers)
- Cardiac history not complying with safety parameters, including serious arrhythmias or history of congestive heart failure

**Study Design:**
Participants will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive treatment: Arm A (pertuzumab administered at 840 mg IV on Day 1, followed by 420 mg IV every 3 weeks, combined with trastuzumab and the SERD) or Arm B (trastuzumab with the SERD alone). Endocrine therapy with the SERD will be initiated on Day 1 of treatment. This trial emphasizes rigorous monitoring of cardiac function throughout the study duration to safeguard against potential cardiotoxicity while promoting informed management of treatment-related AEs.

**Background and Rationale:**
Prior trials have demonstrated the enhanced efficacy of pertuzumab and trastuzumab in HER2-positive breast cancers, especially when paired with aromatase inhibitors in hormone receptor-positive populations. The groundbreaking introduction of SERDs increases the potential for synergistic action against both HER2 and estrogen receptor pathways, aligning with recent literature that advocates for multi-faceted treatment strategies in hormone receptor-positive malignancies. This study seeks to address the need for effective therapeutic options for this population, with particular emphasis on achieving significant improvements in PFS and ORR.

**Patient Engagement:**
Throughout the study, patient engagement will be reinforced through education and resources aimed at fostering an understanding of both treatment goals and potential side effects. The incorporation of quality of life assessments will ensure a holistic approach to patient care and treatment outcomes.

**Statistical Analysis:**
PFS will be analyzed using Kaplan-Meier estimates, with comparisons made through the log-rank test between treatment arms. Continuous variables will be compared using t-tests and categorical variables via Fisher's exact test. A p-value of <0.05 will define statistical significance.

**Anticipated Outcomes:**
This trial aims to provide robust evidence supporting the combination of pertuzumab, trastuzumab, and SERDs as a transformative option for women with advanced HER2-positive and estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer, fostering progress toward reduced disease progression, improved survival rates, and favorable patient quality of life.

**Conclusion:**
This innovative study builds upon foundational research in targeted therapies for breast cancer and has the potential to shape future treatment protocols, expanding therapeutic options for patients significantly affected by this aggressive disease. The results are anticipated to contribute valuable insights into optimal treatment paradigms, paving the way for more successful patient outcomes.
