**TITLE: A Multi-Center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Tadalafil in Patients With Heart Failure and Reduced Ejection Fraction**

**BACKGROUND:** Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) is a significant clinical challenge affecting millions of patients globally. Current therapies have shown limited efficacy, highlighting the need for innovative treatment options. Tadalafil, a phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor, has demonstrated vasodilatory properties and potential benefits in enhancing cardiac output and exercise tolerance through the modulation of cGMP pathways. Prior successful trials have established tadalafil’s efficacy, particularly utilizing a robust double-blind, placebo-controlled design, comprehensive inclusion criteria, and clear pharmacological understanding.

**OBJECTIVES:** The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of tadalafil in improving the 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) after 16 weeks of treatment in patients with HFrEF. Secondary objectives include assessing improvements in functional capacity, quality of life indices, and safety profile throughout the study duration.

**STUDY DESIGN:** This phase 3 trial will employ a multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled methodology. A total of 300 eligible patients with HFrEF (LVEF ≤ 40%) will be randomly assigned in a 2:1 ratio to receive either tadalafil (40 mg once daily) or placebo over a 16-week treatment duration, followed by a 36-week extension phase for long-term safety and efficacy evaluation.

**INTERVENTION:** Participants will receive tadalafil at the dosing of 40 mg, administered orally once daily, or matching placebo.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Adult patients aged 18-80 years.
2. Diagnosis of HFrEF with a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≤ 40%.
3. Stable on guideline-directed medical therapy for at least 3 months.
4. World Health Organization (WHO) functional class II or III.
5. 6-minute walk distance < 400 meters at baseline.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. History of significant arrhythmias or myocardial infarction within 3 months prior to entry.
2. Uncontrolled hypertension defined as systolic blood pressure >160 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure >100 mmHg.
3. Use of nitrates or contraindicated medications.
4. Any current investigational drug use.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Change in 6-minute walk distance from baseline to Week 16.
- **Secondary Endpoints:**
  - Changes in NYHA functional class.
  - Quality of life assessed through the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ).
  - Safety evaluated through adverse events monitoring and laboratory assessments.

**RESEARCH PROCEDURES:** Participants will undergo baseline assessments, including medical history, physical exams, echocardiograms, and blood tests. Post-treatment evaluations will be conducted at Weeks 8 and 16, including a follow-up every 12 weeks during the extension phase.

**SIGNIFICANCE:** With its basis in prior successful applications in distinct populations, this trial aims to establish tadalafil as a therapeutic modality for HFrEF by demonstrating concrete improvements in functional outcomes and patient quality of life. Moreover, the thoughtful design aligns with rationale pharmacotherapy principles, advocating for enhanced treatment options in heart failure management and potentially extending tadalafil's clinical applicability.

**EXPECTED RESULT:** We hypothesize that patients receiving tadalafil will experience significant improvements in 6MWD, advancing our understanding of this therapeutic avenue and bolstering its potential integration into clinical practice guidelines for heart failure.

**RESEARCH TEAM:** The trial will be conducted by an experienced multi-disciplinary team, including cardiologists, clinical researchers, and dedicated research coordinators, ensuring adherence to regulatory standards and upholding the integrity of data collection and analysis.
