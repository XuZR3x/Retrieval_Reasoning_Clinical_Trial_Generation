<ClinicalTrial>
  <TrialID>0488-457</TrialID>
  <Title>A Multicenter, Double-Blind, Randomized Control Trial to Evaluate the Long-Term Efficacy of Montelukast Sodium Versus Standard Care in Adults With Persistent Asthma Exhibiting Seasonal Allergic Responses</Title>
  <Objective>This trial aims to assess the long-term effects of montelukast sodium in managing persistent asthma in adult patients with documented seasonal allergic responses, focusing on lung function, asthma symptom control, and overall health-related quality of life over a treatment duration of six months.</Objective>
  <Design>
    <Type>Multicenter, Double-Blind, Randomized, Parallel-Group Study</Type>
    <Duration>6 Months</Duration>
  </Design>
  <InclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>Adults aged 18-65 years with a clinical diagnosis of persistent asthma</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Patients demonstrating a positive skin prick test to at least one seasonal aeroallergen</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Documented history of asthma exacerbation requiring rescue medication during previous allergy seasons</Criterion>
  </InclusionCriteria>
  <ExclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>History of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) or other significant pulmonary disorders</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Current use of systemic corticosteroids or biologic therapies for asthma</Criterion>
  </ExclusionCriteria>
  <Endpoints>
    <PrimaryEndpoint>Percentage of asthma-control days, as recorded in patient diaries over the 6-month treatment period</PrimaryEndpoint>
    <SecondaryEndpoints>
        <Endpoint>Change in Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 Second (FEV1) from baseline to 6 months</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Frequency of rescue beta-agonist use during the treatment period</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Health-related quality of life assessed using the Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire (AQLQ)</Endpoint>
    </SecondaryEndpoints>
  </Endpoints>
  <Rationale>
    <Point>The lengthy duration of 6 months allows for assessment of cumulative effects, promoting a comprehensive understanding of montelukast's impact on lung function and overall asthma control.</Point>
    <Point>The study participants are specifically selected adults with documented seasonal allergic responses, thus closely aligning the patient population with those most likely to benefit from montelukast sodium.</Point>
    <Point>The inclusion of a control group receiving standard care, comprising inhaled corticosteroids and long-acting beta-agonists, enables a direct comparison and validation of montelukast as a viable alternative.</Point>
    <Point>Application of rigorous metrics, such as change in FEV1 and quantification of asthma-control days, allows for precise and objective measurement of treatment efficacy.</Point>
    <Point>The parallel-group design enhances the robustness of statistical analysis, ensuring reliable comparisons and thereby supporting the potential of montelukast sodium to improve clinical outcomes in chronic asthma management.</Point>
  </Rationale>
  <Status>Completed</Status>
  <Results>Data analysis demonstrated a statistically significant increase in the percentage of asthma-control days and improvement in FEV1 compared to the standard care group, confirming the superior efficacy of montelukast sodium in the targeted patient population.</Results>
</ClinicalTrial>
