**Title**: Evaluation of Cilostazol in Alleviating Biochemical Aspirin Resistance in High-Risk Patients: A Phase 3, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial

**Background**: Cilostazol, a phosphodiesterase III inhibitor, has shown promise in modulating platelet function and enhancing vascular function, yet previous studies have indicated variable efficacy in addressing biochemical aspirin resistance. This clinical trial aims to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of cilostazol in patients exhibiting aspirin resistance, specifically assessing its impact on reducing biochemical aspirin resistance as measured by the VerifyNow Aspirin Assay.

**Objective**: To investigate whether the addition of cilostazol to standard dual antiplatelet therapy improves outcomes in patients with identified biochemical aspirin resistance following an ischemic event.

**Design**: Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Randomized, Multicenter Trial

**Participants**: An estimated 500 patients aged 40 and older with a history of ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA) currently undergoing treatment with aspirin.

**Methodology**:
- **Randomization**: Eligible participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either 100 mg of cilostazol twice daily or matching placebo, along with low-dose aspirin (81 mg daily), for a duration of 6 months.
- **Inclusion Criteria**:
  - Documented ischemic stroke or TIA within the past 6 months
  - Persistent aspirin resistance defined as a VerifyNow Aspirin Assay output of ≥550 aspirin reaction units (ARUs)
  - Written informed consent obtained
- **Exclusion Criteria**:
  - Concurrent use of other antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapies
  - Significant bleeding disorders or history of major bleeding events
  - Uncontrolled hypertension (systolic BP ≥180 mmHg or diastolic BP ≥110 mmHg) or significant hepatic or renal impairment
  - Any comorbidity deemed likely to influence study outcomes or participant safety

**Primary Outcome**: Proportion of patients achieving ARUs <550 at the 6-month follow-up visit.

**Secondary Outcomes**: 
- Composite of major vascular events including stroke, myocardial infarction, and transient ischemic attack occurrence during the treatment period.
- Analysis of bleeding complications through patient reports and clinical examinations, focusing on serious adverse events.

**Sample Size**: The study anticipates enrolling 500 participants to provide adequate power to detect a significant difference in the primary endpoint among the two treatment groups.

**Data Collection**: Baseline characteristics, laboratory values, and clinical assessments will be collected at the beginning of the trial and at scheduled follow-up visits. Adverse events will be monitored closely throughout the study duration.

**Ethical Considerations**: The study protocol will adhere to Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and ethical guidelines for clinical research. Final approval will be obtained from the respective Institutional Review Boards (IRB) at participating sites.

**Expected Outcomes**: It is hypothesized that cilostazol will enhance the response to aspirin in patients with biochemical resistance, reducing ARUs significantly. However, prior studies' inconsistent efficacy profiles raise concerns over cilostazol's potential clinical benefits, with the risk of significant bleeding complications anticipated due to the additional antiplatelet therapy. 

**Statistical Analysis**: Intention-to-treat analyses will be performed to assess the primary and secondary outcomes. Statistical significance will be established at p < 0.05.

**Conclusion**: This trial aims to validate the role of cilostazol in managing aspirin-resistant patients post-stroke while carefully monitoring the associated safety profile. Given previous studies' failure to demonstrate consistent efficacy, this trial is designed to address potential population-specific limitations and suboptimal treatment duration while elucidating cilostazol's unique contribution to therapy.
