**Title: A Phase II Study Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Capecitabine in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Previously Treated Advanced Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Lung**

**RATIONALE:** Capecitabine has shown potential as a chemotherapeutic agent; however, its efficacy may be hindered by factors such as extensive prior treatment history and biological variability among tumor types. Previous trials have demonstrated limited engagement with the intended therapeutic targets when combined with agents like pembrolizumab. This study evaluates whether combining capecitabine with pembrolizumab could offer clinical benefit in a population with advanced squamous cell carcinoma who have limited treatment options.

**PURPOSE:** This Phase II trial attempts to elucidate the safety and efficacy of capecitabine in conjunction with pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab administered alone in patients with advanced squamous cell carcinoma of the lung.

**OBJECTIVES:**  
- **Primary Objective:** Evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months in patients receiving capecitabine and pembrolizumab compared to pembrolizumab alone.  
- **Secondary Objectives:** Assess overall survival rates, response rates, and safety profiles in the treatment cohorts.
  
**OUTLINE:** The study is a randomized, open-label trial. Patients will receive:  
- **Arm A:** Capecitabine orally twice daily for 14 days followed by a 7-day rest period in combination with pembrolizumab administered intravenously every 3 weeks.  
- **Arm B:** Pembrolizumab alone administered intravenously every 3 weeks.  
Cycles repeat every 21 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Patients are monitored for disease progression via radiological assessments every 8 weeks.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 120 patients will be enrolled in this study.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Histologically confirmed squamous cell carcinoma of the lung.  
- Must have received at least one prior line of systemic therapy which includes chemotherapy.  
- No evidence of active disease at the time of trial entry.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Performance status: ECOG 0-2.  
- Life expectancy > 3 months.  
- Adequate hematological, hepatic, and renal function (as detailed in eligibility criteria).

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**  
- Patients may have received any systemic therapy, including prior checkpoint inhibitors or chemotherapeutic agents.  
- Prior treatment with capecitabine is not permitted.

**FAILURE JUSTIFICATION:**  
The trial was terminated early based on interim analysis, which indicated statistical futility; the anticipated increase in PFS associated with capecitabine was not observed. The substantial prior treatment history of participants contributed to altered pharmacokinetic profiles of capecitabine, thereby reducing its efficacy (as indicated in justification points 1 and 4). Additionally, potential drug-drug interactions noted between pembrolizumab and capecitabine raised concerns over achieving optimal therapeutic concentrations of capecitabine, negating anticipated improvements in treatment outcomes (as per point 2).

The trial faced challenges regarding sample size and low event rates, which compounded issues surrounding assessment of therapeutic efficacy (point 3). In particular, adverse events linked to capecitabine in combination regimens resulted in increased treatment discontinuations and dose adjustments, further undermining the expected clinical benefits (point 5).

Due to these intricate factors, the independent Data Monitoring Committee assessed the study as unlikely to yield clinically meaningful outcomes, necessitating early termination in the interest of patient welfare and efficient resource use. This clinical trial underscores the complexities involved in drug combinations and the critical need for precise patient selection in oncology trials.
