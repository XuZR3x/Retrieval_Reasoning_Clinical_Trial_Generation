**Efficacy and Safety of Vandetanib (ZD6474) in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients With Advanced Thyroid Carcinoma: A Phase II Study**

A Randomized, Double Blind, Placebo-controlled Phase II Study to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of Vandetanib (ZD6474) Combined with Pembrolizumab in Patients With Advanced Metastatic Thyroid Carcinoma No Longer Responding to Standard Treatment

This phase II study was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vandetanib (ZD6474) in combination with pembrolizumab, a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitor, in an advanced cohort of patients diagnosed with metastatic thyroid carcinoma that had shown resistance to established therapies. The rationale for this intervention is grounded in the enhanced target specificity of vandetanib towards the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR-2), mechanisms that can be synergistically augmented by immune checkpoint inhibition.

**Participants' Assessment**  
The study included patients with confirmed locally advanced or metastatic thyroid carcinoma who had progressed following conventional treatment modalities. Relevant clinical entries were based on robust efficacy profiles established in phase I trials, which demonstrated improved progression-free survival (PFS) rates upon treatment with vandetanib. Patients were included only if they exhibited at least one measurable lesion in accordance with RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**Study Design**  
Participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either a combination therapy of vandetanib (300 mg orally daily) and pembrolizumab (200 mg intravenously every three weeks) or placebo plus pembrolizumab, administered over a treatment period of up to two years or until disease progression was evident. Primary outcome measures focused on PFS, while secondary endpoints included overall survival (OS) rates, clinical response rates, and safety evaluations spanning the duration of therapy as well as a post-treatment follow-up of 24 weeks.

**Safety Monitoring and Evaluation**  
A systematic approach to monitoring adverse events was integral to the design, involving comprehensive longitudinal safety assessments with regular follow-up visits every four weeks. Special attention was given to recognizing any overlapping toxicity profiles, particularly in light of the known side effects of both vandetanib and pembrolizumab in prior studies.

**Inclusion Criteria**  
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of locally advanced or metastatic thyroid carcinoma, resistant to standard therapies.
- Presence of one or more measurable lesions ≥ 1 cm in diameter confirmed by imaging. 
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status of 0 or 1.
- Adequate bone marrow function: ANC > 1500/mL, platelets > 100,000/mL.
- Adequate renal and hepatic function.

**Exclusion Criteria**  
- Previous treatment with PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors.
- Prior systemic therapies (e.g., chemotherapy, targeted therapy) within four weeks of enrollment.
- Concurrent active malignancies or significant cardiac events within the past six months.
- Presence of any medical condition precluding the safe administration of the study drug.

**Statistical Analysis**  
The anticipated enrollment aimed for a minimum of 200 participants to ensure adequate statistical power to detect a difference in PFS between the treatment arms. Survival curves for PFS and OS were estimated using Kaplan-Meier methods, with comparison between groups made using log-rank tests.

**Outcomes and Results**  
Preliminary results indicated a statistically significant improvement in PFS for the vandetanib and pembrolizumab group compared to the placebo cohort, with a median PFS of 12.5 months versus 6.8 months (p < 0.001). An interim analysis revealed a favorable safety profile with manageable adverse events attributed to both agents, particularly rash and immune-related adverse events, which were consistent with prior observations. 

This study emphasizes the potential of optimal combination strategies involving vandetanib in conjunction with immune modulation through pembrolizumab, suggesting a promising therapeutic pathway for patients afflicted with advanced thyroid carcinomas resistant to conventional treatments. Future studies should investigate the underlying mechanisms of synergy and determine further optimization of this therapeutic regimen.
