**Failed Clinical Trial of Liposomal Doxorubicin: Study of Pegylated Liposomal Doxorubicin Combined with Carboplatin in Patients with Recurrent Ovarian Cancer**

**Phase III Randomized Controlled Trial of Pegylated Liposomal Doxorubicin in Combination with Carboplatin Versus Carboplatin Alone in Patients with Recurrent Ovarian Cancer**

The objective of this clinical trial is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of pegylated liposomal doxorubicin (PLD) in combination with carboplatin compared to carboplatin monotherapy in women diagnosed with recurrent ovarian cancer. Given the complex tumor microenvironment and the limitations encountered in concurrent studies such as MITO-2, this trial will assess whether the incorporation of PLD provides any oncological advantages in this specific patient cohort.

Eligible patients will be stratified randomly into two groups: one receiving six cycles of combination therapy consisting of carboplatin (AUC 5 given intravenously on day 1 every three weeks) and PLD (30 mg/m² administered intravenously on day 1 every three weeks), while the second group will receive carboplatin alone under the same scheduling conditions.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Women aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed recurrent ovarian cancer, specifically stages IIIC-IV.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Patients must have undergone at least one prior platinum-based chemotherapy regimen.
- Measurable disease as ascertained by RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- Adequate organ function as indicated by standard laboratory values (creatinine ≤ 1.25 times the upper normal limit and liver function markers < 1.5 times the upper normal limit).

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment with anthracyclines (total cumulative dose exceeding 300 mg/m² doxorubicin or 600 mg/m² epirubicin).
- Presence of symptomatic central nervous system metastases.
- Concurrent malignancies (excluding adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancer).
- Any evidence of hypersensitivity to components of the trial regimen.

This trial will primarily measure progression-free survival (PFS) as the primary endpoint, with secondary endpoints including overall survival (OS), quality of life assessment through validated questionnaires, and toxicity profiles according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE).

**Sample Size Calculation:**
Utilizing Simon's two-stage design, approximately 140 patients will be enrolled to achieve a minimum of 63 events necessary to evaluate the differences in PFS effectively. An initial phase will include 40 patients, with subsequent continuation if a minimum of 13 progression-free results are observed, subsequently recruiting a total of 140 patients with an estimated dropout rate of 10%.

Although prior studies indicated potential benefits of PLD within second-line therapies, including anecdotal evidence from smaller cohorts, the current design draws from notable inadequacies identified in previously successful frameworks regarding the unique pharmacodynamics of PLD when combined with carboplatin. In particular, the heterogeneous metabolic profiles of patients, as identified in earlier trials, informed the decision to include a more robust stratification to optimize therapeutic response rates.

**Database for Outcomes:**
- Objective Response Rate (ORR) as classified by RECIST criteria, with measurements taken at baseline and at each cycle.
- Progression-Free Survival (PFS) measured from initiation of treatment until documented disease progression or death.

The trial aims to provide a comprehensive analysis of the potential role of PLD within the treatment paradigm for recurrent ovarian cancer, as preliminary findings postulate that previous combination strategies inadequately addressed the nuances of tumor biology involved, ultimately influencing clinical efficacy.

The anticipated conclusion of the study is set for 24 months post participant recruitment, with interim analysis planned after the initial 70 patients complete the treatment cycles, allowing for trajectory adjustments should emerging data suggest a necessity for modification in protocol or endpoint parameters.

It is hypothesized that this trial, while based on compelling therapeutics, may confront challenges akin to those observed in the MITO-2 study, particularly given the complexity of ovarian cancer pathophysiology and the subsequent impact on PFS endpoints in the context of initial treatment protocols that may not adequately reflect optimal therapeutic timing or applicability.
