### Protocol Title: Efficacy of Low-Dose Interleukin-2 in Reducing Relapse Rates After Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation in Standard Risk Acute Myeloid Leukemia.

#### Background:
Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) remains a significant challenge post-transplantation, with relapse following hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT) contributing significantly to poor overall survival. Recent studies suggest that low-dose interleukin-2 (IL-2) might enhance immune reconstitution, promoting anti-leukemic immunity while simultaneously facilitating the expansion of regulatory T cells to mitigate graft-versus-host disease (GVHD). This clinical trial aims to investigate the potential of low-dose IL-2 administration to improve relapse-free survival and overall outcomes in patients undergoing HSCT for standard risk AML.

#### Study Hypothesis:
The administration of low-dose IL-2 following allogeneic HSCT in patients with standard risk AML can reduce the relapse rate and improve overall survival compared to a control group receiving standard post-transplant care without IL-2.

#### Objectives:
1. To evaluate the efficacy of low-dose IL-2 in reducing the relapse rate post-transplantation.
2. To assess overall survival at 24 months post HSCT.
3. To characterize the immunologic changes induced by IL-2 treatment in the post-transplant setting.

#### Study Design:
This is a Phase II, multi-center, randomized controlled trial. Eligible participants will be randomly assigned to receive either low-dose IL-2 treatment in conjunction with standard supportive care or standard supportive care alone for 24 months.

#### Inclusion Criteria:
- Adults aged 18 years and older.
- Confirmed diagnosis of standard risk AML (excluding high-risk subtypes).
- Recipients of allogeneic HSCT from matched sibling or unrelated donors.
- At least 60 days post-transplantation with sustained complete remission (CR).
- Adequate organ function and performance status (ECOG 0-2).

#### Exclusion Criteria:
- Ongoing requirement for high-dose corticosteroids (>1 mg/kg/day).
- Active uncontrolled infections or other malignancies.
- Exposure to novel immunosuppressive agents or GVHD prophylaxis regimens in the last 30 days.
- Pregnancy or lactation.

#### Intervention Details:
All participants in the intervention group will receive low-dose IL-2 (1.0 x 10^6 IU/m²) via subcutaneous injection daily for 8 weeks beginning 60 days post-transplant. Following this period, patients will be assessed for treatment response and side effects. A tapered corticosteroid regimen will accompany the intervention as indicated.

#### Study Outcomes:
**Primary Outcomes:**
- Relapse-free survival rate at 24 months, as defined by the absence of malignant disease recurrence in the bone marrow.

**Secondary Outcomes:**
- Overall survival at 24 months.
- Assessment of immunologic changes, including the quantification of T regulatory cells and natural killer cell populations using flow cytometry pre- and post-intervention.
- Clinical characterization of GVHD occurrences following IL-2 treatment compared to control.

#### Sample Size:
An estimated 300 participants will be enrolled, with a power of 80% to detect a significant difference in relapse rates between groups.

#### Statistical Analysis:
Efficacy will be evaluated using Kaplan-Meier survival estimations, and comparisons between groups will employ log-rank tests for survival analysis and Chi-square tests for categorical outcomes.

#### Timeline:
Estimated study start date: July 2024. Expected primary completion by: July 2026.

#### Conclusion:
This trial aims to rigorously evaluate the immunotherapeutic potential of low-dose IL-2 in post-HSCT patients with acute myeloid leukemia, leveraging previously documented benefits of IL-2 in promoting T regulatory cell expansion and potentially impeding the relapse of malignancy while diminishing the severity of GVHD manifestations. Standardized response evaluations will ensure the robustness of the clinical and immunologic data, paving the way for future therapeutic strategies in the management of AML post-transplantation.
