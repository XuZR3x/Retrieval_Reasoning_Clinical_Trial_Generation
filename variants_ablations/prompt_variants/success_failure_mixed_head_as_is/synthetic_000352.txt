**Failed Clinical Trial of Ranibizumab: Investigating Efficacy of Ranibizumab as Adjunctive Therapy in Retinal Detachment Repair**

**Title:** Assessment of Ranibizumab as an Adjunctive Treatment During Surgical Repair of Rhegmatogenous Retinal Detachment: A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind Clinical Trial

**Objective:**  
The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of ranibizumab administered as an adjunctive therapy during surgical repair of rhegmatogenous retinal detachment (RRD) in improving surgical outcomes in terms of retinal reattachment rates and visual acuity enhancements compared to standard procedures that do not incorporate anti-VEGF therapy. Secondary objectives include the evaluation of the safety profile of ranibizumab in this setting as well as the identification of any adverse ocular events.

**Background:**  
The pathophysiology of RRD involves complex interactions between retinal detachment and proliferative vitreoretinopathy (PVR), leading to a requirement for multifaceted treatment strategies. While anti-VEGF therapy such as ranibizumab suggests potential benefits in mitigating PVR-related complications post-surgery, preliminary feasibility studies indicate that its inclusion may fail to enhance clinical outcomes compared to conventional surgical interventions.

**Design:**  
This multicenter, randomized, double-blind study enrolled 220 participants diagnosed with uncomplicated RRD scheduled for surgical repair. Participants were stratified into two groups: one receiving ranibizumab (0.5 mg/0.05 mL) subretinally at the time of surgery and the other receiving sham therapy. Follow-up evaluations were performed at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months post-operatively, with comprehensive assessments of postoperative retinal reattachment and best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA).

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients aged 18 years and older.  
- Diagnosis of rhegmatogenous retinal detachment requiring surgical intervention.  
- Signed informed consent.  
- BCVA ≥ 20/200 in the affected eye prior to surgery.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Prior anti-VEGF treatment in the study eye.  
- History of ocular surgery within the last 6 months.  
- Existing severe ocular comorbidities (e.g., active ocular infection or inflammation).  
- Pregnant or lactating women.  
- Significant systemic diseases that may complicate study evaluations.

**Endpoints:**  
- Primary Endpoint: Rate of successful anatomical retinal reattachment as assessed by optical coherence tomography (OCT) at 12 weeks post-operatively.  
- Secondary Endpoint: Change in BCVA from baseline to 12 months post-surgery and incidence of ocular adverse events, including but not limited to endophthalmitis and retinal hemorrhage.

**Results:**  
Despite expectations based on preliminary reports suggesting potential benefits of ranibizumab as an adjunct treatment, the trial's outcomes demonstrated no statistically significant difference in the primary endpoint of retinal reattachment rates between the ranibizumab and sham groups (p = 0.482). Furthermore, secondary analyses revealed no considerable improvement in BCVA (p = 0.377), corroborating findings from previous failed trials where treatment complexity and suboptimal patient selection potentially obscured beneficial outcomes.

**Adverse Events:**  
Ocular adverse events reported were consistent across both groups, with an increased incidence of adverse effects noted in conjunction with ranibizumab, comprising 15% of participants compared to 8% in the sham group. Specifically, delayed wound healing and increased intraocular pressure were documented, further diminishing the perceived value of ranibizumab as adjunctive therapy.

**Conclusion:**  
This clinical trial underscores the insufficient evidence of efficacy, suboptimal patient selection, and the complexity of treatment regimens as critical factors leading to the failure of ranibizumab to achieve desired outcomes in the context of retinal detachment repair. Further research is warranted to investigate alternate dosing regimens or combination therapies that may address the multifactorial nature of RRD more effectively. Additionally, an exploration of the adverse event profile suggests that the integration of ranibizumab with standard surgical practices may not provide the anticipated clinical advantages and could compromise patient safety.
