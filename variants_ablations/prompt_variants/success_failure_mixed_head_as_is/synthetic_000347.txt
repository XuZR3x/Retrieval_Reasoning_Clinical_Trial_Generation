<ClinicalTrial>
  <Title>A Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Assess the Efficacy of Tadalafil in Improving Quality of Life in Patients with Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia and Comorbid Conditions</Title>
  <Objective>The primary objective of this trial is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of tadalafil in improving quality of life in participants with benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and various comorbid conditions, including cardiac dysfunction and hypertension.</Objective>
  
  <StudyDesign>This study is designed as a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial with two parallel groups. Participants will undergo a 12-week treatment period during which they will receive either daily tadalafil (10 mg) or matching placebo. Randomization will be stratified by the presence of specific comorbidities.</StudyDesign>

  <InclusionCriteria>
    <Criteria>- Men aged 50 to 80 years with a diagnosis of benign prostatic hyperplasia.</Criteria>
    <Criteria>- Total International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) ≥ 12 at screening.</Criteria>
    <Criteria>- Documented history of stable cardiovascular conditions (e.g., hypertension, coronary artery disease) with no changes in medications over the last 3 months.</Criteria>
    <Criteria>- Participants must be compliant with any pre-existing BPH therapy for at least 4 weeks prior to study entry.</Criteria>
  </InclusionCriteria>

  <ExclusionCriteria>
    <Criteria>- History of malignant prostate conditions, bladder cancer, or significant pelvic surgery.</Criteria>
    <Criteria>- Use of any nitrate medication or antiplatelet therapies within 4 weeks prior to enrollment.</Criteria>
    <Criteria>- Systolic blood pressure exceeding 160 mmHg or diastolic pressure < 50 mmHg at screening.</Criteria>
    <Criteria>- Documented history of significant adverse reactions to phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors.</Criteria>
  </ExclusionCriteria>

  <Endpoints>
    <PrimaryEndpoint>- Change from baseline in quality of life measured by the BPH Impact Index (BPHII) at Week 12.</PrimaryEndpoint>
    <SecondaryEndpoint>- Change in IPSS scores, urinary flow metrics, and overall treatment satisfaction using a patient preference questionnaire.</SecondaryEndpoint>
  </Endpoints>

  <ReasonforFailure>
    <Reason>- The study was terminated prematurely due to unexpectedly high variability in patient responses tied to comorbid conditions, particularly among those with cardiovascular dysfunction. These confounding factors diluted any observable treatment effect from tadalafil.</Reason>
    <Reason>- The sample size was insufficient to robustly assess efficacy; it became evident during the trial that the heterogeneity of participant characteristics significantly affected the overall statistical power.</Reason>
    <Reason>- Adherence to study protocols was notably low, particularly among subjects with existing treatment modalities, which affected the fidelity of outcome measures. Non-compliance rates were reported at over 25%, leading to the ineffectiveness of the intervention.</Reason>
    <Reason>- Endpoint measures failed to capture clinically significant improvements. The BPHII outcome did not reflect meaningful changes that correlated with subjective assessments from the participants.</Reason>
    <Reason>- Adverse events related to cardiovascular side effects were higher than anticipated, resulting in a greater proportion of study withdrawals, which further impacted efficacy results and safety profiles.</Reason>
  </ReasonforFailure>

  <Conclusion>This trial underscores the complexity of managing patient populations with benign prostatic hyperplasia, particularly when compounded by pre-existing comorbidities, emphasizing the need for more tailored therapeutic approaches and well-defined inclusion criteria in future studies.</Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
