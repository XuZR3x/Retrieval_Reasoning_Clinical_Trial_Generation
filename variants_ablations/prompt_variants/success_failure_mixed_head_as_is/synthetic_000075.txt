**Title:** Efficacy of Tranexamic Acid in Reducing Hemorrhage during Elective Neurosurgical Procedures: A Double-Blind, Randomized Controlled Trial

**Abstract:** Hemorrhage during neurosurgical procedures can lead to severe complications and prolonged recovery. This study aims to determine the efficacy of intravenous tranexamic acid (TXA) in reducing intraoperative blood loss and the need for blood transfusions in patients undergoing elective neurosurgery. Given the pharmacokinetic advantages of TXA, this multicenter randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial investigates its effectiveness in a carefully selected patient population.

**Introduction:** Intraoperative bleeding is a significant concern in neurosurgical interventions, associated with increased morbidity and longer hospital stays. Tranexamic acid, an antifibrinolytic agent, has demonstrated efficacy in various surgical fields. However, its role in neurosurgery remains inadequately explored. The established pharmacokinetics of TXA indicate favorable absorption and distribution, suggesting a strong potential for minimizing hemorrhagic complications in this context.

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving 150 participants aged 18 years and older, undergoing elective neurosurgical procedures involving craniotomy or laminectomy. Patients will be randomized to receive either TXA or a placebo on the morning of surgery.

**Primary Objective:** To assess the effect of TXA on intraoperative blood loss quantified by the total volume of blood collected in suction devices, gauged sponges, and drainage systems.

**Secondary Objectives:** 
1. To evaluate the need for allogeneic blood transfusions during the perioperative period.
2. To analyze postoperative complications, including thromboembolic events and seizure activity within 30 days following surgery.
3. To correlate TXA plasma levels with erythrocyte volume loss, as well as renal function parameters prior to and following surgery.

**Inclusion Criteria:** 
- Adults aged 18 years or older.
- Patients scheduled for elective craniotomy or laminectomy.
- Informed consent provided.

**Exclusion Criteria:** 
- Known hypersensitivity to TXA.
- History of significant thromboembolic disease.
- Active bleeding disorders.
- Severe renal or hepatic impairment.
- Presence of any contraindicating neurological condition.

**Intervention:** Participants in the TXA group will receive a loading dose of 15 mg/kg IV administered over 20 minutes before surgical incision, followed by a continuous infusion of 2 mg/kg/hour during the procedure. The placebo group will receive an equivalent volume of saline under the same conditions.

**Data Collection:** Baseline demographic and clinical data, including age, sex, weight, comorbidities, and preoperative hemoglobin levels, will be documented. Intraoperative data, including estimated blood loss, duration of surgery, and coagulopathy markers, will be monitored. Postoperatively, blood products administered and adverse events will be logged and evaluated in accordance with established protocols.

**Statistical Analysis Plan:** A power analysis indicates that 150 subjects will provide 80% power to detect a reduction in estimated intraoperative blood loss of 30% in the TXA group compared to placebo (α = 0.05). Data will be analyzed using ANOVA for continuous variables and chi-square tests for categorical data.

**Ethics and Safety Monitoring:** The study protocol has been approved by the institutional review board (IRB). Adverse events will be monitored continuously, with any serious adverse reactions reported immediately for prompt resolution. A Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will review accumulating data and may recommend alterations to the trial based on safety considerations.

**Expected Outcomes:** We hypothesize that TXA administration will significantly reduce intraoperative blood loss compared to placebo, potentially leading to decreased blood transfusions and improved patient outcomes, aligning with prior evidence from randomized trials in other surgical domains. Should the results be favorable, they will support TXA's wider adoption in neurosurgical practice, enhancing blood management protocols in this high-risk population.

**Conclusion:** This trial seeks to contribute comprehensive data on the efficacy of tranexamic acid in elective neurosurgery, addressing gaps in current literature and guiding future clinical practice regarding hemorrhage prevention in neurosurgical procedures.
