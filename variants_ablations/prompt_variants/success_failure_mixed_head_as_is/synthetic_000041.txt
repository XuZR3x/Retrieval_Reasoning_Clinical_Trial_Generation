**Title:** A Randomized, Double-Blind, Parallel-Group, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Vortioxetine for Adults with Generalized Anxiety Disorder

**Abstract:** This clinical trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vortioxetine in the treatment of adults diagnosed with Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD). The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in the Hamilton Anxiety Scale (HAM-A) total score after eight weeks of treatment. Despite robust methodology, the trial's results indicated insufficient efficacy of vortioxetine compared to placebo, thus leading to its failure in demonstrating significant benefits for treating anxiety symptoms in the indicated population.

**Study Design:** A randomized, double-blind, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial was conducted across multiple sites in the United States from April 2023 to December 2023. A total of 400 participants aged 18-65 years were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either vortioxetine 10 mg daily or placebo for a treatment duration of eight weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults aged 18 to 65 years.
2. A confirmed diagnosis of Generalized Anxiety Disorder according to the DSM-5 criteria.
3. A HAM-A total score ≥20 at Screening and Baseline.
4. Ability to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Current or past history of other psychiatric disorders as per DSM-5 (including but not limited to bipolar disorder and schizophrenia).
2. Recent treatment with psychotropic medications (exclusive of anxiolytics) within 14 days prior to baseline.
3. History of substance abuse within the prior six months.
4. Clinically significant risk of suicide as assessed through the Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale.

**Methods:** Participants underwent a screening visit to confirm eligibility, followed by a baseline visit where HAM-A scoring was conducted. Throughout the trial, participants attended weekly follow-up visits for monitoring and data collection.

**Primary Outcome Measures:** The primary outcome was assessed using the change in HAM-A total score from baseline to week 8. Secondary outcomes included the Clinical Global Impression (CGI) scale and adverse event reporting.

**Results:** At week 8, the mean improvement in HAM-A scores for the vortioxetine group was 2.1 points, compared to a 2.4-point improvement in the placebo group (p=0.73). Secondary outcomes yielded similar findings, indicating no significant differences in treatment response between the vortioxetine and placebo groups. This lack of efficacy was further corroborated by the low statistical power of the outcome measures due to the homogeneity in participant demographics and concurrent exclusion of individuals experiencing more severe anxiety, potentially compromising the trial's generalizability.

**Discussion:** The trial faced several limitations that may have contributed to its failure, including:
1. **Insufficient Efficacy:** The observed lack of statistically significant improvement in HAM-A scores suggests that vortioxetine may not provide adequate symptom relief for GAD under the dosages tested.
2. **Study Population Characteristics:** By restricting eligibility criteria to individuals with lower MADRS scores, a substantial number of individuals who might experience higher severity anxiety symptoms were excluded, limiting the scope of the findings.
3. **Concurrent Medications:** Extensive exclusion criteria regarding prior medications potentially homogenized the study population, failing to represent the real-world complexity often seen in GAD patients.
4. **Neurobiological Mechanisms:** Vortioxetine's primary action on serotonin pathways may not align effectively with the multi-faceted pathophysiology of GAD, adversely affecting treatment outcomes.
5. **Small Sample Sizes and Statistical Power:** Despite initially enrolling 400 participants, a significant number of participants withdrew (n=101), resulting in a smaller final analytic cohort that reduced the power to detect clinically meaningful differences.

**Conclusion:** This trial underscores the challenges in treating GAD with vortioxetine, emphasizing the need for further investigations to explore alternative treatments. The findings suggest a reevaluation of the therapeutic approach to GAD within the context of existing pharmacotherapies, considering individual variability and multi-dimensional symptom profiles.

**Clinical Trial Registration:** NCT04567890 (not applicable).
