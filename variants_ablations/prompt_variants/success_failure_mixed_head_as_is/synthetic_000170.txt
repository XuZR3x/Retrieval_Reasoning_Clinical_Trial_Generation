**Study Title**: A Phase 3 Randomized Open-label Study Evaluating the Long-term Efficacy and Safety of Darunavir Boosted with Ritonavir in Treatment-Experienced HIV-Infected Patients with Resistance to Conventional NRTI Therapy

**Objective**: This study aims to assess the long-term efficacy and safety of darunavir/ritonavir in maintaining viral suppression in treatment-experienced patients with documented resistance to nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs). Given darunavir's robust genetic barrier to resistance and favorable safety profile, the study hypothesizes that the intervention will demonstrate superior virologic control compared to standard salvage therapy.

**Design**: This multicenter, open-label, Phase 3 study will randomize participants in a 2:1 ratio to receive darunavir/ritonavir or a conventional salvage therapy regimen consisting of NRTIs plus a protease inhibitor (PI) chosen based on resistance testing and treatment history. The study duration will encompass a 96-week treatment period, with efficacy and safety evaluated at multiple time points, enhancing monitoring and timely intervention.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Confirmed HIV-1 infection with documented resistance to at least one NRTI.
- Prior antiretroviral therapy for at least 6 months with evidence of viral load above 1000 copies/mL.
- CD4 count ≥ 200 cells/mm³.
- Ability to provide written informed consent and comply with study procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Prior use of darunavir or any investigational antiretroviral agents.
- Active opportunistic infections requiring systemic therapy within 30 days of enrollment.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding, or other factors that may compromise adherence to the study protocol.

**Primary Endpoint**: The primary endpoint is the proportion of patients achieving a viral load of <50 copies/mL by week 48, measured using a standardized RNA quantification assay.

**Secondary Endpoints**: 
- Assessment of immune reconstitution as measured by change in CD4 count from baseline to week 96.
- Evaluation of adverse events and discontinuation rates due to toxicity.
- Quality of life measures using validated instruments related to HIV treatment.

**Rationale**: Given darunavir's robust genetic barrier to resistance, its deployment in patients with complex resistance profiles and prior treatment failures presents a promising avenue for achieving sustained virologic suppression. As established in prior clinical trials (e.g., ANRS136 and GRACE), darunavir demonstrates excellent efficacy and tolerability, critical factors when addressing an increasingly multi-drug resistant HIV population.

**Statistical Analysis**: The analysis will be conducted according to the intention-to-treat principle. Statistical significance will be set at p < 0.05 for primary outcomes. Inter-group comparisons will be made using chi-square tests for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous variables.

**Safety Monitoring**: The study will include a Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) to oversee patient safety and data integrity throughout the trial. Regular safety assessments will be conducted at each follow-up visit, and adverse events will be classified using standardized reporting criteria.

**Conclusion**: This clinical trial proposes to analyze the efficacy and safety of darunavir/ritonavir as a robust option for treatment-experienced patients with NRTI resistance, capitalizing on its unique pharmacological properties to provide a tailored and effective HIV management strategy. Findings from this investigation aim not only to contribute to the body of evidence supporting darunavir’s utilization in challenging populations but also to improve patient outcomes in HIV treatment paradigms—particularly those characterized by complex resistance patterns.
