**Phase II Randomized Trial of Regorafenib in Combination with Immunotherapy for Patients with Advanced Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors**

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, open-label, randomized, phase II trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of regorafenib in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors in patients with advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs) who have progressed after standard therapy (imatinib and sunitinib). 

**Objectives:** The primary objective is to assess progression-free survival (PFS) in participants receiving regorafenib and an immune checkpoint inhibitor versus those receiving regorafenib alone. Secondary objectives include overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), and safety profile of the combination therapy.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Signed informed consent obtained prior to the initiation of any trial-specific procedures.
- Histologically confirmed GIST with documented progression on imatinib and sunitinib.
- Presence of detectable mutations in c-KIT or PDGFRα.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST 1.1, with at least one lesion ≥ 10 mm in longest diameter.
- ECOG performance status of 0-1, indicating fully active or restricted only in physically strenuous activities.
- Adequate hematological, hepatic, and renal function, assessed by:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L.
  - Platelet count ≥ 100 x 10^9/L.
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 × ULN and AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 × ULN (or ≤ 5 times ULN for patients with liver metastasis).
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 × ULN.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Major surgery within four weeks before the initiation of study drug.
- History of central nervous system metastasis or conditions impacting neurological function.
- Ongoing severe infections or hypersensitivity to regorafenib.
- Cardiovascular events such as myocardial infarction or unstable angina within last six months.
- Uncontrolled hypertension (systolic BP ≥ 150 mmHg or diastolic BP ≥ 100 mmHg).
- Previous exposure to immune checkpoint inhibitors.
- Patients currently receiving medications that may interfere with the pharmacokinetics of regorafenib.

**Intervention and Comparison:** Patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either:
1. Regorafenib (160 mg once daily) administered vertically on a continuous dosing schedule, with dual therapy including an immune checkpoint inhibitor administered at standard dosing, or
2. Regorafenib alone (160 mg once daily).

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Progression-free survival (PFS), defined as the time from randomization to the first documented disease progression or death from any cause.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), and an assessment of adverse events using CTCAE v5.0 criteria.

**Rationale and Biological Mechanism:**
The compelling rationale for incorporating regorafenib with immunotherapy in patients with GIST arises from its biological mechanism as a multi-kinase inhibitor, effectively targeting pathways critical to the survival of tumor cells driven by c-KIT mutations. The trials of regorafenib in GISTs have established its therapeutical efficacy, particularly in drug-refractory patient populations, optimizing treatment regimens through continuous dosing, and permitting robust patient stratification anchored on genetic profiles.

**Statistical Analysis:** Statistical significance for PFS will be determined using the Kaplan-Meier method, comparing treatment arms using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards models will be employed to adjust for potential confounding factors.

**Monitoring and Regulatory Compliance:** Rigorous monitoring of side effects and compliance with eligibility criteria will be conducted, ensuring that adverse effects are documented and managed promptly, thereby safeguarding participant safety and allowing efficacy of treatment to be reliably assessed.

This clinical trial is anticipated to provide critical insights into the combined modality of regorafenib with immune checkpoint inhibitors, potentially extending the therapeutic horizons in the management of advanced GISTs while underscoring the importance of genetic mutations in treatment stratification.
