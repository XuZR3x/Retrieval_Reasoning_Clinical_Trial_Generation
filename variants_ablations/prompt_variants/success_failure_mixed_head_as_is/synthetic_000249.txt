**Study of Nivolumab Plus Chemotherapy in Patients With Previously Untreated Advanced Stage Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Phase 2, Randomized, Open-Label Trial of Nivolumab in Combination With Chemotherapy for Patients With Previously Untreated, Advanced NSCLC.** 

The primary objective of this study is to investigate the efficacy of nivolumab combined with standard chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone in terms of objective response rate (ORR) in patients with previously untreated, advanced stage non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). 

**Background and Rationale**: Recent studies have demonstrated that nivolumab's immune modulation via PD-1 inhibition contributes to significant anti-tumor responses, particularly in patients presenting with advanced malignancies (Reason 1 - Strong Immune Modulation). Additionally, the therapeutic synergy between immune checkpoint inhibitors and conventional chemotherapy has been posited to enhance the overall therapeutic efficacy by further modulating the tumor microenvironment favorably (Reason 2 - Synergistic Effect). 

Inclusion of biomarkers for patient selection will optimize treatment outcomes, ensuring that only those with high likelihood of response to nivolumab will be included in this trial (Reason 3 - Optimized Patient Selection). Comprehensive biomarker analyses will be employed to stratify patients based on relevant genomic and immunological characteristics (Reason 4 - Comprehensive Biomarker Analysis), thereby ensuring a robust dataset for evaluating the therapeutic benefit. 

Endpoints will also include overall survival (OS) assessments and progression-free survival (PFS), reflecting on the prolonged survival benefits that have been previously associated with nivolumab therapy (Reason 5 - Prolonged Survival Benefits).

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced stage (IIIB/IV) non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).
- No prior systemic therapy or radiation for NSCLC.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1.
- Measurable disease as defined by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) 1.1.
- Adequate organ function as per laboratory criteria, including:
  - Absolute neutrophil count >= 1500/mm³
  - Platelet count >= 100,000/mm³
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Any history of or current central nervous system (CNS) metastases.
- Active autoimmune disease or conditions requiring systemic corticosteroids.
- Prior treatment with PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors.
- Presence of interstitial lung disease or significant pulmonary dysfunction.

**Trial Design**: This trial will assign subjects into two arms: one receiving nivolumab in combination with a standard chemotherapy regimen (carboplatin and pemetrexed), and the control arm receiving only chemotherapy. Treatments will continue for a maximum of six cycles, followed by maintenance nivolumab therapy (480 mg IV every 4 weeks) for those in the nivolumab arm.

**Endpoints**:
- **Primary Endpoint**: Objective response rate (ORR) as assessed by a central review committee utilizing RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- **Secondary Endpoints**: Progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety and tolerability profiles assessed through adverse event reporting per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

**Exploratory Objectives**: 
- Evaluation of tumor biomarkers including PD-L1 expression levels and mutation profiling (e.g., EGFR, ALK, KRAS) will inform future therapeutic strategies.
- Correlative studies on T-cell infiltration and cytokine profiling from tumor specimens will assess the immune microenvironment's relationship to clinical outcomes.

This randomized, open-label trial will contribute to the understanding of nivolumab's efficacy in a treatment landscape potentially enhanced through conventional chemotherapy, thereby reinforcing its role in first-line therapy for advanced NSCLC. The findings are anticipated to aid in establishing a therapeutic benchmark and pave the way for more tailored immunotherapeutic approaches in lung cancer management.
