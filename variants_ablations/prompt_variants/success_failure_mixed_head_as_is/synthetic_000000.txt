**Phase II Study of Taxotere in Combination with Pembrolizumab for the Treatment of Metastatic Triple-Negative Breast Cancer**

**Abstract:**
This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Taxotere (docetaxel) in combination with pembrolizumab in patients diagnosed with metastatic triple-negative breast cancer (mTNBC) who have previously received at least one line of systemic therapy. Given the dismal prognosis associated with mTNBC and the compelling evidence of immune checkpoint inhibitors enhancing anticancer responses, this study hypothesizes that the combination of Taxotere and pembrolizumab will yield improved clinical outcomes compared with historical controls utilizing chemotherapy alone.

**Study Design:**
The study is structured as an open-label, multi-center, phase II trial enrolling approximately 120 participants. Each patient will receive Taxotere intravenously at a dosage of 75 mg/m² on day 1 of a 21-day cycle, alongside pembrolizumab at a fixed dose of 200 mg on day 1 of each cycle. Treatment will consist of up to six cycles depending on patient tolerance and clinical response.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histological confirmation of triple-negative breast cancer, defined as the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2).
- Documented metastatic disease not amenable to curative therapy.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Presence of measurable disease according to RECIST v1.1 criteria.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with Taxotere or other taxanes.
- History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to Taxotere, pembrolizumab, or their components.
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment.
- History of non-infectious pneumonitis requiring steroids.
- Uncontrolled intercurrent illness.

**Response Assessment:**
Tumor response will be assessed every two cycles using radiologic evaluations in accordance with RECIST v1.1 criteria, categorized as Complete Response (CR), Partial Response (PR), Stable Disease (SD), or Progressive Disease (PD). Furthermore, overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) will be primary endpoints, measured from the commencement of treatment until documented progression or death from any cause.

**Safety Evaluation:**
Adverse events will be monitored continuously throughout the trial according to the NCI CTCAE version 5.0 criteria. A data safety monitoring committee will review the tolerability profile which will include assessment of hematological and non-hematological toxicities associated with both Taxotere and pembrolizumab.

**Rationale for Success:**
This clinical trial leverages several attributes expected to contribute to its success:
1. The synergistic effects of Taxotere and pembrolizumab may enhance the therapeutic index against mTNBC by concurrently targeting tumor cells and stimulating an immune-mediated anti-tumor response, thus potentially surpassing the efficacy of Taxotere alone.
  
2. The incorporation of a structured monitoring schedule, which includes regular assessments of objective tumor responses and toxicity profiles, will allow for timely intervention and adaptation of patient management strategies to optimize therapeutic outcomes.
   
3. Utilizing stringent inclusion criteria will create a homogenous cohort of patients with confirmed mTNBC, effectively minimizing variability in therapeutic responses and allowing clearer conclusions about treatment efficacy.

4. The outpatient administration model of the treatment regimen is anticipated to promote patient adherence and participation in the trial while reducing the burden of hospitalizations often associated with conventional chemotherapy regimens.

5. A focus on patient-centered outcomes, which includes the evaluation of quality of life and subjective assessments of symptom relief, is expected to foster higher patient satisfaction and compliance, thus enhancing the overall success of the intervention.

The overall goal of this trial is to advance the treatment landscape for patients with mTNBC by providing a potentially more effective therapeutic option that could significantly improve survival and quality of life.
