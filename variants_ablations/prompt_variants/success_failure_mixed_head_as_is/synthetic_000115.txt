**Clinical Trial Report: Assessment of Pioglitazone on Quality of Life and Renal Function in Patients With Chronic Kidney Disease**

**Title:** A Double-Blind, Randomized, Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy of Pioglitazone on Quality of Life and Renal Function in Patients With Chronic Kidney Disease but Without Diabetes

**Study Identifier:** PCKD-2024-01

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to assess the impact of pioglitazone therapy on quality of life (QoL) and renal function in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) who do not have diabetes. Secondary aims include evaluating the effects of pioglitazone on inflammatory biomarkers and assessing the safety profile of the given treatment in this unique population.

**Background:** Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is associated with significant morbidity and an increased risk of cardiovascular events. Despite advancements in kidney disease management, the need for novel therapeutic interventions remains critical. Pioglitazone is known for enhancing insulin sensitivity, but its efficacy in non-diabetic populations, particularly in CKD, lacks substantial evidence. Hence, we aim to investigate pioglitazone's potential benefits in these patients, although prior studies have indicated insufficient mechanistic efficacy due to the absence of insulin resistance in CKD patients.

**Hypothesis:** Chronic administration of pioglitazone will improve quality of life and prevent deterioration in renal function in non-diabetic patients with CKD, potentially through mechanisms unrelated to insulin sensitivity.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients aged 18 years and older.
- Diagnosed with Stage 2 to Stage 3 CKD (creatinine clearance 30-60 ml/min).
- Ability to provide informed consent.
- Baseline quality of life score (measured by EQ-5D index) below the median for CKD patients.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Current diagnosis of diabetes mellitus (type 1 or type 2).
- Evidence of significant cardiovascular disease (NYHA class III or IV).
- History of liver disease or alanine aminotransferase (ALT) levels greater than 1.5 times the upper limit of normal.
- Active malignancy or previous malignancy not in remission for at least 5 years.
- Concurrent use of thiazolidinediones or any experimental medications in the past 30 days.

**Methods:** Participants will be randomized to receive either pioglitazone (30 mg/day) or matching placebo for a period of 12 months. Quality of life will be assessed using the EQ-5D-5L questionnaire at baseline, 6 months, and study completion. Renal function will be monitored through serum creatinine and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) measurements at the same intervals. Additionally, inflammatory markers (e.g., C-reactive protein) will be assessed at baseline and endpoint.

**Endpoints:**
- Primary Endpoint: Change in QoL score measured by EQ-5D-5L from baseline to 12 months.
- Secondary Endpoint: Change in eGFR and levels of inflammatory biomarkers.

**Statistical Analysis:** Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline characteristics. Changes in QoL and renal function between groups will be evaluated using mixed-effects models. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Safety Monitoring:** Adverse events will be recorded throughout the study, with particular attention given to edema, heart failure, and any hepatic indicators. Safety assessments will include routine laboratory tests and patient-reported outcomes.

**Expected Outcomes:** A change in QoL and renal function is hypothesized to be superior in the pioglitazone cohort; however, it is anticipated that concurrent confounding factors such as polypharmacy and inherent disease characteristics may obscure results, similar to previous studies. The findings may reinforce existing evidence surrounding the limited efficacy of pioglitazone in the non-diabetic CKD population.

**Conclusion:** This study aims to further elucidate the role of pioglitazone in a population historically underrepresented in diabetes medications. Initial failures of pioglitazone in renal and cardiovascular contexts suggest that, despite theoretical benefits, practical outcomes may not translate into significant clinical efficacy, primarily due to non-ideal patient selection and mechanistic limitations in this population.
