**Title:** A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Vinorelbine in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Therapy for Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

**Objective:** This multicenter, open-label, Phase II clinical trial aims to assess the efficacy and safety of vinorelbine combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors (pembrolizumab or nivolumab) in patients diagnosed with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have previously received platinum-based chemotherapy. The study hypothesizes that the combination therapy will result in improved response rates and progression-free survival compared to historical controls.

**Methods:**
- **Study Design:** This is a multicenter, randomized, open-label, two-arm Phase II trial. Patients will be randomized to either Arm A (vinorelbine combined with pembrolizumab) or Arm B (vinorelbine combined with nivolumab). Participants will be equally distributed in a 1:1 ratio.
- **Sample Size:** Approximately 160 patients will be enrolled, with an estimated 80 patients in each treatment arm.
- **Treatment Regimen:**
  - **Arm A:** Vinorelbine 25 mg/m² intravenously on days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle plus pembrolizumab 200 mg intravenously on day 1 of each cycle.
  - **Arm B:** Vinorelbine 25 mg/m² intravenously on days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle plus nivolumab 240 mg intravenously on day 1 of each cycle.
- **Endpoints:** The primary endpoint is overall response rate (ORR) as per RECIST criteria. Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), safety, and toxicity profiles, alongside quality of life measures evaluated using validated tools such as the EORTC QLQ-C30.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced (stage IIIB or IV) non-small cell lung cancer.
2. Evidence of progression following at least one prior platinum-based chemotherapy regimen.
3. Patients aged 18 years or older, with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 to 1.
4. Adequate organ function, including liver (AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal), renal (serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL), and bone marrow (neutrophils ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L, platelets ≥ 100 x 10^9/L).
5. Written informed consent obtained.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior treatment with vinorelbine or immune checkpoint inhibitors.
2. Active autoimmune disease or history of severe allergic reactions to monoclonal antibodies.
3. Central nervous system metastases that are symptomatic or require immediate treatment.
4. Concurrent malignancies, other than non-melanoma skin cancer or in-situ carcinoma.
5. Pregnant or lactating individuals.

**Safety Monitoring:** A safety run-in phase will occur during the initial treatment period for the first 30 patients in each arm to carefully evaluate toxicity profiles before full enrollment. Continuous monitoring for adverse events will be performed in line with CTCAE criteria.

**Statistical Considerations:** The study will employ a stratified design to adjust for prior treatment regimens and disease stage to enhance the robustness of the results. Descriptive statistics will be utilized to report baseline demographics and outcome measures. Efficacy will be evaluated using Chi-square tests, while PFS and OS will be analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method.

**Anticipated Benefits:** The synergistic effect of vinorelbine, acknowledged for its antitumor activity and effective tolerability, in combination with immune checkpoint inhibition is expected to enhance tumor response rates in this refractory population. Additionally, this novel treatment paradigm aims to improve patients' quality of life through fewer toxicities and effective disease control, thereby creating a pathway for further exploration in clinical practice.

This study aims not only to demonstrate the clinical efficacy of vinorelbine in combination with immune therapies but also to provide foundational data that could enhance treatment protocols in advanced NSCLC, showcasing a potential paradigm shift in therapeutic strategy within this patient population.
