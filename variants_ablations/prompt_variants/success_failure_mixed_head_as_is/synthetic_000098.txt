**Bosentan Efficacy in Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension: A Multi-center, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Trial**

Background: Chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH) is characterized by impaired pulmonary blood flow due to organized clots within the pulmonary arteries. The use of bosentan as an endothelin receptor antagonist (ERA) could provide significant therapeutic effects by alleviating elevated pulmonary arterial pressure and improving hemodynamic parameters. This trial aims to investigate the efficacy of bosentan in enhancing exercise capacity in a well-defined cohort of patients with CTEPH.

Participants: The trial enrolled adults aged 18 to 75 years diagnosed with CTEPH, defined by a resting mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) ≥ 25 mm Hg and a pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) ≤ 15 mm Hg. A total of 180 participants were randomly assigned to receive either bosentan (125 mg twice daily) or placebo for a duration of 16 weeks, with follow-up assessments at regular intervals.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Male or female patients aged 18-75 years.
- Confirmed diagnosis of CTEPH as per the above-stated hemodynamic criteria.
- Patients with a 6-minute walk test (6-MWT) distance ≥ 100 meters and ≤ 400 meters at baseline.
- Patients stabilized on anticoagulation therapy for at least 3 months prior to screening.
- Signed informed consent obtained from all participants.

Exclusion Criteria:
- History of significant concurrent lung diseases or pulmonary complications.
- Patients undergoing surgical intervention for CTEPH within 6 months prior to the study.
- Administration of any investigational drug or device within 3 months preceding screening.
- Significant current liver dysfunction with ALT or AST > 2.5 times the upper limit of normal.
- Known hypersensitivity to bosentan or any of its excipients.
- Other comorbidities severely impacting study compliance or results.

Outcome Measures: The primary endpoint of the study was the change from baseline to week 16 in 6-MWT distance. Secondary endpoints included changes in hemodynamic parameters measured by right heart catheterization, quality of life assessments via the Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ), and evaluation of biomarkers related to inflammation and oxidative stress.

Results: The bosentan treatment group exhibited a statistically significant improvement in 6-MWT distance compared to placebo, with an increase of 70 meters (p < 0.01). Additionally, significant reductions in mPAP and pulmonary vascular resistance were observed in the bosentan cohort (mean mPAP reduction of 7 mm Hg, p < 0.01). Furthermore, improvement in MLHFQ scores suggested enhanced quality of life among patients receiving bosentan.

Safety and Tolerability: Bosentan was well-tolerated, with adverse events primarily of mild to moderate intensity. Monitoring for adverse effects highlighted no unexpected safety signals, and rates of treatment discontinuation due to adverse events were similar between both groups (bosentan 5% vs. placebo 4%).

Conclusion: The findings of this trial indicate that bosentan not only improves functional exercise capacity in patients with CTEPH but also demonstrates a favorable safety profile. These results reinforce the therapeutic potential of bosentan as an effective intervention for managing this condition, contributing to enhanced patient outcomes. The inclusion of comprehensive hemodynamic assessments and quality-of-life measures further adds to its clinical relevance, supporting evolving strategies for managing CTEPH. Further studies are warranted to solidify long-term outcomes and adaptations in therapy for impacted populations.
