**Title:** Investigating the Efficacy of Doxorubicin in Comparison to Dose-Dense Sequential Therapy in Patients with Early Stage Triple-Negative Breast Cancer: A Multicenter Phase II Study

**Abstract:** 
This clinical trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy of doxorubicin in the treatment of early-stage triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) using a dose-dense sequential chemotherapy approach compared to conventional dose-dense regimens. Unfortunately, the trial was unsuccessful in demonstrating a statistically significant improvement in pathological complete response (pCR) rates. The failure is attributed to the inherent heterogeneity of TNBC, high levels of chemoresistance, and challenges in patient stratification based on biomarkers.

**Introduction:** 
Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) represents a highly aggressive subtype of breast cancer characterized by the absence of estrogen receptors, progesterone receptors, and HER2 expression. Previous trials have underscored the variability in treatment responses associated with different molecular subtypes of breast cancer, particularly in TNBC, where doxorubicin has been traditionally utilized. This study sought to explore the efficacy of doxorubicin compared to a dose-dense sequential chemotherapy regimen, influenced by previous experiences delineating suboptimal outcomes linked to tumor biology variations.

**Methods:** 
This multicenter, randomized, open-label Phase II trial included treatment-naive female participants aged 18 to 70, diagnosed with early-stage TNBC (stage II and III). Eligible patients were randomized in a 1:1 fashion to receive either standard dose-dense doxorubicin or a sequential regimen composed of dose-dense anthracycline followed by taxane therapy. The primary endpoint was the rate of pCR as assessed by histopathological analysis post-surgery.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed early-stage TNBC (clinical stage II or III)
- Age ≥ 18 and ≤ 70
- Measurable disease (breast and/or lymph nodes)
- Adequate bone marrow function: WBC ≥ 3.0 x 10^9/L, neutrophils ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L, platelets ≥ 100 x 10^9/L
- Adequate renal function (creatinine clearance ≥ 50 mL/min)
- Normal hepatic function (bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN, transaminases ≤ 2.5 x ULN)
- Signed informed consent

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of prior chemotherapy or radiation therapy for breast cancer
- Presence of any distant metastases (M1)
- Significant cardiovascular disease (NYHA class II or higher)
- Concurrent malignancies (except for adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancers)
- Pregnant or lactating females
- Psychological or geographical circumstances limiting compliance with trial protocol

**Endpoints:** 
The primary endpoint was defined as the rate of pathological complete response (pCR) among participants determined by the presence of no invasive cancer in breast or lymph nodes post-surgery. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety profile assessment based on the occurrence of adverse events.

**Results:** 
The anticipated pCR rates failed to exceed the benchmark set by historical controls. Results indicated that both treatment arms demonstrated comparable pCR rates, thus rendering the sequential therapy approach ineffective. The study observed moderate-to-severe cardiotoxicity associated with doxorubicin leading to dose reductions and discontinuations.

**Discussion:** 
The results of this trial underscore the challenges associated with treating TNBC using doxorubicin, particularly in light of the tumor's chemoresistant nature and the lack of validated biomarkers for patient stratification. The failure to demonstrate improvement over conventional dose-dense therapy elucidates the necessity for tailored treatment strategies that incorporate genomic profiling to better identify responsive patient cohorts.

**Conclusion:** 
This clinical trial of doxorubicin in early-stage triple-negative breast cancer has yielded negative results, mirroring previously documented tendencies related to chemoresistance and heterogeneity inherent in this cancer subtype. Future investigations must prioritize the development of biomarkers that can accurately predict patient responses to anthracycline-based therapies to enhance treatment efficacy.

**Keywords:** Doxorubicin, Triple-Negative Breast Cancer, Chemotherapy, Pathological Complete Response, Clinical Trial, Chemoresistance.
