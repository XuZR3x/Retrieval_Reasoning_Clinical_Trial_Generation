**Erlotinib in Combination with Radiotherapy for Early-Stage Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: A Phase II Study**

**Study Overview:**
This multicenter, open-label, Phase II study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of erlotinib in combination with radiotherapy in patients with early-stage Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC). The study is designed to assess the overall response rate (ORR) and to improve local control of disease in patients who are considered operable but refuse surgical intervention. The treatment regimen will leverage the favorable therapeutic index established in previous erlotinib trials.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults aged 18 years or older.
2. Histologically or cytologically confirmed, early-stage NSCLC (T1-T2, N0) that is considered operable.
3. Patients must have an ECOG Performance Status (PS) of 0-1.
4. Adequate hematologic function defined as:
   - Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) ≥1.5×10^9/L.
   - Platelet count ≥100×10^9/L.
   - Hemoglobin concentration ≥9.0 g/dL.
5. Satisfactory renal function: Creatinine ≤1.25×ULN and Ccr ≥60 mL/min.
6. Patients must sign informed consent prior to initiation of treatment.
7. Confirmed EGFR mutations (exons 19 or 21).

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or targeted therapy for NSCLC.
2. Known hypersensitivity to erlotinib or any of its components.
3. Presence of any unresolved or uncontrolled medical conditions that would interfere with study adherence.
4. Pregnancy or lactation.
5. History of interstitial lung disease or other pulmonary complications.

**Study Design:**
Eligible patients will receive oral erlotinib at a dosage of 150 mg daily in combination with stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT). The radiotherapy regimen will consist of 3 fractions of 15 Gy to the primary tumor site administered over a week. 

**Endpoints:**
- Primary endpoint: Assessment of the ORR, defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) after 12 weeks of treatment, confirmed by imaging and clinical evaluation according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), quality of life assessments utilizing the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire, and toxicity profile analysis graded by the NCI-CTCAE v5.0 criteria.

**Safety Monitoring:**
Toxicity will be monitored closely with assessment intervals at 4-week intervals throughout the treatment period. Adverse events and serious adverse events will be documented and reviewed by an independent safety monitoring board to ensure patient well-being and protocol adherence.

**Rationale and Hypothesis:**
The rationale for investigating erlotinib in combination with SBRT lies in previous successful clinical trials showcasing erlotinib's selective inhibition of EGFR mutations in NSCLC, suggesting its potential synergistic effect with radiotherapy. Additionally, the systematic evaluation of therapeutic response and the capacity for individualized dosing adjustments, as seen in prior erlotinib studies, underpin this investigation's design, reinforcing the hypothesis that combining these modalities may lead to improved outcomes in early-stage NSCLC.

**Statistical Considerations:**
The study aims to enroll approximately 75 patients to provide adequate power for detecting a significant difference in the ORR compared to historical control rates of around 30% for radiotherapy alone in similar patient cohorts. Statistical significance will be determined at a significance level of α=0.05.

**Conclusion:**
This Phase II study aims to explore erlotinib's utility in improving clinical outcomes in early-stage NSCLC patients, potentially establishing a new therapeutic paradigm that harnesses its favorable profile while addressing local disease control.
