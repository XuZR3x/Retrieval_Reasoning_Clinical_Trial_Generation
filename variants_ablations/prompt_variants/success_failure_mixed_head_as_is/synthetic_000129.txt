**A Study of Vemurafenib Combined with Immunotherapy in Patients with Advanced BRAF V600 Mutation-Positive Melanoma: A Multicenter, Randomized, Phase II Trial**

This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vemurafenib in combination with immunotherapy in patients with advanced BRAF V600 mutation-positive melanoma. Participants will receive either vemurafenib plus an anti-PD-1 antibody or vemurafenib plus placebo until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult participants aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed metastatic melanoma (surgically unresectable Stage IIIC or IV) with documented BRAF V600 mutations confirmed by the Cobas 4800 BRAF V600 Mutation Test.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 to 2.
- Measurable disease according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) Version 1.1.
- Participants may have no prior systemic therapy for advanced melanoma or may have received prior treatment that has been completed at least 4 weeks prior to the first dose of study drug.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Evidence of active or symptomatic central nervous system lesions (stable CNS metastases treated with stereotactic radiosurgery are permissible provided they have been stable for at least 3 months before enrollment).
- History of or current spinal cord compression or carcinomatous meningitis.
- Concurrent use of any other anti-cancer therapy outside of the investigational drug regimen.
- Pregnant or lactating women.
- Clinically significant cardiovascular disease or event within the last 6 months, including myocardial infarction or symptomatic pulmonary embolism.
- Prior BRAF or MEK inhibitor therapy, with the exception of sorafenib.
- Participation in any other investigational drug study within 4 weeks prior to starting the study treatment, or having been previously treated with vemurafenib.

**Intervention Administration:**
Participants will receive vemurafenib at a standardized dosage of 960 mg orally twice daily combined with either an anti-PD-1 antibody or placebo, ensuring a consistent and effective dosing regimen. 

**Primary Objectives:**
- To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of BRAF V600 mutation-positive melanoma to the combination of vemurafenib and immunotherapy.
- To determine the safety profile and incidence of adverse events associated with the treatment regimen through systematic monitoring and regular assessments based on the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) Version 5.0.

**Secondary Objectives:**
- To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) compared to placebo.
- To perform biomarker analyses correlating treatment response with expression levels of PD-L1 among patients, thus augmenting the understanding of treatment mechanisms involved in the synergistic action between targeted therapy and immunotherapy.

**Rationale for Design:**
This trial is designed upon previously established successful parameters of vemurafenib studies, specifically addressing the diverse demographics and treatment histories of participants, inclusive of those who are treatment-naïve or have received prior treatment. Rigorous monitoring protocols will enhance confidence in interpreting efficacy and safety data, which is critical in this combination therapy.

Furthermore, by excluding participants with active central nervous system lesions and other confounding conditions, this study enhances the clarity of observed outcomes, thus facilitating a more robust correlation between drug administration and clinical efficacy in a representative population of advanced melanoma patients. 

**Study Duration:**
The anticipated duration of the study is 24 months, with a scheduled interim analysis after the initial 12 months to evaluate efficacy and safety outcomes.

**Conclusion:**
This trial aims to provide critical insights into the synergy between targeted therapy with vemurafenib and immunotherapy in the treatment of BRAF V600 mutation-positive melanoma, with a hope of improving therapeutic outcomes and survival rates in a challenging patient population.
