**Title**: Evaluation of Mirabegron Efficacy and Safety in Patients with Overactive Bladder Symptoms Refractory to Prior Antimuscarinic Therapy: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Multicenter Trial

**Objective**: To assess the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of Mirabegron 50 mg compared to placebo in patients with overactive bladder (OAB) who have previously demonstrated inadequate response to antimuscarinic medications.

**Study Design**: A double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial. Patients will be randomized to receive either Mirabegron 50 mg or matching placebo once daily for 12 weeks. The study will enroll a total of 180 participants across multiple sites.

**Primary Endpoint**: Change from baseline in mean number of micturitions per 24 hours as measured by a 3-day bladder diary period at week 12.

**Secondary Endpoints**: Change from baseline in the mean volume voided per micturition, daily urinary urgency episodes, and quality of life scores evaluated by the Overactive Bladder Questionnaire (OAB-Q).

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
1. Aged ≥ 18 years.
2. Diagnosis of OAB with symptoms for ≥ 6 months.
3. Documented experience of urinary frequency and urgency with or without incontinence, and dissatisfaction with at least one prior antimuscarinic treatment within the last 6 months.
4. The willingness and ability to comply with study procedures and complete the micturition diary accurately.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
1. Pregnancy or breastfeeding, or plans to become pregnant during the study.
2. Significant comorbid conditions including multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injuries, or other neurological conditions affecting bladder function.
3. Previously treated with Mirabegron or exhibiting hypersensitivity to Mirabegron or its excipients.
4. Uncontrolled comorbidities such as severe hypertension (systolic BP ≥ 180 mmHg and/or diastolic BP ≥ 110 mmHg), clinically significant cardiovascular disease, or severe renal impairment.
5. Recently undergone any bladder surgery or treatment involving anticholinergics, or receiving other medications contraindicated with Mirabegron.

**Expected Duration**: The total study duration is 15 weeks, which includes a 2-week run-in period for screening and stabilization, followed by 12 weeks of randomized treatment, concluding with a final assessment.

**Results and Discussion**: Preliminary analyses indicated no significant difference between the Mirabegron and placebo groups in the primary endpoint of mean number of micturitions per 24 hours. Furthermore, secondary endpoints assessing volume voided, urgency episodes, and patient-reported outcomes corroborated the primary findings. Adverse events were comparable between both arms, with discontinuation rates attributed predominantly to gastrointestinal and cardiovascular symptoms, albeit not exceeding anticipated thresholds. Importantly, the results suggest that the differential efficacy may stem from the specific patient population, highlighting prior antimuscarinic treatment responses, and further emphasizes the complexity of managing patients with refractory OAB. 

**Conclusion**: This clinical trial did not demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in OAB symptoms with Mirabegron compared to placebo in the defined population. The results suggest consideration of patient selection and treatment history as fundamental factors influencing therapeutic effectiveness of Mirabegron in this context, corroborating earlier findings regarding challenges posed by historical treatment dissatisfaction and comorbid neurological conditions that may affect treatment responsiveness. Further studies are warranted to elucidate mechanisms of action and optimize treatment pathways for patients with OAB.
