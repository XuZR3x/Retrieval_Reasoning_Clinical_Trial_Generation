**Failure Clinical Trial of QVA149: Assessment of Efficacy, Safety, and Patient-Reported Outcomes in Patients with Moderate to Severe COPD**

Study Title: A Multi-center, Randomized, Double-blind, Active Controlled Trial Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of QVA149 versus Umeclidinium/Vilanterol in Patients with Moderate to Severe Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

**Objective:** This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of QVA149 (110/50 μg once daily) compared to an active comparator, umeclidinium/vilanterol (62.5/25 μg once daily), in a population of patients suffering from moderate to severe COPD.

**Study Design:** A 24-week, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study, controlled with an active comparator.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male and female patients aged ≥ 40 years.
- Diagnosis of stable moderate to severe COPD as classified by the GOLD 2019 guidelines.
- Post-bronchodilator FEV1 < 70% and ≥ 30% of the predicted normal value, and a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.70.
- Smoking history of at least 10 pack years.
- Patients must have a modified Medical Research Council (mMRC) dyspnea scale score of ≥ 2.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with a history of asthma.
- Significant comorbidities such as cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled diabetes.
- Patients with recent respiratory infections within the last 4 weeks prior to screening or COPD exacerbations within 6 weeks prior to screening.
- Patients with contraindications to inhaled anticholinergic agents or β2 agonists.
- Other protocol-defined inclusion/exclusion criteria may apply.

**Primary Efficacy Endpoint:** 
- Change from baseline in FEV1 Area Under the Curve (AUC) over 0-24 hours at week 24.

**Secondary Endpoints:** 
- Change from baseline in patient-reported dyspnea using the mMRC scale.
- Frequency of COPD exacerbations as recorded in a patient diary.
- Safety endpoints including adverse events, laboratory evaluations, and vital signs.

**Rationale for Study Design:**
Previous trials of QVA149 identified critical gaps which could hinder efficacy, including insufficient performance compared to active comparators, variability in patient selection criteria, and a higher-than-acceptable adverse event profile leading to study discontinuation. The present study design aims to standardize inclusion criteria to mitigate variability and increase the validity of findings. The treatment period has been extended to 24 weeks to ascertain a more comprehensive evaluation of efficacy within chronic conditions. Furthermore, a focus on patient-reported outcomes aims to capture clinically meaningful benefits that might be overlooked in solely spirometric assessments.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Using a mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM), the analysis will incorporate treatment, baseline values, and various demographic and clinical factors as covariates. Missing FEV1 AUC0-24h data at week 24 will not be imputed.

**Study Status:**
The study was terminated prematurely after interim analysis revealed that QVA149 did not demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in FEV1 AUC0-24h relative to the comparator arm. The planned primary endpoint was not met, leading to doubts surrounding the efficacy of QVA149 in the target demographic of patients classified as having moderate to severe COPD. Concerningly, patient-reported outcomes indicated no improvement in dyspnea, while a heightened rate of adverse events led to significant discontinuation among participants.

This trial highlights the critical need for careful selection of endpoints and methodologies in evaluating the therapeutic efficacy of COPD treatments. Future studies should prioritize streamlined inclusion criteria and robust monitoring of adverse events to ameliorate dropout and bolster findings of efficacy.
