Study of GSK1120212 in Combination With Anti-PD-1 Therapy in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

A Phase III, Randomized, Double-Blind Study of GSK1120212 in Combination With Anti-PD-1 Therapy Versus Anti-PD-1 Therapy Alone in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Harboring BRAF Mutations

This Phase III study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of GSK1120212, a selective MEK inhibitor, when administered in combination with anti-PD-1 therapy in patients diagnosed with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who harbor BRAF mutations. Preclinical models indicate that MEK inhibition enhances the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade via modulation of the tumor microenvironment and promotion of T-cell infiltration, providing a strong rationale for this combination approach.

Objectives:
The primary objective will be to assess overall survival (OS) in patients receiving GSK1120212 in conjunction with anti-PD-1 therapy compared to those receiving anti-PD-1 therapy alone. Secondary objectives will include progression-free survival (PFS), frequency of adverse events, and overall response rate (ORR) as determined by RECIST 1.1 criteria. Exploratory endpoints will involve assessment of tumor gene expression profiles and immune cell infiltration correlating with treatment response.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Adults ≥ 18 years of age.
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced (Stage III/IV) non-small cell lung cancer with BRAF mutation status confirmed (V600E or V600K).
- No previous systemic therapy for metastatic NSCLC allowed; patients who have received adjuvant chemotherapy or radiation are eligible if recurrence occurs greater than 6 months post-treatment.
- ECOG performance status score of 0 or 1.
- Adequate organ function as defined by standard laboratory parameters.
- Patients must be willing to provide tumor tissue for biomarker analysis, either archival or fresh.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Active autoimmune disorders requiring systemic immunosuppressive therapy.
- History of severe adverse reaction to prior anti-PD-1 and/or MEK inhibitor therapies.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding; women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during the study.
- Presence of untreated symptomatic central nervous system metastases, or other significant concurrent medical conditions that may compromise study compliance.

This study will be conducted at multiple centers, facilitating broad patient enrollment and increasing the generalizability of findings. The rationale for the combination therapy derives from the successful integration of targeted therapy observed in the melanoma population where MEK inhibition led to significant therapeutic responses in BRAF mutation-positive tumors. Additionally, as this trial utilizes comprehensive inclusion criteria, it aims to identify a larger cohort of patients who could potentially benefit from this combinatorial approach, thus enhancing the chances of observing a meaningful improvement in OS.

Safety monitoring will be diligently performed throughout the trial duration. Data will be collected until the occurrence of sufficient overall survival events, enabling robust statistical analysis of results.

This robust study design, coupled with the promising findings in preclinical and early-phase clinical trials, supports a favorable outlook for the evaluation of GSK1120212 combined with anti-PD-1 therapy in this challenging patient population with advanced BRAF-mutated NSCLC.
