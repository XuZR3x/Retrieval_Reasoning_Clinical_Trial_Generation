**Negative Clinical Trial of OSI-906: A Phase II Study Evaluating OSI-906 in Combination with Chemotherapy for Advanced Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma**

A Randomized, Open-Label Phase II Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of OSI-906 in Combination with FOLFIRINOX in Patients with Advanced Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma

The primary objective of this study is to investigate the antitumor efficacy of OSI-906, an insulin-like growth factor-1 receptor (IGF-1R) inhibitor, in combination with the FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy regimen in patients with advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma, hypothesizing that the dual inhibition of IGF-1R and the chemotherapy agents may exert synergistic anticancer effects. Prior studies indicated a potential enhancement of chemotherapy efficacy with IGF-1R inhibition; however, this trial aims to determine the actual impact on progression-free survival (PFS) in a rigorously selected population.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Patients must provide written informed consent.
- Patients must be ≥18 years of age.
- ECOG performance status 0-1.
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma, stage IV or recurrent disease.
- Patients must have received no prior systemic therapy for advanced disease.
- Presence of measurable disease as per RECIST (Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors) v1.1 criteria.
- Adequate hematologic function defined as: 
  - ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³ 
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 9.0 g/dL
- Adequate hepatic function: 
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 X ULN
  - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 X ULN
- Patients must have normal renal function (creatinine ≤ 1.5 X ULN).

Exclusion Criteria:
- Prior treatment with an IGF-1R inhibitor or any investigational agents targeting the IGF-1R pathway.
- Previous chemotherapy or systemic therapy for pancreatic cancer.
- Concurrent malignancies, except for adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancer.
- Significant cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled hypertension.
- Known history of hypersensitivity to any components of the FOLFIRINOX regimen.

Study Design:
This study utilizes a randomized, open-label design, assigning participants in a 1:1 ratio to receive either OSI-906 combined with FOLFIRINOX or FOLFIRINOX alone. Treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal. The primary endpoint is progression-free survival, measured from the start of treatment to disease progression or death.

Expected Outcomes and Hypothesis:
The trial anticipated that the combination of OSI-906 with the FOLFIRINOX regimen would lead to an improved progression-free survival compared to chemotherapy alone. However, based on the previously mentioned reasons leading to negative outcomes in clinical trials, such as the insufficient overlap of target pathway inhibition, potential patient selection bias, and pharmacokinetic interactions, the study outcomes revealed no significant improvement in progression-free survival when compared to historical controls.

Results:
The trial of OSI-906 in combination with FOLFIRINOX was prematurely halted due to inadequate antitumor activity observed in early interim analyses. Patients exhibited varying levels of progression, indicating the possibility that the tumors had circumvented IGF-1R signaling through alternative survival pathways. Furthermore, the pharmacokinetic interactions between OSI-906 and the chemotherapeutic agents may have hindered the expected efficacy of the proposed combination therapy. Clinical endpoints showed time to progression and overall survival metrics did not differ notably from matched historical cohorts receiving FOLFIRINOX alone.

Conclusion:
While the combination of OSI-906 with FOLFIRINOX was hypothesized to enhance therapeutic efficacy in advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma, the trial ultimately demonstrated that such combinations may fail to translate into meaningful clinical benefits. The results underscore the complexity of tumor biology, emphasizing the need for a more tailored approach in future clinical trials targeting advanced pancreatic cancer pathophysiology.
