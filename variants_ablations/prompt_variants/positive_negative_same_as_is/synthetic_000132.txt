**Positive Clinical Trial of Cytarabine: Combination Chemotherapy with Cytarabine and Targeted Immune Therapy in Patients with Newly Diagnosed Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

**A Phase II Study of Induction (Cytarabine and Daunorubicin) and Targeted Immune Therapy (Cibisatamab) in Treating Patients with Newly Diagnosed Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

**Purpose:** This phase II trial aims to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of a combination regimen involving cytarabine, daunorubicin, and Cibisatamab, a monoclonal antibody targeting CD47, in patients with newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukemia (AML). This innovative approach seeks to enhance cytotoxic efficacy through synergistic action on leukemic cells and immune modulation.

**Rationale:** The integration of Cibisatamab into a standard chemotherapy regimen may leverage the immunotherapeutic properties against AML while enhancing the cytotoxic effects of cytarabine and daunorubicin. This strategy targets multiple pathways involved in leukemogenesis, reinforcing the potential for improved clinical outcomes in patients displaying varying cytogenetic and molecular profiles.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the safety and tolerability of Cibisatamab in combination with cytarabine and daunorubicin in newly diagnosed AML patients.
II. To evaluate the 30-day survival rate during the induction phase.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess clinical outcomes such as complete response (CR) rate, event-free survival (EFS), overall survival (OS), and cumulative incidence of relapse (CIR).
II. To monitor the incidence and severity of adverse events associated with the combination therapy.
III. To explore the interaction of pretreatment characteristics, including cytogenetic and molecular features, on clinical outcomes and therapeutic response.

**OUTLINE:**

**INDUCTION THERAPY:**
Patients receive daunorubicin intravenously (IV) on days 1-3 and cytarabine IV continuously over 168 hours on days 1-7. Cibisatamab is administered as an IV infusion on day 8 and subsequently on a weekly basis for the duration of therapy. Patients demonstrating responsive disease by day 21 will continue with consolidation therapy, while non-responders will receive a second cycle of induction therapy.

**CONSOLIDATION THERAPY:**
Patients achieving complete remission will undergo consolidation with high-dose cytarabine IV over 3 hours on days 1, 3, and 5, followed by Cibisatamab dosing per established protocol on subsequent weeks. This consolidation phase is repeated every 28 days up to 4 cycles in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**FOLLOW-UP:**
Patients will be monitored at regular intervals post-therapy for long-term outcomes, including assessments of functional recovery of natural killer (NK) cells and their potential role in sustaining remission.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Documented diagnosis of newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukemia based on WHO criteria, with molecular confirmation.
- No previous treatment for AML or myelodysplastic syndromes.
- Adequate organ function as determined by standard laboratory assessments.
- Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- History of central nervous system leukemia.
- Any active uncontrolled medical conditions that could interfere with treatment.
- Prior therapies including chemotherapy, except for specified emergency interventions.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**
The primary outcome will assess survival rates within 30 days of initiating induction therapy. Secondary endpoints will include rates of CR, EFS, and OS durations at specified follow-up intervals. Safety assessments will detail adverse events categorized by severity using standard grading scales.

**DESCRIPTIVE STATISTICS:**
Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics will be summarized. Response rates and survival curves will be analyzed using Kaplan-Meier methodology.

This clinical trial represents an advancement in the treatment of AML by combining established chemotherapeutic agents with innovative immunotherapy, aligning with the rationale of promoting enhanced therapeutic efficacy through multi-targeted interventions, thus supporting the clinical utility of cytarabine in complex medical regimens. Patients are followed for comprehensive evaluation of long-term outcomes, which may influence subsequent therapeutic strategies in AML management.
