<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>
        Prospective Evaluation of Carvedilol on Functional Capacity and Quality of Life in Patients with Heart Failure and Reduced Ejection Fraction (CHF-RE)
    </Title>
    <StudyDesign>
        Multicenter, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Randomized Clinical Trial over 24 months.
    </StudyDesign>
    <Objective>
        The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the effect of carvedilol on functional capacity, as measured by the 6-minute walk test (6MWT), and quality of life (QoL) in patients diagnosed with chronic heart failure (CHF) and reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF < 40%).
    </Objective>
    <Background>
        Chronic heart failure is associated with significant morbidity and mortality, especially among patients with reduced LVEF. Carvedilol has demonstrated multiple beneficial effects including vasodilation, reduction in myocardial oxygen demand, and modulation of neurohormonal activity, which may enhance functional outcomes and quality of life in this patient population. Clinical literature suggests that carvedilol can significantly improve LVEF and long-term survival outcomes in CHF patients. However, comprehensive assessments of functional performance and QoL have yet to be fully explored within a rigorous study design.
    </Background>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>1. Adult patients aged 18 to 80 years.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>2. Documented diagnosis of chronic heart failure with LVEF < 40% confirmed by echocardiography.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>3. NYHA (New York Heart Association) functional class II-III.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>4. Patients must provide written informed consent.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>5. Stable on standard heart failure therapy for at least 3 months prior to enrollment.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>1. Patients with obstructive coronary artery disease requiring revascularization.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>2. Any recent myocardial infarction or hospitalization for heart failure within the past 30 days.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>3. Significant comorbidities affecting QoL or functional capacity (e.g., severe renal or hepatic impairment).</Criterion>
        <Criterion>4. Patients with contraindications to beta-blockade (e.g., symptomatic bradycardia, asthma).</Criterion>
        <Criterion>5. Concurrent participation in any other investigational drug trial.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <Intervention>
        Patients will receive carvedilol starting at 3.125 mg BID, titrated up to a maximum tolerated dose of 50 mg BID over 12 weeks, compared to placebo in a 1:1 randomization ratio.
    </Intervention>
    <Endpoints>
        <PrimaryOutcome>
            Change in 6-minute walk distance from baseline to 24 months.
        </PrimaryOutcome>
        <SecondaryOutcome>
            Improvement in quality of life assessed by the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ) score at 12 and 24 months.
        </SecondaryOutcome>
        <TertiaryOutcome>
            Safety and tolerability of carvedilol therapy over the study duration, assessed via adverse event reporting and vital sign monitoring.
        </TertiaryOutcome>
    </Endpoints>
    <StatisticalAnalysis>
        Primary analysis will involve comparing the changes in 6MWT distance between the carvedilol and placebo groups using an Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) model, adjusted for baseline values. A two-sided significance level of 0.05 will be maintained.
    </StatisticalAnalysis>
    <PatientManagement>
        Patients will be monitored at baseline, 12 weeks, 6 months, 12 months, and 24 months for functional and clinical assessments, alongside standard laboratory tests.
    </PatientManagement>
    <EthicalConsiderations>
        The study protocol will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval will be obtained prior to initiation, and all patients will provide informed consent prior to enrollment.
    </EthicalConsiderations>
    <Conclusion>
        The anticipated outcome is that carvedilol will demonstrate significant improvements in functional capacity and quality of life among patients with chronic heart failure and reduced LVEF, supporting its use in standard clinical practice while addressing the multifactorial aspects of heart failure management.
    </Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
