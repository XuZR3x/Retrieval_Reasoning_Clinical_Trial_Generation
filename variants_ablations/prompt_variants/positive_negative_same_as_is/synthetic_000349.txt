**Positive clinical trial of montelukast: The CHILD Study (Cysteinyl Leukotriene Inhibition in Pediatric Allergic Rhinitis)**

A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Montelukast in Pediatric Patients Aged 2-12 Years with Allergic Rhinitis Induced by Seasonal Allergens

**1.1 Background**  
Allergic rhinitis is a common and chronic condition characterized by an IgE-mediated inflammatory response to allergens, leading to symptoms such as nasal congestion, sneezing, and itching. The condition significantly impacts the quality of life of affected individuals, particularly in pediatric populations. Montelukast, a selective antagonist of cysteinyl leukotrienes (CysLTs), has demonstrated therapeutic efficacy in managing similar allergic conditions, thereby warranting its investigation in the context of allergic rhinitis.

**1.2 Investigational Agent**  
Montelukast functions by inhibiting the CysLT1 receptor, thus effectively mitigating the pathological inflammatory responses associated with leukotrienes in allergic conditions. This unique mechanism addresses the core etiology of allergic rhinitis by blocking the bronchoconstrictive and inflammatory effects of the CysLTs, which are known to exacerbate symptoms.

**1.3 Study Design and Duration**  
The study will employ a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design involving 200 pediatric participants diagnosed with allergic rhinitis induced by seasonal allergens. The intervention will last for a duration of 12 weeks, with participants randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either montelukast 4 mg (for ages 2-5 years) or 5 mg (for ages 6-12 years) daily, or matching placebo.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Male and female participants aged 2-12 years with a clinical diagnosis of allergic rhinitis.  
2. Positive skin prick tests or specific IgE tests indicating sensitivity to seasonal allergens documented within the past year.  
3. Symptom scores for allergic rhinitis exceeding 6 on a validated scale for 3 consecutive days prior to randomization.  
4. Ability to comply with the study protocol as determined by the investigator.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. History of hypersensitivity or adverse reaction to montelukast or any of its components.  
2. Diagnosis of severe asthma requiring oral corticosteroids or frequent use of rescue inhalers.  
3. A concurrent diagnosis of other chronic respiratory conditions or severe allergic disorders.  
4. Recent treatment with other leukotriene inhibitors or immunotherapy within 4 weeks prior to enrollment.  
5. Any recent respiratory tract infection within 14 days of randomization.  

**Primary Objective:**  
To assess the change in total nasal symptom score (TNSS) from baseline to the end of treatment at week 12, comparing montelukast against placebo.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
1. Evaluate the impact of montelukast on quality of life using the Pediatric Allergic Rhinitis Quality of Life Questionnaire (PARQLQ).  
2. Assess the safety and tolerability of montelukast in the pediatric population.  
3. Analyze the need for rescue medications and the frequency of symptom-related medical consultations during the study period.  

**Study Monitoring and Assessments:**  
Consistent with the rigorous methodologies established in previous montelukast studies, daily symptom diaries will be utilized to record TNSS and any concomitant medication usage. Standardized quality of life assessments will be given at baseline, week 6, and week 12. Safety evaluations will encompass adverse event reporting, laboratory assessments, and physical examinations on a specified schedule throughout the trial.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Data will be analyzed using intention-to-treat principles, with appropriate statistical methodologies employed to compare the efficacy of montelukast against placebo. Descriptive statistics will summarize participant characteristics, while inferential analyses (e.g., ANOVA for repeated measures) will evaluate treatment effects.

This study aims to provide robust evidence regarding the efficacy of montelukast in alleviating the symptoms of allergic rhinitis and improving quality of life in pediatric patients, thereby enhancing therapeutic strategies targeting this prevalent condition.
