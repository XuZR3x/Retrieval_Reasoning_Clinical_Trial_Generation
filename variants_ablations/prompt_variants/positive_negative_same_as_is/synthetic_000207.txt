<clinicalTrial>
    <title>Improvement in Non-Motor Symptoms and Quality of Life in Early Parkinson’s Disease Patients Treated with Rasagiline: A Multi-center, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Trial</title>
    <objective>To evaluate the efficacy and safety of rasagiline in improving non-motor symptoms and overall quality of life in early-stage Parkinson's disease (PD) patients not receiving concurrent dopaminergic therapy.</objective>
    <background>The impact of Parkinson's disease extends beyond motor symptoms, significantly impairing quality of life through non-motor features such as depression, cognitive dysfunction, and sleep disturbances. Rasagiline, a selective and irreversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase B (MAO-B), has been noted for its favorable pharmacodynamic profile, including neuroprotective properties, which are anticipated to benefit non-motor symptoms. This trial aims to study these effects in a controlled population.</background>
    
    <studyDesign>Multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled.</studyDesign>
    
    <intervention>Rasagiline 1 mg once daily for 24 weeks compared to placebo.</intervention>
    
    <inclusionCriteria>
        <criterion>Male or female outpatients aged 40 to 75 years.</criterion>
        <criterion>Independently diagnosed idiopathic Parkinson's disease (PD) according to UK Brain Bank criteria.</criterion>
        <criterion>Hoehn & Yahr stage I-III.</criterion>
        <criterion>Evidence of relevant non-motor symptoms as indicated by a score of ≥ 10 on the Non-Motor Symptoms Scale (NMSS).</criterion>
        <criterion>Able to understand and provide written informed consent.</criterion>
        <criterion>Stable non-dopaminergic medication for at least 4 weeks prior to screening.</criterion>
    </inclusionCriteria>
    
    <exclusionCriteria>
        <criterion>History of hypersensitivity to rasagiline or similar compounds.</criterion>
        <criterion>Pre-existing psychiatric disorders including untreated depression (BDI score > 17).</criterion>
        <criterion>Presence of significant systemic or neurological diseases that would interfere with the study.</criterion>
        <criterion>History of seizures or significant cognitive impairment (MMSE < 24).</criterion>
        <criterion>Patients with serious health conditions affecting sleep (OSAHS, severe insomnia).</criterion>
        <criterion>Any current use of medications that may interact with MAO-B inhibitors.</criterion>
    </exclusionCriteria>
    
    <primaryOutcome>
        <measure>Change from baseline in the Non-Motor Symptoms Scale (NMSS) scores at week 24.</measure>
    </primaryOutcome>
    
    <secondaryOutcomes>
        <measure>Change from baseline in the Parkinson’s Disease Questionnaire-39 (PDQ-39) Quality of Life scale.</measure>
        <measure>Improvement in cognitive function as measured by the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA).</measure>
        <measure>Change in sleep disturbances evaluated using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI).</measure>
    </secondaryOutcomes>
    
    <results>
        <result>Rasagiline treatment resulted in statistically significant reductions in NMSS scores compared to placebo (p < 0.01), indicating improvement in non-motor symptoms, particularly in areas of sleep and cognitive function.</result>
        <result>Patients receiving rasagiline showed a notable enhancement in overall quality of life, with PDQ-39 scores improving significantly from baseline (p < 0.01).</result>
        <result>Quality of sleep as measured by PSQI scores improved significantly in the rasagiline group, with greater sleep efficacy and reduced nocturnal disturbances (p < 0.05).</result>
        <result>Safety profile remained favorable with no significant adverse effects compared to placebo group, enhancing patient retention and compliance throughout the trial.</result>
    </results>
    
    <conclusion>This trial demonstrates that rasagiline not only alleviates motor symptoms but significantly improves non-motor symptoms and quality of life in early-stage Parkinson’s disease patients. The neuroprotective properties, coupled with a favorable tolerability profile, reinforce the rationale for utilizing rasagiline in managing Parkinson’s disease comprehensively.</conclusion>
</clinicalTrial>
