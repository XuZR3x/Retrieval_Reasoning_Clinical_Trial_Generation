**Study Title**: A Phase II Randomized Controlled Trial of Plerixafor Combined with G-CSF for Enhanced Hematopoietic Stem Cell Mobilization in Patients with Multiple Myeloma Undergoing Autologous Stem Cell Transplantation

**Study Design**: This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind Phase II trial comparing the efficacy of plerixafor in combination with granulocyte-colony stimulating factor (G-CSF) versus G-CSF alone for mobilization of peripheral blood stem cells (PBSC) in patients diagnosed with multiple myeloma (MM) who are candidates for autologous stem cell transplantation.

**Objectives**: The primary objective of the study is to evaluate the proportion of patients achieving sufficient mobilization of CD34+ cells as evidenced by collection of ≥ 2.0 x 10^6 CD34+ cells/kg recipient body weight in no more than two leukapheresis sessions. Secondary objectives include assessing safety profiles, overall response rates post-transplant, and the time to neutrophil and platelet engraftment.

**Population**: The target enrollment will be 100 patients stratified into two arms: one receiving a combination of plerixafor and G-CSF and the other receiving G-CSF alone. Inclusion criteria consist of adult patients aged 18-65 years with a diagnosis of multiple myeloma, adequate renal function (serum creatinine <1.5 mg/dL), and Karnofsky performance status ≥70%. Exclusion criteria include prior allogeneic stem cell transplantation, active infection, and significant comorbidities that would preclude safe participation.

**Methodology**: Randomization will be conducted via computer-generated random numbers. All patients will receive G-CSF at the standard dose beginning on day -5 before stem cell harvest, while the plerixafor group will receive subcutaneous plerixafor (0.24 mg/kg) on the evening prior to the first apheresis session. The trial employs an intention-to-treat analysis, ensuring robust evaluation of the primary and secondary endpoints.

**Rationale**: The rationale for this clinical trial includes the previously established synergy between plerixafor and G-CSF in enhancing stem cell mobilization, as a CXCR4 antagonist allowing greater egress of hematopoietic stem cells from the bone marrow into peripheral circulation. Consistent with findings from earlier studies, this approach is anticipated to optimize patient outcomes by achieving a higher yield of CD34+ cells, directly contributing to the success of subsequent transplantation.

**Endpoints**: 
- **Primary Endpoint**: The primary outcome will ascertain the proportion of patients in the plerixafor plus G-CSF arm achieving ≥ 2.0 x 10^6 CD34+ cells/kg recipient weight following leukapheresis.
- **Secondary Endpoints**: These will include incidence of adverse events (graded per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events), rates of successful engraftment defined by neutrophil recovery >0.5 x 10^9/L by day +14 and platelet recovery >20 x 10^9/L by day +21, and overall survival rates at 1-year post-transplant.

**Data Analysis**: Statistical analyses will use Fisher's exact tests for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous variables, with a significance level set at p < 0.05. The sample size of 100 is calculated to detect an increase in mobilization success with adequate power.

**Conclusion**: This Phase II trial is poised to provide crucial insights into the role of plerixafor in conjunction with G-CSF for stem cell mobilization in the multiple myeloma patient population. Given the favorable safety profile and effective mobilization results noted in previous trials, the study aims to reinforce plerixafor as an integral component of pre-transplant care for these patients. The unique design allows for a comprehensive evaluation of clinical efficacy while ensuring adherence to stringent safety standards.
