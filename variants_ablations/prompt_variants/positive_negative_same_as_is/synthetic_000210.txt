**Negative Clinical Trial of Methotrexate: A Study to Evaluate the Efficacy of Methotrexate in Combination with Anti-TNF Therapy for Italian Patients with Moderate-to-Severe Ulcerative Colitis**

**A Phase III, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study of Methotrexate Combined with Anti-TNF Agents Versus Placebo in Patients with Moderate-to-Severe Ulcerative Colitis**

**RATIONALE:** The combination of methotrexate and anti-TNF therapy has been hypothesized to enhance clinical remission rates and mucosal healing in patients with moderate-to-severe Ulcerative Colitis. However, pharmacological limitations of methotrexate, along with patient population heterogeneity and potential drug interactions, may limit the success of this therapeutic strategy.

**PURPOSE:** This phase III trial aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of methotrexate combined with anti-TNF agents compared to placebo in achieving clinical remission and endoscopic improvement in patients with moderate-to-severe ulcerative colitis.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** To determine the clinical efficacy of methotrexate in combination with anti-TNF therapy in achieving clinical remission (defined by a Mayo score ≤2 with no individual sub-score >1) relative to placebo over 24 weeks.
- **Secondary:** To evaluate mucosal healing through endoscopic assessment, assess the safety profile in terms of adverse events, and identify pharmacokinetic parameters of the methotrexate and anti-TNF agents in this population.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study. Patients will be assigned to receive either methotrexate (15 mg weekly) in conjunction with an anti-TNF agent (adalimumab or infliximab) or corresponding placebo (placebo procedure remains identical) for a total duration of 24 weeks. Concomitant use of corticosteroids is permitted, and patients will be monitored for adverse events throughout the study. Follow-up assessments will occur every four weeks for 24 weeks.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 250 patients will be accrued for this study within 18 months.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Diagnosis of moderate-to-severe Ulcerative Colitis (Mayo score ≥3)
- History of inadequate response or intolerance to standard therapies including 5-ASA compounds or corticosteroids
- No prior treatment with methotrexate or anti-TNF agents

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: 18 to 75 years
- Performance status: ECOG 0-2
- No significant renal dysfunction (creatinine clearance <60 mL/min)
- No evidence of active infection at screening
- Women of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- Biologic therapy: No prior treatment with anti-TNF agents
- Chemotherapy: No prior treatment with immunosuppressive agents, including methotrexate
- Corticosteroids: No steroids within 4 weeks prior to baseline
- Surgery: No prior bowel resection within 6 months prior to the study

**Participation will not be permitted if the following conditions exist:**
- Previous exposure to methotrexate without tolerance
- Severe allergic reactions to anti-TNF agents
- Active systemic infections, malignancies, or liver conditions beyond mild transaminase elevation

**MONITORING OF OUTCOMES:** Clinical remission rates, rates of mucosal healing, incidence of adverse events, and pharmacokinetic profiles will be monitored continuously as per protocol, with primary analysis of efficacy occurring at the conclusion of the 24-week treatment regimen.

**Rationale for Expected Outcome:** The inherent pharmacological limitations of methotrexate, such as its immunosuppressive effects leading to complications, might contribute to inadequate disease management and heightened susceptibility to infections. Furthermore, issues related to patient population heterogeneity may confound efficacy results, as varying responses to methotrexate could arise due to differing disease severities and previous treatment histories.

**Results:** The study demonstrated that the combination of methotrexate with anti-TNF agents did not yield significantly superior clinical outcomes compared to placebo among the study population, indicating that inherent pharmacological limitations and patient variability may play significant roles in the failure of combination therapy approaches in moderating ulcerative colitis symptoms.

**CONCLUSION:** The results of this study suggest a lack of efficacy of methotrexate in conjunction with anti-TNF agents in the treatment of moderate-to-severe ulcerative colitis, supporting the need for alternative therapeutic strategies that may prove more beneficial in this patient population. Further investigation into optimized dosing regimens and the role of individual genetic markers in patient responses may be warranted.
