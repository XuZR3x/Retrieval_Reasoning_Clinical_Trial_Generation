**Positive Clinical Trial of Rivaroxaban: Efficacy and Safety of Rivaroxaban in the Prophylaxis of Venous Thromboembolism in Obese Patients Undergoing Bariatric Surgery**

**Background**: Bariatric surgery has become a crucial intervention for morbid obesity; however, it is also associated with a significant risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE). The use of direct Factor Xa inhibitors, such as rivaroxaban, presents a promising pharmacological profile to mitigate postoperative thrombotic events. Given its oral formulation, rivaroxaban offers an accessible and convenient alternative to traditional anticoagulants, enhancing patient compliance and potentially improving outcomes in this high-risk population.

**Objective**: To evaluate the efficacy and safety of rivaroxaban in the prevention of VTE in obese patients undergoing bariatric surgery compared to standard low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) prophylaxis.

**Study Design**: A multicenter, randomized, open-label clinical trial enrolling 300 obese patients (BMI > 35 kg/m²) scheduled for elective bariatric surgery. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either oral rivaroxaban 10 mg daily or subcutaneous LMWH (enoxaparin 40 mg daily) postoperatively for a duration of 35 days.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Adult patients (aged 18-65 years) eligible for bariatric surgery.
2. BMI ≥ 35 kg/m² with related comorbid conditions.
3. Provision of informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. History of active bleeding or high risk of bleeding events.
2. Renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 ml/min).
3. Allergies to rivaroxaban or related anticoagulants.
4. Previous major thrombotic events within the last six months.
5. Use of drugs contraindicated with rivaroxaban (e.g., strong CYP3A4 inducers or inhibitors).

**Methods**: Following surgical intervention, patients received their assigned prophylaxis. Both treatment groups underwent regular monitoring for the incidence of VTE, bleeding complications, and other adverse events, assessed at baseline, day 7, day 21, and at the end of treatment (day 35).

**Results**: A total of 300 patients were randomized, with 150 patients in each group. The incidence of VTE was significantly lower in the rivaroxaban group (2%) compared to the LMWH group (7%) (p<0.05), demonstrating rivaroxaban’s superior efficacy in this high-risk setting. Furthermore, there were fewer bleeding events reported in the rivaroxaban cohort (1%) versus the LMWH cohort (4%), reaffirming the safety profile of rivaroxaban in this patient population.

**Conclusions**: This study demonstrates the efficacy and safety of rivaroxaban in preventing VTE in obese patients undergoing bariatric surgery. The innovative pharmacological characteristics of rivaroxaban, combined with a robust clinical trial methodology, support its use as a first-line prophylactic agent in this specific high-risk population. The findings contribute significantly to the body of evidence supporting rivaroxaban's clinical utility in diverse settings and emphasize the need for protocol adaptations in VTE prevention strategies within bariatric surgical populations.

**Keywords**: rivaroxaban, venous thromboembolism, bariatric surgery, obesity, anticoagulation therapy, clinical trial.
