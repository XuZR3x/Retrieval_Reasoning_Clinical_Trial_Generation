**Negative Clinical Trial of Dabigatran: Dabigatran for Prevention of Venous Thromboembolism in Cancer Patients**

**Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Dabigatran in Preventing Venous Thromboembolism in Cancer Patients: A Phase II Randomized Controlled Trial

**Abstract:** The incidence of venous thromboembolism (VTE) in cancer patients necessitates effective anticoagulation strategies. This study evaluates dabigatran as a potential alternative to low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) for VTE prophylaxis in patients with active malignancy. The trial aimed to assess the efficacy and safety profiles of dabigatran compared to LMWH, focusing on thromboembolic and bleeding events. Results indicated a higher rate of major bleeding and VTE events in the dabigatran cohort compared to LMWH, suggesting limited clinical utility in this population.

**Study Design:** This was a multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial conducted at five tertiary cancer centers. Patients were allocated to receive either dabigatran or LMWH in a 1:1 ratio.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults aged 18-75 years with an active malignancy requiring anticoagulation.
2. Intermediate to high risk for VTE as per the Khorana criteria.
3. Provision of written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Current use of anticoagulants other than LMWH or scheduled surgery.
2. Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance <30 mL/min).
3. History of major bleeding or significant bleeding disorders.
4. Pregnancy or lactation.

**Sample Size:** A total of 200 patients were enrolled (100 in each group).

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary outcome was the incidence of symptomatic VTE over six months of treatment, assessed through ultrasound and clinical follow-up.

**Secondary Endpoints:** Secondary outcomes included major bleeding events, transfusion requirements, and adverse effects related to dabigatran therapy.

**Results:** The trial was terminated early due to safety concerns. The dabigatran group exhibited a statistically significant increase in major bleeding incidents compared to the LMWH cohort (11% vs. 3%). Moreover, 15 patients (15%) in the dabigatran group experienced symptomatic VTE, contrasting with only 5 patients (5%) in the LMWH group, suggesting significant shortcomings in the efficacy of dabigatran particularly emphasized by the inherent variability in patient response.

**Discussion:** The results highlight concerning aspects previously noted in dabigatran trials, including the insufficient exploration of serious adverse events, particularly with respect to the bleeding complications unique to this patient cohort with cancer. The limited follow-up duration also hindered the detection of cumulative VTE events, reiterating the need for robust long-term studies establishing safety profiles in heterogeneous populations, including those with concurrent malignancies.

**Conclusion:** The trial did not demonstrate the superiority of dabigatran over LMWH for VTE prevention in cancer patients. The findings underscore the importance of the thorough evaluation of anticoagulants in specific high-risk patient populations and raise substantial concerns regarding drug interactions and the need for contextual comparisons that dabigatran trials inadequately addressed.

**Funding and Ethics:** The study was granted ethical approval by the institutional review boards of participating centers and funded through institutional research grants. Informed consent was obtained from all participants.
