**Negative Clinical Trial of Aldesleukin: Aldesleukin Combined with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors for Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**A Randomized Phase II Study of Aldesleukin with or without Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** Aldesleukin (IL-2) has been employed to enhance the immune response against tumors by stimulating T-cell activation; however, its efficacy in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) has been limited. Immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 therapies, have emerged as pivotal players in NSCLC treatment. This study aims to investigate whether the combination of aldesleukin with immune checkpoint inhibitors can result in improved clinical outcomes.

**PURPOSE:** This trial investigates the safety and efficacy of aldesleukin combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors in patients diagnosed with advanced NSCLC, seeking to determine impact on overall survival, progression-free survival, and response rates compared to aldesleukin alone.

**OBJECTIVES:** 
- Primary: To compare the overall survival (OS) of patients with advanced NSCLC treated with aldesleukin in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors versus aldesleukin alone.
- Secondary: To assess progression-free survival (PFS), response rates (complete and partial), and toxicities associated with both treatment regimens.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, open-label, randomized phase II trial. Patients will be randomized into two treatment arms:
- **Arm I:** Patients receive aldesleukin (IL-2) subcutaneously on days 1, 2, and 8, in combination with anti-PD-1 therapy administered intravenously every 3 weeks for up to 12 cycles.
- **Arm II (control):** Patients receive aldesleukin (IL-2) subcutaneously on days 1, 2, and 8 without additional checkpoint inhibitor therapy.

Patients exhibiting disease progression will be monitored for an additional year after treatment culminates.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Adults aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed advanced or metastatic NSCLC.
2. Presence of measurable disease as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.
3. No prior therapy with IL-2 or immune checkpoint inhibitors.
4. Sufficient organ function defined by: 
   - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L 
   - Platelet count ≥ 100 x 10^9/L
   - Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) > 60 mL/min
5. ECOG performance status of 0-1.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Current autoimmune disease requiring immunosuppressive therapy.
2. History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to IL-2 or previous immunotherapy.
3. Concurrent treatment with systemic corticosteroids.
4. Active pulmonary infections or history of recent respiratory failure.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** 
Approximately 200 patients will be enrolled over 18 months.

**RESULTS:** 
This trial has obtained a 3-month follow-up data encapsulating 75 patients across both arms. Unfortunately, interim analysis revealed that the combination of aldesleukin and immune checkpoint inhibitors led to an overall survival rate of only 13% (p=0.35) when compared to 11% in the aldesleukin alone arm. Progression-free survival was minimally impacted, with the combined arm exhibiting a PFS of 4 months versus 4.5 months in the single agent arm. Additionally, a concerning trend in treatment-related adverse events was noted in Arm I, including increased incidence of cytokine release syndrome leading to treatment discontinuation in 25% of the cohort.

**CONCLUSION:** 
Based on the observed toxicity, modest survival benefit, and limited response rates in this patient population, the study concluded that the combination of aldesleukin with immune checkpoint inhibitors fails to significantly enhance therapeutic outcomes in advanced NSCLC compared to aldesleukin alone. Future investigations should explore alternative combinations or scheduling adjustments to maximize the respective mechanisms of action while minimizing immunosuppressive effects evident within tumor microenvironments.
