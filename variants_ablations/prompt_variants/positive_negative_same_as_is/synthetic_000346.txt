**Negative Clinical Trial of Brexpiprazole: A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind Trial of Brexpiprazole Versus Placebo for the Treatment of Major Depressive Disorder with Mixed Features**

**Study Protocol ID:** BXP-2024-001

**Objective:** The primary objective of this multicenter, randomized, double-blind clinical trial is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of brexpiprazole compared to placebo for the treatment of major depressive disorder (MDD) with mixed features in adults aged 18 to 65 years.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Males or females aged 18 to 65 years.
- Diagnosis of MDD with mixed features as per DSM-5 verified by the Mini International Neuropsychiatric Interview (M.I.N.I.).
- Participants must exhibit persistent depressive symptoms for a minimum duration of 6 weeks.
- History of an inadequate response to at least one prior antidepressant treatment, defined as < 50% reduction in depressive symptom severity on the MADRS score.
- Baseline MADRS score ≥ 22.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Current diagnosis of bipolar disorder or any psychotic disorder.
- Active substance use disorders within the past 6 months, or any other DSM-5 Axis I diagnosis that could confound results.
- Recent hospitalization for any psychiatric illness within the preceding 4 weeks.
- Known allergy or intolerance to brexpiprazole or related compounds.

**Primary Endpoint:** 
- Change from baseline in the Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) total score at Week 6, comparing brexpiprazole with placebo.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Change from baseline in the Young Mania Rating Scale (YMRS) to assess manic symptoms.
- Assessment of overall mental health stability using the Clinical Global Impression-Severity (CGI-S) scale.
- Safety and tolerability measured through adverse event reporting.

**Study Design:** 
This trial is designed as a double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study with a sample size of 450 participants, randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either brexpiprazole or placebo for a duration of 6 weeks. 

 **Statistical Analysis:** 
Efficacy analysis will be conducted using a mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) approach to account for variations in baseline characteristics and to control for potential dropouts, while adverse events will be analyzed descriptively.

**Results Summary:**
- The study concluded with findings indicating no significant difference in the MADRS scores between the brexpiprazole group and the placebo group (p=0.67), suggesting a lack of comparative efficacy in treating major depressive disorder with mixed features.
- Secondary outcomes similarly demonstrated non-significant changes. The brexpiprazole group exhibited no notable improvement in YMRS scores, indicating limited efficacy in managing manic symptoms secondary to MDD.
- An unfavorable adverse event profile including increased rates of akathisia and sedation led to reduced treatment adherence, thus potentially compounding efficacy-related challenges derived from participant dropouts.
- The placebo effect heavily influenced participant perceptions of improvement, obscuring objective assessments of pharmacological benefits.

**Conclusion:** 
The trial results underscore critical aspects that likely contributed to the overall negative outcome, comprising a lack of substantial improvements in a treatment-resistant population, heightened influence of placebo responses, and adverse effects that dampened treatment adherence. These findings highlight the complex interplay within mood disorders, indicating that brexpiprazole may not sufficiently address the multifaceted manifestations inherent in MDD with mixed features. Further research is necessary to explore alternative therapeutic approaches that might yield more favorable outcomes in this specific patient population.
