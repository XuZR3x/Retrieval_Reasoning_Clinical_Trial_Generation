**Negative Clinical Trial of Pregabalin: Efficacy of Pregabalin in the Management of Chronic Pain in Patients with Fibromyalgia**

A Phase 3, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Clinical Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Pregabalin in Adults with Fibromyalgia

**Objective:** The objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of pregabalin compared to placebo in reducing pain severity in patients diagnosed with fibromyalgia. The trial was designed to assess whether pregabalin can significantly improve clinical outcomes relating to pain and associated symptoms in this patient population.

**Design:** This study was conducted as a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial across several sites to ensure diverse patient recruitment. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18-65 years with a confirmed diagnosis of fibromyalgia based on the American College of Rheumatology criteria.
- Patients reporting daily pain levels of ≥5 on a Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) for pain for a duration of at least three months before screening.
- Individuals stable on current treatments for fibromyalgia (e.g., cognitive behavioral therapy, low-dose antidepressants) for no less than three months prior to enrollment.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Current or past substance abuse disorders, specifically opioid use, that could influence pain perception or response to treatment.
- Significant comorbid psychiatric disorders requiring emergent treatment or hospitalization.
- Patients with contraindications to pregabalin or those utilizing agents such as gabapentinoids in the preceding month prior to study commencement.

**Outcome Measures:** The primary outcome measure was the change in average daily pain severity recorded via the NRS over a period of 12 weeks. Secondary outcomes included assessments of sleep quality, functional impairment, and quality of life, quantified through standardized instruments such as the Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire (FIQ) and the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI).

**Sample Size:** The trial aimed to enroll 300 participants, but enrollment faced significant challenges attributed to the stringent inclusion/exclusion criteria and variability in patient responses to treatments.

**Results:** Despite the initial recruitment goal, only 45 participants were successfully enrolled over a two-year period. Issues related to inadequate patient enrollment were compounded by substantial patient heterogeneity, including differing prior treatment exposures and diverse symptom profiles within the fibromyalgia cohort. Additionally, the presence of comorbid conditions such as chronic pain syndromes and psychiatric comorbidities introduced confounding variables that significantly obscured the treatment effects of pregabalin.

Upon completion of the study, data were analyzed, revealing that pregabalin did not yield a statistically significant reduction in pain severity compared to placebo, casting doubt on its role in managing fibromyalgia symptoms. The overall risk-benefit profile of pregabalin for fibromyalgia remained unchanged despite the lack of efficacy observed in this trial.

**Conclusions:** This trial was terminated prematurely due to inadequate patient enrollment and the inability to achieve statistically meaningful results following the analysis of the enrolled subjects. In light of the challenges faced, including pharmacological mechanism constraints in addressing the multifaceted nature of fibromyalgia pain and elevated baseline pain severity among participants, the study underscores the critical need for refined inclusion criteria and a better understanding of the underlying mechanisms of action of pregabalin in heterogeneous patient populations.

Key Study Metrics:
- Enrollment Period: 24 months
- Total Enrolled Participants: 45 out of 300 targeted
- Primary Outcome: Mean change from baseline in NRS for Pain at Week 12
- Secondary Outcomes: Mean change in FIQ and PSQI scores at Week 12

In conclusion, the trial results suggest that pregabalin may not be an effective treatment option for fibromyalgia, warranting further research to explore alternative therapeutic pathways and enhance treatment modalities for this complex condition.
