### Positive Clinical Trial of Aspirin: Efficacy and Safety of Lower-Dose Aspirin in Patients with Recent Transient Ischemic Attack (ASTRIDE)

#### Study Overview
The trial, Aspirin Safety and THrombo-Reducing Efficacy in patients with acute Transient Ischemic Attack: A double-blind randomized controlled trial (ASTRIDE), aims to assess the efficacy and safety of lower-dose aspirin (20 mg twice daily) compared to the standard dose (75 mg once daily) in patients who have experienced a recent transient ischemic attack (TIA). This study postulates that lower doses of aspirin may provide improved antiplatelet activity without increasing the risk of gastrointestinal bleeding, while maintaining adequate endothelial function in a population at heightened vascular risk.

#### Background
Aspirin remains a cornerstone of antithrombotic therapy in the secondary prevention of ischemic events, particularly following TIA. A significant body of evidence indicates that the enhancement of antiplatelet activity when combined with other agents, such as ticagrelor, can markedly reduce the incidence of ischemic complications (Hannawald et al., 2021). Moreover, adopting lower doses of aspirin has been associated with lower complication rates from gastrointestinal hemorrhage, and maintenance of endothelial function due to selective COX-1 inhibition, making it an appealing intervention in patients prone to both ischemic and hemorrhagic events.

Additionally, the concept of aspirin resistance, which has been observed in a sizeable cohort of patients with cerebrovascular disease, suggests that lower doses may enhance pharmacodynamic responsiveness, thereby yielding additional clinical benefit in patients post-TIA (Zhang et al., 2020).

#### Objectives
The primary objective of the ASTRIDE study is to compare the efficacy of 20 mg of aspirin administered twice daily versus 75 mg once daily in reducing the composite outcome of recurrent TIA, ischemic stroke, and myocardial infarction over a 12-month follow-up period. Secondary objectives include assessing the rates of major and minor bleeding complications, patient adherence, and quality of life metrics as measured by standardized questionnaires.

#### Study Design
This is a multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled trial involving 1,500 participants across various neurological and cardiology clinics. Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either the 20 mg twice-daily aspirin regimen or the 75 mg once-daily standard regimen.

Inclusion Criteria:
1. Provision of informed consent prior to any study-specific procedures.
2. Male or female aged greater than 18 years.
3. Diagnosis of TIA within the last 30 days.
4. No documented contraindication to aspirin.

Exclusion Criteria:
1. History of cryptogenic stroke or cardioembolic stroke.
2. History of significant gastrointestinal bleeding or peptic ulcer disease.
3. Ongoing treatment with other antiplatelet agents or anticoagulants.
4. Significant hepatic or renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min).
5. Participation in another clinical study within the last 30 days.

#### Treatment and Intervention
The trial medication will be provided in a blinded formulation, where patients allocated to the intervention group receive capsules containing 20 mg of aspirin taken twice daily, and the control group receives capsules containing 75 mg of aspirin taken once daily. Both groups will be monitored bi-monthly to assess adherence and adverse event reporting.

#### Endpoints
The primary outcome is defined as the composite occurrence of recurrent TIA, ischemic stroke, and myocardial infarction by the end of the 12-month follow-up. Secondary outcomes include the incidence of major bleeding events, documented by clinical investigation, and patient-reported adherence measured by the Morisky Medication Adherence Scale (MMAS-8).

#### Significance
The results from the ASTRIDE trial will be pivotal in addressing both the efficacy and the safety profiles associated with lower-dose aspirin in the context of secondary vascular prevention after TIA. It is hypothesized that lower dosing may yield similar antithrombotic efficacy with a markedly reduced risk of gastrointestinal hemorrhage, thus enhancing the overall risk-benefit profile of aspirin therapy in this high-risk cohort.

The implications of this trial could reshape clinical guidelines surrounding anticoagulation therapy, especially in populations demonstrating aspirin resistance or susceptibility to unwanted adverse effects from conventional dosing strategies.

#### Conclusion
The ASTRIDE trial is designed to establish a novel dosing strategy for aspirin that emphasizes not only efficacy in the prevention of future vascular events but also prioritizes patient safety. The detailed investigation will inform clinicians on optimizing anticoagulation regimens for patients recovering from TIA and will contribute substantially to the ongoing discourse on antithrombotic therapy within stroke prevention.
