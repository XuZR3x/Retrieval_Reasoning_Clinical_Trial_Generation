**Negative clinical trial of dasatinib: A Multicenter Phase II Study of Dasatinib in Patients With Advanced Solid Tumors Expressing DDR2 or B-RAF Mutations**

**Purpose:**  
This multicenter Phase II clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of dasatinib in patients diagnosed with advanced solid tumors characterized by deleterious DDR2 or B-RAF mutations. Given the prior inconsistent outcomes and specific genetic targeting issues observed in earlier studies, this trial seeks to analyze both objective responses and overall survival in a more broadly defined patient cohort.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients aged 18 years or older with pathologically confirmed advanced solid tumors exhibiting DDR2 or B-RAF mutations, including but not limited to non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and melanoma.
- At least one measurable lesion as per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Patients must have experienced disease progression after at least one prior systemic therapy.
- ECOG performance status 0-2 with no significant intercurrent illness.
- Adequate organ function as defined by laboratory assessments.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Prior treatment with dasatinib or any other BCR-ABL inhibitors.
- High-grade cardiovascular conditions, including but not limited to uncontrolled hypertension or a history of myocardial infarction within the past year.
- Coexisting malignancies, excluding basal cell carcinoma or localized squamous cell carcinoma treated curatively.
- Presence of significant pleural effusions or ascites requiring therapeutic intervention.
- Other specific criteria related to QT interval prolongation, significant renal or hepatic insufficiency, and psychiatric illness impacting compliance.

**Primary Objective:**  
To determine the objective response rate (ORR) within 16 weeks of treatment initiation, defined as complete or partial response in accordance with RECIST 1.1.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
- To assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates.
- To evaluate the pharmacokinetic profile of dasatinib within the studied patient demographic.
- To analyze biomarker expression levels before and after therapy, including alterations in Src and related signaling pathways.

**Treatment Plan:**  
Participants will receive dasatinib at 100 mg orally once daily for 28-day cycles, continuing until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.

**Statistical Analysis Plan:**  
A single-stage Bayesian hierarchical model will be employed to assess the treatment effect with a target accrual of 100 participants. ORR will be analyzed using exact binomial confidence intervals alongside PFS and OS calculated via the Kaplan-Meier method.

**Anticipated Challenges and Considerations:**  
1. **Patient Demographics and Genetic Profile:** Previous trials have indicated that a predominant representation of patients with high-specificity mutations may limit the observed responses to dasatinib, potentially obscuring the intervention’s efficacy.
   
2. **History of Resistance to Prior Treatments:** Investigating the impact of prior treatments may complicate the interpretation of dasatinib's effectiveness, especially in subjects with acquired resistance mechanisms to other kinases.

3. **Strict Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria:** Though rigorously defined criteria are essential for trial integrity, they risk excluding patients with more favorable prognosis who could potentially benefit from dasatinib, creating bias in the sample representation and misaligning the application of the drug’s mechanistic actions.

4. **Variability in Pharmacodynamics:** The pharmacokinetic diversity among participants may pose challenges, influencing drug exposure levels and consequent therapeutic responses, thereby necessitating careful monitoring.

5. **Response Evaluation Metrics:** The deployment of stringent response evaluation metrics may inadvertently suppress detected clinical benefits, leading to conclusions that may not fully represent dasatinib's pharmacological potential.

**End of Trial Report**  
This investigation will present valuable insights into the therapeutic role of dasatinib for advanced solid tumors with DDR2 or B-RAF mutations, with the goal of aligning clinical practice with emerging evidence, notwithstanding historical setbacks reflected in previous clinical assessments.
