<clinical_trial>
    <title>A Double-Blind, Randomized, Multicenter Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Vildagliptin Versus Sitagliptin in Enhancing Glycemic Control in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and Suboptimal Glycemic Control on Metformin Therapy</title>
    <objective>The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy of vildagliptin compared to sitagliptin in improving HbA1c levels in patients with type 2 diabetes inadequately controlled on metformin therapy, with secondary outcomes evaluating safety, incidence of hypoglycemic events, and patient-reported outcomes.</objective>
    <background>Vildagliptin, a DPP-4 inhibitor, has been previously investigated in the context of type 2 diabetes management. However, emerging evidence indicates potential limitations in its clinical efficacy, particularly regarding inadequate glycemic control, altered gastric dynamics, and elevated hypoglycemia incidences in certain populations. This trial aims to address these concerns by contrasting vildagliptin with sitagliptin in a cohort of patients already on a stable metformin regimen.</background>
    <inclusion_criteria>
      <criterion>Adult patients aged 18 to 75 years with a diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus.</criterion>
      <criterion>Currently on a stable dose of metformin (≥1500 mg/day) for at least 3 months.</criterion>
      <criterion>HbA1c levels between 7.5% and 9.5% at screening.</criterion>
      <criterion>Body Mass Index (BMI) between 25 and 40 kg/m².</criterion>
      <criterion>Provide written informed consent.</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
      <criterion>History of type 1 diabetes mellitus or secondary diabetes.</criterion>
      <criterion>Cardiovascular diseases including myocardial infarction or unstable angina in the past 12 months.</criterion>
      <criterion>Renal impairment as indicated by eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m².</criterion>
      <criterion>History of hypoglycemic episodes defined as blood glucose < 70 mg/dL within the past month.</criterion>
      <criterion>Clinically significant gastric motility disorders.</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <endpoints>
      <primary_endpoint>Change from baseline in HbA1c at 24 weeks.</primary_endpoint>
      <secondary_endpoints>
          <endpoint>Frequency of hypoglycemic events as defined by blood glucose measurements.</endpoint>
          <endpoint>Quality of life assessed using the Diabetes Quality of Life Scale.</endpoint>
          <endpoint>Incidence of adverse events and withdrawals due to adverse events.</endpoint>
      </secondary_endpoints>
    </endpoints>
    <study_design>
      <description>Multicenter, double-blind, randomized, parallel-group study with participants assigned to receive either vildagliptin (50 mg) or sitagliptin (100 mg) daily for 24 weeks.</description>
      <description>Patients will undergo assessments at baseline and biweekly through the study period, with final assessments occurring at 24 weeks.</description>
    </study_design>
    <results>
        <finding>Results demonstrated no statistically significant difference in HbA1c reduction between vildagliptin and sitagliptin at the 24-week endpoint, indicating inadequate glycemic control in the intervention group.</finding>
        <finding>Analysis revealed a non-significant trend towards increased hypoglycemic events in the vildagliptin group compared to sitagliptin, failing to meet the pre-specified safety expectations regarding the incidence of hypoglycemia.</finding>
        <finding>Patient-reported outcomes indicated no significant difference in quality of life measures, suggesting that vildagliptin may not confer additional patient-centered benefits over sitagliptin in this cohort.</finding>
    </results>
    <conclusion>This study concludes that vildagliptin does not demonstrate superior efficacy or safety compared with sitagliptin in patients inadequately controlled on metformin. Given the identified challenges such as inadequate glycemic control and increased incidence of hypoglycemic events, vildagliptin may not be a preferred therapeutic option in this population.</conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
