**Positive Clinical Trial of Rasagiline: Efficacy and Tolerability of Rasagiline in Patients with Advanced Parkinson’s Disease**

**A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled, Phase III Trial Evaluating Rasagiline's Efficacy and Safety in Patients with Advanced Parkinson's Disease**

Rasagiline has been developed for the treatment of Parkinson's Disease (PD) as both a monotherapy and an adjunct therapy. This trial aims to evaluate the efficacy, tolerability, and safety of rasagiline in patients with advanced PD who are experiencing motor fluctuations despite optimal dopaminergic therapy.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 40 to 80 years, diagnosed with idiopathic Parkinson's Disease according to UK Brain Bank criteria.
- Patients presenting with Hoehn and Yahr stage ≥3 and ≤4 at screening, demonstrating significant bilateral symptoms.
- Patients currently receiving stable doses of levodopa and dopamine agonists for at least 8 weeks prior to enrollment.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with a significant psychiatric disorder, including major depression, as indicated by a Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) score greater than 14.
- History of hypersensitivity to rasagiline or other monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors.
- Patients with unstable cardiovascular disease, severe hepatic impairment, or significant renal dysfunction.
- Any concurrent treatment with non-permitted medications that may interact with rasagiline, including other medications with contraindications for MAO inhibitors.

**Primary Endpoint:**
Change from baseline to Week 26 in the Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) Total Score for Parts I, II, and III, which evaluates motor function, daily living activities, and non-motor experiences.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Assessments of sleep quality using validated measures such as the Parkinson's Disease Sleep Scale (PDSS-2) and polysomnographic evaluations to study sleep architecture and efficacy.
- Patient-reported outcomes on cognitive function, quality of life (QoL), and any adverse events experienced during the study, as these additional metrics affirm the multifaceted benefits of rasagiline.

**Rationale:**
The study is premised on the observed efficacy of rasagiline in previous trials initiated prior to the incorporation of robust patient stratification methodologies. By selectively targeting patients who display advanced symptoms and are maximally treated, confounding variables are minimized, allowing a clearer assessment of rasagiline's therapeutic utility. Additionally, the use of validated tools for both motor and non-motor symptom evaluation ensures the effectiveness and tolerability of rasagiline are thoroughly assessed.

**Monitoring and Results:**
The monitoring plan includes comprehensive documentation of side effects as well as active surveillance of secondary endpoints to encompass cognitive function and quality of life. Objective measures, combined with patient-reported outcomes, establish an enriched database to evaluate the drug's overall profile.

**Outcome Measures:**
Anticipated outcomes are a statistically significant reduction in the UPDRS score compared to placebo, indicating an improvement in both motor and non-motor symptoms. Furthermore, it is hypothesized that rasagiline will provide not only motor symptom control but also contribute positively to parameters such as sleep quality and cognitive function due to its selective MAO-B inhibition.

This study seeks to further solidify rasagiline's role in advanced Parkinson's Disease management and elucidate its comprehensive effects on both physical and non-physical aspects of the disease. Robust adherence to stringent inclusion criteria and validation of evaluations will project confidence in the findings, aimed at establishing rasagiline as a cornerstone therapy in advanced PD management.
