<clinicalTrial>
    <title>Evaluation of Eplerenone in Preservation of Cardiopulmonary Function and Muscle Strength in Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy: A Multicenter Randomized Controlled Trial</title>
    <background>
        Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is characterized by progressive muscle degeneration and associated cardiopulmonary dysfunction, leading to severe morbidity and mortality. Recent evidence suggests that myocardial injury precedes clinical manifestations detectable by echocardiography in DMD patients, necessitating early intervention. Eplerenone, a selective mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, may offer unique therapeutic benefits in preserving cardiac function and muscle strength, as noted in preclinical studies.
    </background>
    <objectives>
        The primary objective of this study is to assess the effectiveness of eplerenone in improving cardiac function and muscle strength compared to placebo in patients with DMD and preserved left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). Secondary objectives include evaluating the impact of eplerenone on pulmonary function and quality of life.
    </objectives>
    <methods>
        A total of 150 male subjects aged 7-18 years, diagnosed with DMD confirmed by genetic mutation analysis and with LVEF ≥ 45%, will be enrolled from multiple clinical sites. Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either eplerenone (25 mg/day) or placebo for 12 months.
        
        Comprehensive adherence will be monitored through weekly drug diaries. At baseline and at 12 months, subjects will undergo cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging to quantify left ventricular circumferential strain and assess myocardial injury via late gadolinium enhancement. Additionally, pulmonary function tests (PFTs) and muscle strength assessments using standardized dynamometers will be conducted. The quality of life will be measured through validated questionnaires at the same time points.
    </methods>
    <inclusionCriteria>
        <criteria>Boys aged 7-18 years with genetically confirmed DMD</criteria>
        <criteria>LVEF of 45% or greater</criteria>
        <criteria>Ability to cooperate with study procedures, including CMR</criteria>
    </inclusionCriteria>
    <exclusionCriteria>
        <criteria>Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 ml/min/1.73 m<sup>2</sup>)</criteria>
        <criteria>Known hypersensitivity to eplerenone or components of the study medication</criteria>
        <criteria>Current use of other mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists</criteria>
    </exclusionCriteria>
    <endpoints>
        The primary endpoint is the change in left ventricular circumferential strain from baseline to 12 months. Secondary endpoints include changes in pulmonary function tests, muscle strength, and quality of life scores between the two groups.
    </endpoints>
    <statisticalAnalysis>
        Statistical significance will be determined using Student's t-test for continuous variables and chi-square tests for categorical data. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Non-inferiority margins will be established based on previous literature regarding clinically relevant changes in cardiac function.
    </statisticalAnalysis>
    <expectedOutcomes>
        Based on prior studies suggesting eplerenone's efficacy in reducing myocardial fibrosis and preserving cardiac structure in early stages of heart involvement, it is anticipated that treatment with eplerenone will significantly improve cardiac function and muscle strength when compared to placebo. Additionally, this trial aims to address the prevalent challenges in managing DMD-related cardiopulmonary dysfunction and aims to provide a foundation for future therapeutic strategies.
    </expectedOutcomes>
    <conclusion>
        The results of this trial could potentially establish eplerenone as a cornerstone in the management strategy of DMD, emphasizing its role in mitigating cardiopulmonary decline and improving the quality of life among affected individuals.
    </conclusion>
</clinicalTrial>
