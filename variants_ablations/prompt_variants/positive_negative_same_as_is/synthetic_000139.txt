<clinical_trial>
    <title>Azithromycin for Enhanced Recovery in Chronic Rhinosinusitis: A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind Trial</title>
    <design>A Multi-center, Phase III Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Trial</design>
    <objective>
        The primary objective of this clinical trial is to investigate the efficacy of azithromycin in improving olfactory function and reducing symptom severity in patients diagnosed with chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS), specifically those without asthma, over a 24-week treatment period. 
    </objective>
    <background>
        Chronic rhinosinusitis is a prevalent condition characterized by prolonged inflammation of the nasal and sinus mucosa. Previous studies have suggested that azithromycin, a macrolide antibiotic with notable anti-inflammatory properties, may contribute to the modulation of sinonasal inflammation and improve patient outcomes. This trial aims to evaluate the utility of azithromycin in a patient cohort that exhibits minimal bacterial infection, thereby focusing on its therapeutic anti-inflammatory mechanism.
    </background>
    <methods>
        <participants>
            A total of 300 adult participants aged 18 to 65 years with definitive diagnosis of CRS, established through clinical evaluation and imaging techniques, will be enrolled. Eligibility is contingent upon the presence of symptomatic nasal congestion, facial pain or pressure, and a history of symptom duration exceeding 12 weeks. 
        </participants>
        <inclusion_criteria>
            - Meeting criteria for chronic rhinosinusitis based on diagnostic guidelines.
            - Symptom severity score ≥ 6 on the 10-point Nasal Symptom Questionnaire (NSQ).
            - No concurrent upper respiratory tract infection within the past month.
        </inclusion_criteria>
        <exclusion_criteria>
            - History of significant allergic rhinitis, nasal polyps, or asthma.
            - Recent use of systemic corticosteroids or antibiotics (30 days prior).
            - Any contraindications to macrolide antibiotics, including known allergy to azithromycin.
        </exclusion_criteria>
        <intervention>
            Participants will be randomized into two groups: one receiving azithromycin 500 mg orally once daily for 7 days, followed by 250 mg 3 times weekly for 24 weeks, and the other receiving a matched placebo regimen. 
        </intervention>
        <outcomes>
            The primary outcome will be the change in olfactory function assessed through the University of Pennsylvania Smell Identification Test (UPSIT) from baseline to 24 weeks. Secondary outcomes will include overall symptom relief measured via the NSQ, quality of life assessed using the Sino-Nasal Outcome Test (SNOT-22), and incidence of adverse events.
        </outcomes>
    </methods>
    <statistical_analysis>
        Statistical significance will be considered at a p-value of <0.05. Analysis will be conducted using an intention-to-treat approach. Changes in primary and secondary outcomes will be analyzed using appropriate statistical tests, including analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) for continuous endpoints.
    </statistical_analysis>
    <locations>
        The trial will be conducted across multiple sites, including tertiary referral hospitals and outpatient clinics in urban and rural settings, ensuring a diverse patient population and reinforcing the generalizability of the findings.
    </locations>
    <expected_outcomes>
        Given the established efficacy of azithromycin in reducing inflammatory responses, it is anticipated that this intervention will lead to significant improvements in olfactory function and overall CRS symptoms compared to placebo. The multi-faceted approach to outcome measurement will facilitate a comprehensive evaluation of azithromycin's therapeutic advantage in this patient cohort.
    </expected_outcomes>
</clinical_trial>
