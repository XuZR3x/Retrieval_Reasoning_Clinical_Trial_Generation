**Positive Clinical Trial of Bupivacaine: Efficacy of Bupivacaine Plus Adjuvant Ketorolac for Postoperative Pain Management in Patients Undergoing Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy**

**Background/Significance:** Postoperative pain control remains a significant challenge in patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy. The combined use of bupivacaine, a long-acting local anesthetic, and ketorolac, a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID), may optimize postoperative analgesia while minimizing opioid consumption and its associated side effects. Previous studies indicate that multi-modal analgesic approaches lead to improved patient outcomes and satisfaction. This trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of bupivacaine in conjunction with ketorolac for postoperative pain management.

**Methods:**

**Study Design:** This prospective randomized controlled trial will recruit patients scheduled for elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either a combination of bupivacaine and ketorolac (intervention group) or placebo (control group). Randomization will be achieved using a computerized allocation sequence.

**Intervention:** Patients in the intervention group will receive an intraperitoneal instillation of 0.25% bupivacaine (2 mg/kg) combined with ketorolac (0.5 mg/kg) prior to abdominal closure after laparoscopic procedure completion. In contrast, the control group will receive normal saline.

**Sample Size:** Based on a previous study indicating a mean postoperative VAS pain score of 6.0 with a standard deviation of 2.0, a sample size of 60 patients (30 per group) will be necessary to detect a clinically relevant difference in pain management, with a power of 80% and a significance level of 0.05.

**Data Collection:** Key data points will include:

- Demographic data (age, sex, BMI)
- Baseline pain score using Visual Analog Scale (VAS)
- VAS pain scores at 30 minutes, 1, 6, 12, and 24 hours post-operation
- Total opioid consumption in the first 24 hours
- Any reported adverse events or complications
- Patient satisfaction assessed through a 7-point Likert scale

**Data Analysis:** Statistical analysis will be conducted using SPSS software, employing T-tests and Chi-square tests to compare outcomes between groups. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered significant.

**Inclusion Criteria:**

- Adults aged 18-65 years undergoing elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy for benign conditions
- American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) status I or II

**Exclusion Criteria:**

- Known allergy to bupivacaine or ketorolac
- History of chronic pain conditions or opioid use
- Intraoperative complications such as conversion to open cholecystectomy
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding

**Hypothesis:** The administration of bupivacaine in combination with ketorolac prior to laparoscopic cholecystectomy will significantly reduce postoperative pain scores and opioid consumption when compared to placebo.

**Primary Outcome:** The primary endpoint is the assessment of VAS pain scores at 6 hours postoperatively.

**Secondary Outcomes:** Secondary outcomes include total opioid consumption within the first 24 hours, VAS scores at additional time points, and patient satisfaction ratings.

**Strength/Innovation:** By employing a multi-modal pain management strategy that integrates bupivacaine and ketorolac, this trial aims to enhance patient outcomes, reduce reliance on opioids, and improve post-surgical recovery. This is particularly significant in a clinical landscape increasingly focused on minimizing opioid use and optimizing pain management protocols.

**Ethical Considerations:** The study protocol has been approved by the institutional review board, and informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to study enrollment.

**Risks:** Potential risks associated with the local anesthetic and NSAID administration will be monitored, including local anesthetic toxicity and gastrointestinal bleeding.

**Benefits:** Successful outcomes from this trial may provide critical data to shift standard postoperative analgesia practices in laparoscopic cholecystectomy, establishing a model for future clinical pain management strategies.

**Time Frame:** The anticipated duration for patient enrollment is 12 weeks, followed by a data collection phase lasting an additional 6 weeks.

This study represents a forward-thinking approach to improve postoperative pain management leveraging the established efficacy of bupivacaine in conjunction with an NSAID, potentially leading to a noteworthy advancement in the management of postoperative pain in the context of laparoscopic cholecystectomy.
