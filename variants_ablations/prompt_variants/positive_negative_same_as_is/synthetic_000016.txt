**Title:** A Phase II Study of RAD001 Combined with Chemotherapy and Immunotherapy in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

**Objective:** This study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of RAD001 (everolimus) in combination with a standard chemotherapy regimen (Carboplatin and Paclitaxel) and a PD-1 inhibitor (Nivolumab) in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The primary endpoint will be Progression-Free Survival (PFS), with secondary endpoints assessing overall response rate, overall survival, and safety profile.

**Background:** Non-small cell lung cancer remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Despite advancements in systemic therapies, the prognosis for patients with advanced NSCLC remains dismal. RAD001, a potent mTOR inhibitor, has demonstrated promising pharmacodynamic properties that exert significant anti-tumor effects alongside modulation of immune responses. The rationale for this combination lies in RAD001’s ability to suppress tumor proliferation and enhance the therapeutic effects of both chemotherapy and immunotherapy.

**Study Design:** This is an open-label, multicenter, two-arm Phase II clinical trial.

- **Arm A:** Patients will receive RAD001 (10 mg orally once daily) in combination with Carboplatin (area under the curve [AUC] of 5) and Paclitaxel (175 mg/m² intravenously) once every three weeks for four cycles, followed by Nivolumab (3 mg/kg intravenously) every two weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
  
- **Arm B:** Patients will receive the same chemotherapy regimen without RAD001, followed by Nivolumab as described. 

**Eligibility Criteria:**
- Inclusion:
  - Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced NSCLC (stage IIIB or IV).
  - Measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
  - ECOG performance status of 0 to 2.
  - Adequate bone marrow and organ function as shown by specified laboratory values.
  - No prior systemic therapy for advanced disease.
  
- Exclusion:
  - Previous treatment with mTOR inhibitors or PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors.
  - Active central nervous system metastases or leptomeningeal disease.
  - Concomitant malignancies (except adequately treated skin cancers).
  - Significant comorbid conditions that could compromise participation.

**Monitoring Plan:** 
Patients will be monitored with routine laboratory assessments every 2 weeks during cycle 1, followed by assessment every 3 weeks. Imaging studies (CT scans) will be conducted every 8 weeks to evaluate treatment response. Comprehensive toxicity assessments will be conducted at each visit, utilizing CTCAE v5.0 criteria.

**Statistical Analysis:** With a target sample size of 120 patients, powered to detect a significant increase in PFS of at least 30% in Arm A versus Arm B, statistical significance will be set at p<0.05. Kaplan-Meier methods will be employed for survival analysis.

**Conclusion:** Considering the compelling rationale for RAD001's mechanism of action, particularly its synergy with both chemotherapy and immunotherapy, this study is designed to elucidate the potential clinical benefits of integrating RAD001 into the treatment paradigm of advanced NSCLC. The anticipated outcomes are expected to provide valuable insights into the optimization of therapeutic strategies for this patient population, potentially leading to improved survival outcomes.
