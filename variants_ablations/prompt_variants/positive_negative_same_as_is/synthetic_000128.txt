## Negative clinical trial of alisertib: Alisertib in Treating Patients With Advanced or Recurrent Endometrial Carcinoma
### A Phase II Evaluation of MLN8237 in Patients With Advanced or Recurrent Endometrial Carcinoma

This phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of alisertib in patients diagnosed with advanced or recurrent endometrial carcinoma who have received at least one prior line of systemic therapy. Alisertib, an inhibitor of Aurora A kinase, is hypothesized to exert antitumor effects by disrupting mitotic processes in rapidly proliferating tumor cells.

#### PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:
I. To assess the overall response rate (complete response [CR] + partial response [PR]) of alisertib in patients with advanced or recurrent endometrial carcinoma as per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST v1.1).
  
II. To evaluate the duration of response and progression-free survival (PFS) in this patient population.

#### SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:
I. To characterize the safety and tolerability profile of alisertib, focusing on the frequency and severity of adverse events as classified by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5 (CTCAE v5).

II. To explore the pharmacokinetics of alisertib in relation to patient demographics and biomarkers of response.

III. To assess molecular characteristics of endometrial carcinoma, including Aurora A expression levels, and correlate these with therapeutic outcomes.

#### OUTLINE:
Eligible patients will receive alisertib orally (PO) at a dosage of 50 mg twice daily (BID) on days 1-7 of a 21-day treatment cycle. Treatment will continue in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Patients will be followed up for response assessment and toxicity evaluations every 21 days until disease progression or for a maximum of 24 months.

#### INCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Patients must be histologically confirmed to have advanced or recurrent endometrial carcinoma, including but not limited to type I and type II endometrial cancers.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Patients must have received at least one prior line of therapy for advanced or recurrent disease.
- Measurable disease per RECIST version 1.1.
- Adequate hematologic and hepatic function (absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/μL, platelet count ≥ 100,000/μL, total bilirubin ≤ institutional upper limit of normal (ULN), AST/ALT ≤ 3 × ULN).
- Signed informed consent.

#### EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Patients with prior treatment with any Aurora kinase inhibitors.
- Presence of significant comorbidities that may interfere with participation in the study, including uncontrolled cardiac conditions, active infections, or chronic inflammatory diseases.
- Documented central nervous system metastases.
- Patients on concomitant medications that affect the absorption or metabolism of alisertib, including strong CYP3A4 inducers or inhibitors.

#### STATISTICAL CONSIDERATIONS:
The primary endpoint, overall response rate (ORR), will be analyzed based on the proportion of patients achieving CR or PR. A Simon two-stage design will be implemented; a confirmed response rate of at least 10% will be considered promising. If fewer than one confirmed response occurs in the first 10 patients, further enrollment will be terminated early.

#### RESULTS:
The anticipated outcome of the study was to determine whether alisertib could provide clinically meaningful responses in this patient population. However, the trial was terminated due to the following observed challenges and outcomes:

1. **Suboptimal Target Engagement:** Preclinical data failed to predict the response seen in patients. Compensatory signaling pathways and altered expression of Aurora A or associated molecular targets in endometrial carcinoma cells were identified, leading to insufficient target inhibition.

2. **Insufficient Efficacy Profile:** The overall response rate observed was below the pre-specified threshold, with only 5% of participants achieving an objective response despite expectations based on earlier-phase studies demonstrating activity in preclinical models.

3. **Heterogeneity in Patient Cohort:** The heterogeneity of endometrial carcinoma subtypes and prior treatment histories resulted in variable drug responses, making it challenging to define a consistent efficacy signal across the cohort.

4. **Treatment Modifications Due to Toxicities:** Significant toxicity rates, including neutropenia and gastrointestinal disturbances, necessitated frequent dose adjustments or treatment discontinuations, impacting the drug’s therapeutic potential.

5. **Inherent Drug Resistance Mechanisms:** Molecular profiling revealed that a subset of the tumors expressed resistance mechanisms, which might have led to diminished sensitivity to alisertib, as seen with other kinase inhibitors in similar malignancies.

In conclusion, the findings from this trial illustrate a consistent trend with prior investigations, whereby alisertib exhibited limited clinical efficacy in treating advanced or recurrent endometrial carcinoma. Future studies should consider stratifying patients based on specific molecular characteristics to better identify those who may benefit from Aurora kinase inhibition. The anticipated trials of alisertib in this field will be significantly revised to address identified shortcomings. Follow-up will be conducted for all participants to ensure safety and monitor long-term outcomes related to the treatment.
