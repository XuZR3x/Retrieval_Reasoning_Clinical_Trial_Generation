Negative clinical trial of ticagrelor: The Efficacy of Ticagrelor in Reducing Postoperative Thrombus Formation in High-Risk Cardiac Surgery Patients

This randomized, controlled trial seeks to evaluate the efficacy of ticagrelor as an antiplatelet agent in reducing postoperative thrombus formation in patients undergoing high-risk cardiac surgeries. Given the challenges surrounding residual thrombus burden attributed to inadequate efficacy and safety profiles observed in previous investigations, this study aims to provide clarity on the therapeutic potential of ticagrelor in a postoperative setting.

Participants scheduled for major cardiac surgery—specifically, coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) or valve surgery—will be enrolled. Following informed consent, participants will be randomized to receive either ticagrelor or placebo starting 24 hours prior to surgery and continuing through 30 days postoperatively. The primary endpoint is the assessment of thrombus volume within the cardiovascular apparatus using advanced imaging modalities such as transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) and postoperative computed tomography (CT).

Inclusion Criteria:
1. Adults aged 45-80 years scheduled for CABG or valve surgery.
2. Evidence of significant coronary artery disease or structural heart disease requiring surgical intervention.
3. Provision of informed consent prior to enrollment.

Exclusion Criteria:
1. Acute coronary syndrome or myocardial infarction within 2 weeks.
2. Prior history of significant bleeding events, including intracranial hemorrhage or major gastrointestinal bleeding.
3. Known hypersensitivity or contraindications to ticagrelor.
4. Current use of any other antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapies, except for aspirin.
5. Presence of hemodynamic instability or other comorbid conditions deemed unsuitable for the trial by the investigator.

The primary outcome measure is the total thrombus formation assessed via TEE and CT imaging, comparing thrombus volumes between the ticagrelor and placebo groups. Secondary endpoints will include incidence rates of major adverse cardiac events (MACE), including myocardial infarction, stroke, and all-cause mortality within 30 days post-surgery, as well as safety profiles focused on measurable bleeding events and medication tolerability.

Operationally, the trial design addresses prior concerns regarding study generalizability by including a more diverse patient demographic reflective of real-world surgical populations. It also seeks to mitigate the impact of genetic polymorphisms on drug metabolism by monitoring participants across different genotyping variations.

In light of previous findings indicating potential inadequacies in ticagrelor's efficacy, alongside genetic influences affecting pharmacokinetics, this trial’s objectives strive to dissect these intricacies further and gauge drug efficacy in a postoperative high-risk cohort, ultimately contributing to the ongoing discourse on ticagrelor's role in managing thrombotic events.

Randomization will be stratified based on the type of surgical intervention (CABG versus valve surgery) to better ascertain the drug's impacts across various cardiac procedures. The anticipated outcomes from this trial may lead to revised therapeutic indications or contraindications for the use of ticagrelor in clinically relevant scenarios, thereby informing future guidelines and clinical practice. 

This investigation is duly registered, and ethical approval has been obtained from the Institutional Review Board with provisions established for rigorous monitoring of adverse events and adherence to good clinical practice protocols.
