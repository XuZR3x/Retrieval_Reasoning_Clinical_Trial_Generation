**Positive clinical trial of rasagiline: Rasagiline in Patients with Mild Cognitive Impairment Associated with Parkinson's Disease**

**Title:** A Double-blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Rasagiline in Patients with Mild Cognitive Impairment Associated with Parkinson's Disease

**Background:** Parkinson’s disease (PD) not only affects motor functions but is also associated with cognitive decline, which can significantly impact the quality of life. The presence of mild cognitive impairment (MCI) in PD patients represents a critical challenge as it may precede more severe forms of dementia. Rasagiline, as a selective monoamine oxidase-B (MAO-B) inhibitor, not only enhances dopaminergic activity but may also offer cognitive benefits, warranting investigation in this demographic.

**Objective:** To evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of rasagiline compared to placebo in improving cognitive functioning, as measured by performance on the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), in patients with mild cognitive impairment due to Parkinson's disease.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with a confirmed diagnosis of idiopathic Parkinson's disease according to UK Brain Bank criteria.
- Individuals aged between 50-85 years.
- Patients meeting the criteria for mild cognitive impairment as defined by a MoCA score of ≥ 18 and ≤ 25.
- Patients on a stable regimen of dopaminergic therapy for at least 60 days prior to screening.
- Legally competent to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of neurologic disorders other than Parkinson's disease (e.g., dementia, cerebrovascular accident).
- Significant psychiatric disorders (such as major depression) as assessed by the Beck Depression Inventory with a score greater than 14.
- Severe motor fluctuations requiring additional symptomatic therapy within the last 90 days prior to baseline.
- Contraindicated medical conditions connected with MAO inhibitors, including severe hepatic impairment.
- Patients on cognitive-enhancing medications or those who have had a recent stroke or acute psychiatric episode.

**Methodology:** This study will employ a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design to ensure methodological rigor. Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either a fixed dose of rasagiline (1 mg daily) or matching placebo for a period of 24 weeks. Both patients and investigators will remain blinded to the treatment allocation throughout the study duration, minimizing bias and confounding variables.

**Primary Outcome Measure:** Change from baseline in the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) score at 24 weeks. The MoCA total score ranges from 0 to 30, with higher scores indicating better cognitive functioning.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:** 
- Change from baseline in UPDRS Part III (motor examination) scores at 24 weeks, assessing parkinsonian motor symptoms.
- Improvement in quality of life as measured by the Parkinson's Disease Quality of Life Questionnaire (PDQ-39) over the same timeframe.
- Safety profile assessment involving the frequency and severity of adverse events, including treatment discontinuations.

**Statistical Analysis:** Efficacy analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle. The primary endpoint will be analyzed using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) adjusting for baseline MoCA scores and other relevant covariates. Statistical significance will be determined at a two-tailed alpha level of 0.05.

**Expected Results:** Given the favorable pharmacological action of rasagiline and its potential for enhancing cognitive function through increased dopaminergic transmission, we hypothesize that patients treated with rasagiline will show statistically significant improvements in cognitive performance compared to placebo. Furthermore, the robust selection criteria and rigorous study design aim to minimize bias, enhancing the reliability of findings.

**Conclusion:** The proposed trial seeks to elucidate rasagiline's role beyond motor symptom management in early-stage PD, addressing the often-overlooked cognitive impairment aspect. This could have substantial implications for clinical practice, potentially advancing treatment protocols for patients with mild cognitive impairment secondary to Parkinson's disease.
