**Positive Clinical Trial of Aldesleukin: Aldesleukin and Entinostat in a Novel Protocol for Treating Metastatic Ovarian Cancer**

**Phase I/II Study of Aldesleukin Combined with Entinostat in Patients with Recurrent or Metastatic Ovarian Carcinoma**

**RATIONALE:** The combination of aldesleukin, an immune-response modulator, and entinostat, a potent histone deacetylase inhibitor, aims to synergistically enhance the immune-mediated anti-tumor activity against recurrent or metastatic ovarian carcinoma. By leveraging the distinct but complementary mechanisms of action—wherein aldesleukin may promote T-cell activation and proliferation while entinostat modifies chromatin to enhance gene expression relevant to immune response—this protocol seeks to improve overall therapeutic efficacy.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**

I. To assess the safety and tolerability profile of aldesleukin administered concurrently with entinostat in patients presenting with recurrent or metastatic ovarian carcinoma. (Phase I)

II. To evaluate the efficacy of the aldesleukin and entinostat combination by analyzing progression-free survival and overall survival rates compared to historical control data. (Phase II)

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**

I. To investigate the pharmacodynamics of entinostat in relation to aldesleukin therapy by assessing biomarker expression in circulating tumor cells and immune cell populations. (Phase II)

II. To explore the effects of the combination treatment on tumor metabolic activity as exemplified by [18F]FDG-PET imaging. (Phase II)

III. To analyze time-to-treatment failure and response rates as determined by RECIST v1.1 criteria. (Phase II)

IV. To establish the relationship between baseline immune profiling and treatment outcomes, focusing on CD4+, CD8+ T-cell activation markers, and PD-1/PD-L1 expression levels. (Phase II)

**OUTLINE:** This is a dual-phase, interventional study commencing with a Phase I safety evaluation of dose escalation followed by a Phase II efficacy assessment. Eligible patients will receive entinostat orally every two weeks starting day -14, followed by high-dose aldesleukin administered intravenously every 8 hours for a maximum of five days across multiple treatment cycles.

**EXPECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 60 patients with recurrent or metastatic ovarian carcinoma are projected to be enrolled, emphasizing diverse clinical backgrounds and stages of disease.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**

- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of recurrent or metastatic epithelial ovarian carcinoma, with documented measurable disease.
- Patients who have received up to three prior lines of therapy, including taxane/platinum-based regimens.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2 with a life expectancy greater than 3 months.
- Adequate hematologic parameters with absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³ and platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**

- Patients with active central nervous system metastases, uncontrolled comorbidities, or severe systemic diseases.
- Concurrent administration of other investigational drugs or anti-cancer therapies.
- Pregnancy or lactation; women of childbearing potential must utilize effective contraception for the duration of the study.

**OUTCOMES MEASUREMENTS AND FOLLOW-UP:** 

Patients will undergo regular assessments, including comprehensive blood work, imaging studies, and immune profiling at baseline, every three months, and upon completion of therapy. Statistical analysis will include Kaplan-Meier methods for progression-free and overall survival, and logistic regression for toxicity assessment.

This clinical trial seeks to elucidate the promise of combined aldesleukin and entinostat therapy, potentially heralding a new avenue of effective treatment for patients suffering from recurrent or metastatic ovarian carcinoma. The integration of biomarker analysis and PET imaging aims to enhance our understanding of treatment response and underlying mechanisms impacting patient outcomes.
