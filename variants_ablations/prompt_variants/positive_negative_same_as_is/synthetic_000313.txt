**Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Pregabalin in Managing Pain Associated with Fibromyalgia: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial

**Abstract:**  
This clinical trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of pregabalin (300 mg/day) in patients suffering from fibromyalgia, a chronic pain condition characterized by widespread pain and fatigue. Although previous studies suggested potential benefits in neuropathic pain management, this trial faced significant challenges that led to its premature termination.

**Study Design:**  
- **Duration:** 12 weeks  
- **Type:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled  
- **Centers:** Multi-center across several hospitals specializing in pain management  

**Background:**  
Fibromyalgia impacts a considerable number of patients worldwide and is often associated with increased pain sensitivity and comorbid psychological conditions. Pregabalin has previously shown efficacy in treating neuropathic pain; however, this trial set out to investigate its applicability in a fibromyalgia cohort, drawn by the promising hypotheses around central pain processing mechanisms.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Male and female participants aged 18 years or older  
- Clinically diagnosed with fibromyalgia as per the American College of Rheumatology criteria  
- Baseline pain score of ≥ 5 on the Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) over the past week  
- Willing to comply with study procedures, including regular assessments and medication adherence  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Any condition that may confound pain assessment, including major depressive disorder or unresolved substance dependency  
- Prior treatment with pregabalin, other anticonvulsants, or opioids within 30 days before screening  
- Patients with known hypersensitivity to pregabalin or its components  

**Endpoints:**  
- Primary: Change in pain score measured by the NRS from baseline to week 12   
- Secondary: Assessment of sleep quality via the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index and functional disability using the Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire  

**Results:**  
The trial was terminated prematurely due to the following reasons:

1. **Inconsistent Treatment Response:** Significant variability in subjects' responses was observed, with a notable subgroup lacking the expected analgesic effects. This disparity rendered averages misleading and obscured the assessment of both efficacy and safety.

2. **High Variability in Pain Assessment:** The reliance on subjective scales led to discrepancies in the measured efficacy. Patients exhibited diverse pain perception thresholds, complicating the interpretation of overall treatment impact.

3. **Limited Sample Size:** The trial faced recruitment challenges, with only 45 participants enrolled instead of the planned 150. The insufficient sample size severely undermined statistical power, making it impossible to draw valid conclusions about treatment differences.

4. **Stringent Exclusion Criteria:** Rigorous exclusions—targeting patients with opioid use or psychiatric conditions—resulted in a too-narrow sample. This potentially overlooked a substantial number of patients who might benefit from treatment.

5. **Insufficient Duration of Treatment:** Lengthy assessments may have been necessary to capture the delayed onset of therapeutic response. The 12-week duration was deemed inadequate to observe significant changes in pain relief or quality of life indicators among the cohort.

**Conclusion:**  
The trial's findings indicated that pregabalin could not be supported as an effective treatment modality for fibromyalgia when assessed under the outlined methodology. The premature termination underlines the necessity for rigorous patient selection and adequate follow-up durations to examine the drug's therapeutic potential for chronic pain syndromes fully. The implications suggest a need for further investigation, utilizing refined approaches that minimize recruitment bias and enhance the accuracy of pain assessment constructs.
