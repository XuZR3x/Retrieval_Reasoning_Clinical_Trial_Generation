#### Negative Clinical Trial of Rosiglitazone: A Study Evaluating Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and Chronic Kidney Disease

**Study Title**: Evaluating the Impact of Rosiglitazone on Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and Stage 3 to 4 Chronic Kidney Disease: A Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial

**Study Design**: This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial aims to assess the effect of rosiglitazone on cardiovascular outcomes in patients diagnosed with concurrent type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and chronic kidney disease (CKD) stages 3 and 4. The trial commenced on January 15, 2022, and was anticipated to enroll 100 patients but was terminated early on December 10, 2023, citing concerns regarding the safety profile of rosiglitazone and low patient recruitment.

**Objectives**: 
- Primary Objective: To evaluate the cardiovascular safety profile of rosiglitazone by measuring the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), including myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular-related mortality.
- Secondary Objectives: To analyze the impact of rosiglitazone on metabolic parameters, including fasting glucose levels, HbA1c, lipid profiles (total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, triglycerides), and inflammatory markers (hsCRP).

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
- Adults aged 40-75 years diagnosed with T2DM for no less than 5 years, with HbA1c levels between 7.0%-10.0%.
- Confirmed diagnosis of CKD stage 3 (eGFR 30-59 mL/min) or CKD stage 4 (eGFR 15-29 mL/min).
- Patients who are treatment-naive or have been on metformin or dietary management exclusively.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
- History of significant cardiovascular disease (prior myocardial infarction, coronary artery bypass grafting, or stroke).
- Active hepatic disease (elevated ALT/AST > 2.5 times the upper limit of normal, active hepatitis).
- Significant psychiatric illness affecting compliance.
- Patients currently using other antidiabetic medications (excluding metformin).
- Any contraindication to MRI due to pacemakers, metal implants, or claustrophobia.

**Intervention**: Eligible participants received either rosiglitazone (4 mg/day) or matching placebo. After an initial 12-week period, if the safety profile was assessed as acceptable, participants would have their dose adjusted to 8 mg/day for the remainder of the study duration.

**Outcome Measures**:
- Primary Outcome: Incidence of MACE within a period of 12 months, assessed using patient adverse event reports and follow-up by cardiology staff.
- Secondary Outcomes: Changes in metabolic parameters (glycemic control and lipid profile) and inflammatory markers (hsCRP) measured at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months via standard clinical assays.

**Statistical Analysis**: Continuous data were evaluated using paired t-tests for baseline versus follow-up measures, while incidence rates of MACE were compared using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis.

**Results**: The study faced considerable challenges during the recruitment phase. Ultimately, only 35 patients were randomized, far below the target sample size, resulting in an underpowered analysis. Furthermore, during the trial, cardiovascular event rates were notably higher among participants receiving rosiglitazone compared to placebo, emphasizing safety concerns. Patients exhibited worsening metabolic parameters and significant elevations in hsCRP levels, raising doubts about rosiglitazone's efficacy and safety in this unique population. There was a marked reluctance among potential participants, stemming from previously reported cardiovascular risks associated with rosiglitazone, which further compromised patient enrollment and adherence.

**Conclusion**: The trial was terminated due to significant safety concerns regarding the cardiovascular event profile associated with rosiglitazone in patients with T2DM and CKD. In view of prior studies highlighting elevated cardiovascular risks, combined with complications in recruitment from patient skepticism toward the agent, conducting a follow-up investigation into tailored diabetes management strategies in this difficult-to-treat population is warranted. 

**Implications for Future Research**: Future studies should consider the individualization of diabetes treatment approaches, particularly in the context of CKD, emphasizing efficacy and safety in vulnerable populations, as well as the inclusion of diverse co-morbid conditions in trial designs to better reflect real-world patient experiences. The restrictive nature of inclusion criteria should also be reevaluated to enhance generalizability and maximize recruitment potential.
