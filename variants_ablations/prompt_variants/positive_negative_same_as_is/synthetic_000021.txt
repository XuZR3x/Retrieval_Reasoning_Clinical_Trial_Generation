**Positive Clinical Trial of Panitumumab: A Study of Panitumumab in Combination with Oxaliplatin and Capecitabine in Patients with Previously Untreated Metastatic Colorectal Cancer**

**A Randomized Phase II Study of Panitumumab in Combination with Oxaliplatin and Capecitabine vs. Oxaliplatin and Capecitabine Alone in Patients with Untreated Metastatic Colorectal Cancer with Wild-Type KRAS**

**RATIONALE:** The multi-faceted mechanisms of action of panitumumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody targeting the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), present a compelling therapeutic option amid the context of standard chemotherapeutic regimens. This clinical trial aims to elucidate the enhanced therapeutic potential when panitumumab is administered alongside oxaliplatin and capecitabine, capitalizing on the synergistic mechanisms previously shown to elucidate improved disease control, particularly in patients harboring wild-type KRAS, who inherently demonstrate better responses to anti-EGFR therapies.

**PURPOSE:** The primary objective of this randomized Phase II trial is to determine whether the administration of panitumumab in conjunction with oxaliplatin and capecitabine results in an improvement in overall survival and objective response rate in patients with untreated, metastatic colorectal cancer.

**OBJECTIVES:**

- **Primary:** To assess the impact of combination therapy with panitumumab on overall survival and objective response rates in patients with wild-type KRAS status.
- **Secondary:** To evaluate progression-free survival, time to treatment failure, quality of life measures, and adverse event profiles across both treatment cohorts. Additionally, to analyze the impact of KRAS mutation status and other biomarkers in correlation with treatment efficacy outcomes.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter study. Patients will be stratified based on ECOG performance status (0 vs. 1) and prior adjuvant therapy (yes vs. no). Participants will be randomly assigned to one of the following treatment arms:

- **Arm I:** Patients receive oxaliplatin IV on day 1 and capecitabine orally for 14 days, with treatment cycles repeated every 21 days for 6 cycles.
- **Arm II:** Patients receive oxaliplatin IV on day 1, capecitabine orally for 14 days, and panitumumab IV over 1 hour on days 1 and 15, with treatment cycles repeated as above.

Stool and tissue samples will be collected for downstream biomarker analysis, specifically focusing on KRAS mutations assessed through genotyping methodologies at baseline and during treatment.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**

- Histologically confirmed metastatic colorectal adenocarcinoma.
- Presence of wild-type KRAS tumor status.
- Measurable disease defined per modified RECIST criteria.
- No prior systemic therapy for metastatic disease.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**

- ECOG performance status 0-1.
- Adequate hematologic function: ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³, Hb ≥ 10 g/dL.
- Normal liver function: AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 times ULN, total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times ULN.
- Life expectancy ≥ 3 months.
- No uncontrolled intercurrent illnesses or contraindications to chemotherapy.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**

- No prior cytotoxic chemotherapy for metastatic disease.
- No previous treatment with panitumumab or other EGFR inhibitors.
- Patients must be recovered from any prior therapies and not having received investigational agents within 30 days before enrollment.

**PRIMARY ENDPOINT: Overall Survival** 
Overall Survival is defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause, assessed using the Kaplan-Meier method. The primary analysis employs a log-rank test to compare survival distributions between treatment arms.

The expected outcomes from this trial endeavor to establish both improved therapeutic efficacy due to panitumumab's synergistic action and its role in a tailored treatment course for patients indicative of skillful biomarker-driven oncology practice. This study aims for substantial contributions toward delineating effective treatment strategies for metastatic colorectal cancer with an emphasis on personalized medicine principles.
