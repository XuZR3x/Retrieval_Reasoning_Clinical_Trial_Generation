Negative clinical trial of curcumin: Curcumin in Patients With Treatment-Resistant Major Depressive Disorder: A Phase II Randomized Clinical Trial

Curcumin in Patients With Treatment-Resistant Major Depressive Disorder: A Phase II Randomized Clinical Trial

This study aims to assess the efficacy and tolerability of curcumin in patients diagnosed with treatment-resistant major depressive disorder (MDD) who have not responded to conventional antidepressant therapies. Curcumin is hypothesized to have neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory effects, potentially alleviating depressive symptoms.

PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:
I. To evaluate the overall change in depressive symptom severity as measured by the Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) at week 8.

SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:
I. To assess changes in inflammatory biomarkers (C-reactive protein, interleukin-6) and their correlation with symptomatic improvement.
II. To evaluate the safety profile and tolerability of curcumin based on reported adverse events and laboratory assessments.

OUTLINE:
Patients receive curcumin orally (PO) at a dosage of 1000 mg daily for 8 weeks, in addition to their current stable antidepressant regimen. Patients will be monitored biweekly for depressive symptoms, safety assessments, and laboratory evaluations.

After completing the 8-week treatment regimen, participants will undergo follow-up assessments 30 days post-treatment conclusion.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Adults aged 18-65 years with a primary diagnosis of treatment-resistant MDD as per DSM-5 criteria.
- Currently on a stable regimen of conventional antidepressants for at least 8 weeks prior to enrollment.
- MADRS score of ≥ 20 at screening.
- Informed consent obtained from participants.

Exclusion Criteria:
- History of hypersensitivity to curcumin or turmeric.
- Current use of investigational agents or unapproved biomolecules within 30 days prior to enrollment.
- Active substance use disorder or severe alcohol use disorder.
- Significant concurrent medical or psychiatric conditions that may interfere with study participation.
- Pregnant or lactating women.

Overall Change in Depressive Symptoms
The primary efficacy endpoint will be measured as the change from baseline in MADRS scores from randomization to week 8. Secondary endpoints will explore correlations between changes in inflammatory biomarkers and depressive symptom severity.

Despite methodological rigor and comprehensive patient assessments, this trial experienced several obstacles that contributed to its negative findings:

1. **Biological Mechanism Limitations**: The complex neurobiological underpinnings of MDD were not adequately addressed; curcumin's multifaceted pathways may not sufficiently target the dysregulated neurotransmitter systems or neuroinflammation present in treatment-resistant populations.

2. **Dosing Inefficiency**: The chosen oral dosage of curcumin may not have achieved bioavailability necessary for therapeutic engagement within the central nervous system. Several participants reported gastrointestinal disturbances, complicating adherence.

3. **Study Population Variability**: The heterogeneity in DSM-5 MDD diagnoses, including varying degrees of treatment resistance, potentially introduced significant variability in individual responses, obscuring any potential therapeutic effects attributed to curcumin.

4. **Short Study Duration**: An 8-week intervention may inadequately capture the delayed therapeutic benefits believed to be offered by curcumin due to the chronic nature of MDD, which requires longer evaluation periods.

5. **Insufficient Inflammatory Biomarker Assessment**: Although the study aimed to correlate clinical outcomes with inflammatory biomarker changes, a lack of baseline inflammatory profiling hindered the ability to establish causative links between curcumin administration and symptom resolution.

Outcome measurements and data analysis suggested no significant differences in symptom improvement versus control after the study period, leading to the conclusion that the adjunctive use of curcumin is not supported within the context of treatment-resistant major depressive disorder. Further investigations exploring alternative methodologies and formulations are warranted.
