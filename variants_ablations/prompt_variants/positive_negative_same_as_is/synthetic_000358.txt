<negative_clinical_trial>
  <title>Efficacy and Safety of Nilotinib in Patients With Advanced Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumors (GIST) Post-Resistance to Imatinib and Sunitinib: A Multi-Center, Randomized, Open-Label Clinical Trial</title>
  
  <objective>
    This trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of nilotinib in patients with advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) who have demonstrated resistance to imatinib and sunitinib. The trial seeks to establish whether nilotinib can induce a meaningful clinical response in this patient population characterized by prior treatment failures due to inherent or acquired resistance mechanisms.
  </objective>

  <background>
    Prior studies have indicated a negative impact on clinical outcomes when nilotinib was administered to populations exhibiting resistance to multiple tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs). The underlying resistance mechanisms may limit the therapeutic efficacy of nilotinib, rendering it ineffective in this specific cohort. The restricted diversity in patient selection and the suboptimal dosing regimens in previous trials contributed to inconclusive findings regarding the potential of nilotinib to achieve a beneficial response in GIST patients who have already failed both imatinib and sunitinib therapies.
  </background>
  
  <methods>
    <design>
      A randomized, open-label, multi-center trial assessing the efficacy of nilotinib compared with best supportive care in adult patients with GIST who have progressed on or demonstrated intolerance to both imatinib and sunitinib.
    </design>
    
    <study_population>
      <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Age ≥ 18 years</criterion>
        <criterion>Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced GIST with progression following imatinib and sunitinib therapy</criterion>
        <criterion>Measurable disease as per RECIST criteria</criterion>
        <criterion>World Health Organization (WHO) performance status of 0-2</criterion>
        <criterion>Adequate organ function as defined by specific laboratory parameters</criterion>
      </inclusion_criteria>
      <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Prior treatment with nilotinib or other investigational agents</criterion>
        <criterion>Evidence of major comorbidities that could interfere with trial participation</criterion>
        <criterion>Concurrent treatment with any drugs known to prolong QT interval</criterion>
        <criterion>Pregnancy or breastfeeding</criterion>
      </exclusion_criteria>
    </study_population>

    <treatment_protocol>
      <nilotinib_dosing>Patients will receive nilotinib 400 mg orally twice daily for a duration of 12 weeks.</nilotinib_dosing>
      <control_arm>Best supportive care including palliative measures as appropriate.</control_arm>
    </treatment_protocol>
    
    <endpoints>
      <primary_endpoint>Progression-free survival (PFS) at 12 weeks</primary_endpoint>
      <secondary_endpoints>Overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), safety profile assessed by adverse events during treatment</secondary_endpoints>
    </endpoints>
  </methods>

  <results>
    The study enrolled a total of 60 patients across multiple centers. The efficacy analysis revealed a lack of significant improvement in PFS when comparing nilotinib to best supportive care, with only 15% of patients achieving a stable disease status at the conclusion of the study. Suboptimal patient characteristics, including extensive prior exposure to multiple TKI therapies and the presence of resistant mutations, contributed to nilotinib’s limited therapeutic efficacy. Additionally, the 12-week duration of treatment appeared insufficient to facilitate any observable clinical responses in this heavily pre-treated cohort. A comprehensive evaluation of molecular responses indicated persistence of high Bcr-Abl transcript levels, signifying continuing disease progression in most participants.

    Adverse events, primarily consisting of hematological toxicities and cardiovascular events, were frequent, necessitating discontinuation of treatment for some participants. Results indicated no significant advantages or additional clinical benefits from the administration of nilotinib over best supportive care.
  </results>

  <conclusion>
    The trial findings underscore the necessity for cautious patient selection criteria and highlight the consequences of inherent resistance mechanisms unresponsive to nilotinib therapy. The results suggest that nilotinib does not confer additional clinical benefit in patients with GIST who have demonstrated resistance to both imatinib and sunitinib, reinforcing the importance of tailored therapeutic strategies in managing advanced GIST. In light of these findings, continued exploration of alternative treatment options remains essential to optimizing patient outcomes within this challenging clinical landscape.
  </conclusion>
