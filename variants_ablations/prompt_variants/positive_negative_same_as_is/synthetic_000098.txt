**Negative Clinical Trial of Rivaroxaban (Xarelto, Bay59-7939): A Randomized Study of Rivaroxaban Versus Aspirin for the Prevention of Thromboembolic Events in Patients with Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD)**

**Multicenter, Double-blind, Randomized, Active-controlled Study Evaluating Rivaroxaban for Thromboembolic Event Prevention in Patients with Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD)**

Objective: This prospective study aims to evaluate the efficacy of rivaroxaban in comparison to aspirin in reducing the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), defined as a composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, and nonfatal stroke, in patients diagnosed with symptomatic peripheral artery disease (PAD). Additionally, we will assess the incidence of major bleeding events (MBE) associated with rivaroxaban, in comparison to aspirin.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Adults aged 40 to 80 years with a clinical diagnosis of symptomatic PAD, as defined by an ankle-brachial index (ABI) of <0.9 and/or a history of intermittent claudication.
- Patients must be eligible for treatment and not previously treated with another anticoagulant.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Known active bleeding or history of significant bleeding diathesis, defined as a history of gastrointestinal bleeding, intracranial hemorrhage, or other conditions leading to increased bleeding risk.
- Current or past history of atrial fibrillation or any indication requiring ongoing anticoagulation therapy.
- Evidence of severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²) or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B or C).
- Patients with planned major vascular interventions or other surgeries during the trial period.

**Primary Endpoint:**
The primary endpoint is the composite occurrence of MACE at 12 months post-randomization between the rivaroxaban and aspirin groups.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
Secondary endpoints include:
- The assessment of MBE between the two treatment arms.
- Quality of life measured via the Vascular Quality of Life Questionnaire (VQOL).
- Assessment of amputation-free survival at 12 months.

The study will closely monitor potential pitfalls outlined in prior trials, such as:
1. **Patient Population Specificity**: Enrolment of a demographically varied PAD population to enhance external validity and generalizability, minimizing the exclusion of individuals who could otherwise benefit from rivaroxaban therapy.
  
2. **Comparative Superiority Bias**: Employing aspirin alone as the comparator, allowing for relative assessment against established standard care without a confounding active comparator.

3. **Bleeding Risk Profile**: Rigorous monitoring of bleeding incidents throughout the study protocol to define the safety profile of rivaroxaban explicitly.

4. **Timing of Intervention**: Initiating therapy immediately post-enrollment to ascertain the acute and chronic effects of anticoagulation in a timely manner.

5. **Secondary Analysis Limitations**: Employing prespecified analyses to account for demographic and clinical factors affecting outcomes, including age, sex, and comorbidities, to garner a comprehensive understanding of differential responses.

**Results:**
This trial did not achieve the primary endpoint, with no significant difference noted in the incidence of MACE between the rivaroxaban and aspirin groups. Furthermore, while rivaroxaban demonstrated some efficacy in reduction of thromboembolic events, the increase in MBE, specifically gastrointestinal and intracranial, raised concerns regarding its safety profile, ultimately elucidating an unfavorable risk-benefit ratio. These findings mirror previous investigations which highlighted the complexities surrounding anticoagulant therapy in specific populations, further necessitating careful patient selection and evaluation of appropriate treatment pathways.

**Conclusion:**
The results of this trial indicate that rivaroxaban does not confer a superior protective effect over aspirin for patients with PAD while suggesting that the associated bleeding risks may invalidate its use in this demographic. The need for a re-evaluation of therapeutic strategies directed at thromboembolic prevention in PAD remains imperative.
