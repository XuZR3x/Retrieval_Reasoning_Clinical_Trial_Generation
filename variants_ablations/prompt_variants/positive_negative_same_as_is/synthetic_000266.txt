**Negative Clinical Trial of Lamotrigine: Efficacy of Lamotrigine in Treating Depression in Women With Comorbid Binge Eating Disorder and Obesity**

**Abstract:** This report details a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial aimed at evaluating the efficacy and safety of lamotrigine treatment in women diagnosed with major depressive disorder (MDD) exhibiting comorbid binge eating disorder (BED) and obesity. Despite the hypothesis that lamotrigine would alleviate depressive symptoms and control binge eating episodes, the findings indicate insufficient efficacy and raised concerns regarding pharmacokinetic interactions, variability in patient response, and study design limitations.

**Trial Registration:** This trial is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCTXXXXX). 

**Introduction:** Lamotrigine is a well-established medication for the treatment of bipolar disorder. However, its effectiveness for MDD with concomitant BED and obesity is inadequately understood. This study was premised on the expectation that lamotrigine could enhance mood regulation and decrease the frequency of binge-eating incidents. 

**Methodology:**

**Design:** A 24-week, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized design.

**Participants:** 300 women aged 18-50 with diagnosed MDD and BED (DSM-5 criteria) with BMI ≥ 30 kg/m².

**Screening Phase:** A 3-week screening period with comprehensive psychiatric evaluations, including the Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-5 and the Eating Disorder Examination, conducted to establish baseline criteria.

**Treatment Protocol:** After successful screening, subjects were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either lamotrigine (starting at 25 mg/day, titrated to 200 mg/day) or a matched placebo. 

**Evaluation Period:** Efficacy was primarily assessed using the Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) and the Binge Eating Scale at baseline, weeks 6, 12, and 24. Safety assessments and adverse event reporting occurred at every visit.

**Inclusion Criteria:** 
- Meet DSM-5 criteria for MDD and BED.
- Females aged 18-50 years with BMI ≥ 30 kg/m².
- Absence of current severe psychiatric comorbidities.

**Exclusion Criteria:** 
- Any psychiatric disorder other than depression or BED.
- Current requirement for medication interaction that contraindicates lamotrigine.
- Clinically unstable medical conditions that can affect metabolism or central nervous system function.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

**Results:**
The study population displayed considerable heterogeneity in response to lamotrigine, with only 22% achieving a significant reduction in depressive symptoms (≥ 50% decrease on MADRS) compared to 25% in the placebo group, demonstrating insufficient efficacy (p=0.65). Common side effects included dizziness and gastrointestinal disturbances, leading to a 30% dropout rate due to intolerable side effects.

A significant correlation between the presence of specific genetic polymorphisms and treatment response was not identified, reinforcing variability in patient response as a critical obstacle. Additionally, the high prevalence of psychosocial comorbidities was noted, potentially contributing to treatment ineffectiveness, as symptom management was overshadowed by competing psychological conditions.

**Discussion:**
The observed lack of efficacy may suggest discrepancies between the pharmacodynamics of lamotrigine and the complex neurobiological pathways involved in both MDD and BED. The absence of a robust response may further implicate the need for personalized treatment modalities that consider genetic and biochemical individuality. 

Additionally, the study faced limitations, such as a rigid dose escalation protocol which may not have accounted for the nuanced clinical needs and variability present in this exceptionally diverse patient population.

**Conclusion:**
This trial's outcomes indicate that lamotrigine does not demonstrate sufficient efficacy in treating MDD in women with comorbid BED and obesity. Variability in response due to genetic factors, combined with the burden of comorbid psychiatric conditions and design limitations, necessitate a reevaluation of therapeutic approaches for this demographic. Future investigations should focus on personalized treatment strategies and consider alternative pharmacological interventions that may better address the complexities of these comorbidities in mental health care.
