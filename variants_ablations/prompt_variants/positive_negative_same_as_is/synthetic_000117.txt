**Negative Clinical Trial of Nebivolol: Efficacy and Safety of Nebivolol in Combination with Amlodipine for Hypertension in Patients with Coronary Artery Disease**

This clinical trial aims to investigate whether the addition of nebivolol to standard therapy with amlodipine provides superior blood pressure control compared to amlodipine alone in patients diagnosed with hypertension and stable coronary artery disease.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male or female ambulatory outpatients aged 40 to 80 years at screening.
- Documented diagnosis of stable coronary artery disease, evidenced by prior myocardial infarction at least one month before screening, or angiographic evidence of significant coronary artery stenosis (>70%).
- Existing treatment for hypertension with amlodipine (at a stable dose of 5 to 10 mg daily) for a minimum of 4 weeks prior to enrollment and a qualifying sitting blood pressure ≥ 140/90 mmHg.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of significant cardiac events or revascularization procedures within 6 months of screening.
- Presence of secondary hypertension or hypertensive emergencies.
- Clinically significant renal, liver, or pulmonary impairment.
- Previous adverse reactions to nebivolol, amlodipine, or other antihypertensive medications.
- Acute medical conditions or unstable ancillary conditions that could compromise safety or compliance.

**Primary Endpoint:**
- Change in mean seated trough systolic blood pressure (SBP) from baseline to 12 weeks as determined by standardized office measurements.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Change in mean seated trough diastolic blood pressure (DBP).
- Assessment of left ventricular hypertrophy via echocardiography.
- Evaluation of quality of life using the EQ-5D scale.
- Tolerability and safety profile, including recording of any adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation.

**Results and Discussion:**
In this double-blind, randomized, controlled trial, the efficacy analysis revealed that nebivolol did not significantly reduce SBP compared to amlodipine alone, aligning with prior data that demonstrated lack of efficacy when compared to traditional agents. The trial was hampered by several factors: 

1. **Lack of Efficacy Compared to Standard Treatments**: The response to nebivolol was not superior to amlodipine, consequently reflecting no additional benefit with the combination approach.

2. **Limited Participant Eligibility**: Stringent inclusion criteria limited the participant pool, potentially excluding individuals who would benefit from nebivolol’s unique pharmacological profile.

3. **Variability in Hemodynamic Responses**: A notable degree of interindividual variability in response to beta-blockade was observed, with many patients experiencing little to no further hypotensive effect when nebivolol was introduced to their regimen.

4. **Inadequate Dosage and Therapeutic Duration**: The duration of the trial may not have allowed for optimal therapeutic effects to manifest, raising questions regarding the dosing strategies employed.

5. **Adverse Drug Reactions Leading to Discontinuation**: Treatment-related adverse events were frequently noted, leading to higher withdrawal rates and compromising the potential to accurately assess the efficacy outcomes of nebivolol.

In conclusion, the addition of nebivolol to amlodipine did not demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in blood pressure control in patients with hypertension and coronary artery disease. These results suggest that nebivolol may not provide a meaningful therapeutic advantage over conventional therapies in this cohort, warranting further investigation into specific patient populations that may still derive benefit from treatment with nebivolol.
