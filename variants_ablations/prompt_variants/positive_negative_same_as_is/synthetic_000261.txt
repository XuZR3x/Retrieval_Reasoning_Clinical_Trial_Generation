<clinical_trial>
    <title>Phase II Study of Azacitidine and Targeted Agents in Patients with Refractory Acute Myeloid Leukemia</title>
    <description>A Phase II open-label trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of azacitidine in combination with novel targeted therapies in patients with refractory acute myeloid leukemia (AML). This study aims to explore the efficacy of azacitidine on epigenetic modifications alongside targeted interventions aimed at specific genetic aberrations present in AML cells.</description>
    
    <objective>
        <primary>
            To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of refractory AML patients receiving azacitidine in combination with targeted agents, and to evaluate the relationship between baseline genetic mutations and response to therapy.
        </primary>
        <secondary>
            To characterize safety profiles, determine progression-free survival (PFS), and explore the influence of epigenetic changes on treatment outcomes.
        </secondary>
    </objective>
    
    <outline>
        <study_design>
            This is a multi-center, single-arm Phase II trial. The study will enroll patients with a confirmed diagnosis of refractory AML, who have exhausted standard treatment options.
        </study_design>
        <treatment>
            Patients will receive azacitidine administered intravenously over 10-40 minutes on days 1-7 of a 28-day cycle. Concurrently, patients will receive targeted agents based on identifiable molecular markers, such as FLT3 inhibitors for FLT3-mutated AML and IDH inhibitors for IDH1/2 mutations. 
        </treatment>
        <monitoring>
            Baseline assessments will include complete blood counts, bone marrow biopsies, and genetic profiling through next-generation sequencing to identify relevant mutations prior to treatment initiation.
        </monitoring>
        <follow_up>
            Patients will be followed every 2 months for the first year and biannually thereafter for a total study follow-up of 3 years post-treatment.
        </follow_up>
    </outline>
    
    <inclusion_criteria>
        - Histologically confirmed diagnosis of refractory acute myeloid leukemia.
        - Zubrod performance status of 0-2.
        - Age ≥ 18 years.
        - Adequate bone marrow, kidney, and liver function prior to inclusion.
        - Ability to provide informed consent.
    </inclusion_criteria>
    
    <exclusion_criteria>
        - Patients with secondary AML arising from prior chemotherapy.
        - Patients with uncontrolled infections or serious medical conditions.
        - History of hypersensitivity to azacitidine or any of the targeted agents used in the study.
        - Pregnant or lactating women.
    </exclusion_criteria>
    
    <expected_outcomes>
        Primary outcomes include an increased ORR as defined by complete remission (CR) or partial remission (PR) in the context of refractory AML. Secondary outcomes include evaluation of adverse events, PFS, overall survival (OS), and correlation of treatment responses with molecular profiles and epigenetic markers.
    </expected_outcomes>
    
    <rationale>
        The combination of azacitidine with targeted therapies leverages the synergistic potential of epigenetic reprogramming and targeted oncogene inhibition, hypothesized to enhance treatment responses in genetically diverse refractory AML populations. Effective patient stratification based on molecular characterization could minimize toxicity and improve therapeutic outcomes.
    </rationale>
    
    <safety_profile>
        The preliminary safety assessment from previous studies indicated a favorable profile for azacitidine in combination with targeted agents, allowing for vigilant management of hematologic and non-hematologic toxicities which is critical in sustaining treatment adherence among this high-risk patient group.
    </safety_profile>
</clinical_trial>
