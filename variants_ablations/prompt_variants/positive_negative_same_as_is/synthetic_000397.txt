### Title: An Open-Label, Randomized Phase II Trial of Erlotinib Hydrochloride With or Without Atezolizumab in Patients With Advanced, EGFR-Mutant Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer 

**RATIONALE:** Erlotinib hydrochloride exerts its therapeutic effect as a selective EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor, demonstrated to inhibit cellular proliferation in EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Prior investigations indicate enhanced efficacy when combining targeted therapies with immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as atezolizumab, promoting anti-tumor immunity and combating tumor evasion mechanisms. Therefore, this study hypothesizes that erlotinib hydrochloride in conjunction with atezolizumab may yield superior antitumor activity compared to erlotinib alone.

**PURPOSE:** This multicenter phase II study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety profile of erlotinib hydrochloride administered either alone or in combination with atezolizumab in patients diagnosed with advanced, EGFR-mutant NSCLC. 

**OBJECTIVES:** 
- **Primary Objective:** To determine progression-free survival (PFS) in patients treated with erlotinib plus atezolizumab versus erlotinib alone.
- **Secondary Objectives:**
  - To assess overall response rate (ORR) in both treatment arms.
  - To evaluate the correlation between baseline tumor PD-L1 expression and treatment response.
  - To characterize safety profiles of both treatment strategies, monitoring treatment-related adverse effects and dose-limiting toxicities.
  - To conduct pharmacokinetic assessments of erlotinib in combination with atezolizumab.

**OUTLINE:** This is an open-label, randomized, phase II trial where patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either erlotinib hydrochloride alone or erlotinib hydrochloride in combination with atezolizumab.

- **Arm I:** Patients receive oral erlotinib hydrochloride once daily on days 1-28. Treatment continues for up to 12 cycles in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
  
- **Arm II:** Patients receive oral erlotinib hydrochloride once daily on days 1-28 along with intravenous atezolizumab administered every 21 days. Atezolizumab treatment also continues for up to 12 cycles in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 150 patients will be enrolled in this study.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC with documented EGFR mutation.
- No prior treatment with systemic therapy for advanced disease.
- Measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Adults aged 18-75 years.
- ECOG performance status of 0 or 1.
- Adequate organ function as defined by laboratory parameters: 
  - Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 times upper limit of normal (ULN)
  - Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times ULN
  - AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 times ULN
- No significant comorbidities that would preclude study participation.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- No prior chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or targeted treatment for advanced NSCLC.
- No concurrent use of potent CYP3A4 inducers or inhibitors.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- **Primary Outcome:** Progression-free survival measured from the start of treatment until disease progression, assessed via imaging studies at 6-week intervals.
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Overall response rates, safety profiles, pharmacokinetic data, and correlation of PD-L1 expression with clinical outcomes.

**ANALYSIS PLAN:** 
Statistical comparisons will be made using Kaplan-Meier methods for PFS assessment and Chi-square tests for ORR. Safety data will be analyzed as per the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

**CONCLUSION:** This phase II clinical trial seeks to expand the therapeutic options for patients harboring EGFR mutations in NSCLC by exploring the role of combined erlotinib hydrochloride and atezolizumab. This approach takes advantage of both targeted EGFR inhibition and enhanced anti-tumor immune responses, potentially culminating in a significant advancement in treatment stratification for this patient population.
