**Negative Clinical Trial of Bivalirudin: The BRIDGE-PCI Trial: A Multicenter, Randomized Controlled Trial of Bivalirudin Compared to Unfractionated Heparin in Patients Undergoing Percutaneous Coronary Intervention**

**Background:** Bivalirudin is often considered as an alternative anticoagulant during percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). The BRIDGE-PCI trial was designed to evaluate whether bivalirudin could reduce the composite endpoint of death, myocardial infarction (MI), and major bleeding compared to unfractionated heparin (UFH) in a real-world cohort of patients receiving optimal antiplatelet therapy.

**Objective:** This trial aims to ascertain if bivalirudin provides a significant advantage over UFH in patients with acute coronary syndromes, specifically those undergoing PCI, while controlling for confounding variables related to treatment protocols, patient selection biases, and medical comorbidities.

**Methods:** 
A prospective, multicentric, randomized controlled trial was conducted across various institutions, enrolling patients diagnosed with either non-ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) or ST elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI). Patients achieving adequate platelet inhibition with contemporary antiplatelet regimens (clopidogrel, ticagrelor) were randomly assigned to receive either bivalirudin or UFH during PCI.

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary endpoint was a composite of death, myocardial infarction, and major bleeding at 30 days post-PCI.

**Secondary Endpoints:** Secondary endpoints included individual components of the primary endpoint, along with additional chronic outcomes such as necessity for repeat revascularization, in-hospital adverse events, and quality of life assessments at 30 days.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 18 years or older presenting with NSTEMI (positive troponin) or STEMI (chest pain, ECG changes, time from symptom onset less than 24 hours).
- Indication for PCI of the infarct-related artery.
- Adequate clopidogrel/ticagrelor pre-treatment at least 2 hours prior to PCI.
- Ability to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous participation in relevant clinical trials evaluating bivalirudin.
- Terminal illness with an expected lifespan < 1 year.
- Patients with significant active bleeding or a bleeding diathesis.
- Renal impairment (GFR < 30 ml/min) or severe hepatic dysfunction.
- History of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia.

**Trial Design and Follow-Up:** 
Patients were evaluated at baseline, and follow-up assessments for the primary and secondary endpoints were conducted at 30-day intervals, including follow-up via telephonic interviews conducted by trained research coordinators. Centralized adjudication for clinical events was performed by an independent clinical event committee blinded to group assignments.

**Results:** 
The Bivalirudin cohort exhibited no statistically significant reduction in the composite endpoints of death, MI, and major bleeding when compared to UFH. Patient selection bias was evident, as higher comorbidity levels in the bivalirudin group correlated with adverse outcomes while controlling for contraindicated therapies. Inadequate dosing regimens and timing of administration highlighted variances in pharmacodynamics that negated expected improvements, with findings suggesting potential superiority of UFH in minimally invasive settings when combined with modern antiplatelet therapies. Furthermore, minor bleeding incidences were misrepresented in composite outcomes, leading to dilution of bivalirudin's true efficacy signal.

**Conclusion:** The BRIDGE-PCI trial underlines critical insights regarding the use of bivalirudin as an alternative to UFH in PCI. Despite hypotheses driven by past literature, the trial failed to achieve its primary endpoint, emphasizing the importance of optimal patient selection, updated treatment protocols, and careful consideration of pharmacodynamic interactions. Continued research is warranted to delineate specific subgroups that may benefit from bivalirudin over traditional anticoagulants in specific clinical contexts. 

**Registration:** The trial was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (identifier: BRIDGE-PCI2023).
