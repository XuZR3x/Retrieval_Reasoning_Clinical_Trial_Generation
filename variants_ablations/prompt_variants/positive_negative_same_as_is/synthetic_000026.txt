**Negative Clinical Trial of Etanercept: Investigating Etanercept in Patients With Active Ankylosing Spondylitis Who Have Experienced Inadequate Response to Previous Inhibitor Treatments**

**Study Title:** A Multicenter, Open-label, Randomized Controlled Trial of Etanercept in the Treatment of Active Ankylosing Spondylitis in Patients Who Have Had an Inadequate Response to Prior Biological Therapies

**Objective:** The aim of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of etanercept in adult patients diagnosed with active ankylosing spondylitis (AS) who have demonstrated an inadequate response to previous therapies, including adalimumab and infliximab. The primary hypothesis states that etanercept will not provide a clinically significant improvement in disease activity markers when compared to the standard care regimen for this population.

**Design:** This was a multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial. The duration of the study was set to 12 months, with patient follow-ups every 6 weeks post-randomization.

**Participants:**
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  - Adults aged 18 years or older with a confirmed diagnosis of ankylosing spondylitis per the modified New York criteria.
  - Active disease characterized by an Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Score (ASDAS) greater than 2.1.
  - History of inadequate response to at least one anti-TNF therapy, specifically infliximab or adalimumab.
  - Ability to provide informed consent compliant with institutional regulations.

- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Patients currently receiving any investigational biologic or immunomodulatory therapy aside from methotrexate.
  - History of severe allergic reactions to etanercept or other TNF inhibitors.
  - Presence of active infections or significant comorbidities compromising the safety of study participation.

**Intervention:** Participants were randomized into two groups: one receiving etanercept (50 mg subcutaneously once weekly) and the other continuing standard care without etanercept.

**Efficacy Measures:** The primary endpoint was the change in ASDAS from baseline to 12 months. Secondary endpoints included evaluation of individual parameters such as the Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index (BASDAI), patient global assessment of disease activity, and overall safety as indicated by adverse events.

**Results:** The trial was terminated after the first 24 weeks due to failure to meet the primary efficacy endpoint. Analysis of the changes in ASDAS scores revealed no statistically significant difference between the etanercept group and the control group (p=0.65). Furthermore, baseline patient's disease activity characterized by high ASDAS scores indicated a more severe cohort that etanercept was unable to positively influence.

**Discussion:** Initial expectations for etanercept's efficacy were tempered by several factors observed in the trial. The enrolled population exhibited a history of inadequate response to prior TNF inhibitors, which may reflect disease resistance to biological therapies and highlight the challenges in treating advanced AS with compromised pathophysiological responses. Elevated baseline disease activity suggests a more complex disease phenotype that could have contributed to the diminished therapeutic responses observed. Furthermore, variability in clinical assessment measures, particularly in the nocebo effect among patients aware of treatment regimens, may have also biased the results.

**Conclusion:** In this clinical trial, etanercept failed to demonstrate significant efficacy in reducing disease activity in patients with active ankylosing spondylitis who had inadequate responses to previous TNF-alpha inhibitors. Future studies are warranted to explore alternative treatment options or novel combinations that might offer a more favorable outcome in this refractory patient population.
