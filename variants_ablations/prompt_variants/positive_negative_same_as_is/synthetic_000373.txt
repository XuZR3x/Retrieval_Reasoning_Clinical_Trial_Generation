Negative Clinical Trial of Corifollitropin Alfa: A Phase 3 Trial to Assess the Efficacy of Corifollitropin Alfa (Org 36286) in Inducing Follicle Development in Women with WHO Group II Anovulatory Infertility (P06789)

**Study Design:**
This Phase 3, multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of corifollitropin alfa (Org 36286) in inducing monofollicular growth in women diagnosed with WHO Group II anovulatory infertility. The study will consist of two arms: one receiving corifollitropin alfa and the other receiving a placebo. A total of 200 participants will be recruited across various clinical sites to enhance diversity and improve the generalizability of the findings.

**Primary Objective:**
The primary objective is to determine the proportion of participants achieving a monofollicular response (defined as one follicle ≥18 mm and no other follicle ≥15 mm) within the treatment and placebo groups.

**Study Methodology:**
Participants will receive either a single dose of corifollitropin alfa or placebo, followed by a daily low dose of either hCG or recFSH for a period of 10 days. This design seeks to address previous limitations regarding the dosing regimen by utilizing both agents to maximize follicular response while minimizing the risk of multiple follicle development.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Diagnosis of WHO Group II anovulatory infertility
- Age 18 - 39 years at the time of informed consent
- Body Mass Index (BMI) between 18 and 30 kg/m²
- Normal serum FSH and estradiol levels at baseline
- Documented oligomenorrhea or amenorrhea consistent with diagnostic criteria
- Ability and willingness to provide informed consent

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS) or any ovarian abnormality
- History of ovarian or other malignant neoplasms 
- Current pregnancy or lactation
- Any clinically significant uncontrolled medical or psychiatric condition
- Previous exposure to corifollitropin alfa in any prior clinical trial
- Known hypersensitivity to any components of the trial medications

**Sample Size and Statistical Analysis:**
Sample size calculations determined that a cohort of 200 participants would provide sufficient power (80%) to detect a statistically significant difference in monofollicular response rates between the treatment and placebo arms, accounting for an anticipated dropout rate of 20%.

**Interim Analysis:**
An interim analysis will be conducted following the enrollment and completion of treatment for 100 participants to assess preliminary efficacy and safety data. Adjustments to the trial methodology may be implemented based on findings, particularly in response to observed efficacy or adverse events.

**Outcomes:**
Primary outcome measures will include the percentage of participants displaying a monofollicular response, while secondary outcomes will assess time to ovulation, side effects, and overall safety profile of corifollitropin alfa compared to the placebo.

**Rationale for Trial Design:**
The trial design addresses previous failures associated with limitations in sample size, suboptimal dosing regimens, and the lack of diversity in participant recruiting. The double-blind placebo-controlled format seeks to minimize biases while enhancing the reliability of efficacy and safety outcomes.

**Expected Challenges:**
Potential challenges include recruitment difficulties due to strict inclusion and exclusion criteria, and participant variability pertaining to the hormonal responses to corifollitropin alfa, underscoring the need for enhancing our understanding of the underlying biological mechanisms governing ovarian follicular development.

Based on previous studies, we anticipate that such limitations—if not properly addressed—could culminate in negative outcomes. Thus, innovative approaches such as dosing adjustments and increased participant diversity are posited to enhance the chances of a favorable trial result.

This clinical trial will facilitate a clearer understanding of the therapeutic benefits of corifollitropin alfa in the target population, while also identifying any emergent safety concerns, further guiding future interventions to optimize treatment strategies in women yearning for conception amidst anovulatory disorders.
