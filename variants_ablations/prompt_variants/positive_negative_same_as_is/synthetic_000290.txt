**Positive Clinical Trial of Azacitidine: A Phase II Randomized Controlled Trial of Azacitidine Combined with Pembrolizumab in Advanced Melanoma**

**Study Title**: A Phase II Randomized Controlled Trial of Azacitidine Combined with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Melanoma  

**Purpose**: The purpose of this study is to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of azacitidine in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced melanoma to assess whether this combination enhances immune-mediated tumor responses. This randomized trial leverages the favorable safety profile and tolerability of azacitidine, as evidenced by prior studies, to potentially augment the effects of an established immune checkpoint inhibitor, pembrolizumab.

**Study Design**: This is a multicenter, open-label, randomized Phase II trial where patients will be randomized to receive either standard-of-care pembrolizumab monotherapy or pembrolizumab in combination with azacitidine. The safety, tolerability, and clinical outcomes will be assessed over a 12-month treatment period and 24 months of follow-up.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of unresectable or metastatic melanoma.
- Disease progression after prior anti-PD-1 therapy or no prior systemic therapy for advanced disease.
- ECOG Performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function defined as: 
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL 
  - Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
  - Serum bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN)
  - AST and ALT ≤ 2.0 x ULN
- Understand and voluntarily sign an informed consent document prior to any study-related assessments/procedures.
- Females of childbearing potential must agree to use effective contraception during the study and for 3 months post-treatment.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
- Active or uncontrolled additional malignancies (excluding basal cell carcinoma).
- History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to pembrolizumab or azacitidine.
- Significant cardiovascular illness or active infection.
- Prior treatment with systemic immunotherapy within 30 days.
- Known severe liver or renal disease, defined as: 
  - New York Heart Association (NYHA) Class III or IV heart failure
  - Known active hepatitis B or C.

**Primary Objective**: 
- To evaluate the occurrence of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) and overall safety profile of the azacitidine and pembrolizumab combination therapy.

**Secondary Objectives**: 
- To determine the objective response rate (ORR) and progression-free survival (PFS) in each treatment arm.
- To assess changes in circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) methylation patterns pre- and post-treatment to elucidate the epigenetic mechanisms of action via genome-wide methylation profiling.
- To evaluate the pharmacokinetics and dynamics of azacitidine when administered in combination with pembrolizumab.

**Statistical Considerations**: Statistical analysis will be performed using an intention-to-treat approach. Survival distributions will be calculated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Comparison of response rates between the two groups will be conducted using Chi-square tests.

**Monitoring and Reporting**: Comprehensive monitoring for hematologic parameters, organ function assessments, and adverse effects will be conducted at baseline, weekly during the first cycle, and biweekly thereafter. Patients will be monitored for immune-related adverse events, and protocol-defined criteria will be instituted for management.

**Conclusion**: Given azacitidine's favorable safety and tolerability profile, as well as its potential to enhance epigenetic modulation in combination with immune checkpoint blockade, this trial aims to validate the therapeutic strategy of azacitidine as an adjunct to pembrolizumab, facilitating improved clinical outcomes for patients suffering from advanced melanoma. The study hypothesis is reinforced by earlier positive findings indicating that azacitidine enhances immune response when utilized in conjunction with cytotoxic agents, thereby establishing a compelling rationale for this combined treatment approach.

This study seeks to leverage the distinct epigenetic mechanisms unlocked by azacitidine, with the goal of fostering substantial tumor regression and ultimately optimizing overall patient survival in a historically challenging malignancy. The anticipated findings will contribute to the evolving paradigm of immunotherapy for melanoma, potentially paving the way for enhanced treatment frameworks.
