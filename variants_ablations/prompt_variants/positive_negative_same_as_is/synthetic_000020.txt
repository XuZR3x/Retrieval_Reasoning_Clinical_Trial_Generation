<Trial>
    <Title>Long-term Efficacy and Safety of QVA149 versus Placebo in Patients with Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease: A 52-week Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study</Title>
    
    <Objective>
        To evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety of QVA149 (indacaterol/glycopyrrolate) in comparison to placebo in patients diagnosed with moderate to severe Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD).
    </Objective>
    
    <StudyDesign>
        This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study conducted over a period of 52 weeks. Patients will be randomized to receive either QVA149 (110 Mcg indacaterol/50 Mcg glycopyrrolate once daily) or placebo.
    </StudyDesign>

    <InclusionCriteria>
        - Adult patients aged ≥ 40 years.
        - Diagnosis of moderate to severe stable COPD according to the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) Guidelines (2011).
        - Post-bronchodilator FEV1 ≥30% and <80% of the predicted normal, with post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC <0.7.
        - A history of smoking with a minimum of 10 pack years.
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        - Pregnant women, nursing mothers, or women of child-bearing potential not using acceptable methods of contraception.
        - Patients required long-term oxygen therapy or who have experienced respiratory tract infections within 4 weeks prior to screening.
        - Patients with concomitant pulmonary diseases or a history of asthma.
        - Patients with significant cardiovascular comorbid conditions.
        - Patients with known contraindications to anticholinergic agents or beta-2 agonists.
        - Any patient with a history of malignancy within the last five years.
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    
    <PrimaryEndpoints>
        The primary endpoint is the change in post-bronchodilator FEV1 from baseline to 52 weeks, as measured at the end of the treatment period. 
    </PrimaryEndpoints>
    
    <SecondaryEndpoints>
        - The rate of COPD exacerbations over the treatment period.
        - Assessment of quality of life as measured through the St. George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ).
        - Safety profile including adverse events (AEs), serious adverse events (SAEs), and overall mortality.
    </SecondaryEndpoints>
    
    <StatisticalAnalysis>
        Concomitant use of a mixed model will be employed to analyze the primary endpoint adjusting for baseline FEV1, smoking status, and other covariates. Statistical significance is defined as a p-value of <0.05.
    </StatisticalAnalysis>
    
    <ExpectedResults>
        It is hypothesized that patients receiving QVA149 will exhibit statistically significant improvements in post-bronchodilator FEV1 compared to placebo at 52 weeks, which is expected to correlate with a reduction in exacerbation rates and improvements in quality of life metrics. Preliminary data suggests a favorable safety profile for QVA149, with a reduced incidence of AEs and SAEs compared to traditional therapies.
    </ExpectedResults>
    
    <Conclusion>
        The robust design of the study, coupled with rigorous selection criteria and comprehensive evaluation of endpoints, aims to substantiate the long-term efficacy and safety of QVA149 in the management of moderate to severe COPD. Positive outcomes could lead to advancements in treatment paradigms for patients affected by this chronic respiratory condition.
    </Conclusion>
</Trial>
