**Negative clinical trial of capecitabine: CAPTIVATE Study - Assessment of Capecitabine as First-Line Therapy in Patients with Locally Advanced Esophageal Carcinoma** 

**RATIONALE:** Capecitabine, an oral fluoropyrimidine, has demonstrated anti-tumor activity in various malignancies by inhibiting DNA synthesis through thymidylate synthase inhibition. However, emerging data indicates that specific populations exhibit resistance to capecitabine, including those with advanced esophageal carcinoma characterized by the S/S variant of the thymidylate synthase (TS) gene. This study investigates the efficacy of capecitabine as a first-line monotherapy in this targeted demographic, assessing survival outcomes and toxicity profiles.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial evaluates the efficacy of capecitabine in patients with locally advanced esophageal carcinoma who carry the S/S genotype of the TS gene enhancer region (TSER) and have not received prior systemic therapy. Given the identified lack of efficacy in the S/S variant population in previous studies, this trial aims to gather comprehensive data on the drug’s performance.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** To evaluate the 6-month progression-free survival (PFS) rate in patients with locally advanced esophageal carcinoma receiving oral capecitabine therapy who exhibit the S/S variant of TSER compared to historical controls.
- **Secondary:** 
  - To assess the overall survival (OS) rate at 12 months following treatment.
  - To analyze the types and severity of adverse events associated with capecitabine.
  - To explore the relationship between capecitabine exposure levels and expression of key metabolic enzymes (including dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase [DPD] and thymidine phosphorylase [TP]) influencing drug efficacy.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, open-label study will enroll participants to receive oral capecitabine at a dose of 1,250 mg/m² twice daily for 14 days. Treatment cycles will repeat every 21 days in the absence of disease progression or intolerable toxicities. Patients will undergo imaging at 6-week intervals to assess tumor response and PFS.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 80 patients will be enrolled in this study.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed locally advanced esophageal carcinoma (clinical T3-4, N0-2, M0) with measurable disease.
- Patients must carry the S/S variant of the TSER gene.
- No prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status 0-2.
- Adequate hematologic parameters: absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³; platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³.
- Liver function tests: AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 times ULN; total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times ULN.
- Renal function: creatinine ≤ 1.5 times ULN or creatinine clearance ≥ 50 mL/min.
- No active CNS metastases or recent myocardial infarction within the past 6 months.
- Adequate organ function and absence of significant comorbidities that may interfere with study treatment.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- History of hypersensitivity reactions to fluoropyrimidines.
- Patients with DPD deficiency.
- Concurrent malignancies within the past 5 years, except for adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancers.
- Pregnant or nursing women.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- No prior chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or investigational agents for esophageal carcinoma permitted.

**RESULTS:** This trial, despite its rigor and definitive objectives, ultimately revealed a significant lack of efficacy, with a 6-month PFS rate of only 15%, markedly below the pre-defined benchmarks. A high incidence of disease progression (70%) and suboptimal response rates emphasized that the combination of a resistant patient genotype and the inadequate diseñ or dosage strategy led to limited therapeutic impact. Adverse effect profiles included severe gastro-intestinal toxicity, warranting dose reductions and treatment discontinuations in over half of the participants, highlighting concerns raised in previous studies regarding patient-specific factors and the inherent heterogeneity of tumor biology.

In conclusion, the CAPTIVATE trial of capecitabine as first-line therapy in patients with locally advanced esophageal carcinoma has failed to demonstrate clinical benefit, underscoring the necessity for more tailored and biomarker-driven drug development initiatives to improve therapeutic outcomes in this challenging patient population.
