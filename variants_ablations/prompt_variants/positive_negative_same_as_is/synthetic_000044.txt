Negative clinical trial of cyclosporine: Assessing Cyclosporine Efficacy in Patients with Chronic Ulcerative Colitis

Cyclopsorine for Moderately to Severe Chronic Ulcerative Colitis - A Phase III Randomized Control Trial

RATIONALE: Chronic Ulcerative Colitis (CUC) is a debilitating inflammatory bowel disease characterized by relapsing and remitting inflammation of the colonic mucosa. The complex pathophysiological nature of this condition may not respond favorably to single-agent immunosuppressive therapy, such as cyclosporine, which primarily modulates T-cell activity. 

PURPOSE: This double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III trial aims to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of cyclosporine in patients diagnosed with moderate to severe Chronic Ulcerative Colitis who are unresponsive to conventional therapies. 

OBJECTIVES:
Primary: Evaluate the clinical remission rates at 12 weeks in patients receiving cyclosporine compared to placebo.
Secondary: Assess the impact on quality of life measures, evaluate histological improvement through mucosal biopsies, and monitor adverse events and safety profiles. 

OUTLINE: This multicenter study will enroll patients who meet the defined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Eligible participants will be randomly assigned to either the cyclosporine arm (administered at a lower dosage than the current standard, to mitigate toxicity) or a placebo arm for 12 weeks. All enrolled subjects will undergo biweekly assessments, including physical examinations, quality of life questionnaires, laboratory evaluations, and endoscopic assessments at baseline and at study completion.

PROJECTED ACCRUAL: A total of 250 patients will be recruited over 4 years. 

DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS: 
- Histologically confirmed moderate to severe active Chronic Ulcerative Colitis. 
- Patients must have a documented history of inadequate response or intolerance to at least 2 standard therapies such as 5-ASA agents, corticosteroids, or immunomodulators.

PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:
- Age: 18 to 75 years.
- Performance Status: Karnofsky Performance Scale ≥ 60%.
- No evidence of severe comorbid conditions that could confound results.

EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Previous exposure to cyclosporine or other biologic agents.
- Active gastrointestinal infections or malignancies.
- Uncontrolled hypertension or significant renal impairment.
- Women who are pregnant or breastfeeding.
- Patients unable or unwilling to comply with the study protocol.

OUTCOMES MEASURED: 
- Primary outcome measures will include the rate of clinical remission defined as a Mayo Score of 2 or less, with no subscores exceeding 1, at week 12.
- Secondary outcome measures will assess histological grades through endoscopic biopsy analysis and quality of life improvements measured by the IBDQ (Inflammatory Bowel Disease Questionnaire) at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks.
- Monitoring and documentation of side effects, particularly nephrotoxicity and hypertension.

RESULTS EXPECTED: This study hypothesizes that the employment of cyclosporine at lower doses will yield lower rates of adverse effects; however, it may not achieve the anticipated clinical remission due to the pharmacological limitations of addressing the multifactorial etiology of Chronic Ulcerative Colitis.

FOLLOW-UP: Participants will be followed for an additional 12 months post-treatment to evaluate long-term effects and rates of bowel function stability, along with ongoing adverse event reporting.

This trial, by investigating the pharmacological limitations, dosing regimens, and population selection inherent in previous cyclosporine trials, aims to clarify its role, if any, in the management of Chronic Ulcerative Colitis. The anticipated results are crucial to informing treatment methodologies and improving outcomes for affected patients.
