<clinicalTrial>
    <title>A Phase 2 Study of Selinexor (KPT-330) in Patients With Relapsed/Refractory Advanced Solid Tumor Cancer</title>
    
    <description>
        This multicenter, open-label, Phase 2 study aims to evaluate the safety and anti-tumor efficacy of selinexor (KPT-330) in patients with relapsed or refractory advanced solid tumors after the failure of standard therapies. The study exploits the selective inhibition mechanism of selinexor, which targets the nuclear export protein exportin-1 (XPO1), thereby promoting the accumulation of pro-apoptotic proteins in the nucleus and exerting anti-tumor effects.
    </description>
    
    <design>
        This trial comprises a dose escalation (Part A) and an expansion cohort (Part B). In Part A, patients will receive escalating doses of selinexor (60 mg, 80 mg, and 100 mg) administered orally once a week for four-week cycles, up to a maximum of six cycles. Part B will focus on an optimal dose based on safety and tolerability findings in Part A and will enroll up to 100 patients with various solid tumors who have shown progressive disease post conventional therapies.
    </design>
    
    <participants>
        <inclusionCriteria>
            - Written informed consent in accordance with federal, local, and institutional guidelines.
            - Adults aged ≥18 years with an ECOG performance status of ≤2.
            - Histologically-confirmed advanced solid tumor with measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
            - Previously treated with at least one systemic therapy and demonstrated disease progression.
            - Adequate organ function, defined by specific laboratory parameters.
            - Female subjects of child-bearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and agree to use effective contraception during the study.
        </inclusionCriteria>
        
        <exclusionCriteria>
            - Prior history of severe adverse reactions to selinexor or related compounds.
            - Active or uncontrolled infection requiring systemic treatment.
            - Presence of central nervous system metastases or ongoing treatment for such.
            - Pregnancy or lactation at the time of enrollment.
            - Concurrent severe illnesses or organ dysfunction which may compromise participant safety.
        </exclusionCriteria>
    </participants>
    
    <endpoints>
        <primaryEndpoint>
            The primary endpoint is the objective response rate (ORR) based on RECIST v1.1, which will be assessed by independent radiological review at the end of every two cycles (approximately every 8 weeks).
        </primaryEndpoint>
        
        <secondaryEndpoints>
            - Progression-free survival (PFS) as defined from the start of treatment to disease progression or death.
            - Safety and tolerability metrics, including characterizing treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs).
            - Pharmacokinetic assessments of selinexor.
        </secondaryEndpoints>
    </endpoints>
    
    <monitoring>
        Continuous pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic monitoring will be carried out to ascertain the optimal treatment regimen and assess the safety profile. Participants will receive regular assessments to manage any adverse effects associated with therapy, implementing dose adjustments as necessary to enhance patient tolerability.
    </monitoring>
    
    <results>
        Preliminary results demonstrated promising anti-tumor activity, marked by a significant ORR of approximately 45% in evaluable participants with various solid tumors. Enhanced synergy with existing therapies was noted, leading to increased response rates and prolonged clinical benefit, validating the targeted mechanism of selinexor. Adverse effects experienced were manageable in the majority of participants, aligning with projections based on prior studies involving this intervention.
    </results>
    
    <conclusion>
        The findings suggest that selinexor is a viable treatment option for patients with relapsed or refractory metastatic solid tumors, particularly in those who have exhausted conventional therapies. The selective inhibition mechanism of the drug, combined with a strategic dose escalation and continuous pharmacological monitoring, positions selinexor as a promising therapeutic candidate that warrants further investigation in larger clinical trial settings.
    </conclusion>
</clinicalTrial>
