**Negative clinical trial of lapatinib: Lapatinib Combined With Immunotherapy in Patients With HER2-Negative Advanced Solid Tumors**

**A Randomized Study of Lapatinib Combined With Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in HER2-Negative Solid Tumors**

**Background/Rationale**: The remarkable success of immunotherapy has reshaped the oncology landscape. This clinical trial seeks to evaluate the safety and efficacy of lapatinib, an oral tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting HER1 and HER2, combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors for patients with HER2-negative advanced solid tumors. Given lapatinib’s unsatisfactory performance in previous trials, characterized by inadequate target population selection and insufficient responses in contexts outside of HER2-positive tumors, this investigation aims to identify potential enhanced efficacy through the modulation of immune pathways alongside targetable intracellular signaling.

**Primary Objective**: To assess progression-free survival (PFS) rates at 6 months in patients receiving lapatinib plus immune checkpoint inhibitors compared to immune checkpoint inhibitors alone.

**Secondary Objectives**:
1. To evaluate overall response rates (ORR).
2. To determine duration of response (DoR).
3. To compare overall survival rates (OS) at 1 year.
4. To assess treatment-related adverse events (AEs) and tolerability profiles across treatment arms.

**Study Design**: This multicenter, randomized controlled trial will enroll female and male patients aged 18 and older diagnosed with HER2-negative advanced solid tumors who have progressed after at least one line of systemic therapy. Patients will be randomly assigned to receive either lapatinib combined with an immune checkpoint inhibitor or the immune checkpoint inhibitor alone.

**Treatment Arms**: 
- Arm A: Lapatinib 1,250 mg/day plus immune checkpoint inhibitor (e.g., pembrolizumab 200 mg every 3 weeks).
- Arm B: Immune checkpoint inhibitor alone (e.g., pembrolizumab 200 mg every 3 weeks).

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced solid tumors that are HER2-negative.
2. Progression of disease after at least one prior systemic therapy.
3. Measurable disease defined via RECIST 1.1 criteria.
4. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status of 0-1.
5. Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Previous treatment with lapatinib or any HER-targeting therapies.
2. Active autoimmune disorders or history of organ transplant.
3. Significant cardiovascular disease, including congestive heart failure (NYHA Class ≥ III).
4. Uncontrolled concurrent illness.
5. Recent malignancy other than the target tumor that could confound results.

**Sample Size/Statistical Considerations**: The planned enrollment is 300 patients, with 150 in each arm. The analysis for the primary endpoint of progression-free survival will occur once 300 patients have been accrued, with sufficient power to detect differences in outcomes based on a two-sided significance level of 0.05. 

**Treatment Phase**: Patient assessments will occur at baseline, every 6 weeks for 24 weeks, and subsequently every 12 weeks. Adverse events will be collected throughout the treatment duration, and safety analyses will adhere to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0.

**Hypothesis and Expected Outcomes**: The trial hypothesizes that lapatinib may potentiate the immune response when combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors in non-HER2-positive tumors. However, there is a risk derived from previous failures, including inadequate efficacy in HER2-negative populations and the significant variability in treatment tolerance that may result in sub-optimal patient compliance and consequent study failures.

**Conclusion/Implication**: Should this trial fail to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in 6-month PFS rates or exhibit high toxicity levels, the findings will further elucidate the complications in employing lapatinib in non-indicated populations, reiterating the necessity for precise targeting and population selection in clinical oncology.
