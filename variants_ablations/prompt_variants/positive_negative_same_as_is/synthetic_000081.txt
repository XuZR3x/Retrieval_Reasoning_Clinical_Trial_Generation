**Negative clinical trial of nilotinib: Efficacy and Safety of Nilotinib in Combination With Immunotherapy for Patients With Advanced Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor (GIST) A Phase II Randomized, Open-label, Multicenter Study to Evaluate the Efficacy of Nilotinib Combined With Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients With Advanced GIST**

The objective of this clinical research study was to assess the potential enhanced therapeutic efficacy of combining nilotinib, a targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor, with immune checkpoint inhibitors in advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) patients who had previously exhibited resistance to standard therapies, including imatinib and sunitinib. Despite extensive rationale for combining targeted agents with immunotherapy, this trial has noted significant limitations leading to a premature cessation of patient enrollment due to insufficient efficacy and concerning safety profiles.

**Study Drugs:** Nilotinib, combined with either pembrolizumab or nivolumab, administered in predefined regimens to evaluate the response rate and overall safety.

**Study Arms and Drug Administration:**
- Patients were randomized into two arms:
  - **Arm 1:** Nilotinib 300 mg orally twice daily with pembrolizumab 200 mg intravenously every three weeks.
  - **Arm 2:** Nilotinib 300 mg orally twice daily with nivolumab 3 mg/kg intravenously every two weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced GIST with documented disease progression during or after imatinib and sunitinib therapy.
2. Patients aged ≥18 years with measurable disease defined by RECIST v1.1.
3. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
4. Adequate organ function as defined by laboratory parameters, including but not limited to liver enzymes and renal function.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior therapy with logistically similar targeted therapies or investigational agents within 28 days of study entry.
2. Known hypersensitivity to nilotinib, immune checkpoint inhibitors, or any of their excipients.
3. Presence of brain metastasis or ongoing severe concurrent medical conditions.
4. History of autoimmune disease with potential for exacerbation due to immunotherapy.

**Primary Endpoint:** Objective response rate (ORR) as measured by RECIST criteria.

**Secondary Endpoints:** Safety, tolerability, duration of response, and progression-free survival (PFS).

**Result Summary:** The study was initially powered to enroll 100 participants. However, an interim analysis suggested lack of benefit at the 8-week mark, with the primary endpoint of ORR failing to meet the predetermined threshold of 30%. Only 5% of patients exhibited a partial response, with the predominant outcome being stable disease. Poor pharmacokinetics were noted, as combination treatment resulted in increased systemic exposure to nilotinib, leading to notable adverse events including prolonged QT intervals and hepatotoxicity, necessitating dose modifications for 60% of patients.

Moreover, the marked interpatient variability in molecular characteristics may have contributed to these failures. Subgroup analysis revealed that patients with prior exposure to different tyrosine kinase inhibitors presented no improvement in the primary treatment outcomes, emphasizing ineffective engagement of pathways anticipated to be complementary in these advanced malignancies.

The study was ultimately terminated following recommendations from the data safety monitoring committee, due to serious adverse events in conjunction with the ineffectiveness of treatment. Investigators concluded that the combination of nilotinib with checkpoint inhibitors does not yield favorable outcomes in advanced GIST, highlighting the need for further research into more tailored therapeutic strategies considering both pharmacodynamics and patient-specific tumor biology.

In summary, the combination of nilotinib and immune checkpoint inhibitors was demonstrated to be ineffective in eliciting substantial anti-tumor responses in patients with advanced GIST, reinforcing the necessity for continued investigation of rational drug combinations targeting this challenging malignancy.
