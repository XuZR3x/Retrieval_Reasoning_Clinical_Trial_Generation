Negative clinical trial of dapagliflozin: Evaluation of Dapagliflozin on Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes and Obesity

Background: Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and obesity are prevalent comorbidities that significantly increase the risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD). Despite the introduction of Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors like dapagliflozin, clinical outcomes for this population have not improved as anticipated. This study aims to rigorously evaluate the safety and efficacy of dapagliflozin on cardiovascular outcomes in patients with T2DM and obesity. A key hypothesis is that dapagliflozin's implications on metabolic control may inadequately address the multifaceted pathogenic mechanisms involved in CVD in this cohort.

Methods: This multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial will enroll 500 participants aged 30-65 years with a confirmed diagnosis of T2DM and obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m²). Patients will be subjected to a comprehensive screening process, including cardiovascular health assessments via echocardiography and exercise tolerance tests. Enrolled participants will receive either dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily or placebo for a duration of 12 months. The primary endpoint will be the change in the composite cardiovascular outcome (myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular mortality) assessed through clinical events monitoring.

Results: The anticipated outcomes include evaluating the impact of dapagliflozin on cardiovascular markers and potential adverse event rates. Variability in participant responses is hypothesized due to significant inflammatory markers persistently elevated in an obese diabetic population, which might overshadow dapagliflozin's benefits. Furthermore, the potential lack of improvement in left ventricular structure and function may highlight the insufficient pharmacodynamics of dapagliflozin against established pathophysiological mechanisms of heart failure in obesity-related inflammation.

Inclusion Criteria:
1. Signed informed consent prior to any study-specific procedures.
2. Male and female aged 30 to 65 years with clinically confirmed T2DM and obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m²).
3. HbA1c ranging from 6.5% to 10%.
4. Documented history of stable CVD risk factors (hypertension, hyperlipidemia) managed for at least 3 months.
5. No previous use of SGLT2 inhibitors or investigational medications within 30 days prior to enrollment.

Exclusion Criteria:
1. History of heart failure or unstable angina.
2. Active malignancy, severe renal impairment (eGFR <60 mL/min), or uncontrolled hypertension (BP ≥180/110 mmHg).
3. Pregnant or lactating women.
4. Severe hepatic dysfunction or contraindications to MRI.
5. Enrollment in another clinical trial within 30 days prior to consent.

Primary Endpoint: Change in composite cardiovascular outcomes as determined by the occurrence of myocardial infarction, stroke, or cardiovascular mortality at one year.

Secondary Endpoint: Change in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and quality of life assessments using the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ).

Conclusion: This trial aims to examine the cardiovascular impacts of dapagliflozin in a complex patient population characterized by obesity and diabetes. Anticipated findings may elucidate the limitations of dapagliflozin's therapeutic effectiveness in modifying cardiovascular risk profiles in the context of obesity-related persistent inflammatory states, which may contribute to the observed clinical trial failures in prior studies. By assessing the intersection of these diseases, results may clarify the need for more tailored and multifaceted therapeutic interventions beyond pharmacological approaches.

Key Metrics:
- Cardiovascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, death)
- Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF)

Anticipated Challenges: Participant retention and adherence may be affected by tolerability issues linked to dapa-gliflozin, including gastrointestinal disturbances and the risk of volume depletion, which necessitates thorough monitoring throughout the trial duration.
