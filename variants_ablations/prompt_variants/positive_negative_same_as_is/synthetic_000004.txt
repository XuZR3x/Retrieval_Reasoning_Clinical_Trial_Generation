**Study Title:** A Randomized, Open-Label, Phase IIIb Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of Darunavir Versus a Standard Combination Therapy in HIV-1 Infected Patients With Suppressed Viral Loads

**Study Design:** This is a multi-center, open-label, randomized trial comparing a once-daily administration of darunavir/ritonavir versus a standard treatment regimen comprising two nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) plus darunavir/ritonavir in HIV-1 infected adults with documented viral load suppression (HIV RNA <50 copies/mL) for at least 48 weeks prior to enrollment.

**Background:** Given darunavir's high antiviral potency and robust genetic barrier to resistance, as well as its favorable safety and tolerability profile, this study aims to establish darunavir monotherapy as a non-inferior alternative to current standard antiretroviral treatment regimens. The necessity for simplified treatment strategies is underscored by the chronicity of HIV, which often requires lifelong management while minimizing treatment toxicity.

**Objective:** To determine whether darunavir/ritonavir can maintain virological suppression without the addition of other antiretroviral agents compared to a standard regimen of two NRTIs and darunavir/ritonavir.

**Participants:** Approximately 300 participants will be enrolled, targeting both male and female patients aged 18 years and older, from various demographic backgrounds to ensure broad applicability of the findings.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Documented HIV-1 infection.
- Plasma HIV-1 RNA levels consistently <50 copies/mL for at least 48 weeks prior to screening.
- Current antiretroviral therapy regimen including darunavir/ritonavir plus two NRTIs for a minimum of 12 months prior to screening.
- CD4 cell count ≥200 cells/mm³ at screening.
- Providing written informed consent.



**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior history of virologic failure on a darunavir-containing regimen.
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of darunavir/ritonavir.
- Co-infection with hepatitis B or C within the last six months.
- Any significant acute illness requiring immediate medical intervention.

**Randomization and Intervention:** After screening, participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either darunavir/ritonavir 800/100 mg orally once daily or standard combination therapy comprising darunavir/ritonavir with two NRTIs of the investigator's choice, administered for a duration of 48 weeks. 

**Outcome Measures:**
- **Primary Outcome:** Proportion of participants achieving sustained virological suppression (HIV RNA <50 copies/mL) at week 48, demonstrating the high potency and genetic barrier to resistance of darunavir.
  
- **Secondary Outcomes:** 
   - Changes in CD4 cell counts from baseline to week 48, assessing the immunological benefits of darunavir.
   - Safety and tolerability profile compared to standard therapy, with particular focus on adverse events.
   - Quality of life assessments using EQ-5D and HIV-specific patient-reported outcomes.

**Monitoring and Follow-Up:** Participants will be evaluated at baseline and at weeks 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, and 48. Adverse events will be recorded at each visit, along with vital signs and laboratory evaluations to monitor liver function and renal safety.

**Statistical Analysis:** Descriptive statistics will be used for baseline characteristics, while inferential statistical methods, including chi-squared tests for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous variables, will be employed to compare virological outcomes and safety data between the two treatment arms.

**Conclusion:** This clinical trial aims to corroborate darunavir as an effective monotherapy option for HIV-1 infected patients who have achieved virological suppression, potentially transforming treatment paradigms by simplifying regimens through the elimination of additional antiretroviral agents. The findings are expected to enhance patient adherence and improve long-term treatment sustainability amidst ongoing global efforts to combat HIV.
