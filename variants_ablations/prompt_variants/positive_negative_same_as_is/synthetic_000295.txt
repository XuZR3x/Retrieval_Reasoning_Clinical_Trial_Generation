**Negative Clinical Trial of Telbivudine: Efficacy and Safety of Telbivudine in Patients with Chronic Hepatitis B Virus Infection with Advanced Liver Disease**

**A Multicenter, Open-label Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Telbivudine in Patients with Chronic Hepatitis B Virus Infection and Advanced Liver Disease.**

This clinical trial was designed to evaluate the antiviral efficacy and safety of telbivudine in patients presenting with chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, particularly in individuals with advanced liver disease (cirrhosis or significant liver fibrosis). Current antiviral therapies demonstrate limited clinical efficacy in this population, owing to the concerns surrounding the drug's safety profile and antiviral resistance. 

**Objective:**
The primary objective of this study is to determine the viral suppression efficacy of telbivudine at Weeks 24 and 48, assessed by the rate of patients achieving serum HBV DNA levels <200 copies/mL, and to evaluate the safety profile focusing on the incidence of renal impairment and exacerbation of liver conditions during treatment.

**Study Design:**
This trial was structured as an open-label, single-arm study across multiple centers, with a planned enrollment of 150 subjects. Patients were administered telbivudine (600 mg orally once daily) for a total duration of 48 weeks. All participants received intensive monitoring in accordance with defined clinical and laboratory assessments.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years and older.
- Confirmed chronic HBV infection with positive HBsAg for at least 6 months prior to screening.
- Clinically documented advanced liver disease, defined as a METAVIR score ≥F3 or compensated cirrhosis.
- Serum HBV DNA ≥20,000 IU/mL.
- ALT levels at least 1.5x ULN.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Concurrent diagnosis of other chronic viral hepatitis (e.g., HCV, HDV) or HIV.
- Renal impairment defined as estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <30 mL/min.
- History of telbivudine or other antivirals within the previous year.
- Use of immunosuppressive medications or systemic corticosteroids within the last 30 days.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

**Results:**
Despite initial expectations, the trial met with considerable challenges. Only 35% of participants achieved HBV DNA suppression to <200 copies/mL by Week 48, significantly below the pre-established target of 70%. Adverse events reported predominantly included renal function decline and incident hepatic decompensation, underscoring unsatisfactory safety outcomes in this cohort. 

Close examination revealed an alarming rate of resistance mutations in 40% of subjects, elucidating a concerning trend that diminished the long-term efficacy of telbivudine as a therapeutic option for patients with advanced liver disease. 

The duration of treatment and the restricted patient selection criteria may have limited extrapolation of findings to a broader, representative cohort with HBV and advanced liver disease in real-world settings.

**Conclusion:**
This trial underlines the limitations of telbivudine in treating chronic HBV infection in patients with advanced liver disease. Both the limited antiviral efficacy and the adverse safety profile, particularly regarding renal function and liver condition exacerbation, contributed to its unfavorable outcome. Findings advocate for reevaluation of treatment strategies in this vulnerable demographic and suggest further research on alternative antiviral agents with improved efficacy and better safety profiles.
