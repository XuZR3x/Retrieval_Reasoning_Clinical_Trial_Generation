### Negative Clinical Trial of Tamoxifen: A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Tamoxifen in Combination with Novel mTOR Inhibitor in Patients with Hormone Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer

**Study Title:** Phase II Study Assessing the Efficacy of Tamoxifen in Combination with mTOR Inhibitor in Patients with Hormone Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer Progressing on Prior Hormonal Therapy

**Introduction:** Hormone receptor-positive breast cancer (HR+ BC) remains a significant clinical challenge, particularly in cases demonstrating resistance to anti-estrogen therapies including tamoxifen. Prior investigations indicate that signaling pathways involving mTOR contribute to resistance, yet clinical efficacy of combining tamoxifen with mTOR inhibitors remains unverified. This study aims to determine the efficacy of tamoxifen combined with an mTOR inhibitor in HR+ BC patients who have progressed on prior hormonal therapy.

**Objectives:** The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the objective response rate (ORR) of patients receiving the combination of tamoxifen and an mTOR inhibitor following progression on prior aromatase inhibitors or tamoxifen therapy.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed breast cancer with positive hormone receptor status (ER+ and/or PR+).
- Patients aged 18 to 75 years.
- Disease that has progressed following at least one line of endocrine therapy (including aromatase inhibitors or tamoxifen).
- At least one measurable lesion as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- An Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ and bone marrow function, defined by specific laboratory criteria (e.g., Neutrophils ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L, Platelets ≥ 100 x 10^9/L, and adequate liver and renal function).
- Informed written consent prior to any trial-related procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with ER- and PR-negative tumors.
- Prior treatment with mTOR inhibitors or dual hormonal therapy in any setting.
- Presence of visceral crisis or symptomatic metastases requiring immediate chemotherapy.
- Active other malignancies, except non-melanomatous skin cancer or adequately treated in situ cervical cancer.
- Known contraindications to tamoxifen or mTOR inhibitors (e.g., severe liver dysfunction, active thromboembolic disorders).
- Prior uncontrolled chronic physical or psychiatric illness preventing completion of trial requirements.
- Concurrent participation in other interventional clinical trials.

**Methodology:** This multicenter, open-label, single-arm trial will enroll patients who meet the aforementioned criteria. Eligible participants will receive tamoxifen at a standard dosage in combination with the mTOR inhibitor, administered orally once daily. The treatment duration will be assessed at 6-month intervals, with imaging and clinical evaluations performed every two cycles to assess treatment response according to RECIST criteria.

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary endpoint will be the objective response rate, defined as the proportion of patients who achieve a complete or partial response to the treatment based on imaging criteria.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Progression-free survival (PFS) evaluated at 6 and 12 months.
- Safety and tolerability profile, assessed by adverse events (AEs) occurring during treatment using NCI CTCAE v5.0.
- Quality of life assessed using validated tools administered at baseline and every two cycles.

**Results:** The trial will be considered negative if the objective response rate is less than 15%, which reflects insufficient clinical benefit amidst existing resistance mechanisms. Factors such as inadequate optimization of endocrine therapy prior to trial enrollment may influence these results, as prior exposure levels could diminish anticipated treatment efficacy.

**Conclusion:** This Phase II study aims to clarify the role of combining an mTOR inhibitor with tamoxifen in HR+ BC patients with established resistance. Given the persistent challenges of hormonal therapy resistance, outcomes from this trial could offer insight into alternative therapeutic strategies, though existing evidence suggests potential avenues of failure due to underlying resistance mechanisms and patient selection limitations.

**Overall Significance:** The findings from this study could contribute critical data to the ongoing dialogue regarding optimization of therapeutic combinations in hormone-sensitive cancers, providing a deeper understanding of resistance mechanisms and enhancing the framework for future research endeavors.
