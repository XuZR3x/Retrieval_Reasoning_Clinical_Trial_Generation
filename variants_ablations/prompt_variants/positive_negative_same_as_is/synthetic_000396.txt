**Positive Clinical Trial of Entinostat: A Phase II Study to Evaluate Entinostat in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients With Advanced Melanoma**

**Study Overview:**
This Phase II multicenter, double-blind, randomized trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of entinostat in conjunction with pembrolizumab in patients with unresectable or metastatic melanoma that has progressed after at least one prior immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy. The integration of entinostat—a potent histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor—aims to enhance the efficacy of pembrolizumab by potentially modulating the immune response and overcoming resistance mechanisms.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of entinostat combined with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced melanoma.  
II. To compare the progression-free survival (PFS) between the treatment group receiving entinostat in combination with pembrolizumab and the control group receiving pembrolizumab alone.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To evaluate the adverse event profile and tolerability of the combination therapy.  
II. To investigate changes in histone acetylation levels as potential biomarkers of response to treatment.  
III. To assess patient-reported outcomes pertaining to quality of life during the treatment period.

**OUTLINE:**
Patients will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either entinostat and pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab with placebo.  
- **Entinostat** will be administered orally at the optimized maximum tolerated dose (MTD) on days 1 and 15 of a 28-day cycle.  
- **Pembrolizumab** will be administered intravenously at the standard dose of 200 mg on day 1 of each cycle.  
- Treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal from the study.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced (unresectable or metastatic) melanoma.  
- Documented progression of disease after at least one prior treatment with an anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 therapy.  
- At least one measurable lesion per RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
- ECOG performance status of 0-2 and life expectancy of at least 3 months.  
- Availability of archived tumor tissue for correlative studies.  
- Male or female patients aged ≥ 18 years.  
- Adequate hematologic and hepatic function, with specific laboratory thresholds defined by trial protocol.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior therapy with other histone deacetylase inhibitors or reactivation of melanoma following pembrolizumab plus entinostat therapy.  
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy or a history of severe immune-related adverse events.  
- Concurrent use of systemic immunosuppressants or any concurrent anticancer therapies outside of the trial protocol.  
- Brain metastases or central nervous system involvement that has not been treated or is stable.  
- Known hypersensitivity to entinostat, pembrolizumab, or their excipients.

This study harnesses several key factors that may lead to successful outcomes. The rigorous inclusion criteria are designed to carefully select patients who are more likely to respond to the entinostat therapy, thereby ensuring that only individuals with a defined disease etiology are enrolled. The synergistic potential of entinostat combined with pembrolizumab reflects a novel treatment paradigm, grounded in the biological rationale that HDAC inhibition may enhance anti-tumor immunity by increasing antigen presentation and T-cell activation.

Additionally, the collection of comprehensive correlative data, including histone acetylation levels and immune biomarkers, aims to elucidate the mechanisms underlying treatment responses and potentially aid future therapeutic strategies. The adopted methodology of a randomized, double-blind design further strengthens the integrity and reliability of the results, minimizing biases and ensuring a balanced assessment of the intervention's effectiveness.

As outcomes are analyzed, we anticipate that this strategic combination could lead to meaningful clinical benefits for patients suffering from advanced melanoma, particularly those who have previously experienced treatment failure with existing immune checkpoint inhibitors.
