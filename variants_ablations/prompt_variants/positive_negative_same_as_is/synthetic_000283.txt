**Negative Clinical Trial of Lacosamide: Trial Evaluating the Efficacy of Lacosamide in Managing Chronic Pain in Fibromyalgia Patients**

---

**Title:** A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Trial to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of Lacosamide in the Management of Chronic Pain in Patients with Fibromyalgia

**Objective:** This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of lacosamide (LCM) at a daily dose of 600 mg in alleviating chronic pain symptoms associated with fibromyalgia. The trial seeks to determine whether treatment with LCM yields significant improvements in the primary endpoint compared to placebo.

**Study Design:** A Phase III, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Changes in the Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) for Pain Intensity from baseline to the end of the 12-week treatment phase.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** 
  1. Changes in the Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire (FIQ) scores.
  2. Evaluation of depression severity using the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9).
  3. Assessment of quality of life utilizing the Short Form Health Survey (SF-36).

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult men and women aged 18-65 years.
- Diagnosed with fibromyalgia according to the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) criteria.
- Experiencing moderate to severe pain intensity (NRS 4 or greater).

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Current use of opioids, muscle relaxants, or any investigational agents.
- Significant psychiatric disorders or conditions that may impact pain perception.
- Pregnant or lactating women.

---

**Conduct and Enrollment:** This trial projected an enrollment of 200 patients, with participant recruitment initiated at multiple centers. However, throughout the enrollment period, substantial challenges were encountered. Only 50 patients were recruited due to overly stringent eligibility criteria and a high attrition rate during screenings, which prevented achieving adequate sample size.

**Interim Analysis Results:** The first interim analysis indicated that the lacosamide group exhibited no statistically significant change in NRS scores compared to the placebo group at the 6-week mark. This led to the early termination of the trial despite the absence of notable safety concerns, signaling potential futility in treatment effectiveness.

**Primary Outcome Measures:** Changes in the NRS for Pain Intensity scores revealed no meaningful improvement (mean difference of 0.5 points), failing to translate into clinically relevant benefits. Secondary outcomes, including FIQ and PHQ-9 scores, similarly demonstrated no significant treatment effects.

**Pharmacodynamic Concerns:** Observations suggested that the pharmacodynamics of lacosamide did not translate effectively to pain management in fibromyalgia, which is often characterized by complex interacting mechanisms of pain that lacosamide may not address effectively. 

**Conclusion:** Given the failed recruitment efforts, inadequate sample size, and lack of efficacy in primary outcome measures, the trial data led to the conclusion that lacosamide, despite having a favorable safety profile, was ineffective for the management of chronic pain associated with fibromyalgia. This study reflects the challenges inherent in targeting complex syndromes with single pharmacological agents and highlights the need for continued research exploring tailored therapeutic approaches for fibromyalgia patients.

---

**Report Summary:** This clinical trial reinforces the reasons contributing to clinical trial challenges as observed in previous lacosamide studies—including inadequate sample size, lack of efficacy across primary outcomes, and mismatch of therapeutic intervention with complex clinical needs—each of which culminated in a premature termination of the trial owing to futility.
