**Positive clinical trial of sildenafil citrate: The Impact of Sildenafil Citrate on Increasing Exercise Capacity and Quality of Life in Patients with Chronic Heart Failure A Multi-Center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Sildenafil Citrate in Enhancing Exercise Tolerance and Quality of Life in Patients with Chronic Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction.** 

**Introduction:** This study is designed to assess the efficacy of sildenafil citrate in improving exercise capacity and overall quality of life in patients diagnosed with chronic heart failure (CHF) who exhibit preserved left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). The pharmacological properties of sildenafil, which includes the inhibition of phosphodiesterase type 5, may address endothelial dysfunction often observed in CHF, thus enhancing exercise capacity and functional status.

**Objective:** The primary objective of the study is to evaluate the change in exercise capacity, measured as the peak oxygen consumption (VO2) during a cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET). The secondary objective will assess quality of life improvement using the Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ) score.

**Study Design:** This is a multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving 200 patients with CHF and preserved ejection fraction. Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either sildenafil citrate (50 mg TID) or placebo for a duration of 12 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 40 to 75 years with a diagnosis of CHF (NYHA Class II or III).
- LVEF ≥ 50% as determined by echocardiography.
- Peak VO2 (less than or equal to 20 mL/kg/min) as determined during CPET.
- Capable of providing informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Recent myocardial infarction (within the last 6 months).
- Uncontrolled hypertension (systolic blood pressure > 180 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure > 110 mmHg).
- History of significant cardiovascular events, such as stroke or transient ischemic attacks.
- Concurrent medications that contraindicate the use of phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors.

**Methods:** The primary endpoint will be the change in peak VO2 from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints will include changes in quality of life assessed through the MLHFQ, as well as composite safety assessments including adverse event monitoring.

Patients will undergo both CPET and MLHFQ assessment at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks, ensuring comprehensive evaluation of efficacy. Continuous safety assessments will occur throughout the study, and all participants will be monitored for vital signs and potential adverse events.

**Statistical Analysis:** A sample size calculation based on previous studies indicates that 200 total participants will provide adequate power to detect clinically meaningful differences in peak VO2 and MLHFQ scores. Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline characteristics, while comparative analyses will utilize appropriate parametric or non-parametric tests based on data distribution.

**Results:** The anticipated outcome is that treatment with sildenafil citrate will lead to statistically significant improvements in exercise capacity and quality of life compared to placebo, supporting the hypothesis that sildenafil citrate enhances physiological function in patients with CHF.

**Conclusion:** This trial is positioned to contribute critically to the understanding of sildenafil citrate's therapeutic potential in improving functional capacity and quality of life in patients with CHF with preserved ejection fraction. Enhanced hemodynamic functioning may manifest through improved nitric oxide signaling and systemic vasodilation, closely paralleling its established benefits in other vascular-related conditions. 

**In summary, the study will elucidate additional therapeutic avenues for sildenafil citrate beyond its traditional indications, spotlighting its potential role in chronic cardiovascular conditions, thereby advocating for its broader application in clinical practice.**
