**Negative Clinical Trial of Carboplatin: The Efficacy of Carboplatin Plus Bevacizumab in Patients with Advanced Ovarian Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** Carboplatin, a platinum-based chemotherapeutic agent, is utilized in the treatment of various malignancies, including ovarian cancer. The rationale for this trial was to evaluate whether the addition of bevacizumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), enhances the chemotherapy efficacy of carboplatin in patients diagnosed with advanced ovarian cancer.

**PURPOSE:** This multicenter, open-label, Phase II trial aims to ascertain the clinical effectiveness, safety profile, and overall response rate of the combination of carboplatin and bevacizumab compared to carboplatin administered alone in patients with advanced (Stage III/IV) ovarian cancer.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objective:** Determine the overall response rate in patients treated with carboplatin plus bevacizumab compared to carboplatin monotherapy.
- **Secondary Objectives:** Assess overall survival, progression-free survival, and the tolerability of the treatment regimens in the target population.

**OUTLINE:** Patients are randomized to receive either:
- Arm A: Carboplatin intravenous (IV) on day 1 every 21 days for up to 6 cycles, or
- Arm B: Carboplatin as per Arm A in combination with bevacizumab IV on days 1 and 15 for the same treatment cycle duration.

Patients are followed for 2 years post-treatment for assessment of disease response per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) guidelines and adverse event monitoring.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 200 patients will be accrued for this study over approximately 5 years.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced epithelial ovarian cancer (Stage III or IV).
- Measurable and evaluable disease as determined by clinical imaging.
- No prior exposure to systemic anticancer therapy for advanced disease.
- No presence of active central nervous system metastases.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: 18 years or older.
- Performance status: ECOG 0-2.
- Life expectancy: At least 12 weeks.
- Adequate hematologic function: WBC ≥ 3,000/mm³, platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³.
- Renal function within normal limits: Creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Concurrent malignancies or prior malignancy treatment within 5 years.
- Known hypersensitivity or adverse reaction to carboplatin or bevacizumab.
- Any significant uncontrolled comorbidity or active infection.
- Prior treatment with VEGF inhibitors.

**RESULTS:** 
The trial failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in the overall response rate between the two arms, with a notable proportion of patients exhibiting disease progression within the first 6 months of treatment. The combination therapy was associated with increased incidence of high-grade adverse effects, leading to a higher rate of treatment discontinuation, notably due to hypertension and proteinuria.

**CONCLUSION:** The combination of carboplatin with bevacizumab did not yield sufficient therapeutic benefit in improving patient outcomes for those with advanced ovarian cancer as compared to carboplatin alone. The observed high treatment toxicity and lack of synergistic action may contribute to the lack of efficacy. These findings underscore the necessity for optimization in dosing strategies and patient selection to improve therapeutic indices in future investigations.

**TOXICITY:** The adverse event profile indicated significant higher rates of hypertension (grade ≥ 3), gastrointestinal toxicity, and hematological abnormalities (neutropenia and thrombocytopenia), which called for dose adjustments and raised concerns regarding the overall tolerability of the combination regimen. 

This study highlights critical factors contributing to the failure of carboplatin in combination therapies (insufficient efficacy, high toxicity, and unanswered patient selection biases), warranting further exploration of adjunctive strategies in this patient cohort.
