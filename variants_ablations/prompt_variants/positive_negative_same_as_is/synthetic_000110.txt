Negative clinical trial of montelukast sodium: Montelukast for Seasonal Asthma Exacerbations in Pediatric Patients (0476-401)(COMPLETED)  
A Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Montelukast Sodium in Reducing Asthma Exacerbations During Peak Allergy Seasons in Children Aged 5 to 12 Years

This multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized trial aimed to assess the effectiveness of montelukast sodium in managing asthma exacerbations that occur during peak allergy seasons among pediatric patients. The study focused on evaluating the rate of exacerbation-related healthcare utilization in a cohort of children aged 5 to 12 years diagnosed with seasonal allergic asthma.

Inclusion Criteria:  
- Male and female pediatric patients aged 5 to 12 years with a clinical diagnosis of asthma that is active during peak allergy seasons.  
- Patients must provide baseline evidence of asthma symptoms exacerbated by seasonal allergens, corroborated by positive allergen test results.  

Exclusion Criteria:  
- Patients with comorbid respiratory disorders such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).  
- Patients with an asthma action plan indicating severe persistent asthma requiring daily high-dose inhaled corticosteroids or systemic corticosteroids.  

Primary Endpoint:  
- Rate of asthma exacerbations resulting in unscheduled physician visits, oral corticosteroid initiation, or emergency department visits over the 12-week treatment period.  

Secondary Endpoint:  
- Changes in forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1) from baseline at treatment weeks 4 and 12.  
- Frequency of rescue inhaler (β-agonist) use, measured as the mean daily usage compared to baseline throughout the study duration.  

Despite conducting the trial for the specified period, the outcomes demonstrated insufficient pharmacological efficacy of montelukast sodium in this pediatric population. The variability in asthma phenotypes, coupled with a lack of robust endpoint measurement—such as the recurrent occurrence of exacerbations—hindered the analysis of treatment effectiveness. Furthermore, deficiencies in the trial design, including an inadequate treatment duration to assess lasting therapeutic benefits and diverse baseline asthma severities, contributed to non-significant results.

The conclusion drawn from this investigation is that montelukast sodium did not notably reduce the rate of seasonal asthma exacerbations compared to placebo, thereby reinforcing the need for individualized management strategies that account for the multifactorial nature of pediatric asthma. The findings highlight the necessity for future trials to refine patient stratification criteria and enhance nebulized trial methodologies, aiming to address the incongruences observed in previous investigations regarding montelukast efficacy.
