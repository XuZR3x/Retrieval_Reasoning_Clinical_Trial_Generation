**Negative clinical trial of atomoxetine: Longitudinal Evaluation of Atomoxetine in ADHD Cohorts with Comorbid Disorders**

**Longitudinal Efficacy of Atomoxetine on Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder in Children and Adolescents with Comorbid Conditions**

This longitudinal study aims to assess the effectiveness of atomoxetine in reducing symptoms of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) in children and adolescents presenting with comorbid psychological disorders. ADHD is associated with significant impairment across various functional domains, and children suffering from comorbidities may exhibit a unique treatment response due to their complex clinical presentations. Despite the established efficacy of atomoxetine as a non-stimulant option for ADHD, its effectiveness in populations with concurrent psychological disorders remains inadequately explored.

Participants will be recruited for this double-blind, placebo-controlled trial and randomly assigned to receive either atomoxetine or placebo for a period of 12 weeks. Comprehensive baseline assessments will be conducted to evaluate ADHD symptom severity and the impact of coexisting psychiatric conditions, with an aim to elucidate how such conditions may confound atomoxetine's effectiveness.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age range of 6 to 17 years
- Diagnostic confirmation of ADHD as per DSM-5 criteria
- Presence of one or more comorbid conditions including, but not limited to, generalized anxiety disorder, depressive disorders, or conduct disorder
- ADHD severity measured by the ADHD-IV Rating Scale at least 1.5 standard deviations above age and sex norms
- Capacity to provide informed consent alongside guardian support

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Current use of other psychotropic medications that may interfere with atomoxetine
- Active substance use disorder other than tobacco
- Presence of acute suicidal ideation as evidenced by clinical judgment during screening
- Significant medical illnesses that could compromise treatment safety
- History of severe adverse reactions to atomoxetine

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
- Change in ADHD-IV-Rating Scale Total Score at 12 weeks assessed through parent-reported and teacher-reported measures, capturing the multifaceted nature of ADHD symptoms. Higher total scores indicate more severe symptomatology.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
- Assessment of comorbid condition severity as measured by standardized scales specific to anxiety and depressive disorders, allowing for evaluation of the interplay between ADHD and its comorbidities.
- Treatment adherence monitored through electronic medication event monitoring systems, considering the impact of adherence on treatment outcomes, with implications for the potential comorbidities affecting compliance.

Given the multifactorial nature of ADHD and the observed treatment adherence issues in prior studies, this trial hypothesizes that atomoxetine will not yield significant reductions in ADHD symptoms among children and adolescents with additional comorbid conditions compared to placebo. Furthermore, the prolonged study duration is designed to mitigate concerns regarding the short assessment periods previously noted as limitations in assessing atomoxetine's efficacy.

The outcomes will be pivotal in understanding the role of atomoxetine in intricately diagnosed populations and may elucidate important aspects regarding the management of ADHD in the presence of comorbidities, as well as identifying subgroups more likely to benefit from non-stimulant treatment options. This study's findings are anticipated to contribute significantly to clinical guidelines concerning the treatment of ADHD in complex pediatric populations.
