**Positive clinical trial of dapagliflozin: Evaluating the Long-Term Efficacy and Safety of Dapagliflozin in Combination With Lifestyle Modifications in Overweight Adults With Type 2 Diabetes**

A 78-week, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Active-Controlled, International Phase III Study to Investigate the Efficacy and Safety of Dapagliflozin 10 mg Once Daily in Combination With Structured Lifestyle Interventions Versus Standard Care in Overweight Adults With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Who Have Inadequate Glycemic Control on Background Metformin Therapy.

This study aims to evaluate the effects of dapagliflozin, alongside a structured lifestyle modification program, in comparison to standard care on glycemic control and weight management in overweight adults diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus inadequately controlled on metformin monotherapy.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years or older
- Confirmed diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus
- HbA1c levels between 7.5% and 10.5%
- Body mass index (BMI) between 27.0 kg/m² and 40.0 kg/m²
- Currently on a stable dose of metformin (≥ 1500 mg/day) for at least 12 weeks prior to enrollment

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Type 1 diabetes mellitus
- Previous use of SGLT2 inhibitors
- History of diabetic ketoacidosis
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 45 mL/min)
- Acute cardiovascular events within the past six months

**Primary Endpoint:**
Adjusted Mean Change From Baseline in HbA1c Levels at Week 78

The primary endpoint of the study is to assess the adjusted mean change from baseline in HbA1c levels at week 78 for participants receiving dapagliflozin in conjunction with a structured lifestyle intervention versus those receiving standard care, which includes ongoing metformin therapy without additional lifestyle modification support.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Change in Body Weight at Week 78
- Incidence of Adverse Events
- Change in Quality of Life Metrics as Assessed by EQ-5D-5L

This clinical trial incorporates the favorable mechanism of action of dapagliflozin, which effectively promotes glucose excretion and addresses the pathophysiology of type 2 diabetes. Additionally, the robust patient selection criteria target a well-defined population, ensuring minimal variability in treatment response and enhancing the interpretability of outcome measures. The extended duration of the study underpins the analysis of long-term efficacy and safety profiles, catering to the necessity of observing sustained benefits over time.

The integration of structured lifestyle modifications in conjunction with dapagliflozin aims to replicate the complementary treatment strategy observed in previous trials, therefore augmenting the therapeutic outcomes. Given dapagliflozin’s established safety profile characterized by low incidence rates of adverse effects, this study anticipates high patient retention and adherence, bolstering the likelihood of positive clinical results.

In conclusion, the design and rationale of this trial are grounded in evidence-based practices, hypothesizing that dapagliflozin in combination with comprehensive lifestyle management will promote significant improvements in glycemic control and weight reduction among overweight adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus. The trial's findings are expected to provide substantial clinical insights and contribute to the optimization of therapeutic approaches in diabetes management.
