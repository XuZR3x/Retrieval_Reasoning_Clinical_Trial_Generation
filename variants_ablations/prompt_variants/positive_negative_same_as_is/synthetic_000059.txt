### Negative Clinical Trial of Topiramate: Topiramate in the Prevention of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder Symptoms in Adults Following Traumatic Events

**Trial Name:** The Topiramate POST-TRAUMA Study

**Study Design:**
The primary objective of this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial is to assess the effectiveness of topiramate in preventing the development of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) symptoms among adults exposed to traumatic events. This study will enroll 100 participants who have recently experienced a traumatic event, such as a natural disaster, unanticipated violent incident, or catastrophic accident. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either topiramate (maximum dose of 200 mg/day) or placebo over a 12-week period.

**Background:** 
Post-traumatic stress disorder is characterized by debilitating symptoms that can significantly impair an individual’s functioning and quality of life. While pharmacotherapy is used to mitigate PTSD symptoms, the current pharmacotherapeutic arsenal has limited efficacy. Topiramate, an FDA-approved agent for epilepsy and migraine prophylaxis, has shown promise in decreasing symptoms related to trauma exposure in preliminary studies. However, large-scale investigations have yet to validate its efficacy in preventing PTSD symptomatology.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults aged 18-65 years.
2. Self-reported exposure to a traumatic event within the last 30 days.
3. Ability to provide informed consent.
4. No history of psychiatric disorders necessitating immediate intervention other than situational distress.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of severe psychiatric illness, substance use disorder, or suicidality requiring intervention.
2. Current use of any psychotropic medication that could confound outcomes.
3. Significant medical illness (e.g., renal impairment).
4. Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
The primary efficacy endpoint will be the change in PTSD symptom severity, assessed by clinician-administered PTSD Scale (CAPS-5) at baseline and post-treatment (Week 12). Secondary outcomes include alterations in anxiety, depression, and sleep disturbances, as rated by the Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI), Beck Depression Inventory (BDI), and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI).

**Methodology:**
Subjects will undergo a systematic screening and will be followed through four distinct phases:
- **Screening Phase (2 weeks):** Participants will be evaluated with structured clinical interviews and screening assessments.
- **Treatment Phase (12 weeks):** Participants will receive either topiramate or placebo in a double-blind manner. The titration of topiramate will begin at 25 mg/day, escalating weekly to a maximum of 200 mg/day, based on tolerability.
- **Follow-Up (4 weeks):** Participants will undergo assessments to monitor symptom persistence and any adverse events.
 
**Recruitment:**
Participants will be recruited through community partners, local mental health facilities, and advertisements in local media emphasizing the study's focus on PTSD prevention. Recruitment strategies will prioritize diversity, ensuring representation across various demographic groups.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Data analysis will employ intention-to-treat (ITT) methodology, with primary comparisons made using repeated measures ANOVA to evaluate changes in CAPS scores over time between treatment groups. An alpha level of 0.05 will be utilized for statistical significance.

**Adverse Events Monitoring:**
Safety assessments will occur bi-weekly during the treatment phase, focusing on common side effects of topiramate, including cognitive impairment, mood alterations, and metabolic side effects. Any participants demonstrating severe adverse effects will be withdrawn and provided appropriate psychiatric referrals.

**Ethical Considerations:**
The study protocol will be submitted for review and approval by the Institutional Review Board. Informed consent will be obtained from all participants, ensuring full apprehension of study risks and benefits.

---

**Results Anticipated:**
Given the previous failures in demonstrating the effectiveness of topiramate in various clinical populations, we hypothesize a limited reduction in PTSD symptoms when compared to placebo. Factors such as limited efficacy evidence in prior studies, high dropout rates, inappropriate patient selection criteria, potential cognitive side effects, and the inadequate duration of treatment could reflect in the anticipated outcomes. The study aims to elucidate these concerns by providing robust data regarding topiramate's utility in PTSD prevention after trauma exposure.

**Conclusion:**
The Topiramate POST-TRAUMA Study seeks to clarify the role of topiramate in the context of PTSD prevention, contributing to a deeper understanding of this agent's efficacy and safety profile amid clinical trials marked by prior failures and challenges.
