**Negative Clinical Trial of Sorafenib (nexavar, bay43-9006): A Phase II Study of Sorafenib in Combination With Nivolumab in Patients With Advanced Hepatocellular Carcinoma Previously Treated With Atezolizumab**

**Objective:**  
This Phase II, randomized, open-label, multicenter study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of sorafenib in combination with nivolumab versus nivolumab alone in patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who have previously experienced disease progression on atezolizumab therapy. The primary endpoint is progression-free survival (PFS).

**Study Design:**  
Patients will be randomized (1:1) to receive either sorafenib (400 mg orally twice daily) in combination with nivolumab (3 mg/kg intravenously every two weeks) or nivolumab alone. The duration of treatment will be until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed hepatocellular carcinoma.
- Patients must have received at least one line of systemic therapy, including atezolizumab, and demonstrated disease progression according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1.
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST.
- Adequate organ function as assessed by laboratory parameters, which include:
  - Serum bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN)
  - Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) ≤ 2.5 x ULN (≤ 5x ULN if liver metastases)
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/µl

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Evidence of ongoing active infection or history of non-remission of prior malignancies.
- Significant cardiovascular disease or hospitalization for cardiac events within the last six months.
- Patients with previous or concurrent malignancies.
- Known history of autoimmune disease or uncontrolled intercurrent illness.
- Any condition that precludes the patient from obtaining adequate informed consent.
- Prior exposure to PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors besides atezolizumab.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
Progression-Free Survival (PFS) defined as the time from randomization to disease progression or death from any cause, whichever occurs first.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Overall Survival (OS).
- Objective response rate (ORR) determined by RECIST.
- Safety and tolerability assessed via adverse event reporting in accordance with National Cancer Institute (NCI) Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

**Rationale for Possible Negative Outcomes:**  
1. The inclusion of a heavily pretreated cohort, specifically those previously treated with atezolizumab, may show intrinsic resistance mechanisms prevalent in HCC, limiting the efficacy of both sorafenib and nivolumab. 
2. Prior history of intolerance to systemic therapies (including atezolizumab) in the participant population may predispose to increased adverse events, thus affecting treatment adherence and outcomes.
3. The potential for overlapping toxicities between sorafenib and nivolumab could lead to significant treatment discontinuation or dosage modification, adversely affecting PFS assessment.
4. Selection bias arising from stringent inclusion criteria may limit applicability of the findings to the general HCC patient population, particularly those with poorer prognostic indicators or more aggressive disease phenotype.
5. A lack of demonstrable clinical benefit in PFS may indicate the need for alternative treatment strategies in an advanced state of HCC, underscoring the limitations of sorafenib as a successor therapy.

**Progression-Free Survival:**  
PFS is calculated from the date of randomization to the date of first observed disease progression per RECIST or the date of death, if death occurs before progression is documented. Patients with no progression or death will be censored at the last date of tumor evaluation.

**Study Status:**  
The results from this study are anticipated to provide critical data related to the efficacy of sorafenib and nivolumab combination therapy in advanced HCC, with an emphasis on clinical response and safety profile.
