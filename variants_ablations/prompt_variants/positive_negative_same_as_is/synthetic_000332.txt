<clinical_trial>
    <title>A Phase II, Randomized, Open-Label Study of Cabazitaxel Versus Standard Chemotherapy in Patients With Advanced or Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Following First-Line Therapy Failure</title>
    <description>This multicenter, open-label phase II trial aims to evaluate the clinical efficacy of cabazitaxel compared to standard second-line chemotherapy regimens (docetaxel or pemetrexed) in patients with advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have experienced disease progression after previous first-line treatments. The primary endpoint of the study is progression-free survival (PFS).</description>

    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criteria>Histologically confirmed advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.</criteria>
        <criteria>Documented disease progression following one prior line of systemic therapy.</criteria>
        <criteria>Age ≥ 18 years.</criteria>
        <criteria>ECOG performance status 0-2, allowing for active patient engagement.</criteria>
        <criteria>At least one measurable lesion according to RECIST criteria v1.1.</criteria>
        <criteria>Life expectancy ≥ 3 months.</criteria>
        <criteria>Adequate organ function defined as:  
            - Neutrophils ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L  
            - Platelets ≥ 100 x 10^9/L  
            - Hemoglobin ≥ 9.0 g/dL  
            - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN  
            - AST and ALT ≤ 1.5 x ULN  
            - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 x ULN.</criteria>
        <criteria>Informed consent obtained prior to any study-specific procedures.</criteria>
    </inclusion_criteria>

    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criteria>Prior treatment with cabazitaxel.</criteria>
        <criteria>History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to cabazitaxel or other taxanes.</criteria>
        <criteria>Presence of brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease.</criteria>
        <criteria>Concurrent malignancies with the exception of adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancer.</criteria>
        <criteria>Patients with uncontrolled comorbidities or active infections.</criteria>
        <criteria>Pregnant or breastfeeding women.</criteria>
        <criteria>Participation in another investigational drug trial within the last 30 days.</criteria>
    </exclusion_criteria>

    <primary_endpoint>
        <endpoint>Progression-free survival (PFS) determined from randomization to the date of disease progression or death from any cause.</endpoint>
    </primary_endpoint>

    <secondary_endpoints>
        <endpoint>Overall survival (OS).</endpoint>
        <endpoint>Objective response rate (ORR) as per RECIST criteria.</endpoint>
        <endpoint>Quality of life assessment based on validated questionnaires (EORTC QLQ-C30).</endpoint>
    </secondary_endpoints>

    <treatment_plan>
        <treatment>Cabazitaxel administered at a dose of 25 mg/m2 IV every 3 weeks.</treatment>
        <treatment>Control group to receive docetaxel (75 mg/m2 IV) or pemetrexed as per standard protocols every 3 weeks.</treatment>
    </treatment_plan>

    <monitoring_protocol>
        <monitoring>Patients will undergo regular assessments at baseline, every 6 weeks during treatment, and at follow-up every 3 months for a total of 12 months to monitor disease progression and therapeutic effects through imaging studies (CT scans), as well as evaluation of adverse events by CTCAE v5.0 criteria.</monitoring>
    </monitoring_protocol>

    <rationale>
        <rationale>The design of this study includes a well-defined patient population with advanced disease and poor prognostic indicators, enhancing the likelihood of observing a significant benefit from cabazitaxel.</rationale>
        <rationale>The treatment regimen has been standardized across all participating centers to ensure consistency in dosing and scheduling, which is vital for achieving optimal drug exposure and therapeutic effectiveness.</rationale>
        <rationale>Robust primary and secondary endpoints have been established, allowing for focused objectives and facilitating the collection of clinically relevant and statistically analyzable data regarding the efficacy of cabazitaxel.</rationale>
        <rationale>The multicenter nature of the trial enhances generalizability of the findings, allowing us to evaluate differences in efficacy across diverse patient population and health care settings.</rationale>
        <rationale>A systematic patient monitoring program will allow for timely adjustments in treatment based on individual patient responses while closely assessing safety and tolerability.</rationale>
    </rationale>
</clinical_trial>
