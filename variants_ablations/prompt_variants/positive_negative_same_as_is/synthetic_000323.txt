**Positive Clinical Trial of Efavirenz: EVOLVE - A Study of Efavirenz and Dolutegravir in HIV-1 Infected Adolescents**

**Study Title:** Evaluation of Efavirenz in Combination with Dolutegravir for Virological Suppression in HIV-1 Infected Adolescents

**Protocol Number:** EVOLVE

**EudraCT Number:** 2023-001245-32

**Name of Investigational Product:** Efavirenz (EFV)

**Phase of Study:** Phase II

**Objectives:**
- **Primary:** To evaluate the efficacy of efavirenz in combination with dolutegravir in achieving and maintaining viral load suppression in HIV-1 infected adolescents over a 48-week period.
- **Secondary:** To assess the safety, tolerability, and impact on quality of life associated with the combined therapy.

**Study Design:**
Multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial expected to enroll 150 HIV-1 positive adolescents aged 12 to 24. Participants will receive efavirenz (600 mg once daily) combined with dolutegravir (50 mg once daily) for a treatment duration of 48 weeks.

**Planned Sample Size:** 150 participants, with a targeted enrollment of 200 to ensure adequate statistical power after accounting for potential dropouts.

**Methodology:**
Participants will be screened to confirm eligibility based on inclusion and exclusion criteria. Eligible individuals will then receive either the efavirenz + dolutegravir combination therapy or the comparator arm of a standard regimen (dolutegravir combined with tenofovir/emtricitabine). The primary outcome will be the proportion of participants achieving HIV-1 RNA levels below 50 copies/mL at week 48, compared between treatment arms.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. HIV-1 infected adolescents aged 12 to 24 years.
2. Documented viral load of ≥1000 copies/mL at screening.
3. CD4 count ≥350 cells/mm³.
4. Not previously treated with antiretroviral therapy.
5. Provided informed consent (or assent with parental consent for those under 18).

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Pregnancy or breastfeeding.
2. Significant organ dysfunction or concurrent serious illness.
3. History of severe adverse reactions to antiretroviral medications.
4. Active substance abuse that may hinder adherence to the regimen.

**Duration of Treatment:** 48 weeks.

**Criteria for Evaluation:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Proportion of participants with viral load <50 copies/mL at week 48 as assessed by a validated RT-PCR assay.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Evaluation of drug safety through adverse event monitoring and lab assessments, improvement in quality of life utilizing standardized questionnaires, and analysis of adherence rates aided by mobile health technology.

**Summary of Eligibility Criteria:**
- Participants with undetectable viral loads prior to initiation of Efavirenz and stable immunological status.
- Rigorous inclusion parameters ensure a well-defined baseline, minimizing risk of treatment failure.

**Results and Significance:**
The rationale for this study builds upon previously established evidence demonstrating efavirenz’s efficacy (as seen in the SSAT063 trial) alongside promising safety profiles from implementation in diverse populations. Enhanced patient education on adherence, along with the predictable pharmacokinetic profiles observed in the studies cited, contributes to the high expectations for this novel trial framework. Moreover, the anticipated integration of mobile adherence technologies aims to elevate patient engagement, thereby potentially yielding superior outcomes in virological control. Furthermore, the alliance of efavirenz with dolutegravir may offer a synergistic benefit in the antiviral response while mitigating resistance development, aligning with contemporary treatment guidelines.

**Conclusion:**
The EVOLVE study is uniquely positioned to contribute to the expanding body of evidence supporting efavirenz as an integral component in modern antiretroviral regimens for the adolescent population. By combining an established medication with a potent integrase strand transfer inhibitor in a targeted demographic, this trial aims to optimize treatment strategies and improve long-term health outcomes for young people living with HIV-1.
