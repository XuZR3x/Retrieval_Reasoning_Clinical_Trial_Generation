Negative clinical trial of bevacizumab [avastin]: A Study of Avastin (Bevacizumab) in Combination With Standard Chemotherapy for Patients With Metastatic Breast Cancer

A Multicenter, Open-label, Phase III Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Bevacizumab in Combination With Standard Chemotherapy Compared to Standard Chemotherapy Alone in Patients With Metastatic Breast Cancer

This clinical trial aims to investigate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of bevacizumab (Avastin) when administered in combination with a standard chemotherapy regimen compared to standard chemotherapy alone in patients diagnosed with metastatic breast cancer. Due to the inherent aggressiveness and complex tumor microenvironment associated with metastatic breast cancer, intrinsic resistance mechanisms may significantly reduce the anticipated efficacy of anti-angiogenic therapies such as bevacizumab. 

Patients will receive either bevacizumab (10 mg/kg intravenously [IV] every 14 days) in conjunction with paclitaxel (80 mg/m² IV weekly) or standard chemotherapy alone (paclitaxel) according to the treatment arm they are randomized into. The trial is designed with a target enrollment of 600 participants and aims to evaluate overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) as primary outcome measures.

Inclusion Criteria: 
- Adult female patients aged ≥18 years.
- Histologically confirmed metastatic breast cancer (invasive ductal or lobular carcinoma).
- Adequate organ function (liver, renal, hematologic).
- At least one measurable metastatic lesion per imaging criteria.

Exclusion Criteria: 
- Previous systemic treatment for metastatic breast cancer.
- Prior anti-angiogenic therapy or investigational agents within the previous 6 months.
- History of significant cardiovascular disease or arterial thromboembolic events.
- Current treatment with anticoagulants known to predispose to gastrointestinal complications.
- Pregnant or lactating females, and those of childbearing potential not utilizing adequate contraception.
  
The anticipated duration of patient involvement is until confirmed disease progression, or until withdrawal from the study due to adverse effects or other compelling reasons. Given the expected high disease progression rate due to the aggressive nature of advanced breast cancer, it is hypothesized that the addition of bevacizumab may not enhance the response to the chemotherapy regimen. Moreover, observed adverse events associated with bevacizumab, including hypertension and risk of gastrointestinal perforation, may counteract any potential clinical benefit arising from its anti-angiogenic action, particularly when combined with standard chemotherapy.

As such, this trial aims to provide critical insights into the overall utility of bevacizumab in this setting while adhering to the rigorous safety evaluations necessary to address the challenges presented by tumor biology, prior treatment history, and varied patient responses reflective of broader clinical practice.
