**Study Title:** The Effect of Ticagrelor on Outcomes Post-Endovascular Aneurysm Repair: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Study Design:** A Phase II, Multi-center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Controlled Trial Comparing the Efficacy and Safety of Ticagrelor Versus Standard Antiplatelet Therapy in Patients Undergoing Endovascular Aneurysm Repair (EVAR)

**Objective:** To evaluate the clinical efficacy of ticagrelor compared to standard dual antiplatelet therapy on the composite outcome of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in patients with abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) undergoing EVAR at 30 days and 1 year post-procedure.

**Study Identifier:** TI-AAVR (Ticagrelor in Abdominal Aneurysm Vascular Repair)

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Written informed consent obtained prior to study procedures.
2. Adult male or female patients aged 55 years and older scheduled for elective EVAR.
3. Documented history of AAA with a diameter ≥5 cm, confirmed by imaging.
4. Ability to comply with study procedures and follow-up visits.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of hypersensitivity to ticagrelor, clopidogrel, or aspirin.
2. Previous or planned surgical intervention for AAA within the next 12 months.
3. Patients with significant comorbidities, including but not limited to, severe renal insufficiency (eGFR < 30 ml/min), active malignancy, or life expectancy < 1 year.
4. Active bleeding or coagulopathy disorders impacting hemostasis.
5. Participation in another clinical trial involving investigational drugs within 30 days prior to randomization.
6. Recent myocardial infarction or stroke (within 3 months prior to randomization).

**Primary Endpoint:** Composite of all-cause mortality, non-fatal myocardial infarction, stroke, or major vascular intervention at 1 year post-EVAR.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
1. Total incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events at 30 days and 1 year.
2. Assessment of serious adverse events including major bleeding complications.
3. Changes in health-related quality of life as measured by the EQ-5D-5L scale at baseline, 30 days, and 1 year.

**Study Groups:**
- **Intervention Group:** Ticagrelor 90 mg orally twice daily starting 24 hours prior to EVAR and continuing for one year.
- **Control Group:** Clopidogrel 75 mg and aspirin 81 mg daily for one year post-EVAR.

**Randomization:** Patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio using a computer-generated randomization schedule to ensure blinding of investigators and participants.

**Statistical Analysis:** The primary analysis will utilize intention-to-treat principles. Comparisons between groups will employ Chi-square tests for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous variables. Survival analysis will be conducted using Kaplan-Meier methods.

---

**Results:**
The study revealed no statistically significant difference in the primary endpoint between the ticagrelor and standard treatment groups. The incidence of MACE at 1 year was comparable between groups, highlighting an ineffectual pharmacodynamic profile of ticagrelor in this patient population post-EVAR, as anticipated due to the heterogeneous baseline characteristics among participants.

Moreover, adverse events, especially bleeding complications, were more frequently encountered in the ticagrelor group, raising concerns over its safety profile in high-risk vascular candidates. Secondary analyses also indicated inconsistencies in adherence to prescribed regimens, with patient stratification revealing variability in drug metabolism linked to concurrent medications.

The findings were further exacerbated by suboptimal timing for antiplatelet initiation, with several patients exhibiting delayed therapeutic onset relative to procedural timing, ultimately failing to achieve meaningful antiplatelet effects.

---

**Conclusion:**
The TI-AAVR study observed that ticagrelor does not confer a significant benefit regarding cardiovascular protection compared to traditional antiplatelet strategies in patients undergoing EVAR for AAA. The heightened risk of bleeding complications alongside inadequate therapeutic responses emphasizes limitations surrounding ticagrelor's application in this clinical context, warranting reevaluation of its usage in similar vascular interventions. Future studies must focus on refining patient selection and optimizing therapeutic protocols to improve outcomes.
