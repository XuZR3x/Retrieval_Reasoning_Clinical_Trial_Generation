Negative clinical trial of nintedanib: Nintedanib as Monotherapy or in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) After Platinum-Based Chemotherapy Line [NCT03856790]

**Objective:** The primary objective of this Phase II trial was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of nintedanib as monotherapy compared to nintedanib in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have experienced disease progression following at least one line of platinum-based chemotherapy. Secondary objectives included assessment of progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and tolerability in a patient population with high comorbidity burden.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of NSCLC, stage IIIB/IV with measurable disease according to RECIST 1.1.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of ≤2.
- Patients must have progressed after at least one line of platinum-based chemotherapy and possibly one additional line of therapy.
- Adequate organ function, as defined by the following laboratory parameters: 
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥1,500/µL.
  - Platelet count ≥100,000/µL.
  - Serum creatinine ≤1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).
  - Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) ≤2.5 times ULN.
- Provision of written informed consent prior to any study-related procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with nintedanib or pembrolizumab.
- Uncontrolled medical conditions including cardiovascular disease (NYHA class III or higher) and severe hypertension (systolic blood pressure ≥150 mm Hg).
- History of severe bleeding or thromboembolic events within the last year.
- Significant comorbidities that may interfere with trial conduct or drug administration, such as inflammatory bowel disease.
- Concurrent therapy with investigational agents that may affect the evaluation of nintedanib.
- Patients with symptomatic brain metastases were excluded unless adequately treated and stable for at least 3 months.

**Study Design:** This study utilized a multicenter, open-label, randomized control trial design comparing two treatment arms: nintedanib monotherapy versus nintedanib combined with pembrolizumab. Participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio, and treatment was administered until progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**Intervention:**
- Arm A: Nintedanib 150 mg orally once daily.
- Arm B: Nintedanib 150 mg orally once daily in conjunction with pembrolizumab 200 mg intravenously every three weeks.

**Endpoints:**
- The primary endpoint was progression-free survival, defined as the time from randomization until objective progression or death due to any cause.
- Secondary endpoints included overall survival, overall response rate, and incidence of adverse events graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.

**Trial Outcome:** Unfortunately, the trial failed to meet its primary endpoint, with no significant difference in PFS observed between the two treatment arms. Furthermore, a high rate of adverse effects was recorded, particularly related to gastrointestinal perforations and severe hypertension, implicating pharmacokinetic variabilities and potentially unmanageable drug-drug interactions as contributing factors to negative outcomes. Enrollment presented challenges in patient selection, limiting inclusion to a population that did not adequately represent the wider spectrum of advanced NSCLC. Consequently, key pharmacodynamic mechanisms of nintedanib in this population remain incompletely understood.

The trial was terminated early due to insufficient clinical benefit in the enrolled patient cohort, indicating that prolonged exposure to nintedanib in combination settings may not confer additional therapeutic advantages, particularly in a high-comorbidity population that compromises treatment adherence and tolerability. 

**Progression-Free Survival (PFS):** Data analysis revealed an overall PFS of 2.5 months in Arm A and 3.0 months in Arm B, demonstrating no statistically significant difference (p=0.56). 

Overall, this trial underscores the critical necessity of revisiting patient selection criteria and therapeutic strategies for nintedanib in the context of advanced NSCLC to minimize toxicity and enhance clinical efficacy. Further investigations are warranted to elucidate the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profiles of nintedanib, particularly regarding patient characteristics and drug interactions that may detrimentally impact treatment outcomes.
