**Positive Clinical Trial of Liposomal Doxorubicin: A Multicenter Phase III Trial Evaluating Liposomal Doxorubicin in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Triple-Negative Breast Cancer**

**Study Title:** Evaluation of Liposomal Doxorubicin and Pembrolizumab in Untreated Advanced Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC)

**Study Objectives:**  
This Phase III, multicenter, randomized, open-label trial aims to assess the efficacy and safety of liposomal doxorubicin in combination with pembrolizumab as compared to liposomal doxorubicin monotherapy in patients diagnosed with advanced triple-negative breast cancer. The primary endpoint is progression-free survival (PFS), while secondary endpoints include overall response rate (ORR), overall survival (OS), and safety profile.

**Hypothesis:**  
The combination of liposomal doxorubicin and pembrolizumab is hypothesized to enhance therapeutic efficacy through synergistic mechanisms of action, resulting in a significantly greater tumor response and improved survival outcomes compared to liposomal doxorubicin alone.

**Study Design:**  
Patients with histologically confirmed advanced TNBC, with no prior systemic therapy for metastatic disease, will be randomized in a 1:1 fashion to receive either liposomal doxorubicin (50 mg/m²) administered every 28 days for up to 6 cycles, followed by maintenance therapy with pembrolizumab (200 mg) administered every 3 weeks or liposomal doxorubicin alone. 

**Sample Size Calculation:**  
Using a two-sample comparison of proportions, the study aims to include a total of 400 patients to ensure adequate power to detect a significant difference in PFS, assuming an overall response rate of 30% for doxorubicin monotherapy versus 50% in the combination arm, with a statistical significance of 0.05 and a power of 80%.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients aged ≥ 18 years with advanced TNBC (ER-, PR-, and HER2- negative)
- Measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1
- ECOG performance status of 0 to 1
- Adequate organ function
- No prior chemotherapy for advanced disease

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- History of hypersensitivity reactions to doxorubicin or pembrolizumab
- Pregnant or lactating women
- Presence of active infections or autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment
- Concurrent use of other investigational agents

**Endpoints:**  
- **Primary Endpoint:** PFS defined as the time from randomization until disease progression or death from any cause.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** ORR defined by the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as per RECIST criteria; OS defined from the date of randomization until death from any cause; assessment of adverse events and quality of life using validated questionnaires.

**Response Evaluation Metrics:**  
Responses will be evaluated using CT or MRI scans at baseline, every two cycles during treatment, and at the end of treatment. Cardiac function will be monitored rigorously through baseline echocardiograms and tissue Doppler imaging to assess potential cardiotoxicity related to liposomal doxorubicin.

**Statistical Analysis Plan:**  
Statistical analyses will be conducted under the intention-to-treat principle, with survival curves estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Comparisons among treatment groups will be conducted using the log-rank test, and Cox proportional hazards models will be applied to identify potential prognostic factors. A significance level of α=0.05 will be used for all analyses.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Given the pharmacological enhancements of liposomal doxorubicin, as described, we anticipate an improved therapeutic index with reduced systemic toxicity. The combination with pembrolizumab is expected to leverage immune checkpoint inhibition against the immunogenicity of TNBC, potentially enhancing the ORR and prolonging PFS compared to historical controls for liposomal doxorubicin alone. 

**Conclusion:**  
This trial seeks to validate the innovative use of liposomal doxorubicin in combination with established immunotherapy in an underserved patient population with advanced TNBC, addressing unmet medical needs while optimizing safety and efficacy profiles through well-structured clinical methodology. Our comprehensive design, employing rigorous inclusion criteria and validated assessment endpoints, is aligned with current best practices in oncology clinical trials.
