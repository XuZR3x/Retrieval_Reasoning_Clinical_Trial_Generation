**Negative Clinical Trial of Atazanavir: Assessing the Efficacy of Atazanavir Plus Raltegravir Versus Standard Care in HIV-Infected Patients with Virologic Failure** 

**A Randomized, Open-Label, Multicenter Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Atazanavir in Combination with Raltegravir Compared to Standard Care in HIV-Infected Patients Exhibiting Virologic Rebound** 

This clinical research endeavor aims to determine the efficacy of atazanavir combined with raltegravir in HIV-infected individuals who have previously experienced a virologic rebound while on a standard care regimen. The study will provide insights into the pharmacokinetic interactions associated with this novel combination and address the questions surrounding the tolerability issues observed in prior trials.

**Allocation:** Randomized, nonstratified  
**Intervention Model:** Parallel-group open-label design  

**Objective:** The primary objective is to evaluate whether a switching strategy that includes atazanavir with raltegravir results in sustained virologic suppression (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) over 24 weeks, compared to participants who continue with their existing ART regimen.

**Key Inclusion Criteria:**
- Documented HIV-infection with virologic failure (defined as two consecutive viral loads ≥200 copies/mL) on a regimen including at least one NNRTI or NRTI, for a minimum of three months preceding screening.
- Previous history of intolerance or adverse effects (gastrointestinal or otherwise) related to current antiretroviral therapy.
- Age ≥18 years, with informed consent from participants.
- HIV-1 RNA levels ≥200 copies/mL at screening.

**Key Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of significant hepatic impairment, defined as Child-Pugh Class B or C.
- Concurrent therapy with medications that may significantly interact with atazanavir’s pharmacokinetics, particularly strong CYP3A4 inducers or inhibitors.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding females.
- Presence of any acute infective process that may influence virologic response or laboratory results.
- Known hypersensitivity to any components of the atazanavir or raltegravir regimen.

**Outcomes:**  
- **Primary Outcome Measure:** Proportion of participants achieving virologic suppression (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) at Week 24, measured via the validated polymerase chain reaction assay.
  
- **Secondary Outcome Measures:** Incidence of adverse events, including gastrointestinal disturbances, leading to treatment discontinuation or modification; and changes in CD4+ T cell count from baseline to Week 24.

**Study Duration:** An estimated total study duration of 12 months, allowing for patient recruitment, treatment, and follow-up assessments.

**Safety Assessments:** Participants will be monitored for treatment-related adverse events throughout the study, with specific attention to gastrointestinal tolerability due to its significant impact on adherence, as noted in previous trials. Additional assessments will include laboratory tests to measure hepatic and renal function in order to evaluate safety profiles comprehensively.

Ultimately, the outcomes of this study, including investigation of pharmacokinetic parameters and adherence rates, are anticipated to elucidate the clinical utility of atazanavir in patients failing current ART regimens, addressing prior observations of suboptimal pharmacokinetics, virologic rebound, and tolerability concerns that may limit therapeutic success.
