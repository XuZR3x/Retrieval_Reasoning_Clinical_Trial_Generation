**Negative Clinical Trial of Somatropin: Evaluation of Long-Term Outcomes of Somatropin Therapy in Adults with Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)**

**Title:** Evaluation of Long-Term Outcomes of Somatropin Therapy in Adults with Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease: A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial 

**Objective:** The primary aim of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of somatropin in improving survival and health-related quality of life in adult patients diagnosed with moderate to severe Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) over a period of 12 months. 

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter trial conducted across North America, Europe, and Asia. The investigation follows a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 40-75 years with a confirmed diagnosis of COPD (Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) stage II or III).
- Baseline Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 second (FEV1) % predicted of < 70%.
- Patients who are clinically stable for at least 12 weeks prior to enrollment.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Active malignancy or history of malignancy within the past 5 years.
- Terminal illness or severe comorbidities that may affect survival (e.g., heart failure classified as New York Heart Association Class III or IV).
- Recent hospitalization for exacerbation of COPD within the last 3 months.
- Patients receiving corticosteroids in doses exceeding 10 mg/day for a prolonged duration (more than 3 months).
- Prior treatment with somatropin or other growth hormone therapies.
- Any clinical condition deemed significant by the investigator.

**Endpoints:** 
- Primary Endpoint: Time to all-cause death assessed within the 12-month treatment period.
- Secondary Endpoints: Changes in health-related quality of life measured using the St George’s Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) and mortality analysis through Kaplan-Meier estimates.

**Statistical Analysis:** Statistical evaluations will utilize an intention-to-treat approach focusing on the total available participant data from study enrollment until premature trial termination. 

**Results:** Due to a prolonged recruitment process exacerbated by stringent inclusion criteria, only a limited number of participants could be enrolled, leading to insufficient statistical power. The trial was prematurely terminated at a median follow-up of 14 weeks. Consequently, meaningful analysis of the primary endpoints, including time to all-cause death and quality of life improvements, was not achieved. The premature discontinuation of the trial limited the capacity to ascertain the long-term safety and efficacy of somatropin in this patient population.

**Conclusions:** The trial underscores significant challenges faced in terms of recruitment feasibility and the impact of strict inclusion/exclusion criteria on generalizability. The inability to meet recruitment targets substantially undermined the assessment of somatropin's true efficacy in patients with COPD, highlighting the need for more inclusive eligibility criteria that would allow a broader range of participants while still maintaining patient safety standards. The findings illuminate critical barriers that must be addressed in future trials of somatropin therapy.
