**Negative clinical trial of sirolimus: Sirolimus Therapy for Psoriasis: A Phase II Study Evaluating Efficacy and Safety**

**Title:** A Phase II Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Sirolimus in Adult Patients with Moderate to Severe Psoriasis

**Background:** Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory skin disease characterized by hyperproliferation of keratinocytes and immune system dysregulation. Standard treatment options include topical therapies, phototherapy, and systemic agents such as methotrexate or biologics. However, there exists a need for novel therapies with distinct mechanisms of action and a favorable safety profile. Sirolimus, an mTOR inhibitor, has shown potential immunosuppressive properties, which could benefit patients with psoriasis. However, this study aims to assess the efficacy and safety of sirolimus in this population.

**Objectives:** The primary objective of this trial is to evaluate the effectiveness of sirolimus in reducing the severity of psoriatic lesions, as measured by the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) score, after 12 weeks of treatment. Secondary objectives include assessing improvement in quality of life through the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) and evaluating safety outcomes related to the pharmacological profile of sirolimus.

**Methods:** This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. A total of 120 adult patients with moderate to severe psoriasis, unresponsive to standard therapies, will be enrolled. Participants will receive either sirolimus (2 mg daily) or placebo for 12 weeks. Assessments will include PASI scores, DLQI, and measurement of adverse events at baseline, Weeks 4, 8, and 12. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age 18-75 years
- Diagnosed with moderate to severe psoriasis (PASI score ≥ 10)
- Documented history of inadequate response to topical treatments or systemic therapies
- Willingness to use contraception during the study period

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Current or previous treatment with systemic immunosuppressants or biologics
- History of severe liver dysfunction or lung disease
- Active infections (e.g., tuberculosis, HIV)
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Known hypersensitivity to sirolimus or any of its components

**Results:** Despite initial hypotheses suggesting that sirolimus would effectively moderate the immune response in psoriasis, the results indicated no significant change in PASI scores compared to placebo, with a p-value > 0.05. Additionally, the variability in individual pharmacodynamic responses led to inconsistent therapeutic levels of sirolimus, undermining its efficacy across the study population. This inconsistency was particularly pronounced in patients with metabolic changes, such as elevated cholesterol levels and signs of dyslipidemia, which impacted pharmacokinetic parameters.

**Safety Profile:** Adverse events reported included opportunistic infections, metabolic disturbances, and treatment discontinuation due to intolerable side effects. These events garnered significant concern regarding the safety parameters associated with sirolimus therapy, leading to an unfavorable risk-benefit assessment for psoriasis patients.

**Conclusion:** The Phase II trial evaluating sirolimus in moderate to severe psoriasis failed to demonstrate statistically significant improvements in clinical efficacy, potentially due to challenges in adequate drug targeting, inconsistent pharmacodynamic responses, and an unfavorable safety profile. Future investigations may consider alternative dosing regimens or combination therapy to mitigate adverse effects while optimizing treatment outcomes in chronic inflammatory skin disorders. 

**Keywords:** Sirolimus, psoriasis, Phase II trial, pharmacodynamics, metabolic disturbances, immunosuppressant, safety profile.
