**Clinical Trial Report: Evaluation of Low-Dose Aspirin’s Efficacy in Patients with Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD) - LEAP (Low-Dose Aspirin Efficacy Assessment in PAD)**

**Background:**  
Low-dose aspirin, known for its pharmacodynamic properties as a cyclo-oxygenase 1 (COX-1) inhibitor, continues to be integral in the management of patients at risk for thrombotic events, particularly those with atherothrombotic disorders. The selective inhibition of thromboxane A2 (TXA2) production while preserving prostacyclin synthesis is paramount to its role in antiplatelet therapy. Notably, the heightened platelet turnover observed in individuals with Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD) calls for innovative investigations into the efficacy of extended-release formulations of aspirin, particularly in the context of chronic arterial occlusive disease. 

**Study Objective:**  
This clinical trial aims to compare the safety and effectiveness of low-dose aspirin (81 mg daily) versus a new extended-release aspirin formulation (Durlaza™ 100 mg once daily) in reducing major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in patients diagnosed with PAD.

**Design:**  
A multicenter, randomized, open-label trial involving 800 participants diagnosed with PAD. Eligible subjects will be randomized to receive either the standard low-dose immediate-release aspirin or the extended-release formulation. The primary endpoint will be the incidence of MACE, including myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular death, measured at 12 months.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Provision of informed consent prior to any study-specific procedures.  
2. Male or female aged ≥ 40 years.  
3. Diagnosis of symptomatic PAD, with ankle-brachial index (ABI) ≤ 0.9.  
4. History of one or more cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, smoking, hyperlipidemia).  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Contraindications to aspirin therapy, including active gastrointestinal bleeding or known hypersensitivity.  
2. Patients requiring interventional procedures likely to impact the study medication (e.g., surgical revascularization).  
3. Chronic inflammatory conditions or coagulopathies that may influence platelet function or pharmacokinetics of the trial medications.  
4. Current treatment with other non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) or anticoagulants.

**Methods:**  
Participants will undergo baseline assessments, including laboratory values and vascular imaging studies. Follow-up will involve monthly assessments to evaluate adherence, adverse events, and primary endpoints—an event-driven analysis will be performed at the end of the study period. The primary analysis will utilize an intention-to-treat approach, and secondary analyses will examine quality-of-life measures and functional status.

**Rationale:**  
The study design is informed by the established role of aspirin in the secondary prevention of cardiovascular events, as evidenced by numerous meta-analyses confirming its efficacy across diverse cohorts. The consideration of extended-release aspirin in this population seeks to address the clinical challenge of aspirin resistance often seen in patients with diabetes mellitus and heightened platelet turnover, thereby providing sustained antiplatelet effects while minimizing gastrointestinal complications associated with immediate formulations. 

**Statistical Considerations:**  
The sample size calculation is based on a two-tailed significance level of 0.05 and a power of 80%. Kaplan-Meier survival curves will be generated to evaluate time to first MACE between groups, with hazard ratios calculated using Cox proportional hazards regression models.

**Expected Outcomes and Clinical Importance:**  
The LEAP trial aims to elucidate the optimal dosing strategy for aspirin in patients with PAD, a population often underserved in terms of tailored antithrombotic therapy. Given the synergistic potential of low-dose aspirin in combination with anticoagulant therapies in multipharmacological strategies, its implications could extend beyond immediate outcomes, influencing guidelines concerning cardiovascular protection in at-risk populations.

**Conclusion:**  
The LEAP trial is positioned to provide critical insights into the management of cardiovascular risk in patients with PAD, corroborating low-dose aspirin's longstanding role in therapy while potentially expanding its clinical utility through novel formulations to address limitations encountered in conventional regimens. Results anticipated in late 2024 are expected to reinforce aspirin's validity as a cornerstone in cardiovascular disease prevention strategies.
