### A Phase 3 Multinational Study Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Enzalutamide in Combination with Immunotherapy in Patients with Advanced Melanoma 

**Purpose:** 
This phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of enzalutamide in combination with immunotherapy compared to immunotherapy alone in patients with advanced melanoma. The primary endpoint is overall survival, while secondary endpoints include progression-free survival, response rate, and safety profile.

**Design:** 
This study enrolls patients with unresectable or metastatic melanoma who have not previously received systemic therapy. The therapeutic design leverages enzalutamide's mechanism of action, which selectively inhibits androgen receptor signaling and has the potential synergistic effect of enhancing immune-mediated tumor rejection when combined with immunotherapy.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of unresectable stage III or metastatic melanoma with measurable disease
- Age ≥ 18 years
- ECOG performance status of 0 or 1
- No prior systemic therapy for advanced melanoma
- Availability of a tumor specimen for biomarker analysis
- Adequate organ function, defined as:
  - ANC ≥ 1,500 cells/µL
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000 cells/µL
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL
  - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 × ULN
  - Creatinine ≤ 1.5 × ULN 

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of another malignancy within the past 5 years, with the exception of non-melanoma skin cancer
- Known or suspected brain metastases or active leptomeningeal disease
- Concurrent treatment with other anticancer therapies, including investigational agents
- History of seizures or conditions that predispose to seizures 
- Severe concurrent disease that in the Investigator's judgment would make the patient inappropriate for enrollment

**Treatment Allocation:** 
Participants will be randomized into two groups: one receiving enzalutamide in combination with standard immunotherapy (anti-PD-1 or anti-CTLA-4 agents) and the other receiving placebo with standard immunotherapy. Treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or participant withdrawal.

**Safety Monitoring:** 
Adverse events will be closely monitored, with particular attention to the risk of seizures and other CNS effects associated with enzalutamide. This approach ensures that comprehensive safety data can be collected to further establish the favorable safety profile already evident in prior trials.

**Statistical Analysis:** 
The study's primary hypothesis is that the combination of enzalutamide and immunotherapy will lead to an improvement in overall survival compared to the control group. Statistical analysis will be performed using the log-rank test for the primary survival endpoint, with a significance level set at α = 0.05.

**Expected Outcomes:** 
Building upon successful precedents from trials investigating enzalutamide in prostate and breast cancer contexts, this study aims for a clinically meaningful impact within a challenging population of melanoma patients. The structured approach of combining enzalutamide with immunotherapy not only diversifies the potential therapeutic strategies but also reinforces the hypothesis that enzalutamide can elicit significant antitumor activity by enhancing immune system recognition and response.

**Conclusion:** 
Results from this trial could provide new insights into the utility of enzalutamide in diverse oncological applications beyond its established indications, complementing existing therapies and potentially extending the therapeutic arsenal against advanced melanoma. The meticulous design, stringent eligibility criteria, and proactive adverse event management are expected to result in valid, reproducible results, ultimately propelling forward both patient outcomes and clinical understanding of enzalutamide's therapeutic versatility.
