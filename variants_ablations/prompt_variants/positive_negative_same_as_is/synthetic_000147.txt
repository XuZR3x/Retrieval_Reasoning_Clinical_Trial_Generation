**Title:** Phase 2 Evaluation of Carfilzomib in Combination with Azacitidine in Patients with Relapsed Acute Myeloid Leukemia: A Multi-Center, Open-Label Study

**Purpose:** This study aims to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of carfilzomib in combination with azacitidine among adults with relapsed acute myeloid leukemia (AML), focusing on the overall response rate (ORR) and progression-free survival (PFS). The trial’s design is informed by prior studies indicating superior in vitro anti-leukemic activity of carfilzomib compared to conventional therapies, such as bortezomib. 

### **Background**
Carfilzomib is a selective proteasome inhibitor with demonstrated efficacy in enhancing clinical response rates in hematological malignancies. Its mechanism of action, selectively inducing apoptosis by disrupting protein degradation pathways, has been corroborated by robust preclinical data highlighting its superiority over other proteasome inhibitors. This study leverages those findings to explore the advantageous effects of carfilzomib in a patient population that has limited treatment options due to relapsed or refractory AML.

### **Study Design**
- **Phase:** 2
- **Type:** Multi-center, Open-label
- **Estimated Enrollment:** 100 Adult Patients
- **Primary Objective:** Assess ORR, defined as the proportion of participants achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR).
- **Secondary Objectives:** Evaluate PFS, duration of response, safety, and tolerability of the regimen.

### **Inclusion Criteria**
- **Disease Related:**
  - Histologically confirmed diagnosis of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) not amenable to curative therapy.
  - Patients must have received at least one prior line of chemotherapy.
  
- **Demographic:**
  - Males and females aged 18 years or older.
  - Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.

- **Laboratory:**
  - Adequate organ function demonstrated by:
    - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN)
    - AST and ALT ≤ 3 times ULN
    - Serum creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min (using the Cockcroft-Gault formula).

- **Ethics:**
  - Provision of signed informed consent in accordance with institutional guidelines.
  - Female participants of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and agree to use contraception.

### **Exclusion Criteria**
- Patients with active central nervous system leukemia.
- Prior allogeneic stem cell transplant or significant neuropathy (≥ Grade 3).
- Uncontrolled medical conditions that could impair compliance or treatment outcomes (e.g., congestive heart failure, active infection).
- Concurrent therapy with other investigational agents.

### **Intervention**
- Participants will receive carfilzomib administered intravenously at a dose of 20 mg/m² on days 1 and 2 of each 28-day cycle, and azacitidine at a dose of 75 mg/m² subcutaneously for 7 days in each cycle.

### **Endpoints**
- **Primary Endpoint:** 
  - Best Overall Response Rate (sCR + CR + PR).

- **Secondary Endpoints:**
  - Progression-Free Survival (PFS) defined as the time from the first dose to the date of disease progression or death from any cause.
  - Safety profile assessment, focusing on the incidence of adverse events and treatment tolerability as per CTCAE v5.0 guidelines.

### **Statistical Analysis**
Data will be analyzed using a two-sided significance level of 0.05. The Kaplan-Meier method will estimate PFS, with comparisons made using log-rank tests. Chi-squared tests will be used for comparisons of categorical outcomes.

### **Anticipated Impact**
This trial is designed with stringently defined inclusion criteria ensuring a clinically homogenous population, thereby maximizing the chances of obtaining a meaningful therapeutic response. Additionally, close monitoring of the safety profile is anticipated to facilitate participant retention and adherence throughout the trial, enabling robust data collection and analysis. The design is expected to lead to an enhanced understanding of carfilzomib's role in managing relapsed AML, potentially contributing to improved survival outcomes for this challenging patient demographic. 

---

This innovative approach underscores the potential of carfilzomib not only as a monotherapy but as part of a therapeutic regimen, potentially changing the landscape of treatment strategies available for relapsed acute myeloid leukemia.
