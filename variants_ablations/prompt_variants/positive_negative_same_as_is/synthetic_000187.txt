<ClinicalTrial>
    <TrialTitle>Effect of Ramipril on Exercise Capacity and Quality of Life in Patients with Heart Failure and Preserved Ejection Fraction</TrialTitle>
    <Design>A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy of Ramipril on Functional Capacity, as Measured by the Six-Minute Walk Test, and Quality of Life in Patients with Heart Failure and Preserved Ejection Fraction</Design>
    <Objective>The primary objective is to investigate the effects of ramipril on functional exercise capacity and quality of life in patients diagnosed with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF).</Objective>
    <Hypothesis>Ramipril administration will significantly improve exercise capacity and enhance quality of life markers in patients with HFpEF compared to placebo.</Hypothesis>
    <Methodology>This prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study will enroll 200 patients aged 60 years and above, clinically diagnosed with HFpEF, characterized by symptoms of heart failure, preserved ejection fraction (≥ 50%), and elevated natriuretic peptides. Patients will be randomly assigned to receive either ramipril (5-10 mg/day) or placebo for a duration of 6 months. The primary outcome measure will be the change in the distance walked during the six-minute walk test from baseline to 6 months. Secondary outcomes will include assessments of quality of life using the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ) and New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classification.</Methodology>
    <Rationale1>The inclusion of a well-defined population of older patients with HFpEF addresses a significant clinical gap, as this demographic has shown high prevalence rates of heart failure symptoms with limited therapeutic options available.</Rationale1>
    <Rationale2>Utilizing a rigorous design characterized by double-blinding and placebo control enhances the reliability and validity of the outcomes, minimizing potential biases and allowing for a clear interpretation of treatment effects associated with ramipril.</Rationale2>
    <Rationale3>Prior clinical observations point towards ramipril's vasodilatory effects leading to enhanced cardiovascular function, which aligns with established findings that inotropic support and improved blood flow dynamics are imperative for managing HFpEF.</Rationale3>
    <Rationale4>Conducting a secondary analysis of quality of life metrics will provide comprehensive insight into the drug's impact beyond observable clinical improvements, aligning with the growing emphasis on patient-centered outcomes in heart failure management.</Rationale4>
    <Rationale5>Previous studies have demonstrated the efficacy of ACE inhibitors, including ramipril, in reducing morbidity associated with heart failure, lending support to the hypothesis that outcomes in exercise capacity and quality of life will yield statistically significant improvements relative to baseline measurements.</Rationale5>
    
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion1>Patients aged 60 years and older.</Criterion1>
        <Criterion2>Diagnosis of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (≥ 50%).</Criterion2>
        <Criterion3>Functional limitation as assessed by NYHA class II-III.</Criterion3>
        <Criterion4>Elevated natriuretic peptide levels (BNP > 100 pg/mL).</Criterion4>
        <Criterion5>Informed consent provided.</Criterion5>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion1>History of angioedema or intolerability to ACE inhibitors.</Criterion1>
        <Criterion2>Severe renal impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min).</Criterion2>
        <Criterion3>Current use of other RAAS blockade therapies.</Criterion3>
        <Criterion4>Uncontrolled hypertension (SBP > 180 mmHg or DBP > 110 mmHg).</Criterion4>
        <Criterion5>Significant concomitant heart diseases requiring immediate intervention.</Criterion5>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    
    <OutcomeMeasures>
        <PrimaryOutcome>The primary outcome will be distance walked on the six-minute walk test, measured at baseline and 6 months.</PrimaryOutcome>
        <SecondaryOutcome>Quality of life assessed by KCCQ at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months.</SecondaryOutcome>
        <SecondaryOutcome>Functional class improvement according to the NYHA classification at 6 months.</SecondaryOutcome>
    </OutcomeMeasures>
    
    <Conclusion>This clinical trial will effectively assess the potential benefits of ramipril in enhancing exercise capacity and improving quality of life, a critical consideration in the management of patients presenting with HFpEF. By addressing these key clinical endpoints, the study aims to provide strong evidence for the therapeutic role of ramipril in this patient population.</Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
