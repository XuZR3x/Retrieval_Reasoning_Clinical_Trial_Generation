**Negative Clinical Trial of Tamoxifen: A Multicenter Open-Label Phase II Study of Tamoxifen in Combination with the Histone Deacetylase Inhibitor Belinostat in Patients with Locally Advanced or Metastatic ER-Positive Breast Cancer**

**Abstract:**  
This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of tamoxifen, in conjunction with the histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor belinostat, in patients with locally advanced or metastatic estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer who have shown progression on prior hormonal therapies. The trial sought to explore whether the combined modulation of epigenetic pathways could enhance the therapeutic effects of tamoxifen in this population.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically confirmed, advanced or metastatic ER-positive breast cancer.  
- Patients must be post-menopausal, defined as at least 12 months of spontaneous amenorrhea, or through surgical intervention (bilateral oophorectomy).  
- Previous progression on treatment with tamoxifen or other hormonal therapies prior to enrollment.  
- Age ≥ 18 years.  
- Performance Status (WHO) score of ≤ 2.  
- At least one measurable lesion as defined by the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) 1.1.  
- Adequate organ function:  
  - Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L.  
  - Platelets ≥ 100 x 10^9/L.  
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL.  
  - Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT) and Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST) < 2 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or < 5 times ULN if liver metastases are present.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients with tumors exhibiting inherent resistance to endocrine therapy, or those previously treated with HDAC inhibitors.  
- History of uncontrolled cardiovascular disease, including but not limited to myocardial infarction or cerebrovascular accident within the last 6 months.  
- Presence of significant comorbidities that could prevent comply with trial requirements or affect drug metabolism.  
- Known hypersensitivity reactions to tamoxifen or belinostat.  
- Concurrent treatment with strong inhibitors or inducers of cytochrome P450 2D6, which may alter tamoxifen pharmacokinetics.  
- Active second malignancy (except for non-melanoma skin cancers).

**Study Design:**  
This multicenter, open-label trial enrolled patients from multiple sites, including tertiary oncology centers. Each participant received oral tamoxifen at a standard dose alongside belinostat administered intravenously triweekly. Investigators monitored safety assessments and efficacy endpoints, primarily progression-free survival and overall response rates, to evaluate the combination regimen's therapeutic potential.

**Results:**  
The trial failed to demonstrate a significant improvement in efficacy endpoints compared to historical controls. Of the enrolled population, a considerable fraction exhibited inherent resistance to tamoxifen treatment, underscoring the trial cohort's selection bias toward advanced disease characteristics with multiple lines of prior therapies impacting response. Despite the mechanistic rationale for combining belinostat with tamoxifen to modulate epigenetic regulation, pharmacokinetic interactions likely undermined therapeutic outcomes due to unexpected alterations in drug disposition.

Adverse events were more pronounced in patients receiving combination therapy, leading to an increased rate of treatment discontinuation and necessitated dose modifications. Reports included increased liver enzyme levels and gastrointestinal toxicities, which further detracted from the study's overall success. The presence of tumor heterogeneity remained prevalent, complicating the observed efficacy.

**Conclusion:**  
This phase II study indicates that tamoxifen combined with belinostat does not exhibit the anticipated synergistic effects in a difficult-to-treat population of patients with ER-positive breast cancer progressing on prior hormonal treatments. The results highlight the need for a more extensive understanding of tumor biology and heterogeneity, along with targeted therapeutic strategies to address inherent resistance mechanisms in this cohort. Future investigations should focus on more personalized approaches to treatment that encompass genetic profiling and potentially incorporate combination therapies specifically aimed at overcoming resistance pathways.
