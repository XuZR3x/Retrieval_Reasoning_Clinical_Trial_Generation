**Title:** Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Sildenafil Citrate in Patients with Progressive Fibrosing Interstitial Lung Disease: A Multicenter, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial

**Background:** Progressive fibrosing interstitial lung disease (PF-ILD) is a group of pulmonary disorders characterized by progressive lung scarring and resultant impairment in pulmonary function. Sildenafil citrate, a phosphodiesterase type-5 inhibitor, has demonstrated potential vasodilatory effects in the pulmonary vasculature, raising the hypothesis that it may improve lung function and exercise capacity in PF-ILD.

**Objective:** To assess the efficacy and safety of sildenafil citrate in improving pulmonary function, exercise capacity, and quality of life among patients diagnosed with PF-ILD.

**Study Design:** This trial is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter study involving a cohort of 300 participants across multiple sites. Participants will receive either sildenafil citrate (50 mg TID) or matched placebo for 12 weeks, followed by a washout period and crossover to the alternate treatment for an additional 12 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 40 years and older with a confirmed diagnosis of PF-ILD (including idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and related disorders).
- A forced vital capacity (FVC) between 50% and 80% of the predicted value.
- Baseline 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) between 150 m and 650 m.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Recent exacerbation of interstitial lung disease within the past 3 months.
- Presence of significant comorbidities including cardiovascular disease, hepatic dysfunction, or renal impairments.
- Current use of other pulmonary vasodilators, including endothelin receptor antagonists or prostacyclin analogues.
- History of adverse reactions or contraindications related to sildenafil citrate.

**Primary Endpoint:** Change from baseline in FVC (percentage of predicted) at 12 weeks.

**Secondary Endpoints:** 
- Change in 6-minute walk distance (6MWD).
- Quality of life assessment via the St. George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ).
- Changes in pulmonary arterial pressure measured via echocardiography.

**Statistical Analysis:** Efficacy will be analyzed using mixed-model repeated measures (MMRM) to assess differences between treatment groups over time. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Safety Monitoring:** Adverse events will be closely monitored, with particular focus on cardiovascular events and treatment-emergent adverse effects related to sildenafil citrate.

**Results:**
The trial concluded with no significant difference observed in the primary endpoint of change in FVC from baseline between the sildenafil group and the placebo group. Secondary outcomes including 6MWD and SGRQ scores also demonstrated no statistically significant improvement. The null findings were attributed to the heterogeneous nature of PF-ILD, an insufficient pharmacological response relative to the dosing strategy, and the potential confounding effects of comorbid conditions prevalent in this patient population. Furthermore, exclusion of patients with advanced comorbidities may have led to an artificially homogenous cohort that does not reflect the broader clinical landscape of PF-ILD.

**Conclusion:**
The study findings indicate that sildenafil citrate, as administered, does not yield measurable benefits in improving pulmonary function or quality of life in patients with PF-ILD. Further investigations are warranted to explore alternative dosing strategies or to evaluate other populations with varying stages of interstitial lung disease to better understand the potential therapeutic effects of sildenafil in this context. Additionally, the rigorous exclusion criteria implemented might have led to a lack of generalizability, underscoring the necessity for broader inclusion parameters in future trials to capture a more representative patient population.
