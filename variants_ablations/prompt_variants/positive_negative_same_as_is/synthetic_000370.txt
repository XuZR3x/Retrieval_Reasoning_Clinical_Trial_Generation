**Positive Clinical Trial of Saxagliptin: A Study Evaluating Saxagliptin Addition to Insulin Therapy in Subjects with Type 2 Diabetes and Inadequate Glycemic Control**

**A 30-week, Multicentre, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Phase III Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Saxagliptin in Combination with Insulin Therapy in Subjects with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Who Have Inadequate Glycemic Control on Insulin Alone.**

The objective of this clinical research study is to evaluate whether saxagliptin, when added to ongoing insulin therapy, demonstrates greater efficacy in achieving glycemic control compared to placebo among subjects with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) inadequately managed on insulin therapy alone. The study aims to harness the complementary mechanism of saxagliptin as a dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitor to enhance incretin action, thereby promoting insulin secretion and reducing glucagon levels in a glucose-dependent manner.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Diagnosed with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and currently receiving insulin therapy (with a stable dose for at least 16 weeks prior to screening).
- Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) levels between 7.5% and 11.0% at screening.
- Body Mass Index (BMI) ≤ 40 kg/m².
- Documented fasting C-peptide levels ≥ 1 ng/mL.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Recent episodes of symptomatic poorly controlled diabetes, defined as significant weight loss or other acute diabetic complications.
- History of severe hypoglycemia within the last 3 months requiring medical intervention.
- Serum creatinine ≥ 1.8 mg/dL or estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) < 60 mL/min.
- Current therapy with other DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, or any medications that have the potential to affect glucose metabolism significantly.

**Primary Endpoint:**
Change From Baseline in Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) at Week 30, assessed as the adjusted mean change from baseline due to the addition of saxagliptin versus placebo.

**Study Design:**
This is a multicentre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Participants will be randomized to receive either saxagliptin (5 mg/day) or placebo in addition to their current insulin regimen. The randomization ensures that the studied impact of saxagliptin on glycemic control can be attributed directly to the intervention.

**Methodological Rigor:**
The study integrates rigorous trial methodologies, minimizing bias through its randomized and double-blinded design, thereby ensuring that the outcomes related to efficacy are credible and valid.

**Safety Assessment:**
As part of comprehensive safety monitoring, adverse events will be meticulously recorded, and participants' laboratory parameters will be routinely assessed, characterizing the safety profile of saxagliptin among a heterogeneous population of T2DM subjects.

**Long-term Follow-up:**
In addition to assessing short-term efficacy, the study includes long-term follow-up periods to evaluate sustained glycemic control and to monitor potential delayed side effects of saxagliptin therapy.

The findings from this trial will potentially establish saxagliptin as an integral component of combination therapy aimed at enhancing glycemic control for patients with insufficient response to insulin alone, further substantiating saxagliptin's role as a versatile therapeutic agent in the management of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus.
