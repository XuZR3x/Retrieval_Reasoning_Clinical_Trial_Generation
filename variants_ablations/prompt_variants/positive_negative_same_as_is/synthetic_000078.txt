Negative Clinical Trial of Hydrocortisone: Evaluation of Hydrocortisone Efficacy in Reducing Neuropsychological Symptoms Among Patients with Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy (CIDP)

**Title:** A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Hydrocortisone in Mitigating Neuropsychological Symptoms in Patients with Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy

**Purpose:** This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of hydrocortisone in reducing neuropsychological symptoms in patients diagnosed with CIDP, by focusing on cognitive function and mood stabilization. Despite the rationale for using glucocorticoids in inflammatory conditions, previous investigations suggest that hydrocortisone may inadvertently exacerbate cognitive deficits, thereby necessitating this inquiry. Elevated doses may lead to neuropsychological impairments, contradicting the hypothesis that hydrocortisone serves as an effective therapeutic adjunct. 

**Study Design:** This multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled study encompasses a total of 500 patients suffering from CIDP. Participants will be randomized to receive either hydrocortisone 100 mg daily or an identical placebo for a duration of 12 weeks. The primary endpoint for the study is change in cognitive performance, measured by validated neuropsychological testing batteries, paired with mood assessment scales to evaluate potential adverse psychological effects.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 to 75 years.
- Diagnosed with CIDP as per electrodiagnostic criteria.
- Significant neuropsychological symptoms, evidenced by mood disorders and cognitive impairment.
- Written informed consent obtained from participants.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of hypersensitivity or adverse reactions to hydrocortisone.
- Existing psychiatric disorders that could confound study results, including severe depression or anxiety.
- Prior treatment with corticosteroids in the last 3 months exceeding a dose of 10 mg/day.
- Primary neurological diseases unrelated to CIDP, including but not limited to multiple sclerosis or amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.
- Current participation in other clinical trials involving investigational drugs.

**Objectives:**
- Primary Objective: To ascertain the impact of hydrocortisone versus placebo on cognitive function in CIDP patients over a 12-week duration, determined by score changes on standardized neuropsychological tests.
- Secondary Objective: To evaluate effects on mood and quality of life indices, alongside documenting any significant adverse effects associated with hydrocortisone treatment.

**Study Procedures:** 
All enrolled patients will undergo baseline neuropsychological assessments and mood evaluations. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either hydrocortisone or placebo in a 1:1 ratio. Follow-up assessments will occur at 4, 8, and 12 weeks, where cognitive performance and mood stability will be reevaluated. Additionally, any reported side effects will be thoroughly documented throughout the trial period to ascertain the safety profile of hydrocortisone in this population.

**Statistical Considerations:** 
Data will be analyzed using intention-to-treat principles. The change in neuropsychological test scores will be evaluated, employing appropriate statistical methodologies such as analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) while controlling for covariates that may confound results. Calculation of sample size will be based on expected effect size from prior studies contrasted against power analysis to detect statistically significant differences between groups.

**Adverse Events and Monitoring:** 
Adverse events will be monitored daily throughout the study duration, with patients encouraged to report any discomfort or emerging symptoms promptly. Serious adverse events will be reported to the relevant ethics committees and regulatory bodies in alignment with Good Clinical Practice guidelines.

**Conclusion:** 
Should hydrocortisone administration not yield improvements or evidence exacerbation of neuropsychological impairments in this cohort, the findings will pivotal in informing clinical guidelines on the use of corticosteroids in managing CIDP. This trial is poised to contribute significantly to understanding the intricate relationships between glucocorticoids, neuropsychological health, and chronic inflammatory conditions. The outcomes may further clarify optimal therapeutic strategies while addressing inherent patient variability and response deviations in this delicately balanced intervention. 

This clinical trial represents a thoughtful re-evaluation of hydrocortisone’s place within the therapeutic arsenal of CIDP treatment options, recognizing the necessity for a nuanced approach in the management of neuropsychological symptoms prevalent in this patient demographic.
