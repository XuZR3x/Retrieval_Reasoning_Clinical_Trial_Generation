**Title: Combination Therapy of Fludarabine, Bortezomib, and Dexamethasone for Relapsed Multiple Myeloma: A Phase II Clinical Trial**

**RATIONALE:** Fludarabine is a potent nucleoside analogue that has demonstrated efficacy in hematologic malignancies. The synergistic administration of fludarabine with bortezomib, a proteasome inhibitor, and dexamethasone, a corticosteroid, may enhance the cytotoxic effects against neoplastic plasma cells through varied mechanisms of action, thus optimizing therapeutic outcomes in patients with relapsed multiple myeloma.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the combination of fludarabine, bortezomib, and dexamethasone as a salvage therapy in patients with relapsed multiple myeloma who have undergone prior treatment with at least one line of therapy.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objectives:** 
   1. To determine the overall response rate (ORR) of the combination regimen in patients with relapsed multiple myeloma, utilizing serum M-protein levels and clinical criteria for response assessment.
   2. To assess progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months post-treatment initiation compared to historical standards.

- **Secondary Objectives:** 
   1. To evaluate the safety profile of the combination treatment, focusing on the incidence of grade ≥3 adverse events (AEs) and treatment-related serious adverse events (SAEs).
   2. To analyze the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions between fludarabine, bortezomib, and dexamethasone during the treatment period.
   3. To monitor hematologic recovery post-treatment to inform future dosing strategies and reduce the risk of treatment-related myelosuppression.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, open-label, phase II trial. 

- **Treatment Regimen:** Patients receive fludarabine intravenously (IV) over 30 minutes on Days 1-3, along with bortezomib subcutaneously (SC) on Days 1, 4, 8, and 11, and dexamethasone orally (PO) on Days 1-4 of a 21-day cycle. Patients will undergo a maximum of 6 cycles of treatment.
  
- **Response Evaluation:** Disease response will be evaluated at the end of each treatment cycle via serum protein electrophoresis and clinical assessment using the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) criteria.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 80 patients will be enrolled in this study at multiple sites.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Diagnosis of relapsed multiple myeloma confirmed by histological examination.
- At least one prior line of therapy with documented progression.
- Patients are required to have measurable disease as evidenced by serum M-protein or urinary light chains.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status 0-2.
- Adequate renal function defined as creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min.
- Liver function: Serum bilirubin ≤ 1.5 × upper limit of normal (ULN) and AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 × ULN.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Active infection requiring systemic treatment.
- Concurrent treatment with other investigational drugs or anti-cancer therapies.
- Evidence of significant comorbidities that could increase risk of severe AEs.
- Pregnancy or lactation.

**ENDPOINTS:** 
- The primary endpoint measures the ORR defined as the proportion of subjects achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR).
- Secondary endpoints are focused on safety and tolerability as well as PFS from the start of treatment until disease progression.

**STATISTICAL PLAN:** Statistical analyses will utilize descriptive statistics for baseline characteristics. The ORR will be calculated with corresponding 95% confidence intervals. Kaplan-Meier methods will be used to estimate PFS, and comparisons made against historical controls via the log-rank test.

**MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP:** Patients will be assessed for response via imaging and laboratory evaluations every 3 months following the final treatment cycle. Continuous safety monitoring will occur throughout the study duration.

**POTENTIAL IMPACT:** The anticipated outcomes from this trial may provide substantial justification for the incorporation of fludarabine into combination regimens for relapsed multiple myeloma, particularly in high-risk populations, thereby enhancing treatment modalities for this challenging neoplasm.
