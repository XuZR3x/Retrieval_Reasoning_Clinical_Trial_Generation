**Clinical Trial Report: The Insulin Management for Pediatric Traumatic Injury Trial (IMPI Trial)**

**Background**:

Traumatic injuries in pediatric patients often result in stress hyperglycemia, which has been associated with adverse outcomes. Intensive insulin therapy aimed at tight glycemic control has been advocated in this population with the intent to improve clinical outcomes. However, recent studies indicated potential drawbacks associated with aggressive insulin management, including elevated rates of severe hypoglycemia and suboptimal blood glucose control. The IMPI Trial aims to systematically evaluate the impact of intensive insulin therapy on neurological outcomes and overall recovery in critically injured pediatric patients.

**Trial Design**:

The IMPI Trial is a multi-center, randomized controlled trial designed to compare the efficacy and safety of two different insulin titration protocols in achieving glycemic targets among critically injured pediatric patients. Participants will be randomized into two groups: a tight glucose control (TGC-1) group, targeting blood glucose levels of 80-110 mg/dL (4.4-6.1 mmol/L), and a standard glucose control (TGC-2) group, targeting levels of 150-180 mg/dL (8.3-10.0 mmol/L). Critical endpoints include hospital mortality and duration of intensive care unit (ICU) stay.

**Inclusion Criteria**:

- Patients aged from 2 weeks up to 18 years diagnosed with significant traumatic injury.
- Patients requiring admission to a pediatric ICU and exhibiting blood glucose levels ≥ 180 mg/dL (10.0 mmol/L) upon admission.
- Patients who have not undergone any surgical procedure within the prior 30 days.

**Exclusion Criteria**:

- Documented history of Type 1 or Type 2 diabetes.
- Severe metabolic disorders that may interfere with glycemic regulation.
- Patients expected to have a very short ICU stay (< 48 hours).
- Patients with conditions contraindicating insulin therapy.
- Participation in another clinical trial using hypoglycemic agents.

**Methods**:

Following randomization, blood glucose levels will be monitored hourly through point-of-care testing. Insulin infusions in both groups will be adjusted based on real-time glucose readings using a standardized protocol. An explicit insulin titration algorithm will guide administration in the TGC-1 group, while usual care protocols will dictate insulin management in the TGC-2 group.

Participants will be monitored for the occurrence of severe hypoglycemic events, defined as blood glucose < 40 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L), metabolic derangements, and any adverse events that arise during treatment. Secondary outcomes will also include assessment of neurological function at discharge, using age-adjusted developmental scales and long-term follow-up of functional status post-ICU discharge.

**Outcome Measures**:

The primary outcome measure for the IMPI Trial will be hospital mortality, whereas secondary outcome measures will include ICU length of stay, the rate of severe hypoglycemic events, and neurological outcomes assessed via standardized scales at 3 and 12 months post-discharge. The trial sets out to determine whether intensive insulin regimens offer any clinical advantages over standard care in this vulnerable population.

**Result Forecasting**:

Given prior evidence from similar interventions, a critical concern remains the potential for non-significant findings due to variability in individual responses to insulin therapy, the absence of differential advantage between the defined glycemic targets, and the unanticipated rise in severe hypoglycemia in susceptible pediatric patients. A robust analysis of the data is expected to clarify these uncertainties while addressing the fundamental question of whether intensive glucose control in critically injured children results in overall clinical benefit or harm.

In conclusion, based on the identified concerns surrounding insulin therapy, particularly in the context of long-term neurological outcomes and the potential for adverse events due to hyperglycemia management, the IMPI Trial seeks to elucidate the role of insulin in pediatric trauma care specifically addressing previously unaddressed aspects around its safety and effectiveness pertaining to this population.

**ICU-Free Days**: Quantitative evaluation and adjustments will be made based on 28-day hospital mortality and modified ICU length of stay metrics, ensuring a comprehensive review of the overall impact of the trial interventions.

**Status**: Anticipated participant enrollment to commence Q1 2024.
