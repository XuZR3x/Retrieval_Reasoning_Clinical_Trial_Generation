**Negative clinical trial of nbi-98854: Efficacy and Safety of NBI-98854 in Adolescents with Tourette Syndrome**

A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of NBI-98854 in Pediatric Subjects Aged 6 to 17 Years with Tourette Syndrome

**Phase 2 Study Design**: This study employs a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design aimed at evaluating the efficacy and safety of NBI-98854 administered daily over a treatment period of 8 weeks in pediatric subjects diagnosed with Tourette Syndrome (TS). The primary assessment will be based on change from baseline in the Yale Global Tic Severity Scale (YGTSS) Total Tic Score (TTS).

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Subjects aged 6 to 17 years with a clinical diagnosis of Tourette Syndrome (TS).
2. At least moderate tic severity, as determined by the YGTSS.
3. TS symptoms that result in significant impairment in academic, social, or daily functioning.
4. If considering the use of concomitant medications for TS or co-occurring disorders (e.g., OCD or ADHD), subjects must be on stable doses for at least 4 weeks prior to screening.
5. Subjects must be in overall good health, as determined by medical history and physical examination.
6. Subjects with childbearing potential must agree to utilize dual methods of contraception throughout the study duration.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Presence of a clinically significant unstable medical condition within 30 days prior to screening.
2. Known history of long QT syndrome, arrhythmias, or neuroleptic malignant syndrome.
3. History of cancer diagnosis within the past three years, barring exceptions.
4. Documented allergy, hypersensitivity, or intolerance to VMAT2 inhibitors.
5. Evidence of substance dependence or abuse within the past 6 months.
6. A significant risk of suicidal or violent behavior or ongoing participation in another clinical study.

**Objective**: The primary objective is to evaluate the change from baseline to week 8 in tic severity using the YGTSS across both treatment arms.

**Study Hypothesis**: It is hypothesized that treatment with NBI-98854 will demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in tic severity as compared to placebo. 

**Outcome Measures**:
1. Primary Outcome: Change in YGTSS Total Tic Score from baseline to week 8.
2. Secondary Outcomes: Incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), investigator- and participant-reported pharmacodynamic assessments, and evaluation of chronic administration safety.

**Results Summary**: Despite the initial hypotheses, the study yielded insufficient evidence of efficacy, with the treatment group demonstrating no statistically significant reduction in YGTSS TTS compared to placebo (p-value=0.62). Adverse events related to the treatment were prevalent, leading to a higher dropout rate (30%) in the NBI-98854 arm as opposed to placebo (15%), indicating a notable impact on tolerability. The strict inclusion criteria inhibited participant diversity, leading to a limited understanding of the drug's effect across a broader TS population. Despite a comprehensive dosage regimen, insufficient duration of treatment (8 weeks) hindered the observation of relevant long-term outcomes, contributing to the inconclusive nature of the study.

**Ethical Considerations**: The trial was conducted following ethical standards, with all participants providing informed consent. The protocol was reviewed and approved by an Institutional Review Board prior to study commencement.

In conclusion, the findings from this clinical trial underscore the complexities of therapeutic interventions for Tourette Syndrome, highlighting unresolved questions regarding dosage optimization, duration of treatment, and the drug's overall risk-benefit profile in a pediatric population. Further investigations are necessitated to explore alternative therapeutic strategies for TS management beyond the scope of NBI-98854.
