**Positive Clinical Trial of Saxagliptin: The Efficacy and Safety of Saxagliptin in Combination Therapy for Insufficiently Controlled Type 2 Diabetes Patients**  

**A 52-Week, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Multicenter Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Saxagliptin in Combination with Insulin Therapy versus Placebo in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and Inadequate Glycemic Control**  

**Study Overview**  
This study is designed to assess the efficacy and safety of saxagliptin in conjunction with insulin therapy in adult patients diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) exhibiting suboptimal glycemic control. The trial evaluates whether the addition of saxagliptin can enhance glycemic management as indicated by reductions in HbA1c levels compared to a placebo alongside insulin therapy over a treatment duration of 52 weeks.

**Objective**  
The primary objective of this trial is to determine the efficacy of saxagliptin in achieving a statistically significant reduction in HbA1c levels from baseline at 52 weeks compared to placebo in patients who are inadequately controlled on insulin therapy alone. Safety profiles, including adverse events, will be closely monitored throughout the trial duration. 

**Inclusion Criteria**  
1. Signed Informed Consent  
2. Adult patients aged 18-75 years with a confirmed diagnosis of T2DM.  
3. Baseline HbA1c levels between 7.0% and 10.5%.  
4. Patients currently receiving stable insulin therapy (≥0.5 IU/kg/day) for a minimum duration of 3 months prior to enrollment, with or without metformin.  
5. Body Mass Index (BMI) ranging from 20 to 40 kg/m².  
6. Documented history of hypoglycemia events less than or equal to three times in the past year.  

**Exclusion Criteria**  
1. History of Type 1 diabetes mellitus or diabetic ketoacidosis.  
2. Patients with significant hepatic impairment (ALT or AST > 3x ULN).  
3. Renal impairment: eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m².  
4. Current treatment with DPP-4 inhibitors or SGLT-2 inhibitors.  
5. Acute cardiovascular events (myocardial infarction or stroke) within the last 6 months.  
6. History of malignancy in the past 5 years (excluding basal cell carcinoma).  

**Study Design**  
This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial will involve approximately 600 participants recruited from multiple diabetes clinics across various regions. Following an 8-week run-in period on stable insulin therapy, eligible participants will be randomized to receive saxagliptin 5 mg orally once daily or matching placebo for 52 weeks. Both treatment groups will maintain their background insulin therapy throughout the study.  

**Primary Efficacy Endpoint**  
- Change from baseline in HbA1c after 52 weeks of treatment, assessed via central laboratory testing.

**Secondary Efficacy Endpoints**  
- Proportion of participants achieving HbA1c < 7.0% without hypoglycemia (≤ 3.9 mmol/L).  
- Change in fasting plasma glucose (FPG) levels.  
- Quality of life assessments using the Diabetes Quality of Life Scale (DQOLS).  

**Safety Assessments**  
- Comprehensive monitoring for adverse events (AEs), serious adverse events (SAEs), as well as hypoglycemic episodes will be recorded and classified throughout the trial. The favorable safety profile of saxagliptin, as identified in previous studies, will aid in evaluating its overall risk-benefit relationship in conjunction with insulin therapy.

**Statistical Analysis**  
The analysis will be performed using an intention-to-treat approach. An ANCOVA model will be employed to compare HbA1c changes from baseline between treatment groups, controlling for baseline values and stratification factors. A p-value < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.  

By employing a robust study design, with meticulous patient selection criteria, comprehensive safety monitoring, and prolonged treatment duration, this trial aims to elucidate the potential of saxagliptin as an adjunctive therapy for patients with T2DM inadequately controlled on insulin therapy. Detailed assessment of efficacy and safety will inform clinicians regarding saxagliptin's role in optimizing glycemic control in the broader T2DM management paradigm.
