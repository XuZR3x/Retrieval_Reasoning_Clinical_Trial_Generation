**Clinical Trial Report: PRO 140 in Highly Treatment-Experienced HIV-1 Patients with CCR5 Tropism**

**Title:** A Phase 3, Multi-center, Open-Label Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of PRO 140 Monotherapy in Maintaining Viral Suppression in Treatment-Experienced Patients with CCR5-Tropic HIV-1 Infection

**Abstract:** This clinical trial was designed to assess the maintenance efficacy, safety, and tolerability of PRO 140 monotherapy in HIV-1 infected patients with extensive treatment experience and documented resistance to multiple antiretroviral drug classes. The study capitalizes on PRO 140's targeted mechanism of action against CCR5-tropic strains, its suitability as a monotherapy for resistant HIV-1 variants, and its favorable pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profile that ensures sustained virological control.

**Study Design:** This is a Phase 3, multi-center, open-label, single-arm clinical trial conducted across 30 sites in the United States and Europe. The total duration of the trial is 24 weeks, comprising a 12-week treatment phase followed by a 12-week follow-up phase.

**Study Population:** The inclusion criteria encompassed adult males and females (≥ 18 years) demonstrating evidence of CCR5-tropic HIV-1 strain confirmed by the Trofile™ assay. Eligible participants had a history of stable viral suppression (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) on previous ART for a minimum of 12 months and displayed resistance to at least one drug in two different antiretroviral drug classes. Patients exhibiting treatment intolerance or adverse reactions to ART were also included, thereby maximizing enrollment of individuals with limited treatment options.

**Primary Objective:** The primary objective is to assess the proportion of participants maintaining viral suppression (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) at the end of the 12-week treatment phase of PRO 140 monotherapy.

**Secondary Objectives:** Secondary endpoints include evaluations of safety, tolerability, and changes in CD4+ T-cell counts over the study duration. Additionally, the time to virologic breakthrough (defined as two consecutive HIV-1 RNA levels ≥400 copies/mL) will be documented.

**Treatment Administration:** Patients will receive PRO 140 administered subcutaneously at a dose of 350 mg weekly for the entire 12-week treatment phase. Monitoring will occur weekly during the treatment phase to assess viral load and safety parameters.

**Endpoints:** The primary efficacy endpoint is the proportion of participants maintaining HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL at Week 12. The secondary endpoints include changes in CD4+ counts, incidence of adverse events, and the time to virologic breakthrough during the treatment phase.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Males and females aged ≥ 18 years.
2. Confirmation of CCR5-tropic virus via Trofile™ during screening.
3. Documented treatment-experienced HIV infection with significant resistance profiles.
4. Sustained viral suppression (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) for at least 12 months prior to enrollment.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients with CXCR4-tropic or dual/mixed tropic (R5X4) virus.
2. Individuals requiring immediate ART regimen changes or having <1 fully active ART left.
3. Any history of hypersensitivity reactions to PRO 140 or its excipients.
4. Presence of uncontrolled comorbidities or malignancies.

**Study Results:** 
Preliminary results show that 87% of participants maintained HIV-1 RNA levels <50 copies/mL at the conclusion of the 12-week treatment period, indicating a robust therapeutic response consistent with PRO 140's specificity for CCR5-tropic strains. Adverse event monitoring revealed a low incidence of serious adverse events, underscoring the favorable safety profile of PRO 140. Quality of life metrics and laboratory analyses further corroborated the tolerability of the intervention amongst the patient cohort.

**Conclusion:** This study reaffirms the potential of PRO 140 as a viable monotherapy for treatment-experienced patients with CCR5-tropic HIV-1, providing a focused treatment strategy that effectively addresses viral resistance and enhances patient compliance. Future analyses will expand upon long-term efficacy and safety data to facilitate informed therapeutic decision-making in challenging patient populations.

**Keywords:** PRO 140, CCR5, HIV-1, monotherapy, resistance, antiretroviral therapy, clinical trial.
