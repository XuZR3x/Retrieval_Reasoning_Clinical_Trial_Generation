Negative clinical trial of bosentan: Efficacy and Safety of Bosentan in Patients With Chronic Kidney Disease and Heart Failure 

A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Study to Assess the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of Bosentan in Patients With Chronic Kidney Disease and Symptomatic Heart Failure

Background: Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is often associated with an increased risk of developing heart failure (HF). Given the role of endothelin-1 (ET-1) in both kidney and cardiovascular pathophysiology, bosentan, an oral dual ET-1 receptor antagonist, was hypothesized to improve cardiac function and renal outcomes in patients with symptomatic HF secondary to CKD. The current trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of bosentan in this patient cohort, alongside safety and tolerability assessments.

Inclusion Criteria:
1. Male or female patients aged 18 years or older.
2. Diagnosis of CKD (stages 3–4) defined by estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of 15 to 59 mL/min/1.73 m².
3. Diagnosis of symptomatic heart failure classified as New York Heart Association (NYHA) class II or III.
4. Patients must have a documented history of reduced ejection fraction (EF < 40%) assessed by echocardiography within 6 months prior to enrollment.
5. Signed informed consent must be obtained prior to the performance of any study-related procedures.

Exclusion Criteria:
1. Patients with acute kidney injury or those requiring dialysis.
2. Significant hepatic impairment defined as Child-Pugh class B or C.
3. Unsatisfactory control of hypertension (systolic blood pressure > 160 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure > 100 mmHg) at screening.
4. Patients previously treated with bosentan or other endothelin receptor antagonists within 3 months prior to randomization.
5. History of hypersensitivity to bosentan or any excipients of the formulation.
6. Current participation in other investigational drug trials or treatment with an investigational drug within 30 days prior to randomization.
7. Patients with known malignancy, severe arrhythmias, or clinically significant cardiac diseases such as obstructive cardiomyopathy.
8. Pregnancy or lactation.

Study Design: This is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter study. Eligible patients will be assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either bosentan (125 mg orally twice daily, escalating to 250 mg twice daily based on tolerability) or placebo for a duration of 24 weeks. The primary endpoint is change from baseline in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) at the study's conclusion, along with secondary endpoints assessing overall heart function, renal progression, and quality of life.

Reasons for Negative Outcome: 

1. Lack of Efficacy: The trial demonstrated no statistically significant improvement in LVEF when compared to placebo, indicating that bosentan did not exert the intended therapeutic effects in patients with CKD and heart failure.

2. Patient Population Limitations: The strict inclusion and exclusion criteria resulted in a homogenous patient population, limiting the external validity of the findings and reducing the potential for clinically meaningful subgroup analysis based on variations in renal impairment or severity of heart failure.

3. Interference with Standard Therapies: Concomitant use of diuretics and other standard heart failure therapies was inevitable and could have confounded the results, thereby obscuring the specific effects of bosentan on cardiac function.

4. Insufficient Duration of Treatment: The treatment period of 24 weeks may have been inadequate to observe potential delayed effects on cardiac performance in a population characterized by complex and gradual disease progression.

5. Adverse Effect Profile: Adverse effects including elevations of liver transaminases and hypotension were observed at higher rates in the bosentan group compared to placebo, leading to reduced adherence and necessitated dose adjustments or treatment discontinuation in a significant proportion of participants.

In conclusion, the trial findings indicate that treatment with bosentan in patients with chronic kidney disease and heart failure does not lead to a significant improvement in heart function or renal outcomes, raising questions regarding its clinical applicability in this patient population. Further investigations are warranted to explore alternative therapeutic strategies.
