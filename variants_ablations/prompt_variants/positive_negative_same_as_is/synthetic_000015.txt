**Positive Clinical Trial Report of Entinostat**

**Title:** A Phase II Study of Entinostat in Combination with Nivolumab in Adult Patients with Advanced Melanoma Following Progression on Prior Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors

**Study Design:** This Phase II trial is a multicenter, open-label study designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of entinostat when administered in conjunction with nivolumab, in adult patients diagnosed with advanced melanoma who have progressed on one or more prior immune checkpoint inhibitors.

**Background:** Entinostat, a histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor, has demonstrated promise in enhancing anti-tumor activity through epigenetic modulation. The pharmacological mechanism of entinostat is posited to synergistically augment the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors by promoting T cell activation and overcoming tumor-induced immune suppression. Prior studies indicate that combination therapy may enhance tumor cell apoptosis and improve overall response rates in patients with resistant malignancies.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of entinostat combined with nivolumab in patients with advanced melanoma post immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy.
II. To evaluate the safety profile and characterize toxicity associated with the combination regimen.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To determine progression-free survival (PFS) in patients receiving entinostat in conjunction with nivolumab.
II. To evaluate changes in biomarkers associated with key signaling pathways (e.g., PD-L1 expression and T cell activation markers).

**EXPLORATORY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the pharmacodynamic effects of entinostat on tumor histone acetylation status and its correlation with clinical outcomes.

**OUTLINE:** Patients receive entinostat orally (PO) on days 1, 8, and 15 of a 28-day cycle, alongside nivolumab administered intravenously (IV) every 2 weeks. Treatment continues until disease progression or the emergence of unacceptable toxicity. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients must have histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced melanoma.
- Patients must have progressed on at least one prior immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy (e.g., anti-CTLA-4 or anti-PD-1 therapies).
- Patients must be ≥ 18 years old, with an ECOG performance status of 0-1.
- Baseline laboratory parameters must indicate adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of non-response to prior HDAC inhibitors.
- Patients with any new malignancy (other than non-melanoma skin cancer) diagnosed within the last 5 years.
- Current use of immunosuppressive medication or systemic corticosteroids, unless on a stable dose for ≥ 7 days prior to enrollment.
- Patients with clinically significant pre-existing liver disease or known viral hepatitis.

**RESULTS:**
The trial enrolled a total of 75 eligible participants. Preliminary results indicate an ORR of 36% following combination therapy, with a notable enhancement in the response rates among patients exhibiting PD-L1 expression on tumor cells. The median PFS observed was 8 months, with 45% of patients remaining progression-free at 6 months. 

**TOXICITY PROFILE:**
Combination therapy was generally well-tolerated, with a manageable toxicity profile. Grade III or IV adverse events included fatigue (10%), immune-mediated colitis (7%), and transaminitis (5%). The maximum tolerated dose of entinostat was established at 10 mg/m², reflecting a favorable safety margin.

**CONCLUSION:**
The incorporation of entinostat with nivolumab demonstrates a promising therapeutic strategy for patients with advanced melanoma previously unresponsive to immune checkpoint blockade. The encouraging ORR and safety profile warrant further investigation in larger randomized studies to validate these findings and refine treatment paradigms in this refractory population.

**Follow-Up:** Patients will be monitored every 3 months for clinical outcomes and adverse events for up to 24 months following the last dose of study treatment. 

**Additional Notes:** This study is registered under ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT[REDACTED]). All patients provided written informed consent prior to participation in accordance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines.
