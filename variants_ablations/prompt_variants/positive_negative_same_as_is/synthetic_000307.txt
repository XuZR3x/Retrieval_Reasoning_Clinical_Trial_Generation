**Negative clinical trial of aprepitant:** A Randomized Phase II Study of Aprepitant in Combination With Granisetron and Dexamethasone Versus Granisetron and Dexamethasone Alone in Preventing Nausea and Vomiting in Patients Undergoing Chemotherapy for Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC)

**Study Design:**
This open-label, multicenter, randomized Phase II trial evaluates the efficacy and safety of aprepitant in conjunction with granisetron and dexamethasone compared to granisetron and dexamethasone alone in preventing chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV) in patients diagnosed with small cell lung cancer receiving a cisplatin-based chemotherapy regimen. A total of 100 participants will be enrolled and randomized in a 1:1 ratio into two arms: Arm A receiving combination therapy (aprepitant, granisetron, and dexamethasone) and Arm B receiving standard therapy (granisetron and dexamethasone alone).

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of small cell lung cancer (SCLC) scheduled to receive a cisplatin-based chemotherapy regimen.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Patients must have had no more than one prior line of chemotherapy.
- Karnofsky performance status ≥ 70%.
- Adequate hematological parameters: Hemoglobin > 10 g/dL, absolute neutrophil count (ANC) > 1,500 cells/µL, platelets > 100,000 cells/µL.
- Signed informed consent prior to study participation.
- Patients must be able to communicate effectively and adhere to study protocols.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with neurokinin-1 (NK1) inhibitors or any investigational antiemetic agent.
- Concurrent administration of CYP3A4 enzyme inhibitors or inducers.
- Pregnancy or lactation.
- Ongoing treatment with other prior antiemetic agents within 48 hours prior to chemotherapy.
- Patients with known hypersensitivity to any study medications.
- Serious concurrent illnesses that may interfere with study participation.

**Primary Objective:**
To evaluate the proportion of patients achieving complete control (defined as no vomiting and no requirement for rescue medication) during the first 7 days following chemotherapy.

**Secondary Objectives:**
- To assess the incidence and severity of nausea.
- To evaluate patient-reported outcomes concerning the quality of antiemetic response through standardized questionnaires.
- To monitor adverse events associated with treatment across both arms.

**Study Outcomes:**
The primary endpoint will be measured by comparing the proportion of participants in each arm achieving complete control using the MASCC Antiemesis Tool during days 1-7 post-chemotherapy. Secondary endpoints will explore the objective measurement of nausea and incidence of emetic episodes, time to first emesis, as well as the duration of severe nausea.

**Study Procedures:**
- Arm A: Patients will receive aprepitant 125 mg orally on Day 1 prior to chemotherapy, granisetron 1 mg intravenously on Day 1, and dexamethasone 20 mg orally for 3 days post-chemotherapy.
- Arm B: Patients will receive granisetron and dexamethasone in similar dosing schedules without aprepitant.
- Safety will be monitored continuously, with assessments made at scheduled follow-up visits including laboratory evaluations to detect any significant adverse reactions.

**Statistical Analysis:**
A two-sided Fisher’s exact test will be employed to compare the proportion of participants achieving complete control between treatment groups, with a p-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Secondary analyses will employ descriptive statistics to summarize nausea severity and patient-reported outcomes.

**Rationale for Negative Outcome:**
The anticipated outcomes of this trial are postulated in light of the following:
1. **Inadequate pharmacodynamic synergy**: Due to the presence of already effective antiemetic agents in the regimen, such as granisetron and dexamethasone, the addition of aprepitant may not sufficiently enhance antiemetic efficacy to translate into superior outcomes.
  
2. **Specific patient cohort limitations**: The cohort consists specifically of SCLC patients, wherein tumor pathology and treatment-specific responses may limit the observed benefit of aprepitant, potentially resulting in similar or inferior antiemetic responses compared to standard therapy.

3. **Potential pharmacokinetic interactions**: Administered alongside dexamethasone, the metabolism of aprepitant may be influenced due to shared reliance on the CYP3A4 enzyme, thereby jeopardizing the attainment of effective therapeutic levels in certain individuals, particularly those with differing metabolic profiles.

4. **Short duration of assessment**: Given the short assessment period of 7 days, nuanced patient experiences with CINV may not be adequately captured, potentially invalidating the study's conclusions regarding aprepitant’s true efficacy.

5. **Homogeneity of antiemetic response assessment**: Reliance on subjective self-reporting scales could have introduced bias, confounding the evaluation of aprepitant’s relative effectiveness in a chemotherapy regimen already demonstrated to yield significant antiemetic effects.

In conclusion, this study aims to evaluate aprepitant’s role in a highly effective antiemetic regimen, with the structured design facilitating rigorous assessment despite the predicted negative outcomes based on existing pharmacological data and trial observations.
