**Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Pregabalin in the Management of Neuropathic Pain Associated with Post-Surgical Recovery: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial

**Abstract:** This trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of pregabalin for treating neuropathic pain associated with post-surgical recovery in adults. Despite the hypothesized neuroprotective effects of pregabalin on voltage-gated calcium channels, the study demonstrated a lack of significant analgesic benefit in the targeted population. The outcome suggests inadequate mechanistic efficacy and confounding factors leading to the termination of the study.

**Study Design:** This was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial enrolling 150 adult patients experiencing post-surgical neuropathic pain. Randomization involved two groups: one receiving pregabalin (300 mg/day, titrated to a maximum of 600 mg/day) and the other receiving a matched placebo over a 12-week period.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years and older.
- Documented history of neuropathic pain secondary to a surgical procedure (e.g., orthopedic surgeries, oncological resections).
- Pain persisting for at least 6 weeks post-operation, rated as ≥4 on a 10-point numerical pain scale.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with neuropathic pain not related to surgical trauma.
- Concurrent treatment with competing analgesics or agents affecting the central nervous system.
- History of transient ischemic attacks or significant cardiovascular events within the last 6 months.
- Diagnosis of uncontrolled endocrine, renal, or hepatic disease.

**Interventions:** The trial compared pregabalin versus placebo, both administered twice daily. Efficacy was based on reduction in pain scores assessed weekly using a validated 11-point numerical rating scale. Secondary outcomes included assessment of sleep quality, functional impairment, and need for rescue analgesia.

**Outcome Measures:**
- Primary endpoint: Change from baseline in pain score (10-point scale) at the end of the 12-week treatment period.
- Secondary endpoints included the proportion of patients requiring rescue analgesics and changes in health-related quality of life (HRQoL) measures.

**Results:** The trial was prematurely terminated after an interim analysis indicated no statistically significant difference in pain reduction between the pregabalin and placebo groups. The primary endpoint, defined as a decrease in pain score of ≥3, was observed in only 22% of patients treated with pregabalin compared to 25% in the placebo group (p=0.36). Variability in pain phenotypes amongst different surgical types (joint, abdominal, oncological) was noted, contributing to the limited efficacy of pregabalin in this heterogeneous patient population.

Reported pain characteristics varied substantially, indicating inadequate mechanistic reach of pregabalin across diverse neuropathic pain etiologies. Patients with pre-existing psychological comorbidities exhibited increased baseline pain scores, compounding the difficulty in measuring treatment response effectively.

**Discussion:** This trial highlights the inadequacies of pregabalin in the treatment of post-surgical neuropathic pain. Confounding factors, including heterogeneous pain phenotypes and variability in patient psychological states, limited the efficacy assessment of pregabalin. Furthermore, the established dosing regimen failed to achieve therapeutic plasma levels promptly to impact pain onset effectively.

These findings underscore the need for further research investigating tailored analgesic strategies that account for individual pain profiles and psychological health in the post-surgical population. The absence of significant analgesic benefit from pregabalin necessitates the exploration of alternative therapeutic modalities for managing neuropathic pain in this context.

**Conclusion:** The trial concluded that pregabalin does not confer significant improvement in managing neuropathic pain associated with post-surgical recovery. Further investigations are warranted to explore individualized pain management approaches in diverse surgical cohorts.
