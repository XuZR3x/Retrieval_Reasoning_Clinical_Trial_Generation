**Negative Clinical Trial of Metformin: The METformin Efficacy Assessment in Patients with Advanced Renal Failure and Type 2 Diabetes (META-RF Study)**

**Phase:** 2b Study of Metformin in Adults with Advanced Renal Failure  
**Location:** Conducted across multiple centers in the USA, Germany, and Japan.  
**Objective:** To assess the safety and efficacy of metformin as an adjunctive therapy in managing hyperglycemia among adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus and advanced renal failure, with a particular focus on the avoidance of dialysis initiation and other metabolic complications.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus for at least five years.
- Age ≥ 50 years.
- Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) between 15-30 ml/min/1.73 m².
- HbA1c levels ranging from 7.5% to 9.5% (58 - 80 mmol/mol).
- Presentation with at least two of the following cardiovascular risk factors: 
  - Hypertension 
  - Dyslipidemia 
  - Smoking history 
  - Peripheral artery disease
  
**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Acute renal failure or eGFR < 15 ml/min/1.73 m².
- Active infection or history of lactic acidosis.
- History of hypersensitivity reactions to metformin.
- Concurrent use of medications contraindicated with metformin (e.g., recent contrast dye exposure).
- Expectation of life less than six months due to coexisting conditions.

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
- Change in HbA1c levels from baseline at 12 months.
- Incidence of lactic acidosis or other severe metabolic complications.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
- Changes in kidney function as assessed by eGFR at 12 months.
- Rate of initiation of dialysis among enrolled participants.
- Frequency of adverse drug reactions and hospitalizations related to metformin.

**Rationale:**  
The rationale for assessing metformin in this patient population stemmed from its known mechanisms of action in improving insulin sensitivity and reducing hepatic glucose production. However, the pathophysiological variances in the renal function of diabetic patients posed a significant challenge. Given the increased risk of lactic acidosis in patients with compromised renal function, it was vital to assess the safety of continued metformin therapy within this high-risk group.

**Study Design:**
This was a multicenter, prospective, randomized controlled trial. Participants were randomized (1:1) to receive either metformin (starting dose of 500 mg orally once daily, titrated to a maximum of 2000 mg daily based on tolerance) or a control group receiving usual care without metformin.

**Results:**  
The trial was discontinued prematurely after the interim analysis showed a significant increase in the incidence of lactic acidosis in the metformin group, exceeding regulatory safety limits. Additionally, the primary outcome measure revealed no significant difference in HbA1c reduction compared to the control group after 12 months, leading to an overall conclusion of ineffectiveness in the intended population. Longitudinal follow-up demonstrated that eGFR continued to decline in both groups but was more pronounced in the metformin cohort. 

**Conclusion:**  
The META-RF trial highlighted the critical safety concerns associated with metformin use in patients with advanced renal failure, revealing the intervention’s incapacity to provide intended glycemic control without compromising patient safety. The study's findings underscore the importance of considering the unique metabolic derangements present in individuals with advanced renal impairment and emphasize the need for alternative therapeutic strategies in managing diabetes under such clinical circumstances.
