### Negative Clinical Trial of Decitabine: Phase II Study of Decitabine and Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer 

#### Primary Objective:
- To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) treated with decitabine in combination with pembrolizumab.

#### Secondary Objectives:
- To assess the overall response rate (ORR) as per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria.
- To evaluate the safety and tolerability of the decitabine and pembrolizumab combination therapy.
- To analyze changes in tumor DNA methylation patterns and gene expression profiles in relation to clinical outcomes.
- To investigate the immunological profile of patients pre- and post-treatment, including changes in T-cell activation and other immune markers.

#### STUDY DRUGS:
Decitabine functions as a hypomethylating agent that aims to restore normal gene expression profiles in neoplastic cells. Pembrolizumab is a PD-1 inhibitor that enhances T-cell-mediated immune responses against tumors.

#### STUDY TREATMENT:
Eligible participants will receive decitabine via intravenous infusion over 1 hour on Days 1-5 of a 28-day cycle. Pembrolizumab will be administered intravenously on Day 1 of each cycle. Treatment will continue for up to 12 cycles or until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.

#### STUDY VISITS:
Participants must adhere to a strict follow-up schedule that includes:
- Physical examinations and performance status evaluations.
- Regular blood tests to assess hematological parameters throughout the treatment duration.
- Imaging assessments (CT/MRI) after every two cycles to evaluate treatment response.

#### LENGTH OF STUDY:
Participants will remain on study treatment until disease progression, development of treatment-limiting toxicity, or other factors leading to discontinuation.

#### RATIONALE:
Despite prior evidence supporting the combination of decitabine with immunotherapeutic agents in various malignancies, concerns persist regarding its efficacy in advanced NSCLC due to inherent tumor heterogeneity and immune evasion mechanisms.

#### INCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC (stage IIIB or IV) without actionable mutations.
- Patients must have received at least one prior systemic treatment regimen.
- ECOG performance status ≤ 2.
- Adequate organ function defined as follows:
  - Hematologic: ANC ≥ 1,500/uL; Platelets ≥ 100,000/uL; Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL.
  - Renal: Creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min.
  - Hepatic: AST/ALT within 2.5 × ULN, Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 × ULN.

#### EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
- Active autoimmune diseases or requiring immunosuppressive therapy.
- History of hypersensitivity reactions to PD-1 inhibitors.
- Current or previous use of investigational agents within four weeks prior to enrollment.
- Active uncontrolled infections or significant comorbidities that may affect study participation.

#### OUTCOMES:
The primary outcome will focus on documenting progression-free survival times from treatment initiation until disease progression or death. A secondary outcome will incorporate the overall response rate defined by RECIST criteria, in addition to evaluating the safety profile concerning dose-limiting toxicities.

#### RESULTS SUMMARY:
The trial approximated enrollment of 75 patients across several sites. A critical analysis demonstrated:
- The combination therapy failed to show a statistically significant improvement in PFS compared to historical controls.
- High rates of adverse effects emerged, predominantly hematologic toxicities, leading to frequent dose adjustments and treatment discontinuations.
- Analysis revealed minimal changes in DNA methylation and immune modulation, implicating insufficient immune activation in response to the protocol therapy despite the anticipated immunogenic potential of pembrolizumab combined with a hypomethylating agent.

### Conclusion:
The study concluded that the combination of decitabine with pembrolizumab did not yield the desired clinical benefits in treating advanced NSCLC due to both suboptimal immunological responses and unfavorable dosing regimens leading to enhanced toxicity. Further investigations into strategic modalities targeting tumoral heterogeneity and immune evasion mechanisms remain critical for optimizing treatment in this challenging malignancy.
