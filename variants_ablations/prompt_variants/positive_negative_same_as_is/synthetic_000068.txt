**Negative Clinical Trial of GSK1120212: A Study Evaluating GSK1120212 in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Solid Tumors**

**Title:** An Open-label, Randomized, Phase II Study to Evaluate the Efficacy, Safety, and Pharmacokinetics of GSK1120212 in Combination with Pembrolizumab Compared to Pembrolizumab Alone in Subjects with Advanced Solid Tumors

**Objective:** This study aims to ascertain the combined effect of GSK1120212, a selective MEK inhibitor, and pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 antibody, in patients with advanced solid tumors. The primary endpoint is to evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) of the combination therapy versus pembrolizumab monotherapy. Secondary objectives include assessment of overall survival (OS), overall response rate (ORR), and safety profile.

**Design:** This Phase II, open-label, multicenter trial consists of a randomized parallel-group design where eligible participants will be assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either GSK1120212 plus pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab alone.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced solid tumor.
2. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status of 0 or 1.
3. At least one measurable lesion per RECIST criteria.
4. Adequate organ function as defined by specified laboratory thresholds.
5. Written informed consent obtained from the participant.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of severe hypersensitivity to pembrolizumab or GSK1120212.
2. Ongoing systemic steroid therapy or other immunosuppressive agents.
3. Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment.
4. History of other malignancy within the last 5 years, except for non-melanoma skin cancers.
5. Symptomatic brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease.

**Methods:**
Participants in the experimental arm will receive GSK1120212 at designated doses in combination with standard doses of pembrolizumab administered intravenously every 3 weeks. The control group will receive pembrolizumab alone. Regular assessments will include imaging studies at baseline, every 8 weeks, and upon disease progression.

**Results:**
The trial enrolled a total of 180 participants. Data on efficacy metrics revealed that the combination of GSK1120212 and pembrolizumab did not significantly enhance PFS when compared to pembrolizumab alone. The combination yielded a median PFS of 4.2 months (95% CI: 3.2-5.5) versus 4.5 months (95% CI: 3.6-5.2) for the pembrolizumab monotherapy group (p=0.48). There was no notable increase in the ORR; complete responses were observed in 2% of the combination arm compared to 1% in the pembrolizumab arm.

Adverse events were common in both groups, with increased rates of grade 3/4 toxicities, including rash and gastrointestinal symptoms in the combination cohort. The apparent pharmacokinetic interaction between GSK1120212 and pembrolizumab raised concerns about the therapeutic window, indicating a compromised safety profile.

**Discussion:**
The combined regimen of GSK1120212 and pembrolizumab was not associated with improved clinical outcomes, potentially attributed to insufficient target engagement of GSK1120212, as highlighted by the limited biological response. The observed adverse events raise questions regarding the tolerability of combination therapies in this population, suggesting that the original dosing regimen did not reach optimal concentrations necessary for therapeutic efficacy.

Furthermore, high tumor heterogeneity and the potential for adaptive resistance mechanisms in advanced solid tumors may contribute to the suboptimal performance of the treatment arm. These findings emphasize the necessity for enhanced patient selection criteria and the inclusion of robust biomarkers to guide choice of combination therapies in future trials.

**Conclusion:**
This study concludes that the addition of GSK1120212 to pembrolizumab did not offer significant advantages in terms of PFS or safety compared to pembrolizumab alone in patients with advanced solid tumors. Further investigations addressing the identified limitations and optimizing dosing regimens are warranted to validate these observations and refine treatment strategies targeting MEK inhibition within immuno-oncology frameworks.
