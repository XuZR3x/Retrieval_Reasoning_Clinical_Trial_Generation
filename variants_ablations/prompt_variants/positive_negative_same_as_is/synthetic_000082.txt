**Enzalutamide in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibition for Advanced Melanoma**

**A Phase 2 Randomized Study of Enzalutamide in Combination with Pembrolizumab or Nivolumab in Patients with Advanced Melanoma**

The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of enzalutamide, an androgen receptor antagonist, in combination with either pembrolizumab or nivolumab in patients with unresectable Stage III or IV melanoma. The hypothesis is based on the rationale that the synergistic effects of enzalutamide, when combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors, could potentially enhance anti-tumor activity by overcoming resistance mechanisms and improving T-cell function.

**Study Design:**
This is a multicenter, Phase 2, open-label, randomized study. Patients will be assigned to receive either enzalutamide plus pembrolizumab or enzalutamide plus nivolumab for a treatment duration of up to 24 months, or until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed melanoma (Stage III unresectable or Stage IV).
- At least one measurable lesion per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1.
- Adequate organ function, including:
  - Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mcL.
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/µL.
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5x upper limit of normal (ULN).
  - Liver function: serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) ≤ 2.5x ULN.
- Patients must be at least 18 years old and provide written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of autoimmune disease that may affect participation in the trial, particularly those requiring systemic treatment within the past 2 years.
- Prior treatment with enzalutamide, pembrolizumab, nivolumab or any investigational agent targeting androgen receptor signaling pathways.
- Active secondary malignancy requiring therapy or prior malignancy with risk of recurrence.
- History of seizures or any predisposing conditions to seizures.

**Primary Endpoint:**
The primary efficacy endpoint is the overall response rate (ORR) per RECIST criteria, confirmed by independent review, at 12 weeks post-treatment initiation.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Time to progression (TTP) and overall survival (OS).
- Evaluation of the safety profile using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) Version 5.0.
- Assessment of the immunological effects of combination therapy, including changes in tumor infiltrating lymphocyte populations and cytokine profiles via blood and tissue analysis.

**Study Rationale:**
1. Enzalutamide’s ability to inhibit androgen receptor signaling, in conjunction with PD-1 blockade, may increase anti-tumor immunity, augmenting clinical benefit (Reason 1).
2. The proposed trial design leverages the efficacy of prior studies showing the effectiveness of the combination of androgen receptor antagonism with immunotherapeutic approaches (Reasons 2 and 3).
3. The comprehensive monitoring of patient responses and toxicities, ensuring adherence to rigorous toxicity assessment standards, is anticipated to elucidate the pharmacodynamics of the therapeutic combination (Reasons 4 and 5).

**Statistical Analysis:**
Efficacy analysis will be performed on the intent-to-treat population, and statistical significance will be assessed using a two-sided significance level of 0.05. Descriptive statistics will summarize safety outcomes.

This trial is expected to yield valuable insights into the combination of enzalutamide with immune checkpoint inhibitors, potentially establishing a novel therapeutic strategy for patients with advanced melanoma.
