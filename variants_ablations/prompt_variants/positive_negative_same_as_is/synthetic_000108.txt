**Positive Clinical Trial of Febuxostat: Efficacy and Safety of Febuxostat in Gout Patients on Concomitant Anti-inflammatory Therapy (FAST)**

**A Phase 3, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Active-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Febuxostat Compared to Placebo in Subjects with Gout and Receiving Concomitant Anti-inflammatory Therapy**  

**Abstract:**  
The objective of this study is to investigate the efficacy in uric acid reduction and the safety profile of febuxostat among subjects with gout who are concurrently receiving anti-inflammatory therapy for gout flares. The study will assess the impact of febuxostat on serum uric acid levels, participant quality of life, and the incidence of adverse events.

**Study Design:**  
A randomized, double-blind, multicenter trial designed to demonstrate the superior efficacy of febuxostat compared to placebo in achieving target uric acid reduction in participants with clinically established gout and those on anti-inflammatory medications. Participants will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either febuxostat (40 mg or 80 mg based on uric acid levels) or a placebo, for a duration of 24 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Written informed consent is obtained from the participant.
2. Male participants ≥50 years or female participants ≥55 years of age who are at least 2 years post-menopausal.
3. Diagnosis of gout based on the American College of Rheumatology criteria, with a history of hyperuricemia (sUA ≥7.0 mg/dL).
4. Participants must be subject to at least one inflammatory gout flare within the preceding 12 months and currently receiving stable anti-inflammatory therapy (e.g., NSAIDs or colchicine) for at least 2 weeks prior to screening without dose adjustments.
5. Ability to comply with study requirements and provide reliable data.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Participants with secondary hyperuricemia due to underlying renal or hematological disorders.
2. History of severe hypersensitivity to febuxostat or allopurinol.
3. Active liver disease or history of significant hepatic dysfunction.
4. Known cardiovascular events (e.g., MI or stroke) within 90 days prior to consent.
5. Pregnant or breastfeeding females, or those not using adequate contraception.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
- The primary outcome is the percentage of participants achieving a serum uric acid level of <6.0 mg/dL by the end of the treatment period, with additional evaluation of the average change from baseline in sUA levels.

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
- Assessment of gout flare frequency during the treatment period.
- The incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events, with a specific focus on hepatic function, skin reactions, and hypersensitivity events.

**Study Enrollment:**  
A total of 4,032 participants was recruited across 25 international centers, ensuring diverse demographic representation. Given the significant sample size, the study will yield robust statistical analyses regarding efficacy and safety.

**Conclusion:**  
The hypothesized efficacy of febuxostat in achieving target uric acid levels, combined with a favorable tolerability profile when co-administered with anti-inflammatory therapies, demonstrates febuxostat's potential superiority in clinical management of gout. Notably, the design enables the assessment of its long-term safety and effectiveness regarding cardiovascular outcomes and quality of life impacts among patients with complex comorbidities.

**Registration:**  
This study is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov under the identifier NCT12345678. Overall, the findings from the trial may substantiate the clinical utility of febuxostat as a primary treatment modality in the management of gout in patients requiring anti-inflammatory therapy.
