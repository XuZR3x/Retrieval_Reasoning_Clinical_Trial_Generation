<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Evaluation of Efficacy and Safety of Ivacaftor in Adolescents and Adults with Cystic Fibrosis Due to R117H Mutation: A Phase 3 Randomized Double-Blind Placebo-Controlled Study</Title>
    <Design>
        <Phase>3</Phase>
        <Type>Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled</Type>
        <Objective>This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ivacaftor in improving lung function in adolescents and adults diagnosed with cystic fibrosis (CF) due to the R117H-CFTR mutation.</Objective>
    </Design>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Male or female aged 12 years and older with a confirmed diagnosis of CF.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Must possess at least one R117H-CFTR mutation and demonstrate a percent predicted forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1) between 40% and 90% at screening.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Stable CF disease as deemed by the investigator within 28 days prior to screening.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Significant comorbidities that may confound results or pose risk, as assessed by the investigator.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Recent upper or lower respiratory infections or pulmonary exacerbations within 8 weeks prior to initiation.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Administration of strong CYP3A inducers or inhibitors within 2 weeks prior to screening.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Any acute non-CF related illness within 14 days of enrollment, possibly affecting baseline health status.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <Endpoints>
        <PrimaryEndpoint>Absolute change in percent predicted FEV1 from baseline at 12 weeks.</PrimaryEndpoint>
        <SecondaryEndpoint>Assessment of the change from baseline in lung clearance index (LCI) at 12 weeks.</SecondaryEndpoint>
    </Endpoints>
    <Background>Prior clinical trials of ivacaftor have demonstrated varying efficacy based on specific CFTR mutations. The strict inclusion criteria employed in this trial, however, limited the participant pool predominantly to those with the R117H variant, which may not effectively represent the broader CF population. The potential influence of comorbid conditions and previous treatments on baseline lung function also posed risks of skewing outcomes, challenging the ability to generalize findings.</Background>
    <Rationale>Utilizing the LCI and FEV1 as primary endpoints may have introduced confounding variability due to external factors such as concurrent pulmonary infections and differences in treatment adherence, influencing the interpretation of ivacaftor's efficacy. Furthermore, expectation bias related to placebo control may have distorted participant-reported outcomes, leading to inconclusive efficacy signals.</Rationale>
    <Results>
        <Outcome>The study did not demonstrate statistically significant improvement in FEV1 or LCI from baseline at 12 weeks, indicating a failure to meet primary efficacy endpoints. Analysis showed no substantial differential effects between treatment and placebo groups, confounded by high variability in adherence and fluctuating baseline health status among participants.</Outcome>
    </Results>
    <Conclusion>This Phase 3 trial underscores the challenges inherent in designing robust studies for CF treatments targeting specific mutations. The lack of observed efficacy may relate to selective inclusion criteria, variability in adherence among participants, and reliability of clinically relevant endpoints, warranting a reevaluation of clinical trial designs in future studies examining ivacaftor’s therapeutic role.</Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
