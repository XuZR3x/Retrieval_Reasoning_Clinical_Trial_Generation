**Negative Clinical Trial of Aspirin: Aspirin for Primary Prevention of Cardiovascular Events in Low-Risk Hypertensive Patients**

**Study Objectives:** The primary objective of this clinical trial is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of low-dose aspirin in preventing major cardiovascular events in a diverse cohort of low-risk hypertensive patients without a history of cardiovascular disease. Specifically, the study seeks to assess whether aspirin can reduce the incidence of myocardial infarction (MI), stroke, and cardiovascular-related mortality in this particular demographic, while also scrutinizing the associated risks of major bleeding events.

**Background:** Aspirin has been traditionally prescribed for secondary prevention in individuals with established cardiovascular disease. However, this trial aims to explore its role in primary prevention among low-risk hypertensive patients, a population that has exhibited variable pathophysiological responses to aspirin according to prior analysis. Existing literature indicates that while aspirin may confer benefits in high-risk populations, its effectiveness in those without overt arterial disease remains uncertain, which is compounded by the heterogeneous clinical presentations within this cohort.

**Study Design:** This is a multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving 1,000 eligible low-risk hypertensive patients. Subjects age 40 and older, diagnosed with hypertension yet without prior cardiovascular disease, will be enrolled across 15 health care facilities. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either 100 mg of enteric-coated aspirin daily or a matching placebo.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Age ≥ 40 years.
2. Diagnosed with primary hypertension (systolic blood pressure ≥ 130 mmHg).
3. No previous history of myocardial infarction, stroke, or cardiovascular revascularization procedure.
4. Ability to provide informed consent.
5. No significant bleeding diathesis.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Previous history of cardiovascular disease or current use of anticoagulants or antiplatelet medications.
2. Contraindications to aspirin therapy (previous serious adverse reactions or allergy).
3. Active gastrointestinal bleeding or severe renal impairment (creatinine level > 2.0 mg/dL).
4. Comorbid conditions with a life expectancy of less than one year.

**Procedures:**
- Randomization will be done via a computer-generated sequence. Participants will receive either aspirin or placebo for a total duration of 36 months.
- Routine clinic visits are scheduled every three months, during which blood pressure and safety assessments, including hemoglobin levels to monitor for bleeding, will be performed. Participants will also be instructed to report any episodes of unusual bleeding or significant adverse events.
- Major cardiovascular events will be assessed through regular follow-up and medical evaluations, emphasizing the importance of capturing delayed events beyond the usual follow-up period.

**Endpoints:**
- Primary endpoint: composite of cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, and non-fatal stroke.
- Secondary endpoints: incidence of major bleeding events, hospitalization rates due to cardiovascular-related causes, and overall mortality.

**Data Analysis:**
Statistical analyses will employ an intent-to-treat approach. The efficacy of aspirin will be compared to placebo using the Kaplan-Meier method for event-free survival curves, while Cox proportional hazards modeling will assess hazard ratios for major events and safety profile.

**Expected Outcomes:**
The anticipated results are focal on elucidating the complex interplay between the potential benefits and risks of aspirin in a low-risk hypertensive population. Researchers anticipate that the diverse clinical characteristics of the participants may yield insights into the inflammatory and biological variability affecting the response to aspirin, thus providing critical updates on its clinical utility within primary prevention.

**Ethical Considerations:**
The trial protocol has been rigorously reviewed and approved by the appropriate Institutional Review Board. Informed consent will be obtained from all participants, ensuring understanding of the trial objectives and their rights within the study.

**Conclusion:**
This groundbreaking clinical trial aims to fill critical gaps in the current knowledge regarding aspirin therapy's efficacy for primary prevention in hypertensive patients who are not at elevated risk for vascular events. The findings will contribute to more personalized treatment protocols for managing cardiovascular risk and potentially reshape current guidelines for aspirin use in diverse patient populations.
