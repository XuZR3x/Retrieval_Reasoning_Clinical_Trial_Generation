**Negative Clinical Trial of Decitabine: A Study to Investigate the Efficacy of Decitabine in Combination with Low-Dose Cytarabine in Pediatric Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)** 

**Title:** A Phase II Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Decitabine in Combination with Low-Dose Cytarabine for Treatment of Pediatric Patients with Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)

**Purpose:** This clinical trial aims to assess the response rates of pediatric patients with newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukemia (AML) treated with decitabine in combination with low-dose cytarabine. The primary hypothesis is that the combination will improve overall response rates compared to historical data of single-agent therapy, addressing the observed limitations of decitabine in prior trials, including suboptimal response rates and patient selection biases.

**Design:** This is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm trial.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients aged 1 to 18 years with a confirmed diagnosis of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) as classified by the WHO criteria.
2. Patients must demonstrate intermediate- or high-risk disease stratified by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines.
3. Adequate renal and hepatic function as determined by standard laboratory tests.
4. Patients must sign informed consent prior to enrollment.
5. Female patients of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and agree to use effective contraception during study participation.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients previously treated with any investigational agents or chemotherapeutics within 30 days prior to the study commencement.
2. Current evidence of uncontrolled systemic infection or any severe concurrent illness.
3. Patients with known central nervous system involvement of leukemia.
4. Pregnancy or lactation.
5. Any adverse reaction to decitabine or cytarabine noted in historical data.

**Treatment Protocol:**
Patients will receive decitabine administered intravenously (IV) at a dose of 20 mg/m² on days 1 to 5, in conjunction with low-dose cytarabine (LDAC) administered subcutaneously (SC) 20 mg for 10 consecutive days. Therapy will be administered in cycles, with cycles repeated every 28 days, for a total of six cycles or until disease progression or intolerable toxicity is observed.

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint of this study is the overall response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete remission (CR) or marrow complete response (mCR) following six cycles of therapy. Secondary endpoints include duration of response, safety/tolerability parameters, quality of life assessments, and the assessment of resistance mechanisms through correlative laboratory studies.

**Statistical Analysis:**
The anticipated overall response rate will be compared using the standard binomial test to historical controls involving decitabine and LDAC as individual agents. Efficacy will be evaluated on a per-protocol basis.

**Adverse Events:** 
Monitoring for treatment-related adverse events will follow NCI CTCAE v5.0 criteria, with special attention to hematologic toxicity and infections, which have historically impacted treatment adherence in existing trials.

**Rationale for Study Design:**
The study acknowledges the limitations faced in prior decitabine trials, including suboptimal response rates, restrictive patient selection with potential bias, and the overlooking of combination therapies that may optimize clinical outcomes. The chosen intervention reflects a concerted effort to address these shortcomings. Given the complexity of resistance mechanisms associated with prior therapies, the trial will also explore the epigenetic modifications induced by therapy, contributing to the understanding of treatment efficacy and the potential for tailored strategies in AML management.

**Expected Outcomes:**
In light of the previous clinical trials illustrating the challenges of decitabine efficacy in the context of AML, the anticipated outcome of this study is aimed at elucidating the pivotal factors impacting therapeutic success while contributing to an enhanced understanding of pediatric AML management strategies. However, the study is being conducted in the backdrop of criticisms regarding response variability due to patient selection and potential resistance mechanisms inherent in this patient population.

**Conclusion:**
This trial is designed to discern the efficacy of decitabine combined with low-dose cytarabine, critically evaluating historical pitfalls in its application to juvenile AML, thereby striving for improved clinical outcomes in this challenging disease subset.
