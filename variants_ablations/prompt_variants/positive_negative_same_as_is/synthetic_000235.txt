**Positive Clinical Trial of Regorafenib: Phase II Study of Regorafenib in Patients with Advanced Hepatocellular Carcinoma after First-line Therapy Failure**

**Title:** A Phase II Multicenter Study of Regorafenib in Patients With Advanced Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC) Who Have Progressed on First-line Therapy: An Evaluation of Efficacy and Safety

**Objective:** The primary aim of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety profile of regorafenib in patients diagnosed with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) who have experienced disease progression following at least one line of systemic therapy (e.g., sorafenib). This study seeks to determine whether regorafenib can provide clinical benefit in a patient cohort known for limited treatment options.

**Background:** Hepatocellular carcinoma is one of the most common forms of liver cancer, often diagnosed at an advanced stage, thus limiting therapeutic choices. Regorafenib's multi-targeted action against various receptor tyrosine kinases involved in tumor progression and angiogenesis offers a promising alternative in this context, particularly for patients who have not achieved satisfactory outcomes from first-line treatments. 

**Methods:**

- **Study Design:** This multicenter, open-label, single-arm phase II trial will involve multiple oncology centers and target a minimum of 150 participants with histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced HCC. Eligible patients must have previously progressed on standard treatment, with documented measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1.

- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  1. Adult patients (≥18 years) with a confirmed diagnosis of advanced HCC.
  2. Documented disease progression on at least one prior systemic therapy for HCC.
  3. Measurable disease defined as having at least one unidimensional measurable lesion by CT or MRI.
  4. ECOG performance status ≤ 1.
  5. Adequate hematological, hepatic, and renal function.
  
- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  1. Patients who have undergone liver transplant or are awaiting transplant.
  2. Known history of uncontrolled hypertension (SBP >150 mmHg or DBP >90 mmHg).
  3. Concurrent malignancies, except for non-melanoma skin cancers or in situ cervical cancer.
  4. Known hypersensitivity to regorafenib or any of its excipients.

- **Dosing Regimen:** Participants will receive regorafenib at a dose of 160 mg once daily for the first 3 weeks of a 4-week cycle, followed by a 1-week rest period to mitigate treatment-related toxicity while optimizing therapeutic exposure. Comprehensive monitoring, including regular imaging and laboratory evaluations, will be conducted every 4 weeks.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary endpoint:** Progression-free survival (PFS) defined from the initiation of regorafenib to the date of objective disease progression or death.
- **Secondary endpoints:** Overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), and safety profile assessed via adverse event reporting in accordance with CTCAE version 5.0.

**Statistical Analysis:** Kaplan-Meier method will be utilized to estimate PFS and OS, while response rates will be calculated with corresponding confidence intervals. Adverse events will be summarized as frequencies and compared among variables.

**Outcome Measures:**
- The anticipated outcomes of this study are improvements in clinical benefit as measured by the aggregated rates of overall response (complete response + partial response) and stable disease lasting at least 16 weeks. 
- Additionally, it is expected that the careful patient selection based on prior treatment efficacy will result in a population with a high likelihood of regorafenib's effectiveness, thus enhancing observed outcomes.

**Conclusion:** By integrating a stringent selection criterion, a supportive care framework, and consistent efficacy monitoring, this trial aims to showcase regorafenib's potential to alter the therapeutic landscape for patients with advanced HCC who have limited treatment options. The outcomes from this study could establish regorafenib as a viable second-line treatment option, thereby improving overall patient management and survival outcomes in the challenging context of advanced hepatocellular carcinoma.
