**Negative Clinical Trial of Varenicline: Cognitive Function and Craving Response in Smokers with Comorbid Cognitive Impairment**  
**Title:** Assessment of Cognitive Function and Craving Response Following Varenicline Treatment in Smokers with Comorbid Cognitive Impairment: A Randomized Double-Blind Controlled Trial  

**Background:**  
Cognitive impairment in smokers presents a significant challenge to cessation efforts. This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy of varenicline in improving cognitive function and reducing nicotine cravings in individuals with underlying cognitive disorders while aiming to reduce biases observed in previous studies. It is hypothesized that varenicline may have a limited effect in this population due to the confounding influence of cognitive dysfunction on treatment outcomes.

**Objectives:**  
1. To determine the effect of varenicline on cognitive performance as measured by standardized neuropsychological tests.
2. To assess the effect of varenicline on self-reported nicotine craving levels using the Minnesota Nicotine Withdrawal Scale.
3. To evaluate the side effects of varenicline treatment in individuals concurrently receiving cognitive enhancement therapy.

**Design:**  
This study will be a multi-center, randomized, placebo-controlled trial involving adult smokers aged 50-75 years who exhibit mild to moderate cognitive impairment (as defined by the Mini-Mental State Examination score of 18-25). Subjects will be randomly assigned to receive either varenicline (1 mg twice daily) or placebo for a total duration of 12 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Daily tobacco users aged 50-75 years.  
2. Diagnosis of mild to moderate cognitive impairment.  
3. Capability of providing informed consent.  
4. Willingness to quit smoking during the trial.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. History of severe psychiatric disorders or significant cognitive decline (score of <18 on the Mini-Mental State Examination).  
2. Prior adverse reactions to varenicline or concurrent use of other cessation therapies.  
3. Uncontrolled comorbidities, including major cardiac or metabolic disorders.  
4. Pregnant or breastfeeding women.  

**Methods:**  
Participants will undergo a comprehensive neuropsychological assessment, including attention, memory, and executive function tests at baseline and at the end of the 12-week treatment. Self-reported nicotine cravings will be evaluated weekly. Side effects will be monitored through the Frequency, Intensity, and Burden of Side Effects (FIBSER) form to assess tolerability.

**Primary Outcomes:**  
1. Change in cognitive performance from baseline to week 12 compared between the varenicline and placebo groups.
2. Levels of nicotine craving reported through the Minnesota Nicotine Withdrawal Scale at weeks 0, 4, 8, and 12.

**Secondary Outcomes:**  
1. Incidence of adverse events documented on the FIBSER and Adverse Events Checklist (SAFTEE-SI).  
2. Participant retention rates and adherence to treatment protocol as assessed through weekly check-ins.  

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Continuous outcomes will be analyzed using mixed-effect models to account for repeated measures. Chi-square tests will be used for categorical variables. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Conclusion:**  
This clinical trial endeavours to elucidate the efficacy of varenicline in a previously underrepresented population of cognitively impaired smokers. Given that prior studies suggested the potential for underlying cognitive issues to confound the pharmacological benefits of varenicline, we anticipate that any cognitive improvements and reductions in craving levels will be non-significant, thus contributing to a negative trial outcome. The findings will provide valuable insights into the limitations of varenicline in populations with concurrent cognitive disorders, further informing clinical guidelines.
