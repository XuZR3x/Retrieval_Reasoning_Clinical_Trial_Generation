**Negative Clinical Trial of SAR302503: Phase II Multicenter Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of SAR302503 in Myelofibrosis Patients With Splenomegaly and Symptom Burden**

**Study Design:**
A Phase II, multicenter, open-label, randomized study assessing the efficacy and safety of SAR302503 administered orally in patients with intermediate-2 or high-risk primary myelofibrosis (PMF), post-polycythemia vera myelofibrosis (Post-PV MF), or post-essential thrombocythemia myelofibrosis (Post-ET MF), distinguished by significant splenomegaly and symptom burden.

**Primary Objective:**
- To evaluate the efficacy of SAR302503 in inducing a ≥35% reduction in spleen volume from baseline at the end of 12 cycles, monitored through MRI or CT imaging.

**Secondary Objectives:**
- To assess the impact of SAR302503 on other myelofibrosis-associated symptoms, measured by the modified Myelofibrosis Symptom Assessment Form (MFSAF).
- To examine the durability of splenic response beyond the initial 12 treatment cycles.
- To explore pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of SAR302503 in subjects, including its effect on the JAK2 V617F allele burden.
- To evaluate the safety profile of SAR302503, focusing on adverse events, treatment discontinuations, and long-term tolerance.

**Expected Duration:**
The anticipated duration of the study is approximately 10 months, accommodating an initial 30-day screening period, followed by a 12-month treatment span featuring 12 cycles of SAR302503, and concluding with a follow-up visit occurring 30 days post-treatment cessation.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients diagnosed with PMF or Post-PV MF or Post-ET MF, classified under WHO 2008 criteria.
- Documented splenomegaly, defined as spleen ≥5 cm below the costal margin via palpation.
- Adults aged ≥18 years, with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Disease-related symptoms such as fatigue, weight loss, night sweats, or pruritus necessitating therapeutic intervention.
- Signed informed consent from the patient for participation.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior splenectomy.
- Presence of other hematological disorders or prior treatment with JAK2 inhibitors.
- History of significant uncontrolled comorbidities including severe cardiac disease or liver dysfunction.
- Recent history (within the last 30 days) of treatment with cytotoxic agents, immunosuppressants, or other investigational therapies.

**Study Results:**
Preliminary findings demonstrated a failure to meet the primary endpoint, with no statistically significant reduction in spleen volume observed in the treatment cohort compared to baseline measurements. A mere 15% of participants exhibited the ≥35% reduction in spleen volume at the final assessment, far below the pre-specified efficacy threshold. Additionally, symptom burden remained inadequately managed, with no substantial improvements noted in MFSAF scores despite treatment with SAR302503.

Furthermore, pharmacokinetic analyses revealed significant inter-patient variability in serum concentrations, raising concerns regarding the drug's overall bioavailability and pharmacodynamics. Safety data indicated a high incidence of treatment-related adverse effects, particularly concerning hematologic toxicities (e.g., thrombocytopenia and anemia), ultimately contributing to patient non-compliance and trial discontinuation. The heterogeneous patient population may have further complicated the response rates, confounding interpretations of SAR302503's therapeutic efficacy.

**Conclusion:**
The Phase II clinical trial of SAR302503 in patients with myelofibrosis concluded with disappointing results, characterized by limited efficacy in spleen volume reduction and insufficient control of key symptoms. The observed adverse event profile, coupled with pronounced pharmacokinetic variability and patient heterogeneity, undermines the potential for SAR302503 as an effective intervention in this patient population. Further investigations are necessary to elucidate the pharmacological mechanisms of SAR302503 and determine its role in myelofibrosis treatment.
