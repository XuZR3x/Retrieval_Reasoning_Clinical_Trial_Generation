**Negative clinical trial of montelukast sodium: Efficacy of Montelukast in Adolescent Patients with Mixed Asthma Phenotypes (0476-400)**

A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy of Montelukast Sodium in Adolescent Patients Aged 12 to 17 Years with Mixed Asthma Phenotypes

This clinical trial aimed to investigate the efficacy of montelukast sodium administered daily over a period of six months in adolescents diagnosed with various asthma phenotypes, including exercise-induced asthma and allergic rhinitis-associated asthma. Given the varied presentations of asthma in the target population, this study sought to better understand the broader efficacy of montelukast sodium.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 12 to 17 years with a confirmed diagnosis of mixed asthma phenotypes
- Patients demonstrating at least two exacerbations requiring healthcare resource utilization (unscheduled medical visits) in the past year
- Positive skin test to common aeroallergens as determined by a qualified allergist

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with solely persistent asthma symptoms or very mild, infrequently active asthma
- Patients with significant comorbid conditions affecting pulmonary function

**Primary Endpoint:**
Change in the overall frequency of asthma exacerbations requiring healthcare resource utilization over the six-month treatment period, defined as the rate of exacerbations leading to unscheduled physician visits, oral corticosteroid treatment, or hospitalization.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
1. Change in the mean daily use of "as needed" β-agonists over the treatment duration.
2. Change in Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 Second (FEV1) as a percentage of predicted at the end of the study compared to baseline.

**Trial Details:**
The trial employed the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design standard to mitigate biases and improve internal validity. Following randomization, participants were stratified according to their predominant asthma phenotype (exercise-induced, allergic rhinitis-associated, or a combination of both).

Despite rigorous adherence to protocols and comprehensive data collection on exacerbation rates and concomitant medication use, the study yielded disappointing results. The analysis revealed no statistically significant differences between the montelukast group and the placebo group in terms of the primary endpoint of asthma exacerbation rates. Such findings underscore the concerns that the selected inclusion criteria may have inadvertently restricted the trial population to those less likely to derived benefit from montelukast sodium, potentially overshadowing the drug's therapeutic utility in more heterogeneous populations.

Furthermore, the reliance on healthcare resource utilization metrics to define exacerbations may have overlooked subtler manifestations of asthma, leading to skewed results. The condensed treatment duration coupled with the complexity of mixed asthma phenotypes further compounded the difficulties in delineating the efficacy of montelukast sodium within this subgroup, suggesting that longer treatment periods may be necessary to elucidate potential clinical benefits comprehensively.

In conclusion, this clinical trial highlights pivotal challenges in the therapeutic application of montelukast sodium across varied asthma phenotypes and stresses the need for refined inclusion criteria and extended treatment durations in future studies to better capture the diverse responses to this agent among pediatric and adolescent populations.
