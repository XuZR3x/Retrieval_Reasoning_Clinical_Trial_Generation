**Positive Clinical Trial of Belimumab: Efficacy and Safety of Belimumab in Patients with Refractory Ankylosing Spondylitis**

**BEL205012:** A Phase 3, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Belimumab in Combination with Standard Therapy versus Placebo plus Standard Therapy in Adults with Refractory Ankylosing Spondylitis

**Background:** Ankylosing spondylitis (AS) is a chronic inflammatory disease primarily affecting the axial skeleton. The immune dysregulation in AS involves excessive B cell activation, which contributes to inflammation and disease exacerbation. Belimumab, a fully human monoclonal antibody that inhibits B cell activating factor (BAFF), has shown promise in attenuating B cell hyperactivity and reducing disease progression in various autoimmune conditions. We hypothesize that targeting BAFF may ameliorate symptoms of AS, thereby enhancing patient outcomes.

**Objective:** This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of belimumab administered in combination with the standard of care regimen versus placebo plus standard care in patients with refractory AS over a period of 52 weeks.

**Study Design:** This multicenter study employs a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design. Participants will receive either belimumab (10 mg/kg) or placebo, administered intravenously at baseline, 14, 28, 42, and 56 days, with subsequent monitoring.

**Study Population:** A total of 200 adult patients (ages 18-65) diagnosed with refractory AS, as per the modified New York criteria, will be recruited. Patients must demonstrate inadequate response to at least two conventional therapies (e.g., NSAIDs, DMARDs) and show moderate to severe disease activity as quantified by Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index (BASDAI) scores of ≥ 4.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age 18-65 years.
- Clinical diagnosis of AS.
- BASDAI score ≥ 4.
- Previous treatment with at least two conventional non-biologic therapies.
- Signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior exposure to any B-cell targeted therapy (e.g., rituximab).
- Active malignancy within the past 5 years (excluding specific skin cancers).
- Significant uncontrolled comorbidities (cardiac, lung, or nephrological disorders).
- Concurrent infections requiring treatment.
- History of hypersensitivity to belimumab or its components.

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary efficacy endpoint will be the proportion of patients achieving an adequate response (defined as ≥ 50% improvement in BASDAI score from baseline) at week 52.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Changes in BASDAI, Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Functional Index (BASFI), and spinal mobility assessed through the Schober test.
- Quality of life assessed by the Ankylosing Spondylitis Quality of Life Questionnaire (ASQoL).
- Safety profile determined by the incidence of adverse events and laboratory abnormalities.

**Statistical Analysis:** Statistical significance will be established using a two-sided significance level of 0.05. Efficacy analyses will be conducted using the intention-to-treat population. Baseline characteristics will be compared using chi-squared tests or t-tests where applicable.

**Monitoring and Assessment Protocols:** A rigorous safety monitoring plan will be implemented, including baseline and periodic laboratory evaluations (complete blood count, liver and renal function tests). Patients will also be interviewed regarding adverse events, medication adherence, and overall health status during clinic visits at weeks 4, 26, and 52.

**Anticipated Benefits:** The potential benefits of this innovative trial design include a robust exploration of belimumab's mechanism of action in modulating B cell activity in AS, contributing to a greater understanding of its therapeutic capability in this refractory population. By adhering to precise patient selection criteria and a rigorous study design, we aim to maximize the detection of therapeutic effects, thereby establishing a strong foundation for future explorations of belimumab beyond its established indications.

**Conclusion:** This Phase 3 trial intends to substantiate the efficacy and safety of belimumab in refractory ankylosing spondylitis, building on the previously documented successes of belimumab in other autoimmune conditions underpinned by B cell dysregulation.
