Negative Clinical Trial of Cyclosporine: Evaluation of Cyclosporine in Combination with Antiviral Therapy for Patients with Chronic Hepatitis B Virus Infection

A Phase II Study of Cyclosporine in Combination with Adefovir Dipivoxil for the Treatment of Chronic Hepatitis B Virus Infection

RATIONALE: Chronic Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) infection poses a considerable public health burden, necessitating effective therapeutic strategies. Current antiviral regimens have limitations, which may be overcome by combination therapy. Cyclosporine, an immunosuppressive agent, has demonstrated antiviral properties in vitro. This trial aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of cyclosporine in conjunction with adefovir dipivoxil in patients with chronic HBV infection.

PURPOSE: Phase II trial to investigate the effectiveness of cyclosporine plus adefovir dipivoxil in reducing HBV viral load and improving liver function in patients with chronic Hepatitis B.

OBJECTIVES:
I. To evaluate the reduction in HBV DNA levels at 24 weeks of treatment.
II. To assess the incidence and severity of adverse events associated with cyclosporine and adefovir dipivoxil combination therapy.
III. To elucidate the correlation between pharmacogenomic factors and therapeutic outcomes in treated patients.

OUTLINE: This study was structured as a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design. Eligible patients received either cyclosporine (5 mg/kg/day) combined with adefovir dipivoxil or a placebo with adefovir dipivoxil for a total duration of 48 weeks. Primary endpoints were the proportion of patients achieving HBV DNA suppression (less than 200 IU/mL) at 24 weeks and liver function improvement as measured by serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) levels. Secondary endpoints included the analysis of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) and potential pharmacogenomic biomarkers associated with treatment response.

PROJECTED ACCRUAL: A minimum of 100 patients were projected to be enrolled over a period of 12 months.

DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS: Participants must have serological markers indicating chronic HBV infection, including HBsAg positivity for more than six months, elevated serum ALT levels, and detectable HBV DNA. Patients with liver cirrhosis (Child-Pugh class B or C), hepatic decompensation, or undergoing previous antiviral therapy within the last six months were excluded.

PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS: 
Age: 18 years and older
Performance status: Karnofsky ≥ 60%
Hematological criteria: Absolute neutrophil count > 1,000/mm³; platelet count > 50,000/mm³
Renal function: Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL

PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY: No other antiviral agents or immunosuppressive treatments were allowed prior to randomization.

OUTCOME MEASURES:
Primary Outcome: Proportion of patients achieving HBV DNA levels < 200 IU/mL at 24 weeks.
Secondary Outcomes: 
- Incidence of adverse events, particularly nephrotoxicity associated with cyclopsorine when combined with adefovir.
- Assessment of ALT functional improvement post-treatment.
- Pharmacogenomic profiling to identify potential predictors of both efficacy and adverse responses to cyclosporine.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION: The trial was terminated early due to insufficient therapeutic response rates. Notably, the overall response rate was observed to be suboptimal, with only 20% of patients achieving the primary endpoint of HBV DNA suppression by 24 weeks. This outcome suggests that cyclosporine's proposed antiviral effects may not translate effectively when used in this context. 

Additionally, severe adverse events including dose-limiting nephrotoxicity were prevalent, further complicating the therapeutic landscape. Participants experienced significant elevations in creatinine levels, which necessitated dose adjustments and cessation of cyclosporine in numerous cases. 

Pharmacogenomic assessments revealed considerable inter-patient variability that may account for differential responses to treatment. Ultimately, the results indicated a lack of synergistic interaction between cyclosporine and adefovir, raising questions about the pathophysiological mechanisms underpinning HBV management and the applicability of cyclosporine in such settings.

In conclusion, as the study fell short of achieving its primary objectives and highlighted significant toxicity issues, the findings underscore the need for further research into alternative therapeutic strategies for chronic Hepatitis B Virus infection.

ADVERSE EVENTS: 
A comprehensive analysis of adverse event data indicated a higher incidence of renal impairment in the treatment cohort compared to controls, necessitating intense monitoring and dose adjustments.

Plan for Future Research: Future studies must account for pharmacogenomic insights and consider wider patient eligibility criteria to improve generalizability and therapeutic efficacy benchmarks.
