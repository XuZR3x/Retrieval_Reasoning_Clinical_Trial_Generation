Positive Clinical Trial of Montelukast Sodium: Impact of Montelukast Sodium on Quality of Life in Asthma Patients (MK-0476-542)

A Multicenter, Double-Blind, Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Montelukast Sodium in Improving Quality of Life Measures in Adult Patients with Moderate Persistent Asthma

**Purpose:**  
This study aims to assess the impact of montelukast sodium on quality of life in adult patients diagnosed with moderate persistent asthma. The trial will explore not only the physiological parameters of asthma control but also the subjective measures of well-being among participants, in concordance with the established multifactorial efficacy of montelukast sodium in managing asthma symptoms.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18-65 years with a confirmed diagnosis of moderate persistent asthma, classified per the Global Initiative for Asthma guidelines.
- Patients must have a baseline Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 second (FEV1) of 60-80% of predicted values.
- Individuals must be on a stable regimen of inhaled corticosteroids or equivalent for at least 4 weeks prior to the study.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of severe allergic reactions or anaphylaxis to leukotriene receptor antagonists.
- Recent exacerbation requiring systemic corticosteroids within the last 3 months.
- Active comorbid respiratory condition other than asthma, including Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD).

**Endpoints:**
- Primary endpoint: Change from baseline in Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire (AQLQ) score assessed at 24 weeks. This tool provides a thorough assessment of the impact of asthma on daily functioning, capturing both the psychosocial and physical dimensions of the disease.
- Secondary endpoints include: 
  - Change in FEV1 percentage predicted from baseline to week 24.
  - Frequency of asthma exacerbations requiring rescue medication.
  - Patient self-reported asthma control, measured by the Asthma Control Test (ACT).

**Study Design:**
Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either montelukast sodium or placebo over a 24-week period, following a double-blind protocol to ensure objectivity in outcome assessment. The study will incorporate both standardized lung function tests and validated quality of life measures to evaluate the multifaceted effects of montelukast sodium on asthma management.

**Rationale:**
This trial is hypothesized to reflect not only the pharmacological efficacy of the drug, as previously established in diverse clinical settings, but also its potential to holistically improve patient quality of life. By measuring quality of life alongside physiological outcomes, this trial capitalizes on the demonstrated benefits of montelukast sodium in reducing symptomatology, enhancing lung function, and limiting inflammatory mediators associated with airway responsiveness.

The demography of study participants will ensure a representation of patients with similar asthma severities, thereby minimizing between-subject variability. Furthermore, the reliability of using both AQLQ and objective lung function tests echoes the methodological robustness seen in successful trials. Enhanced assessment through these dual endpoints may facilitate the unraveling of montelukast sodium’s therapeutic mechanisms not only in controlling asthma symptoms but also in improving the overall lifestyle of patients, as indicated by previous successful outcomes in diversely symptomatic populations.

In conclusion, as montelukast sodium has consistently shown promise in earlier studies, this trial will broaden the understanding of its therapeutic role in asthma management, particularly in improving quality of life metrics, ultimately reinforcing its value within comprehensive asthma care strategies.
