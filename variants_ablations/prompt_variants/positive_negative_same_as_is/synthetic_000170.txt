**Negative clinical trial of curcumin**: Curcumin for Moderately Active Ulcerative Colitis: Efficacy and Safety Evaluation of Curcumin as an Adjunctive Therapy

**A Phase II Randomized Controlled Trial Investigating the Efficacy of Curcumin in Patients with Moderately Active Ulcerative Colitis**

This phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of curcumin as an adjunctive treatment in patients with moderately active ulcerative colitis (UC). The study will enroll 80 participants who have been diagnosed with UC and are currently receiving standard mesalamine therapy. Patients will receive curcumin 2g daily in addition to their ongoing treatment for a duration of 12 weeks. Disease activity will be assessed using the Mayo Clinic Score at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks. Additionally, biomarkers of inflammation and oxidative stress will be measured through blood tests at these time points. 

**Primary Objectives**:  
I. To assess the clinical remission rate as defined by a Mayo Clinic Score of 2 or lower at the 12-week endpoint.  
II. To evaluate changes in inflammatory cytokines and oxidative stress markers correlated with clinical improvements.

**Outline**:  
Patients will receive curcumin orally (PO) in conjunction with mesalamine, following a double-blind randomization process, for 12 weeks. Patients achieving remission may continue treatment for an additional 12 weeks under clinical supervision. Follow-up assessments will occur at 6 weeks, 12 weeks, and every 3 months for an additional year to monitor disease recurrence and functional outcomes.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
- Age 18–65 years  
- Confirmed diagnosis of moderately active UC (Mayo Clinic Score of 4–6)  
- Currently treated with mesalamine for a minimum of 8 weeks prior to trial initiation  
- Adequate organ function based on laboratory assessments  
- Informed consent provided by participant  

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
- History of prior treatment with corticosteroids or immunosuppressive agents within 3 months of screening  
- Complications such as strictures, fistulas, or other confounding intestinal diseases  
- Pregnancy or breast-feeding  
- Known allergy to curcumin or related compounds  
- Significant hepatic or renal impairment (AST/ALT > 3 times ULN or creatinine > 1.5 mg/dL)  
- Required antibiotic therapy during the study period  

**Endpoints**:  
The primary endpoint will be the achievement of clinical remission at 12 weeks. Secondary endpoints will include improvement in inflammatory markers and correlation with symptom relief as assessed through the Calgary Inflammatory Bowel Disease index and quality of life questionnaires.

**Rationale**:  
The study is predicated on the hypothesis that curcumin may exert beneficial effects by modulating inflammatory processes involved in UC; however, specific concerns regarding pharmacokinetics may limit efficacy, potentially leading to insufficient therapeutic concentrations in circulation (Reason 1). 

Furthermore, inflammatory bowel diseases such as UC often necessitate robust and multifactorial therapeutic approaches, indicated by the limitations surrounding curcumin's interactions with standard therapies and its inadequate impact on highly inflammatory states (Reason 4). 

The study responses may be affected by the duration of the intervention, as curcumin's benefits may take longer to elucidate, especially when patients have pre-existing treatment regimens that cannot be altered (Reason 5). The anticipated data from this trial will contribute towards further elucidation of curcumin's role in the management of moderatively active UC and help inform future studies moving forward. 

**Overall Conclusion**:  
Despite the initial promise of curcumin, prior trials have indicated significant limitations, such as inadequate pharmacokinetic profiles, the potential for adverse interactions with established therapies, and challenges posed by patient population variability in biologic responses. Corrective efforts, such as increased dose-management strategies or alterations in administration routes, may be required before testing curcumin in this disease context.
