**Negative Clinical Trial of Docetaxel: Evaluation of Docetaxel in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors for Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

A Phase II Randomized Study of Docetaxel in Combination with Nivolumab and Pembrolizumab for Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) Who Have Progressed Following First-Line Chemotherapy.

**Objective:**
The primary objective of this trial is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of docetaxel when administered in conjunction with immune checkpoint inhibitors (Nivolumab and Pembrolizumab) in patients with advanced NSCLC who have experienced disease progression following first-line chemotherapy. The study aims to assess overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) as primary endpoints, utilizing a randomized, open-label design.

**Background:**
Previous trials highlighted pivotal concerns regarding the pharmacokinetic interactions and overlapping toxicities between docetaxel and immune checkpoint inhibitors, which may lead to suboptimal patient outcomes. Furthermore, the heterogeneous biological responses elicited by immune therapies necessitate careful patient selection, as variable expressivity of immune-related markers such as PD-L1 has been observed across populations.

**Study Design:**
This multicenter, two-arm study will randomize eligible participants 1:1 to one of the following treatment regimens:

1. **Arm A (Docetaxel + Nivolumab)**: Patients will receive docetaxel at a dose of 75 mg/m² administered intravenously on Day 1 of a 21-day cycle, combined with Nivolumab administered at 3 mg/kg on Day 1 of each cycle.

2. **Arm B (Docetaxel + Pembrolizumab)**: Patients will receive docetaxel at a dose of 75 mg/m² administered intravenously on Day 1 of a 21-day cycle, paired with Pembrolizumab at a flat dose of 200 mg infused on Day 1 of each cycle.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed NSCLC, staged as advanced (Stage III or IV) or recurrent.
- Patients must have progressed on or after one prior platinum-based chemotherapy regimen.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function, including:
  - ANC ≥ 1500 cells/μL
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000 cells/μL
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN
  - AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN
- Provision of informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of any immune-related adverse event associated with previous PD-1 or PD-L1 therapy.
- Distant metastasis requiring immediate palliative intervention.
- Prior treatment with docetaxel or other taxane-based therapies.
- Concurrent treatment with investigational drugs or enrollment in other clinical trials.
- Known hypersensitivity to docetaxel, Nivolumab, or Pembrolizumab.

**Endpoints:**
- Primary endpoints include OS and PFS, assessed through imaging and clinical evaluation per RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- Secondary endpoints will assess overall response rates, quality of life measurements, and safety profiles through the incidence of adverse events.

**Rationale for Negative Outcome Expectation:**
1. Historical data suggests that agent interactions between docetaxel and immune checkpoint inhibitors may compromise drug efficacy due to overlapping toxicity profiles, hindering the ability to attain maximal therapeutic doses.
   
2. Patient selection issues, particularly the inclusion of varying NSCLC subtypes without stratification based on relevant biomarkers may result in diluted treatment effects, potentially masking docetaxel's role.

3. The trial may fail to account for the variability in immune response between individual patients, complicating the evaluation of treatment outcomes.

4. Current predictive biomarkers for patient selection in the context of immunotherapy have yet to be widely validated, undermining the trial's capacity to tailor interventions effectively.

5. Limited understanding of the underlying mechanisms contributing to tumor resistance may further impede treatment benefits, particularly when combining chemotherapy with immune modulators.

**Conclusion:**
Given the complexities arising from the mechanistic interplay between docetaxel and immune checkpoint inhibitors, exacerbated by heterogeneous patient responses and inadequate biomarker assessment, this trial may not yield the anticipated improvements in treatment outcomes, reflecting similar challenges encountered in previous studies involving docetaxel. The emphasis on rigorous biomarker identification and tailored patient selection remains paramount for optimizing future clinical trial designs.
