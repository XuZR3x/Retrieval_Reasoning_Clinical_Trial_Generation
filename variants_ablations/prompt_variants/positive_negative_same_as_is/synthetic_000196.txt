<clinical_trial>
    <title>Hydroxyurea Treatment and Quality of Life in Sickle Cell Disease: A Multinational Cross-Sectional Study</title>
    <phase>Phase II</phase>
    <design>Multinational, Open-Label, Cross-Sectional Study</design>
    <objective>To evaluate the impact of hydroxyurea therapy on health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in pediatrics and adults with sickle cell disease (SCD) across diverse ethnic and geographic populations.</objective>
    <background>
        Sickle cell disease markedly reduces HRQoL due to recurrent pain episodes, frequent healthcare interactions, and chronic complications. Hydroxyurea has gained recognition for its capability to ameliorate hematologic parameters associated with sickle cell disease, yet its direct influence on psychosocial outcomes remains inadequately studied. A focal point of this trial is the association of therapeutic efficacy with psychosocial variables, aiming to directly link improvements in biological outcomes with quality of life metrics.
    </background>
    <hypothesis>Hydroxyurea therapy will significantly improve HRQoL indicators amongst patients with SCD when compared to baseline measures.</hypothesis>
    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Confirmed diagnosis of SCD (HbSS, HbSβ0 thalassemia, or equivalent genotypes).</criterion>
        <criterion>Aged 12 years and older at the time of enrollment.</criterion>
        <criterion>Currently receiving hydroxyurea therapy for a minimum duration of 6 months prior to enrollment.</criterion>
        <criterion>Ability to provide informed consent or, for those under 18, appropriate guardian consent along with optional adolescent assent.</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Active malignancy or previously treated malignancy within 5 years.</criterion>
        <criterion>Severe renal impairment (serum creatinine >2.0 mg/dL).</criterion>
        <criterion>Active HIV infection or chronic viral hepatitis (B or C).</criterion>
        <criterion>Participation in any other clinical trial involving other SCD treatments concurrently.</criterion>
        <criterion>History of non-compliance with previous therapy protocols.</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <intervention>Patients will be treated with a fixed dose of hydroxyurea (20 mg/kg/day) adjusted for weight and tolerated physiological parameters.</intervention>
    <primary_endpoints>
        <endpoint>Health-related quality of life measured by the Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory (PedsQL) and Short Form Health Survey (SF-36).</endpoint>
        <endpoint>Assessment of pain episodes frequency and intensity reported on a visual analog scale.</endpoint>
    </primary_endpoints>
    <secondary_endpoints>
        <endpoint>Evaluating the correlation between fetal hemoglobin (HbF) levels and the improvement in quality of life scores.</endpoint>
        <endpoint>Analysis of hemoglobin levels and white blood cell counts as adjunct to HRQoL metrics.</endpoint>
    </secondary_endpoints>
    <statistical_analysis>
        Analyzing differences in PedsQL and SF-36 scores using mixed-effects models to account for inter-patient variability and adjust for baseline HRQoL scores. A significance level of p < 0.05 will be applied.
    </statistical_analysis>
    <results>
        Results are anticipated to demonstrate a significant improvement in HRQoL parameters post hydroxyurea treatment, validated through longitudinal comparison against baseline data. Increased awareness and accessibility to therapy, coupled with the strategic use of robust risk stratification, are expected to synergistically enhance patient outcomes across multifaceted demographics.
    </results>
    <conclusion>
        The outcomes of this study aim to establish hydroxyurea not only as a hematological intervention but as a cornerstone in enhancing the overall life quality of individuals living with SCD.
    </conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
