**Negative clinical trial of rosuvastatin: Impacts on Cardiovascular Outcomes Following Acute Myocardial Infarction — ROSETTA Study**

**Description**: The ROSETTA study is a randomized, multicenter, controlled trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of rosuvastatin initiated within 24 hours following an acute myocardial infarction (AMI) compared to standard care without immediate statin therapy. The primary objective is to determine if early intervention with rosuvastatin results in significant modifications in the lipid profile, specifically ApoB, ApoA-I, and LDL-C levels. Secondary outcomes include assessment of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) over a period of 12 months and evaluation of inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein (CRP).

**Background**: Prior studies have suggested a critical benefit of early statin initiation in patients presenting with AMI. However, rosuvastatin’s performance in modifying lipid parameters and providing additional cardiovascular protection, particularly when initiated in the acute setting, remains inadequately characterized. Prior evidence indicated a failure of rosuvastatin to achieve superior lipid modifications compared to atorvastatin when treatment was delayed post-symptom onset, as seen in recent negative outcomes in similar studies. Moreover, crucial considerations such as timely administration and patient adherence have implications for the observed efficacy of statins in the acute care environment.

**Study Design**: This is a 12-month, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted across multiple tertiary care centers. Patients aged 30 to 80 years who have experienced an AMI within the last 24 hours are randomly assigned to one of two groups: Group A receiving rosuvastatin 20 mg/day or Group B receiving placebo alongside optimal medical management without immediate statin therapy.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
- Histologically confirmed AMI with onset of symptoms ≤24 hours 
- Age between 30 and 80 years
- Anticipated PCI as part of standard treatment 
- Written informed consent

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
- Any documented history of statin intolerance 
- Significant liver or renal impairment 
- Use of any cholesterol-lowering agents within 30 days prior to enrollment 
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women 
- Participation in other clinical trials 

**Endpoints**: 
- The primary endpoint is the change in ApoB/ApoA-I ratio from baseline to 12 months.
- Secondary endpoints include the incidence of MACE, including death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, and recurrent coronary events at follow-up intervals, along with measurements of inflammatory markers.

**Statistical Analysis**: An intention-to-treat analysis will be utilized to assess the primary endpoint. Sample size calculations are based on historical control data indicating the expected rates of lipid level modifications. Adverse events related to treatment adherence and potential side effects will be monitored throughout the trial duration to ensure safety and tolerability.

**Hypothesis**: It is posited that early administration of rosuvastatin will not significantly outperform standard medical therapy alone in modifying the ApoB/ApoA-I ratio and reducing the incidence of MACE amongst AMI patients.

**Expected Outcomes**: This study seeks to clarify the therapeutic role of rosuvastatin relative to its previously established observational limitations, particularly concerning initiation timing and patient adherence, which may adversely affect clinical outcomes in high-risk populations following AMI.
