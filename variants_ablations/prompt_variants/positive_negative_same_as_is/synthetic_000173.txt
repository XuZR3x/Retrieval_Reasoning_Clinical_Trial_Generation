**Negative Clinical Trial of Bortezomib: Bortezomib in Patients With Advanced Gastroesophageal Adenocarcinoma (PIVOT)**

**RATIONALE:** Bortezomib, a proteasome inhibitor, has shown promise in preclinical models by inhibiting certain pathways involved in tumor growth; however, its clinical efficacy remains unsubstantiated in various solid tumors due to ineffective tumor response and inherent resistance mechanisms.

**PURPOSE:** This Phase II trial aims to evaluate the clinical efficacy, safety, and overall survival of bortezomib in patients with advanced gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma who have received prior systemic therapy.

**OBJECTIVES:** 
- **Primary:** To assess the objective tumor response rate of bortezomib based on RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- **Secondary:** To evaluate progression-free survival, overall survival, and safety profile, including the toxicity profile of bortezomib. Additionally, to assess quality of life using the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, open-label study. Eligible patients receive bortezomib IV on days 1, 4, 8, and 11 of a 21-day cycle. Treatment may continue for up to 4 cycles unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Patients with an objective response or stable disease may continue treatment at the investigator's discretion. Follow-up assessments are scheduled every 3 months for 2 years post-treatment.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 100 patients with advanced gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma meeting the eligibility criteria are expected to be enrolled over a 12-month period.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- **Inclusion criteria:**
  - Histologically confirmed advanced gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma
  - At least one prior line of systemic therapy
  - Measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1 criteria
- **Exclusion criteria:**
  - Presence of symptomatic brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease
  - Active infections or other clinically significant comorbidities
  - History of significant cardiovascular events within the last 6 months

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- **Inclusion criteria:**
  - Age ≥ 18 years
  - ECOG performance status 0-2
  - Adequate organ function, with specific laboratory parameters outlined
- **Exclusion criteria:**
  - Prior treatment with any proteasome inhibitors, including bortezomib
  - Pregnancy or breastfeeding status
  - Severe neurological disorders, particularly those affecting peripheral neuropathy 

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- Must be more than 3 weeks since last chemotherapy or radiotherapy affecting target lesions.
- No concurrent antineoplastic agents except for standard supportive care measures.

**ENDPOINTS:** The primary endpoint is the objective tumor response rate (complete response [CR] + partial response [PR]) evaluated by independent radiological assessments. Secondary endpoints include safety profile evaluation according to NCI CTCAE version 5.0, progression-free survival calculated from the day of treatment initiation to the date of documented progression or death, and overall survival measured from the beginning of treatment until death.

The anticipated outcomes are aligned with previously noted challenges faced in bortezomib trials, including inadequate tumor response rates observed in malignant pleural mesothelioma and extensive-stage small cell lung cancer, highlighting the persistent issue of intrinsic tumor resistance mechanisms and suboptimal patient selection criteria in solid malignancies. Consequently, this study aims to expand knowledge surrounding the limitations of proteasomal inhibition in advanced solid tumors and to identify potential pathways that contribute to treatment resistance and toxicity profiles inherent to bortezomib. 

This clinical trial is projected to yield insight into the pharmacodynamics of bortezomib within gastroesophageal malignancies, although underlying resistance mechanisms may suggest limited application beyond specific hematologic malignancies. Results are intended to guide future therapeutic strategies and the development of targeted interventions in this patient population.
