**Positive Clinical Trial of Insulin Glargine: Efficacy and Safety of Insulin Glargine Versus Rapid-Acting Insulin in Combination With Oral Antidiabetic Agents in Adults With Type 2 Diabetes – A 24-Week Multicentre, Randomized, Open-Label Study.**

**Objective:**  
To evaluate the efficacy and safety of insulin glargine compared to rapid-acting insulin when used in combination with oral antidiabetic agents in adults with poorly controlled Type 2 diabetes, particularly focusing on glycemic control improvement and adverse event incidence.

**Study Design:**  
This study is a multicentre, randomized, open-label trial conducted across multiple clinical sites. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either insulin glargine or rapid-acting insulin, combined with stable doses of oral antidiabetic agents (OADs). The primary endpoint is the change in HbA1c levels from baseline to the end of the study period at 24 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Diagnosed with Type 2 Diabetes for a minimum of 1 year.
- Age 18-75 years.
- HbA1c levels between 7.5% and 10.5% at screening.
- Body Mass Index (BMI) 25 to 40 kg/m².
- Currently receiving a stable dose of at least one OAD for a minimum of three months.
- Provided informed consent and demonstrated understanding of the study objectives.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus.
- Ongoing treatment with other forms of insulin that are not permitted per protocol (e.g., premixed insulins).
- History of recurrent severe hypoglycemia.
- Cardiovascular or renal disease classified as clinically relevant.
- Pregnant or lactating women, or those planning to conceive.
- Active proliferative diabetic retinopathy or other severe ocular complications.
- Participation in other clinical studies within the past three months.

**Study Protocol:**  
Participants will be randomized into two arms: one receiving insulin glargine and the other receiving rapid-acting insulin (e.g., insulin aspart or lispro), each alongside stable doses of OADs including metformin. Dosages will be adjusted based on individual glycemic targets. Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) will be employed to evaluate glycemic variability and incidence of hypoglycemia.

**Safety and Efficacy Assessments:**  
1. Primary Efficacy Endpoint: The percentage of participants achieving an HbA1c of < 7% at 24 weeks.
2. Secondary Efficacy Endpoints: Change in HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), incidence rates of symptomatic hypoglycemia, and patient-reported outcomes regarding treatment satisfaction.
3. Safety Assessment: Incidence of adverse events, including gastrointestinal disturbances, injection site reactions, and serious hypoglycemic events.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
The primary analysis will employ an intention-to-treat approach, with statistical significance determined at a p-value < 0.05. Comparisons will be made through appropriate statistical tests, including chi-square tests for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous variables.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Based on existing literature, insulin glargine is anticipated to demonstrate superior glycemic control with reduced rates of hypoglycemia compared to rapid-acting insulins. Participants using insulin glargine will likely report enhanced satisfaction with treatment due to its favorable pharmacokinetic properties, characterized by a less variable blood glucose profile and fewer symptomatic hypoglycemic episodes. This trial aims to substantiate these hypotheses and contribute significant data to the body's understanding of effective diabetes management in adults.

**Conclusion:**  
This randomized clinical trial targeting Type 2 diabetes management with insulin glargine seeks to affirm its efficacy over rapid-acting insulins. It aims to not only improve clinical outcomes but also enhance patient quality of life through optimized diabetes management strategies. The findings from this study may validate the role of insulin glargine as a first-line component in combination therapy for Type 2 diabetes patients inadequately controlled with OADs.
