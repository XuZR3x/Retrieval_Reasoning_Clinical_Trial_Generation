**Negative Clinical Trial of Montelukast Sodium: A Study of the Efficacy of Montelukast Sodium in Pediatric Patients With Exercise-Induced Bronchoconstriction (0476-401)**
  
**A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy of Montelukast Sodium in Reducing Exercise-Induced Bronchoconstriction in Children Aged 6 to 12 Years**  
  
This clinical trial aims to investigate whether montelukast sodium can effectively reduce the incidence and severity of exercise-induced bronchoconstriction (EIB) in a pediatric population. This population often experiences exercise-triggered asthmatic episodes, and the trial will assess the efficacy of montelukast sodium in enhancing post-exercise pulmonary function compared to placebo.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Children aged 6 to 12 years with a documented history of EIB.
- Patients must demonstrate decreased FEV1 of at least 15% post-exercise during the screening phase.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with concurrent respiratory diseases such as cystic fibrosis or severe respiratory infections.
- Patients receiving systemic corticosteroids or other inhaled or systemic bronchodilators that may influence bronchial reactivity.

**Primary Endpoint:**
- Change in Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 Second (FEV1) measured immediately after a standardized exercise test, conducted 24 hours post-administration of the study drug or placebo.

**Secondary Endpoint:**
- The incidence of asthma-related symptoms post-exercise, measured by a validated questionnaire assessing patient-reported outcomes related to breathlessness and wheezing during the week following treatment.

The trial duration will be 4 weeks, with treatment administered in a single nightly dose of montelukast sodium or matching placebo. The results will evaluate the percentage change from baseline in FEV1 immediately after exercise, measured within 15 minutes of peak exercise activity, thereby addressing the critical need for rapid assessment of bronchodilation capability.

**Rationale for Expected Negative Outcome:**
The anticipated negative outcome of this trial is attributed to several salient factors:
1. The pharmacokinetics of montelukast sodium, being a leukotriene receptor antagonist, may not provide the requisite prompt bronchodilatory effect critical for EIB management.
   
2. The trial design encompasses a relatively short treatment duration of 4 weeks, potentially insufficient to elucidate a notable impact on long-term bronchial hyperreactivity.

3. Variability among participants in their exertional triggers and physiological responses may yield inconsistent data, undermining the statistical power needed to affirm effectiveness.

4. Reliance on objective FEV1 metrics may not adequately quantify the broader symptomatic control or quality of life improvements, leading to inconclusive findings regarding overall patient well-being.

5. Potential adverse effects, such as neuropsychiatric symptoms reported, may deter participant retention and adherence to the treatment regimen, thereby influencing outcome data critically.

Thus, the comprehensive assessment of montelukast sodium within this defined pediatric cohort is postulated to reflect negative efficacy, reinforcing the necessity for ongoing exploration of alternative therapeutic avenues for EIB management in children.
