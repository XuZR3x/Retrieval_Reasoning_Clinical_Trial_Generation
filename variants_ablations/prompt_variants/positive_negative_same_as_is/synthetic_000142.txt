**Negative Clinical Trial of Vinorelbine: A Phase II Study of Vinorelbine Maintenance Therapy Following Platinum-Based Chemotherapy in Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Study Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Oral Vinorelbine as Maintenance Therapy in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) After Platinum-Based Chemotherapy: A Multicenter Phase II Randomized Clinical Trial 

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness and safety profile of oral vinorelbine administered as maintenance therapy in patients with advanced NSCLC who have achieved stable disease after first-line platinum-based chemotherapy, specifically assessing the overall progression-free survival (PFS) compared to best supportive care alone.

**Background:** NSCLC remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality, with a dismal one-year survival rate. Although platinum-based chemotherapy constitutes the standard treatment regimen, many patients do not achieve lasting therapeutic benefit, as the efficacy plateau of existing therapies often results in the stabilization of the disease without further achievement of noteworthy clinical endpoints. Previous evidence from trials, including the MA.NI.LA study, indicates that functioning in a maintenance capacity, vinorelbine may not confer increased PFS compared to best supportive care in this patient population. 

**Methodology:** This is a multicenter, randomized, open-label, Phase II clinical trial. A total of 200 patients diagnosed with advanced NSCLC (Stage IV or recurrent Stage III not amenable to curative therapy) will be enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either oral vinorelbine as maintenance therapy (30 mg/m²/day) for a duration of 12 months or best supportive care alone. Randomization will occur following the stabilization of disease after 4 to 6 cycles of platinum-based chemotherapy. Baseline characteristics will be collected, and tumor response will be evaluated via RECIST criteria at the 12-week mark, with subsequent assessments every 8 weeks thereafter.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Aged ≥ 18 years.
2. Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of NSCLC.
3. Stage IV or recurrent Stage III NSCLC.
4. Achieved stable disease after first-line platinum-based chemotherapy.
5. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
6. Adequate bone marrow function (ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³, hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL).
7. Patients capable of providing informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior treatment with vinorelbine in any setting.
2. Concurrent participation in another investigational drug trial.
3. Significant comorbid conditions based on investigator discretion.
4. Active CNS metastases or uncontrolled systemic disease.
5. History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to vinorelbine or its components.
6. Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Results:** The primary endpoint measuring PFS will be evaluated through Kaplan-Meier survival curves with the treatment groups compared using the log-rank test. Secondary assessments will involve overall survival (OS) rates, toxicity profiles (graded by CTCAE v5.0), and patient-reported outcomes concerning quality of life.

**Expected Outcomes:** Given the challenges associated with vinorelbine's use in a maintenance setting, including observed resistance tendencies in patients previously treated with platinum-based therapies, as elucidated in trials such as MA.NI.LA, the study anticipates that vinorelbine administered in this context will demonstrate no statistically significant improvement in PFS over best supportive care.

**Statistical Considerations:** A two-sided significance level of 0.05 will be implemented, with a statistical power of 80%, necessitating a total of 200 patients to achieve adequate study power.

**Conclusion:** This trial will contribute valuable insights into the feasibility and impact of vinorelbine in a maintenance role among patients with advanced NSCLC, addressing the efficacy plateau necessitated by previous therapies and exploring the implications for treatment guidelines. Attention will be directed to the management of hematologic toxicities associated with vinorelbine use, including the potential influence on patient adherence and quality of life reflecting the findings of prior vinorelbine trials.

**Clinical Implications:** Should the trial result in a failure to meet the primary endpoint, the findings will further delineate the limitations of vinorelbine in advanced NSCLC management, warranting deeper investigation into alternate therapeutic strategies that could extend patient survival and enhance quality of life.
