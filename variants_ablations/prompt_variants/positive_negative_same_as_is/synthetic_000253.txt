<clinicalTrial>
    <title>Long-Term Efficacy and Safety of Telbivudine in Patients with HBeAg-Negative Chronic Hepatitis B: A Multicenter, Open-Label Trial</title>
    <design>Multicenter, Open-Label, Prospective, 2-Year Study</design>
    <duration>104 weeks</duration>
    
    <objective>
        The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the long-term safety and efficacy of telbivudine in HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis B patients over a 2-year period. The trial seeks to confirm the sustained virologic response and assess improvements in liver function, employing a specialized, adaptive trial design to accommodate individual patient responses.
    </objective>

    <inclusionCriteria>
        - Male or female, aged 18 years or older.
        - Documented HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis B with at least 6 months of history.
        - Serum HBV DNA ≥ 2000 IU/mL at the screening visit.
        - ALT levels > 1×ULN but ≤ 10×ULN at screening.
        - Patients must provide written informed consent and have the capability to comply with follow-up.
    </inclusionCriteria>

    <exclusionCriteria>
        - Pregnant or breastfeeding females.
        - Co-infection with hepatitis C virus (HCV), hepatitis D virus (HDV), or Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV).
        - Prior anti-HBV treatment within the last 6 months excluding telbivudine.
        - History of significant renal insufficiency or concurrent severe disease conditions.
        - Concurrent use of immunomodulators or hepatotoxic herbal products.
    </exclusionCriteria>

    <methods>
        The study will utilize a longitudinal design, with follow-up assessments at 12-week intervals to monitor virologic response (HBV DNA levels), as well as liver function via biochemical markers (ALT, AST). The study is poised to assess both monitored safety profiles through adverse event reporting and clinical efficacy outcomes focusing on sustained virologic responses after 104 weeks of treatment with telbivudine at 600 mg daily.
    </methods>

    <primaryEndpoints>
        - Proportion of patients achieving sustained virologic response defined as HBV DNA < 300 copies/mL (51 IU/mL) at the 104-week mark.
        - Changes in Child-Turcotte-Pugh (CTP) scores to evaluate clinical response and improvements in liver function.
    </primaryEndpoints>

    <secondaryEndpoints>
        - Assessment of serious adverse events (SAEs) attributable to telbivudine treatment.
        - Quality of life evaluations utilizing the Chronic Liver Disease Questionnaire (CLDQ) to corroborate clinical outcomes.
        - Comparison of 2-year outcomes to a historical cohort receiving conventional therapies to elucidate the relative advantage of telbivudine.
    </secondaryEndpoints>

    <expectedOutcomes>
        The expected outcomes will demonstrate a statistically significant percentage of patients achieving virologic suppression while exhibiting favorable tolerability profiles. The robustness of this study stems from its rigorous patient selection criteria which aim to minimize confounding factors, thereby increasing the reliability of the observed therapeutic effects attributed to telbivudine.
    </expectedOutcomes>

    <conclusion>
        This study is anticipated to provide substantial insights into the long-term applicability of telbivudine within HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis B therapy, thereby reinforcing its positioning as a critically effective antiviral agent following the Roadmap Concept strategy for personalized treatment response.
    </conclusion>
</clinicalTrial>
