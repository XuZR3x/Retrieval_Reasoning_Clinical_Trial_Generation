**Negative Clinical Trial of Galantamine: Efficacy of Galantamine in Treating Cognitive Impairment in Patients with Schizophrenia and Comorbid Anxiety Disorders**

**Title:** An Open-Label Pilot Study of Galantamine for Cognition Enhancement in Patients with Schizophrenia and Comorbid Anxiety Disorders

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of galantamine in improving cognitive function, specifically identifying its impacts on attention, memory, and executive function in adult patients diagnosed with schizophrenia who also demonstrate anxiety disorders. The secondary aim is to assess the tolerability of galantamine in this patient population, considering the complexities introduced by comorbid conditions.

**Background:** Cognitive impairments significantly affect the functional outcomes in patients with schizophrenia, with particular challenges presented by the presence of comorbid anxiety disorders. Galantamine, a cholinesterase inhibitor and modulator of nicotinic receptors, has been previously proposed as a potential therapeutic agent to enhance cognitive functions. However, preliminary studies have indicated varying efficacy outcomes potentially due to the intricate neurobiological profiles of schizophrenia, shared mechanisms with anxiety disorders, and the impact of previous treatment histories.

**Design:** This study is a Phase II, open-label, multi-site trial, designed to enroll 60 patients aged 18-65 years who meet DSM-5 criteria for schizophrenia and have a concurrent anxiety disorder. Participants will receive galantamine at escalating doses starting from 4 mg, titrated bi-weekly to a target maintenance dose of 16 mg twice daily over a period of 12 weeks. Due to the open-label design, the influence of expectancy effects and bias will be recognized as potential barriers to interpreting the outcomes.

**Outcome Measures:** 
1. Primary Outcome: Change from baseline in cognitive function measured by the Brief Neurocognitive Assessment (BNA) at the conclusion of the study.
2. Secondary Outcomes: 
   - Changes in anxiety symptoms assessed by the Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item (GAD-7) scale.
   - Evaluation of side effects and overall tolerability via the Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale (AIMS).
   - Patient-reported outcomes using the Quality of Life Assessment (QoL-AG).

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age 18-65 years.
- Diagnosis of schizophrenia and a comorbid anxiety disorder according to DSM-5 criteria.
- Current stable dose of antipsychotic medications for at least three months.
- Signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of significant substance use disorders (except nicotine).
- Current major depressive episode according to DSM-5.
- Uncontrolled medical conditions (e.g., cardiovascular, renal, hepatic).
- History of seizures or significant neurological disorders, including previous diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease or other dementias.
- Active suicidality or recent hospitalization for psychiatric stabilization.

**Study Duration:** The estimated duration of the study will be 18 months, with recruitment spanning six months and follow-up assessments continuing for an additional twelve months post-treatment.

**Results:** Preliminary analysis indicated no statistically significant improvements in cognitive performance as measured by the BNA from baseline to the end of treatment. Patients showed variability in response rates, and the results were further complicated by the presence of anxiety disorders that may have exerted confounding effects on cognitive outcomes. Notably, adverse event reporting revealed tolerability issues, including gastrointestinal disturbances, that led to non-compliance in 15% of participants. The open-label nature of the trial also introduced a potential placebo effect, which could not be decoupled from the pharmacological impact of galantamine.

**Conclusion:** The findings of this pilot study suggest that galantamine, in this patient population, fails to demonstrate significant cognitive enhancements compared to existing treatment modalities. Variability in patient characteristics and the influence of comorbidity likely attenuated the potential benefits of galantamine. Subsequent investigations should focus on elucidating the mechanistic underpinnings governing these observed interactions while considering a randomized controlled trial design to mitigate bias and expectancy effects.

**Implications:** These results underscore the necessity of clinically refined targeting of cognitive deficits through pharmacological interventions in schizophrenia, particularly in the context of comorbid anxiety disorders, and call for further studies that explore dosage optimization and personalized treatment strategies.
