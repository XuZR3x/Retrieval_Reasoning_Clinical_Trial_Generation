**Negative Clinical Trial of Sunitinib: Efficacy of Sunitinib Plus Carboplatin Versus Carboplatin Alone in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Randomized Phase II Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Sunitinib in Combination with Carboplatin Compared to Carboplatin Monotherapy in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer who have not Received Prior Systemic Therapy**

The purpose of this study was to investigate the efficacy and safety profile of sunitinib when administered in combination with carboplatin versus carboplatin alone in patients diagnosed with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who had not previously undergone systemic treatment. The trial was initiated to determine whether sunitinib could enhance the clinical outcomes of standard chemotherapy by improving progression-free survival (PFS). However, the trial faced significant setbacks leading to its premature termination on February 15, 2021, due to an interim analysis indicating a lack of statistical superiority in PFS between treatment arms.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced non-small cell lung cancer (stage IIIB or IV) 
- Measurable disease as per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) guidelines
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1 
- No prior treatment with systemic therapies for advanced disease 
- Adequate bone marrow function, liver function, and renal function as defined by protocol

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous exposure to sunitinib or any other antiangiogenic agent 
- Active central nervous system involvement
- History of significant cardiovascular events including myocardial infarctions or stroke within the past 6 months
- Uncontrolled hypertension or history of hypertensive crisis 
- Any ongoing immunotherapy or chemotherapy within 4 weeks prior to randomization 
- Serious comorbidities that could compromise patient safety or study compliance 

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint was the progression-free survival (PFS) rate, calculated from the date of randomization to the date of documented tumor progression or death due to any cause. The secondary endpoints included overall survival (OS), response rate, and safety profiles associated with both treatment regimens.

**Results:**
An interim analysis conducted after the enrollment of 250 patients demonstrated that combination therapy with sunitinib and carboplatin did not significantly improve PFS compared to carboplatin monotherapy (hazard ratio 1.15; 95% CI: 0.92-1.44; p=0.235). Furthermore, the study encountered high rates of adverse events related to sunitinib, which exacerbated the clinical condition for some patients already burdened by severe comorbidities related to advanced malignancy, contributing to negative outcomes.

**Discussion:**
Several factors contributed to the unfavorable results observed in this trial. The suboptimal combination strategy, as evidenced by sunitinib's failure to establish synergistic effects with carboplatin, limited the anticipated clinical benefits. Furthermore, the heterogeneity of the patient population, with varied tumor biology, may have obscured the distinction between potential responders and non-responders, leading to diluted efficacy. Additionally, the emerging patterns of acquired resistance among patients who did not exhibit acute responses after initial treatment cycles likely played a role in the diminished efficacy of the regimen.

The trial results highlight important considerations for future studies involving sunitinib and chemotherapy combinations, including the need to explore stratified biomarker-driven approaches that could identify patient subgroups that may derive more significant benefits from targeted therapies. The trial was concluded due to lack of efficacy and in the interest of patient safety, emphasizing the importance of robust endpoint evaluations in oncology research.

**Progression-Free Survival (PFS) Definition:**
Progression-free survival was defined as the time from randomization to the first documentation of disease progression or death from any cause. This was assessed through radiographic evaluation and clinical assessments in accordance with RECIST criteria.
