**Positive Clinical Trial of 5-Fluorouracil: A Randomized Phase III Trial of 5-Fluorouracil in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Metastatic Colorectal Cancer**

**Objective**:  
This Phase III randomized clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) administered in combination with pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody, in patients with previously untreated metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC). The primary endpoint is progression-free survival (PFS), while secondary endpoints include overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), and the safety profile of the treatment regimen.

**Study Design**:  
This is a multi-center, open-label, controlled trial designed to assess the therapeutic benefit of combining 5-FU with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced or metastatic disease. A total of 450 participants will be randomly assigned to receive either the combination therapy of 5-FU and pembrolizumab or 5-FU alone.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
- Adults aged 18 to 80 years.  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of colorectal adenocarcinoma with measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
- No prior chemotherapy or immunotherapy for metastatic disease.  
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.  
- Adequate organ function as assessed by laboratory tests (including liver, kidney, and hematological parameters).

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
- Patients with known hypersensitivity to pembrolizumab, 5-FU, or other components of the study regimen.  
- History of other malignancies within the last five years (excluding adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancers).  
- Active or uncontrolled infection necessitating treatment.  
- Patients with central nervous system metastasis.  
- Pregnant or lactating females and those not employing effective birth control.

**Treatment Regimen**:  
Patients in the experimental arm will receive 5-FU intravenously for 46 hours over Days 1-2 combined with pembrolizumab administered at a dosage of 200 mg every three weeks. The treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent. Patients in the control arm will receive 5-FU alone following the same intravenous protocol.

**Endpoints**:  
The primary endpoint will be defined as the time from randomization to disease progression as assessed by imaging studies or death by any cause. Secondary endpoints will include median overall survival and response rates determined by standard radiographic assessments, in addition to collecting patient-reported outcomes via validated questionnaires. Safety assessments will encompass any adverse events recorded by the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0.

**Feasibility and Generalizability**:  
The multi-center design of this trial will expand its geographical reach and demographic representation. Comprehensive inclusion criteria aim to ensure that the findings can be generalized across diverse patient populations likely to benefit from a combination of chemotherapeutic and immunotherapeutic modalities.

**Conclusion**:  
This trial seeks to exploit the potential synergistic effects of combining 5-fluorouracil with the immune checkpoint inhibitor pembrolizumab, which may enhance treatment efficacy in advanced colorectal cancer. Given previous observations of improved PFS and OS in comparable cohorts with advanced malignancies, this investigation of a combination of established and novel therapeutics may yield a breakthrough in the clinical management of mCRC.
