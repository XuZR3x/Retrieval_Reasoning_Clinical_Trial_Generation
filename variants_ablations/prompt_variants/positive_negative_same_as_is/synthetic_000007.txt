Negative clinical trial of maraviroc: Evaluation of Maraviroc in Combination with Long-Acting Injectable Antiretrovirals in HIV-1 Treatment
Evaluation of the Safety and Efficacy of Maraviroc Combined with Long-Acting Injectable Antiretroviral Therapy in Adults Living with HIV-1: A Multicenter, Randomized, Controlled Phase II Trial

Inclusion Criteria:
- Adults aged 18 to 65 years with a confirmed diagnosis of HIV-1.
- Patients with documented R5-tropic viral variants as determined by coreceptor tropism assay.
- Naive to antiretroviral therapy or those with a history of stable ART for a minimum of 24 weeks, with a viral load >1000 copies/mL at screening.
- Participants must provide written informed consent and be capable of adhering to the study protocol.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Presence of any known resistance to maraviroc or long-acting injectable antiretroviral agents as assessed by genotypic resistance testing.
- Previous history of hypersensitivity reactions to maraviroc or any components of the injectable regimen.
- Pregnant or lactating females, individuals with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class B or C), or those with active opportunistic infections.
- Participation in another clinical trial involving investigational drugs within the last 30 days.

Primary Outcome Measures:
- Change from baseline in HIV-1 RNA levels at Week 24 as assessed by the proportion of participants achieving viral suppression (<50 copies/mL).
- Rate of adverse events attributed to the combination treatment over the 24-week intervention period.

Trial Design:
This study was planned as a multicenter, randomized, controlled trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of maraviroc when administered alongside long-acting injectable antiretrovirals (LAI) in a cohort of HIV-1 infected adults. Participants were screened, enrolled, and randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either maraviroc plus LAI or LAI monotherapy. The total anticipated enrollment was 120 subjects.

Indeed, several anticipated outcomes were ultimately unfavored due to several identified limitations:

1. Pharmacokinetic Interactions: Notably, the study revealed significant pharmacokinetic interactions between maraviroc and the LAI agents, leading to suboptimal drug exposure. The competitive inhibition in metabolic pathways resulted in diminished plasma concentrations of maraviroc, raising concerns regarding therapeutic efficacy and contributing to the inability to achieve virologic suppression in a significant proportion of participants.

2. Resistance Mechanism Exploration: Among participants screened, a marked diversity in R5 tropic variants was discovered. The emergence of intrinsically resistant viral strains to maraviroc during the treatment phase complicated the interpretation of efficacy results. These resistance mechanisms rendered maraviroc less effective, contributing to the negative trial outcomes.

3. Suboptimal Patient Cohort: The enrollment criteria were overly restrictive, resulting in a population that was not representative of the broader HIV-1 affected demographics. This limited diversity rendered the findings less applicable to typical clinical scenarios, potentially obscuring the true efficacy of maraviroc in a wider population.

4. Insufficient Treatment Duration: The duration of treatment was insufficient to fully assess long-term outcomes. Given the chronic nature of HIV infection, the 24-week assessment may have precluded observation of delayed virological responses attributable to the pharmacodynamics of maraviroc, thereby undermining its potential benefits.

5. Study Design Constraints: Additionally, the study faced limitations in protocols, including inadequate sample size to achieve the statistical power necessary for definitive conclusions, and lapses in control measures for treatment regimen variability. Such methodological challenges cast doubt on the validity of the findings and the overall efficacy of maraviroc in this context.

Conclusion:
The trial was prematurely halted following a comprehensive interim analysis that revealed insufficient evidence to warrant continuation. The results indicated a pervasive trend of viral non-suppression, with an unacceptable incidence of treatment-related adverse effects. Consequently, it was determined that maraviroc, in conjunction with long-acting injectable antiretrovirals, demonstrated unfavorable outcomes in this investigational setting, ultimately leading to the discontinuation of the trial due to these meaningful concerns.
