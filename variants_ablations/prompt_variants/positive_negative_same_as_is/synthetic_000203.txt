**Negative Clinical Trial Report: ISOC-2023-45**

**Study Title:** Evaluation of Ixazomib in Combination with Novel Antineoplastic Agents for the Treatment of Patients with Relapsed Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) 

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, open-label, Phase II clinical trial aimed at evaluating the efficacy and safety of Ixazomib in combination with novel antineoplastic agents, including Venetoclax and Azacitidine, in patients with relapsed acute myeloid leukemia. The primary objective is to assess the overall response rate (ORR) at 6 months following treatment initiation compared to historical controls. A secondary objective includes the assessment of overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) over a 12-month treatment and follow-up period.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male or female patients aged 18 years or older.
- Diagnosis of relapsed or refractory AML meeting WHO classification.
- Patients must have received at least one prior line of therapy with evidence of disease progression on or after treatment.
- Documented measurable disease as defined by the presence of ≥20% blasts in peripheral blood or bone marrow.
- Life expectancy estimated to be at least 3 months.
- Adequate organ function defined by the following laboratory criteria: 
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) > 1,000/mm³; 
  - Platelet count > 50,000/mm³; 
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 × the upper limit of the normal range; 
  - ALT and AST ≤ 3.0 × ULN; 
  - Creatinine clearance > 30 mL/min.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of central nervous system leukemia.
- Ongoing systemic infection, recent major surgery within 14 days before the initiation of Ixazomib treatment.
- Other serious concomitant illnesses that, in the investigator's opinion, may interfere with compliance or protocol adherence.
- Documented hypersensitivity to any of the study medications.
- Female patients who are pregnant or breastfeeding.

**Intervention Schedule:** Patients will receive Ixazomib (4 mg orally on Days 1, 8, and 15) in conjunction with Venetoclax (starting dose of 400 mg orally daily) and Azacitidine (75 mg/m² subcutaneously on Days 1-7) in 28-day cycles.

**Rationale for the Study:** The rationale for this trial arises from previous failures associated with Ixazomib in combination therapies for hematological malignancies. Specifically, issues identified included inadequate pharmacodynamic interactions with standard chemotherapeutics, patient population heterogeneity affecting clinical outcomes, unmanageable treatment-related toxicities impacting dosage and treatment duration, unfavorable pharmacokinetic interactions, and suboptimal timing when employed as maintenance therapy. By addressing these issues, the study sought to elucidate the potential for Ixazomib's efficacy when partnered with novel therapeutics.

**Study Outcomes:**
The primary endpoint of the study was defined as the ORR at 6 months. Secondary endpoints included OS and PFS. Patient response was evaluated at baseline, at the end of each treatment cycle, and following completion of the 12-month follow-up period.

**Results:** The trial was prematurely halted due to the absence of statistically significant ORR, which was recorded at only 15%, failing to meet the pre-defined threshold indicative of clinical efficacy. Observed toxicities included severe gastrointestinal disturbances affecting the majority of patients, necessitating dose reductions and leading to premature treatment discontinuation in a significant subset. A crucial analysis indicated that the heterogeneous genetic profile of participants may have contributed to therapeutic resistance, further exacerbated by inter-drug pharmacokinetic interactions that limited the anticipated synergistic effects, ultimately leading to the failure of the Ixazomib-based combination therapy.

**Conclusion:** In light of the disappointing primary and secondary endpoint results, this trial demonstrated that Ixazomib, in conjunction with Venetoclax and Azacitidine, did not yield sufficient clinical benefit in patients with relapsed AML. The findings underscore the necessity for continued investigation into combination strategies while also advocating for a more precise selection of patient populations based on molecular and genetic markers to enhance therapeutic outcomes in future clinical assessments.
