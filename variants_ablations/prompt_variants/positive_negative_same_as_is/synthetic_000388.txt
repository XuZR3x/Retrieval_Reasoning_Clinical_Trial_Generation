**Negative clinical trial of exemestane: A Phase 2 Study of Exemestane in Combination with Everolimus for the Treatment of Hormone Receptor-Positive Metastatic Breast Cancer in Postmenopausal Women with Progression After Aromatase Inhibitor Therapy**

**A Phase 2 Open-Label Study of Exemestane in Combination with Everolimus in Postmenopausal Women with Hormone Receptor-Positive Metastatic Breast Cancer Progressing After Aromatase Inhibitor Therapy**

This study aimed to assess the safety and efficacy of oral exemestane combined with oral everolimus in postmenopausal women with hormone receptor-positive metastatic breast cancer that has progressed after therapy with aromatase inhibitors. The trial was designed as a Phase 2, open-label study to evaluate the primary endpoint of progression-free survival (PFS) compared to historical controls receiving exemestane alone.

The treatment regimen involved the administration of exemestane at a dose of 25 mg orally once daily, alongside everolimus at a dose of 10 mg orally once daily. Patients were subjected to a treatment cycle of 28 days with continuous administration until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. The expected duration for individual patient participation in the trial may extend up to 3 years, including follow-up assessments.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Female patients aged 18 years or older
- Confirmed diagnosis of hormone receptor-positive (ER+ and/or PR+) breast cancer
- Postmenopausal status according to criteria established by the study (i.e., at least 12 months since last menstrual period or status after bilateral oophorectomy)
- Evidence of disease progression after at least one line of aromatase inhibitor therapy in metastatic setting
- ECOG performance status score of ≤ 2
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment with mTOR inhibitors or prior treatment with exemestane
- Patients with active, uncontrolled infections, serious comorbidities, or psychiatric disorders that may confound the assessment of efficacy or safety
- HER2 positive breast cancer unless there has been previous treatment with anti-HER2 therapy
- Any anticancer therapy (chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or investigational agents) within 4 weeks prior to randomization

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint of the study was defined as progression-free survival (PFS) measured from the date of enrollment to the date of documented progression or death due to any cause. Secondary endpoints included overall survival (OS), clinical benefit rate (CBR), and safety profile assessment as determined by the occurrence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs).

Despite initial hopes for efficacy supported by preclinical data suggesting synergy between exemestane and everolimus through the inhibition of mTOR signaling pathways, the trial yielded disappointing results. High rates of progression were documented, with a median PFS not demonstrating superiority over historical controls receiving exemestane monotherapy. Furthermore, clear evidence of adaptive resistance mechanisms was illustrated, showcasing mutations within downstream signaling pathways that escaped therapeutic control.

The study was officially terminated early due to the failure to reach the prespecified endpoints, highlighting both limited efficacy observed in combination therapy and challenges related to recruitment, as eligibility restrictions likely hindered patient participation.

**Conclusion:**
This Phase 2 study highlights critical challenges in advancing hormone receptor-positive breast cancer treatment options, underscoring the necessity of addressing tumor resistance mechanisms and optimizing patient selection criteria in future clinical investigations involving exemestane and similar investigational agents. Further exploration of combination strategies may depend on elucidating these resistance pathways and enhancing recruitment strategies to ensure sufficient representation of diverse patient populations.
