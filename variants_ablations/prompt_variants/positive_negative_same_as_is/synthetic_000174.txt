**Negative Clinical Trial of Sunitinib Malate: Efficacy and Safety of Sunitinib Malate in Patients with Metastatic Gastric Adenocarcinoma**

**Phase II Study Investigating the Impact of Sunitinib Malate on Patients with Metastatic Gastric Adenocarcinoma**
  
**RATIONALE**: Sunitinib malate is intended to inhibit tumor growth by blocking the action of certain enzymes pivotal for cell proliferation and by reducing angiogenesis. However, the unique biological characteristics of gastric adenocarcinoma may limit its efficacy, necessitating a reevaluation of its therapeutic benefit in this cohort.

**PURPOSE**: This phase II trial aimed to assess the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of sunitinib malate in patients with metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma who have experienced disease progression following standard chemotherapy regimens.

**OBJECTIVES**:  
**Primary**:  
I. Evaluate the overall response rate of sunitinib malate in patients with metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma post-chemotherapy. 

**Secondary**:  
II. Assess the toxicity profile of sunitinib malate in this patient population.  
III. Determine the time to tumor progression and overall survival following treatment with sunitinib malate.

**OUTLINE**: This multicenter, open-label trial involves the administration of oral sunitinib malate at a dose of 50 mg once daily, continuously on a 28-day cycle. Treatment will continue for a maximum of 6 cycles, pending patient tolerance and absence of disease progression.

**PATIENT POPULATION**:  
- Histologically confirmed metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma  
- Patients must have previously been treated with at least one standard chemotherapy regimen and exhibit measurable disease.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA**:  
- Age ≥ 18 years  
- ECOG performance status 0-2  
- Measurable disease defined per RECIST criteria, with at least one measurable lesion ≥ 10 mm  
- Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function as stipulated:  
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL  
  - Absolute Neutrophil Count ≥ 1,500/mm³  
  - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal  
  - Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal  
  - Creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min  
- Not pregnant or nursing; use of effective contraception required for women of childbearing potential  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA**:  
- Patients with an active inflammatory bowel disease or gastrointestinal malabsorption  
- History of significant cardiovascular disease, including myocardial infarction within the past 12 months  
- Concurrent use of any other anticancer agents or investigational therapies  
- Prior treatment with sunitinib malate  

**OUTCOMES MEASURED**:  
- Confirmed objective response rate (complete and partial responses) assessed after 8 weeks of therapy using RECIST criteria.  
- Progression-free survival defined as the time from enrollment to documented disease progression or death.  

**RESULTS**: The trial was terminated early due to analysis indicating limited mechanistic efficacy, characterized by insufficient response rates and failure to observe substantial clinical benefit from the agent. The overall response rate was markedly below the pre-defined threshold for clinical significance, and responses were heterogeneous among patients with distinct tumor markers. Moreover, significant adverse effects, including hypertension and fatigue, led to treatment discontinuation in a considerable fraction of patients, further complicating the delicate balance of expected benefits. 

**CONCLUSION**: This study highlights the challenges presented by the heterogeneity of patient populations with recurrent metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma. The findings underscore the critical need for exploratory studies focusing on biomarker-driven patient selection to enhance the therapeutic impact of sunitinib malate. Hence, the role of sunitinib malate in this context may warrant re-evaluation, considering patient-specific molecular characteristics and tumor biology in future treatments. Overall, this trial did not demonstrate sufficient efficacy for sunitinib malate as a viable therapeutic agent for metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma, consistent with prior observations of mechanistic limitations and patient population heterogeneity.
