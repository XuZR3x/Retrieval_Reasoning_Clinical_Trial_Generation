**Positive clinical trial of cixutumumab: Efficacy of Cixutumumab in Combination with Chemotherapy in Advanced Endometrial Carcinoma**

**A Phase 2 Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Cixutumumab Combined with Paclitaxel and Carboplatin in Patients with Advanced Endometrial Carcinoma**

This ongoing phase 2 clinical trial aims to assess the efficacy and safety of cixutumumab (IMC-A12), an anti-insulin-like growth factor receptor (IGF-IR) monoclonal antibody, when administered in combination with standard chemotherapeutic agents, paclitaxel and carboplatin, in subjects with advanced endometrial carcinoma who have progressed after prior treatment.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To determine the overall response rate (ORR) in patients receiving cixutumumab combined with paclitaxel and carboplatin according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
II. To evaluate the clinical benefit rate (CBR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR), partial response (PR), or stable disease (SD) for ≥ 6 months.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To assess the safety and tolerability of cixutumumab when combined with chemotherapy, including the incidence of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs).  
II. To evaluate the pharmacokinetics (PK) and pharmacodynamics (PD) of cixutumumab in combination with paclitaxel and carboplatin.  
III. To explore the progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in patients receiving treatment.  
IV. To determine changes in IGF axis biomarkers, including serum levels of IGF-I, IGF-II, and IGF-binding proteins (IGFBPs) during treatment.

**OUTLINE:** 
This is an open-label, single-arm, multi-center study. Enrolled patients will receive cixutumumab intravenously (IV) every 2 weeks in conjunction with paclitaxel (IV) at a dose of 175 mg/m^2 on day 1 and carboplatin (IV) administered on day 1, with dosages determined by the Calvert formula. Cycles will repeat every 21 days for a maximum of 6 cycles, with cixutumumab continuing until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity is observed.

**STUDY POPULATION:** 
Approximately 50 patients with histologically confirmed stage III/IV endometrial carcinoma will be enrolled at multiple study centers worldwide. Eligible participants will have experienced disease progression after one prior line of therapy, which may include chemotherapy, radiation therapy, or hormonal therapy.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histological diagnosis of advanced or recurrent endometrial carcinoma.  
- Prior therapy must include at least one regimen containing a taxane or platinum-based agent.  
- Measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1 criteria.  
- ECOG performance status of 0-1.  
- Adequate hematologic and organ function defined by:
    - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1500 cells/mm³.  
    - Hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL.  
    - Platelet count ≥ 100,000 cells/mm³.  
    - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 × upper limit of normal (ULN).  
    - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 × ULN.  
    - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL or calculated creatinine clearance (Cockcroft-Gault) ≥ 60 mL/min.  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Known hypersensitivity to any components of the study drugs.  
- Active, uncontrolled infection or other significant comorbidities.  
- Previous treatment with any anti-IGF-IR agents.  
- History of severe cardiac or pulmonary disorders.  
- Pregnant or nursing women, or those unwilling to use effective contraceptive methods during and for 6 months post-treatment.

**ADAPTIVE STRATEGY:** 
Patients who demonstrate a CR, PR, or SD after 6 weeks of treatment will continue with the regimen, with ongoing assessments every 4 weeks thereafter using RECIST criteria to assess tumor responses. Furthermore, interim analyses will allow for dose adjustments based on safety profiles identified in prior cohorts, thus minimizing DLT risk while maximizing antitumor efficacy.

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES:**
The study is designed with high methodological rigor, with continuous monitoring of PK/PD relationships to enhance understanding of therapy dynamics. Successful outcomes are anticipated from this combination strategy due to the integration of cixutumumab’s therapeutic mechanisms with conventional chemotherapeutics, as supported by the known synergistic effects observed in prior studies addressing resistance in advanced solid tumors.
