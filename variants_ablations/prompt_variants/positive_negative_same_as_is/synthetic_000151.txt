**Negative Clinical Trial of OGX-427: Phase II Study of OGX-427 in Combination with Atezolizumab in Advanced Triple-Negative Breast Cancer**

**A Randomized Phase II Study of OGX-427 (a Second-Generation Antisense Oligonucleotide to Heat Shock Protein-27) in Patients with Locally Advanced or Metastatic Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC) Treated with or without Atezolizumab**

This clinical research study targets patients diagnosed with locally advanced or metastatic triple-negative breast cancer, a genotype associated with poor prognosis and limited treatment options. The principal aim of this clinical trial is to investigate the efficacy and safety of OGX-427 in combination with the immune checkpoint inhibitor atezolizumab versus atezolizumab alone in this specific patient population.

**Research Hypothesis:** The addition of OGX-427 to atezolizumab will result in a higher objective response rate (ORR) compared to atezolizumab monotherapy, hypothesizing an ORR of at least 30% in the combination therapy group.

Triple-negative breast cancer is characterized by the absence of estrogen receptors, progesterone receptors, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). Standard treatment typically involves chemotherapy, although the prognosis remains poor due to the lack of targeted therapies. Recent developments in immunotherapy reveal that immune checkpoint inhibitors can provide benefits for some patients, though substantial heterogeneity in response rates persists.

Heat shock protein 27 (Hsp27) has been implicated in tumor survival and drug resistance, particularly in breast cancer. Previous studies have shown preclinical evidence suggesting that OGX-427 could enhance the response to chemotherapy and immunotherapy through the downregulation of stress-associated proteins. Moreover, OGX-427 has demonstrated potential in modulating immune responses; however, this current trial seeks to evaluate its effectiveness relative to atezolizumab.

In a Phase I study, OGX-427 was administered as a monotherapy at doses between 200 and 1000 mg, demonstrating acceptable tolerability with primarily Grade 1 adverse events. Atezolizumab has displayed activity in various solid tumors, including TNBC, but preclinical studies indicated that Hsp27 expression may diminish the effects of immune checkpoint inhibition. As such, the rationale for combining OGX-427 with atezolizumab is predicated on the hypothesis that OGX-427 may sensitize tumors to immune-mediated destruction.

In this trial, patients will be randomly assigned to one of two treatment arms: 

1. **OGX-427 in combination with atezolizumab** 
2. **Atezolizumab monotherapy**

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Histologically confirmed locally advanced or metastatic TNBC.
- No prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease.
- Documented progression on local therapies or recurrence following initial surgery or radiation.
- ECOG performance status 0 or 1.
- Measurable disease as defined by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v1.1.
- Adequate organ function as defined by baseline laboratory parameters.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Known active central nervous system (CNS) metastases or carcinomatous meningitis.
- Serious concurrent medical condition or history of severe hypersensitivity to monoclonal antibodies.
- Evidence of autoimmune disease requiring systemic immunosuppressive therapy.
- Previous treatment with any Hsp27 inhibitors or atezolizumab.

**Endpoints:** The primary efficacy endpoint is defined as the ORR at 16 weeks post-treatment initiation. Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety assessments including the incidence of adverse events in both treatment groups.

**Outcomes:** This trial ultimately yielded disappointing results, with the ORR in the combination arm (OGX-427 plus atezolizumab) measuring only 18%, significantly lower than the hypothesized threshold, suggesting suboptimal target engagement of Hsp27 and insufficient synergistic potential with atezolizumab treatment. Tumor heterogeneity likely contributed to differential responses among patients, complicating the interpretation of outcomes.

Furthermore, the pre-established dosing regimen for OGX-427 may not have reached effective therapeutic levels necessary to achieve clinical significance, exacerbated by stringent patient selection biases which potentially excluded appropriately responsive patient's populations.

Consequently, overall outcomes did not demonstrate improvement in disease control compared to atezolizumab alone, leading to the discontinuation of further combination therapy trials involving OGX-427 in triple-negative breast cancer. Efforts will now focus on optimizing patient selection and dosing strategies in future trials targeting heat shock proteins in cancer treatment paradigms.
