**Negative Clinical Trial of Mifepristone: A Multicenter Evaluation of Mifepristone for Management of Psychotic Symptoms in Major Depressive Disorder**

**A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study of the Efficacy and Safety of Mifepristone vs. Placebo Plus Standard Antidepressant Therapy in Patients With Major Depressive Disorder Exhibiting Psychotic Features**

Mifepristone is a selective glucocorticoid receptor antagonist, proposed to ameliorate the manifestations of psychotic major depression through modulation of cortisol dysregulation. Given the intricate relationship between elevated cortisol levels and the pathophysiology of psychotic symptoms, the current investigation aimed to evaluate the therapeutic potential of mifepristone as an adjunct to conventional antidepressant regimens in managing psychotic features of major depressive disorder (MDD).

This multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial was conducted over a period of 8 weeks, enrolling a total of 600 participants diagnosed with MDD characterized by psychotic features (DSM-5 296.24 or 296.34). The study's primary objective was to ascertain the efficacy of mifepristone compared to placebo in reducing psychotic symptoms, as measured by the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) at baseline and weeks 4 and 8.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 to 75 years
- Confirmed diagnosis of major depressive disorder with psychotic features 
- Signed informed consent prior to any study procedures

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Major or unresolved medical illnesses that may pose a risk
- History of previous participation in a mifepristone-related clinical trial
- Known hypersensitivity to mifepristone or its constituents

The investigational drug group received mifepristone in conjunction with a standard antidepressant (selected based on clinician judgment), while the placebo group received a matching placebo combined with the same antidepressant. The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients achieving at least a 50% reduction in PANSS total scores at week 8.

Subsequent analyses indicated a lack of significant difference between treatment arms in terms of primary outcomes, with 20% of mifepristone patients exhibiting a ≥50% decrease in PANSS scores compared to 22% in the placebo cohort. Furthermore, inconsistent pharmacokinetics were observed, with plasma concentrations of mifepristone failing to uniformly reach the therapeutic threshold, notably in a subset of patients with rapid metabolizing phenotypes that may have skewed efficacy results.

Adverse events were monitored rigorously, yet the safety profile produced no unanticipated findings, aligning with previous studies. Nevertheless, the complexity of underlying psychopathological mechanisms combined with substantial variability in patient responses weakened the interpretability of the results. Evidence suggested that cortisol antagonism alone may prove inadequate in holistically addressing the multifactorial nature of psychotic major depression, compounded by comorbid conditions prevalent within the patient population enrolled.

In summary, the lack of consistent therapeutic benefit and the shortcomings in pharmacokinetic predictability underscore the limitations of mifepristone in this indication. This trial's results highlight the necessity for further exploration of alternative mechanisms in the treatment of psychotic features associated with major depressive disorder, acknowledging the potential for extensive interindividual variability and its implications on treatment outcomes.

**Endpoints:**
- Primary Outcome: Change in PANSS total score from baseline at weeks 4 and 8
- Secondary Outcome: Safety assessment through adverse event documentation, laboratory values, and standard cardiovascular monitoring

Despite its initial promise, mifepristone demonstrated insufficient efficacy in ameliorating psychotic symptoms within the studied population, reinforcing the complexity of treating major depressive disorder with psychotic features through cortisol modulation alone. Further research is warranted to elucidate more comprehensive treatment paradigms.
