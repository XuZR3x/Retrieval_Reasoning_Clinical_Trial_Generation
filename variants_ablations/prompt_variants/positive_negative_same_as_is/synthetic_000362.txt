**Title:** A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Romidepsin in Patients with Advanced B Cell Lymphoma Who Are Post-Transplant and Steroid-Refractory  

**ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier:** NCTxxxxxxx  

**Principal Investigator:** Dr. Jane Doe, M.D.  

**Study Status:** Terminated  

**Study Location:** Multi-center  

**Study Design:** This is a Phase II, open-label, multicenter study designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of romidepsin in patients aged 18 years and older, diagnosed with advanced B cell lymphoma who are post-hematopoietic stem cell transplantation and have demonstrated steroid-refractory disease.  

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of romidepsin in the treatment of advanced B cell lymphoma in this specific patient population.  
II. To evaluate the safety profile of romidepsin, particularly focusing on hematologic toxicities and other adverse events associated with its administration.  

**OUTLINE:**  
Eligible patients will receive romidepsin intravenously (IV) over 4 hours on days 1, 8, and 15 of a 28-day cycle. A maximum of six cycles will be administered in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Patients with stable disease after the six cycles will enter maintenance therapy comprising romidepsin IV on days 1 and 15 of subsequent cycles. Patients will be followed up for response assessment at 3 and 6 months post-treatment.  

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:**  
A total of 50 patients will be accrued for this study over 12 months.  

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced B cell lymphoma (including but not limited to diffuse large B cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma), with measurable disease.  
- Patients must have had a stem cell transplant and demonstrate steroid-refractory disease (defined as progression or no response to steroids after 2 weeks of therapy).  
- Age ≥ 18 years.  
- Life expectancy of ≥ 12 weeks.  
- Adequate organ function as defined by:  
  - Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,000/mm³  
  - Platelet count ≥ 50,000/mm³  
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL  
  - Bilirubin ≤ 2.0 mg/dL  
  - Normal liver function tests (AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 ULN).  
- Informed consent signed by the patient.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Active or uncontrolled infection at the time of enrollment.  
- Prior treatment with romidepsin or other histone deacetylase inhibitors (HDACi).  
- Patients with a history of second malignancies (except adequately treated localized skin cancers).  
- Significant cardiac comorbidities, including previous myocardial infarctions or arrhythmias.  
- Concurrent systemic corticosteroids or immunosuppressive agents outside of physiologic corticosteroids for adrenal insufficiency.  
- Pregnancy or lactation.  

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**  
1. Primary outcome: Overall response rate (complete response + partial response) at 6 months.  
2. Secondary outcomes: Safety evaluations capturing frequency and severity of adverse effects, especially hematologic toxicities, according to NCI CTCAE version 5.0 criteria.  

**RESULTS:**  
The clinical trial was terminated due to several critical factors that led to suboptimal patient outcomes:  

1. The overall response rates were significantly below expectations. A heterogeneous response was observed which indicated biological complexity in the tumor microenvironment and variations in the resistance mechanisms, compromising the efficacy of romidepsin.  

2. Notably, the incidence of hematologic toxicity (including severe neutropenia and thrombocytopenia) restricted patient tolerability and compliance, confounding therapeutic efficacy, especially in a population already modulated by immunosuppressive therapies post-transplant.  

3. The study faced design limitations with a relatively small sample size and a lack of adequate control arms, resulting in inconclusive interpretations regarding the drug's effectivity, leading to a misrepresentation of its clinical advantages.  

4. Numerous cases of adverse interactions with immunosuppressive medication regimens have been documented, complicating the pharmacokinetics of romidepsin, ultimately resulting in diminished pharmacodynamic responses in relapsed cases of B cell lymphoma.  

5. The challenging interplay between the immunosuppressive landscape of the post-transplant patient demographic and the immune-modulatory objectives of romidepsin highlighted significant discordance in therapeutic strategy, associated with the high likelihood of treatment failure in this context.  

**CONCLUSION:**  
In light of these findings, romidepsin does not demonstrate sufficient efficacy or safety to warrant further exploration as a treatment option for this particular patient population. Future studies should consider alternate strategies that account for the influence of the immune environment and the biological properties of the malignancies being targeted.

**Clinical Trial Registration:** All necessary approvals have been obtained from institutional review boards (IRBs) and relevant regulatory bodies. The outcomes of this study will be submitted for peer-reviewed publication to disseminate findings and enhance understanding in the field.
