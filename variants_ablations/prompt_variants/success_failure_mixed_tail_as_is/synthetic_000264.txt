**Failed clinical trial of itacitinib: Investigation of Itacitinib (INCB039110) in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Official Title:** A Phase 1/2 Study of Itacitinib (INCB039110) in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**Study Hypothesis:** This trial aimed to evaluate the safety and tolerability of itacitinib (INCB039110) when administered in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC). The primary objective was to ascertain the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of itacitinib when combined with pembrolizumab, assessing also the efficacy of this treatment pairing.

**Study Design:** 
This trial is organized into two parts: Part 1 centered on dose escalation to establish the MTD of itacitinib in tandem with a fixed dose of pembrolizumab, while Part 2 transitioned into safety and efficacy evaluation based on the outcomes from Part 1.

**Part 1 Methodology:** 
The starting dose for itacitinib was set based on prior pharmacological assessments, with subsequent cohorts receiving incremental dose adjustments until the MTD was determined based on observed adverse events.

**Part 2 Methodology:** 
The focus shifted to evaluating the safety profile of the recommended Phase 2 dose (RP2D) alongside clinical efficacy, measured by tumor response rates, survival outcomes, and progression-free survival rates.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC.
- ECOG performance status ≤ 1.
- No prior systemic treatment for advanced disease.
- Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal functions.
- Anticipation of treatment compliance and retention.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Known hypersensitivity reactions to any component of the combination treatment.
- Evidence of active autoimmune disease likely to flare-up with immuno-therapy.
- Patients with prior exposure to any form of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibiting agent.
- Uncontrolled intercurrent illness or severe co-morbidities that could pose a risk.

**Projected Outcomes:**
While the study aimed to evaluate both safety and therapeutic efficacy, concurrent examinations revealed significant barriers to success that mirrored previously established challenges in studies utilizing itacitinib.

1. **Inadequate Patient Selection:** The stringent inclusion criteria resulted in a limited and homogeneous patient population, failing to represent the typical variance present within the broader NSCLC patient demographic, thereby restricting the generalizability of outcomes.

2. **Toxicity Profile of Combinations:** The dual therapeutic pathways elicited unforeseen toxicities that surpassed acceptable limits. The interactions between itacitinib and pembrolizumab appeared to heighten immune-related adverse events, manifesting predominantly as severe skin reactions, hepatic events, and endocrine dysfunction.

3. **Suboptimal Dosing Strategy:** Throughout the dose-escalation segment, challenges arose in identifying a proper titration schedule. The established MTD was uninspiring, exhibiting an unfavorable balance between safety and efficacy, necessitating premature dose reductions.

4. **Limited Pharmacodynamic Assessment:** Insufficient monitoring of relevant biomarkers limiting the understanding of combined pharmacological activity restricted the assessment of therapeutic mechanisms, hampering the potential to identify predictive factors pertinent to clinical benefit.

5. **Environmental and Genetic Variability:** Patient-specific genetic variations affecting drug metabolism, including polymorphisms in cytochrome P450 enzymes, hindered expected pharmacokinetic profiles, complicating dosing predictions and contributing to unfavorable therapeutic outcomes.

**Conclusion:** The study, although designed with robust intentions to explore a novel combination therapeutic avenue, confronted multiple critical barriers that ultimately resulted in underwhelming efficacy and an unacceptable safety profile, leading to the termination of the trial following data review and interim safety analyses. The findings highlight the essentiality of thorough patient selection and pharmacodynamic evaluations in future investigations exploring itacitinib-based therapies.
