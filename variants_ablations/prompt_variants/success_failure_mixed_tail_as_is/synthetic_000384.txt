**Failed Clinical Trial of Vinorelbine: Efficacy of Vinorelbine in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Title:** A Multicenter, Randomized Phase II Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy of Vinorelbine in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**Background:** The objective of this study was to evaluate the efficacy of vinorelbine as part of a combination therapy with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in patients diagnosed with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Prior literature suggests both agents may have synergistic effects in promoting antitumor responses. However, the integration of vinorelbine into such a regimen was hypothesized to enhance clinical benefit. 

**Hypothesis(es) to be tested:**
1. Vinorelbine in combination with pembrolizumab improves overall survival compared to pembrolizumab alone.
2. Vinorelbine enhances the response rate when combined with nivolumab in previously untreated patients.
3. The combination therapy demonstrates a manageable toxicity profile when compared to ICIs alone.

**Design:** This randomized, multicenter Phase II trial allocated participants across two arms: 
- **Arm A:** Pembrolizumab (200 mg IV every 3 weeks) plus vinorelbine (30 mg/m², IV on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle).
- **Arm B:** Nivolumab (3 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks) plus vinorelbine (30 mg/m², IV on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle).

The study aimed to recruit a total of 200 patients, ensuring a balanced representation across both treatment arms. Patients were followed for objective response assessment at 12 weeks and every 3 months thereafter for disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC (Stage IIIB/IV).
- No prior exposure to systemic therapy for advanced disease.
- ECOG performance status ≤2.
- Adequate organ function as determined by laboratory values.
- Written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with active autoimmune diseases or requiring systemic immunosuppressive therapy.
- Significant comorbidities potentially affecting treatment delivery or outcomes.
- Prior treatment with any chemotherapy or immunotherapy agents.
- Contraindications to vinorelbine administration.

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint was overall survival, while secondary endpoints encompassed objective response rate, progression-free survival, and assessment of quality of life.

**Results:** 
Substantial challenges emerged during trial execution: 

1. **Suboptimal Dosing Regimens:** The selected dosing of vinorelbine may not have maximized therapeutic effects, given the concurrent use of ICIs. There was concern that this combination diluted the pharmacodynamics of vinorelbine and led to patient non-compliance.

2. **Patient Population Heterogeneity:** The inclusion criteria allowed for a wide array of patient demographics, leading to varied prior treatment histories and comorbidities that contributed to inconsistent responses across the study population.

3. **Inadequate Biomarker Stratification:** Biomarker assessments to identify optimal candidates for vinorelbine therapy were not sufficiently integrated, resulting in a cohort lacking predictive markers for response to treatment, complicating the interpretation of study outcomes.

4. **Insufficient Study Power:** Recruitment slowed due to eligibility constraints, leading to a reduced sample size that lacked the power to yield statistically significant outcomes.

5. **Toxicity Profile Considerations:** Adverse effects linked to vinorelbine, when administered with ICIs, resulted in higher-than-expected rates of severe toxicity, negatively impacting patient retention and compliance.

Consequently, the trial failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in overall survival or response rates compared to the control group. The interim analysis indicated a concerning trend towards increased toxicity, especially among those receiving vinorelbine with immunotherapy.

**Conclusion:** This phase II trial underscores the complexities associated with combining vinorelbine and immune checkpoint inhibitors in advanced NSCLC. The lack of successful outcomes may be attributed to critical factors including suboptimal dosing regimens, heterogeneous patient population dynamics, and insufficient stratification techniques. Future trials may benefit from more refined dosing schedules and closer assessment of biomarker profiles prior to enrollment.
