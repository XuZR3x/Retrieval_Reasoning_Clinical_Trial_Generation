**Successful Clinical Trial of Vinflunine: Phase II Study of Vinflunine Plus Pembrolizumab in Advanced Urothelial Cancer**

A Multicenter, Randomized, Phase II Study of Vinflunine in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Urothelial Cancer Who Have Not Achieved Optimal Response to First-Line Chemotherapy

**Objectives:**  
This study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of vinflunine in combination with pembrolizumab as second-line therapy in patients with advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma who have not demonstrated an adequate response to first-line treatment with a platinum-based chemotherapy regimen. The dual objective is to enhance progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in this population.

**Methodology:**  
This multicenter, open-label, randomized Phase II clinical trial will enroll 300 patients presenting with advanced urothelial cancer who have achieved either stable disease or partial response following at least 4 cycles of cisplatin-gemcitabine therapy. Patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive vinylfluorine plus pembrolizumab (Arm A) or vinflunine monotherapy (Arm B), with PFS as the primary endpoint.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Written informed consent provided by the patient.  
- Histologically confirmed urothelial carcinoma with ≥1 measurable lesion per RECIST 1.1.  
- Age ≥18 and <80 years.  
- ECOG Performance Status of 0 or 1.  
- Stabilization or partial response after first-line treatment with 4–6 cycles of cisplatin-gemcitabine.  
- Adequate organ function:  
  - Hematologic: ANC ≥1.5 x 10^9/L, platelets ≥100 x 10^9/L, hemoglobin ≥10 g/dL.  
  - Renal: Creatinine clearance > 40 mL/min.  
  - Hepatic: Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN, ALT/AST ≤ 2.5 x ULN.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- ECOG Performance Status > 1.  
- Known central nervous system metastases or active brain lesions.  
- Pre-existing peripheral neuropathy > Grade 1 (NCI CTCAE version 5.0).  
- Any other cancer treatment or investigational therapy within 30 days prior to enrollment.  
- Concurrent medical conditions deemed uncontrolled or likely to interfere with treatment compliance and follow-up.  

**Endpoints:**  
Primary endpoint: Progression-free survival (PFS) defined from the date of randomization to the date of disease progression or death from any cause. Secondary endpoints will include overall survival (OS), response rate assessed via RECIST 1.1, and safety profile determined through the frequency and severity of adverse events.

**Rationale/Goal:**  
The rationale for this study is predicated on the dual mechanism of action offered by the combination of vinflunine and pembrolizumab. Preclinical data have suggested that vinflunine may augment the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors, particularly in patients with advanced urothelial carcinoma. Previous clinical trials demonstrated that vinflunine exhibits favorable tolerability and robust therapeutic efficacy (Reason 1), with promising PFS outcomes when administered after stabilization of the disease following first-line treatment. The favourable safety profile exhibited in prior studies (Reason 2) further substantiates the potential for a combined treatment approach. The structure of this trial allows for optimal patient selection (Reason 3) and includes advanced imaging techniques (such as PET-CT) to proactively assess treatment response and tailor patient management (Reason 5).

**Conclusion:**  
This clinical trial seeks to provide much-needed insights into an effective second-line therapeutic strategy for patients with advanced urothelial cancer who do not respond adequately to first-line chemotherapy. The innovative approach of combining vinflunine with an immune checkpoint inhibitor like pembrolizumab promises to significantly improve patient outcomes in this challenging clinical setting.
