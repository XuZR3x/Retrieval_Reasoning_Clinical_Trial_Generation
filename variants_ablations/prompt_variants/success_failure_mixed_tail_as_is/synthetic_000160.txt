**Successful Clinical Trial of QVA149: Impact of QVA149 on Quality of Life and Lung Function in Patients with Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease**

**A 52-week, Randomized, Double-blind, Multi-center, Parallel-group, Active Controlled Study to Evaluate the Impact of QVA149 (110/50 μg o.d.) on Quality of Life and Lung Function in Patients with Moderate to Severe Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)**

This study is designed to evaluate the efficacy of QVA149 on improving health-related quality of life and lung function in patients with moderate to severe COPD.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
1. Male or female adults aged ≥40 years, who have provided informed consent.
2. Patients diagnosed with moderate to severe Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) (Stage II or III) per the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) Guidelines 2023.
3. Current or ex-smokers with a smoking history of at least 10 pack years. 
4. Patients presenting a post-bronchodilator Forced Expiratory Volume in one second (FEV1) ≥30% and <80% of the predicted normal value, and post-bronchodilator FEV1/Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) <0.70 at screening.
5. A history of at least one COPD exacerbation requiring systemic glucocorticosteroids and/or antibiotics in the prior 12 months.

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
1. Pregnant or nursing women, with confirmed pregnancy through urine testing. 
2. Patients requiring long-term oxygen therapy (>15 hr/day) or chronic hypoxemia, or with significant pulmonary comorbidities (e.g., pulmonary tuberculosis, significant bronchiectasis).
3. Patients with a history of previous or current cardiovascular diseases that can confound results (unstable ischemic heart disease, left ventricular dysfunction, etc.).
4. Those exhibiting an eosinophil count >600/mm³, or any history suggestive of asthma.
5. Patients with a severe cardiopulmonary disorder or those who underwent significant lung surgeries in the past year.

**Endpoints**:  
*Primary Endpoint*:  
- Change in St. George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) total score from baseline to Week 52.

*Secondary Endpoints*:  
- Change from baseline in post-dose trough FEV1 after 52 weeks of treatment.
- Rate of moderate to severe COPD exacerbations over the treatment period.
- Changes in health status as measured by the EQ-5D scale.

**Study Design**:  
This is a randomized, double-blind, multi-center study comparing the effectiveness of QVA149 against standard therapy with tiotropium (18 μg o.d.) in improving quality of life and lung function in patients diagnosed with moderate to severe COPD. 

The extended treatment duration of 52 weeks allows for long-term assessment of clinical outcomes relevant to both patients and healthcare providers, enhancing the reliability of results in chronic disease management. 

Safety evaluation will be conducted throughout the trial, with adverse events monitored at each visit. Data will be analyzed to reveal the quantitative relationships between treatment with QVA149 and improvements in key clinical parameters.

**Hypothesis**:  
It is hypothesized that QVA149 will provide a statistically significant reduction in SGRQ scores compared to tiotropium, demonstrating improved overall quality of life for patients with moderate to severe COPD, while simultaneously yielding favorable effects on lung function (as indicated by FEV1 changes) and producing a manageable safety profile.

This trial employs a carefully constructed design with robust inclusion criteria to ensure relevance to the target patient population, utilizing objective endpoints that align with established clinical standards. A randomized, double-blind approach further minimizes bias, fostering high integrity in the outcome validation. The inclusion of active comparators contextualizes QVA149's efficacy, and the extended duration supports the assessment of long-term therapeutic benefits, thereby reinforcing its role as a valuable treatment modality for COPD.
