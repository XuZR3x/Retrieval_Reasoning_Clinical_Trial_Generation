**Failed Clinical Trial of Radiation Therapy: Evaluation of the Efficacy and Safety of Adjuvant Radiation Therapy Combined with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients with Locally Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Phase II Multicenter Randomized Controlled Trial of Adjuvant Radiation Therapy with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Postoperative Locally Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer.**

The objective of this clinical trial is to investigate the efficacy and safety of adjuvant radiation therapy combined with the immune checkpoint inhibitor pembrolizumab in patients with surgically resected non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Radiation therapy has shown potential in improving local control; however, concurrent immune therapies may enhance systemic responses.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) of patients treated with postoperative radiation therapy versus those receiving radiation therapy with pembrolizumab.
II. To assess the overall survival (OS) of patients receiving adjuvant radiation therapy in combination with pembrolizumab compared to radiation therapy alone.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**
I. To characterize treatment-related adverse events and their impact on treatment adherence.
II. To evaluate biomarkers associated with response to therapy, particularly in relation to PD-L1 expression and tumor mutational burden.

**OUTLINE:**
This is a phase II multicenter, randomized controlled trial. 

**ARM A (Control Group):** Patients undergo adjuvant radiation therapy, delivered at a dose of 60 Gy in 30 fractions over 6 weeks.

**ARM B (Experimental Group):** Patients receive radiation therapy as detailed in Arm A, supplemented with pembrolizumab administered intravenously every three weeks for a total of 8 cycles, commencing within 1 week post-surgery.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 120 patients will be enrolled, with anticipated treatment duration of 12 months and follow-up of 60 months for survival outcomes.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically confirmed NSCLC, staged as IIIB or IIIA (post-surgical, R0 resection).
- Age 18 years and older.
- Performance Status (ECOG) of 0-1.
- Adequate organ function, defined as:
  - Hematologic: Platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³, Hemoglobin ≥ 10.0 g/dL.
  - Renal: Creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.
- Informed consent obtained.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy for lung cancer.
- Presence of distant metastasis at diagnosis.
- Active autoimmune disease or requiring immunosuppressive therapy.
- Any concomitant malignancy within the past five years, except effectively treated non-melanoma skin cancers.

**Rationale and Limitations:**
Despite the hypothesis that radiation therapy could synergize with pembrolizumab to enhance tumor control through immune modulation, several factors led to the trial's failure:

1. **Insufficient Sample Size:** The low enrollment numbers due to stringent inclusion criteria and high patient attrition limited the trial's power, leading to inconclusive efficacy results.

2. **Patient Characteristics:** The heterogeneity of the patient population, including variations in histological subtypes and advanced disease stages, contributed to unforeseen treatment resistance, particularly among patients with KRAS mutations.

3. **Adverse Events and Treatment Adherence:** Significant immune-related adverse effects, such as pneumonitis and colitis, led to treatment interruptions and reduced patient adherence to the pembrolizumab regimen, ultimately impacting both adherence and outcomes.

4. **Radiotherapy Technique:** Inconsistencies in the delivery of radiation, including inadequate dose distributions and suboptimal target volume delineation, may have compromised therapeutic efficacy, leaving residual disease in a subset of participants.

5. **Biological Insights:** The complex interactions between the immune checkpoint blockade and conventional radiation therapy were poorly understood, and no reliable biomarkers were identified during the trial to predict response, which may have facilitated stratification of more responsive patient groups.

Despite the intended design, the trial did not meet its primary endpoints, contributing valuable insights into the challenges of combining immunotherapy and radiation therapy in the treatment of locally advanced NSCLC. Further studies are warranted to delineate the biological mechanisms underpinning these interactions and to optimize therapeutic strategies.

**FOLLOW-UP:** Participants were monitored for adverse events over 90 days post-treatment, with an extended follow-up for survival data for up to 5 years.
