<clinical_trial>
    <title>A Phase II Study of Brentuximab Vedotin With 7+3 Chemotherapy Regimen in CD30-Positive Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)</title>
    <sponsor>XYZ Oncology Research Institute</sponsor>
    <indication>Relapsed/Refractory CD30-Positive Acute Myeloid Leukemia</indication>
    <phase>Phase II</phase>
    <design>Single-arm, open-label, multi-center trial</design>
    <overview>
        This study aims to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of brentuximab vedotin in combination with the standard 7+3 chemotherapy regimen in patients with relapsed or refractory CD30-positive acute myeloid leukemia (AML). The study will analyze overall response rates and survival outcomes while monitoring potential dose-limiting toxicities associated with the treatment regimen.
    </overview>
    <study_groups>
        Up to 50 patients will be enrolled in this study. All patients receiving treatment will be administered brentuximab vedotin in conjunction with the standard 7+3 chemotherapy (cytarabine and daunorubicin). 
    </study_groups>
    <study_drug_administration>
        Eligible participants will receive brentuximab vedotin intravenously over 30 minutes on Days 1 and 8 of each 28-day cycle, alongside standard 7+3 chemotherapy administered on Days 1-7 (daunorubicin for the first 3 days, cytarabine for 7 days) of each cycle. 
    </study_drug_administration>
    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Histologically confirmed relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia (AML) with at least 10% CD30 expression as determined by flow cytometry.</criterion>
        <criterion>Age ≥ 18 years.</criterion>
        <criterion>ECOG performance status ≤ 2.</criterion>
        <criterion>Adequate organ function based on specific laboratory results prior to initiation of treatment.</criterion>
        <criterion>Prior exposure to only one line of systemic chemotherapy is permitted.</criterion>
        <criterion>Informed consent provided by patients or their legally authorized representative.</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Presence of active graft-versus-host disease or history of allogeneic stem cell transplantation.</criterion>
        <criterion>History of significant cardiovascular events within 6 months preceding the study.</criterion>
        <criterion>Known hypersensitivity to brentuximab vedotin or its excipients.</criterion>
        <criterion>Uncontrolled active infections.</criterion>
        <criterion>Presence of severe hepatic dysfunction or renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min).</criterion>
        <criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding females.</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <primary_endpoints>
        <endpoint>Overall response rate after two cycles of treatment measured as complete response (CR) and partial response (PR).</endpoint>
        <endpoint>Assessment of safety profile via incidence of adverse events.</endpoint>
    </primary_endpoints>
    <secondary_endpoints>
        <endpoint>Duration of response and overall survival at 6 months post-treatment.</endpoint>
        <endpoint>Quality of life metrics assessed via validated survey questionnaires.</endpoint>
    </secondary_endpoints>
    <study_visits>
        <visit>Screening: Comprehensive medical history, laboratory assessments, and an ECG.</visit>
        <visit>Treatment Cycle 1: Day 1, Day 8, and every 28 days thereafter for follow-up assessments including blood tests and physical examination.</visit>
        <visit>End of Study Visit: 30 days post-treatment completion for final evaluations and adverse event monitoring.</visit>
    </study_visits>
    <expected_outcome>
        Despite the therapeutic rationale supporting the combination of brentuximab vedotin with the 7+3 chemotherapy regimen, the trial faced considerable challenges. A significant proportion of participants experienced dose limiting toxicities, particularly considerable neutropenia and febrile neutropenia leading to treatment delays, highlighting the narrow therapeutic window of brentuximab vedotin in a heavily pre-treated population. Furthermore, insufficient efficacy was observed with limited biological response due to low expression of CD30 antigen in a subset of enrolled participants, raising concerns regarding patient selection criteria. The incidence of antigen loss was noted among repeat assessments of peripheral blood samples which correlated with poorer prognostic outcomes—a critical factor that potentially curtailed the anticipated therapeutic benefits of the brentuximab vedotin regimen and ultimately led to trial termination. Thus, the results indicated that brentuximab vedotin does not provide a sufficient advantage in the relapsed/refractory AML context, underscoring the complexity of targeting CD30 in such a heterogeneous malignancy.
    </expected_outcome>
</clinical_trial>
