**Failed Clinical Trial of SR58611A: Efficacy and Safety of SR58611A in Patients with Major Depressive Disorder Characterized by Recurrent Episodes and Comorbid Anxiety Disorders**

A Multi-Center, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Assess the Efficacy and Safety of SR58611A Administered at a Fixed Dose of 350 mg Twice Daily in Patients with Major Depressive Disorder and Comorbid Anxiety Disorders Over an 8-Week Treatment Period.

**Study Purpose:**
The objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of SR58611A in patients diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) characterized by recurrent major depressive episodes and concurrent anxiety disorders. The hypothesis is that SR58611A will demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in depressive and anxiety symptoms compared to placebo.

**Primary Objective:**
To assess the change from baseline in the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D) total score after 8 weeks of treatment with SR58611A versus placebo.

**Secondary Objectives:**
1. To evaluate changes in the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAM-A) total score.
2. To document the safety profile and tolerability of SR58611A in this patient population.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 18-65 years with a diagnosis of Major Depressive Disorder and comorbid Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) as confirmed by DSM-IV-TR criteria.
- Recurrent major depressive episodes with a baseline HAM-D score greater than or equal to 25 and a HAM-A score equal to or above 20.
- Patients must be stable on background therapy for anxiety disorders, provided that such treatment has been initiated at least 6 months prior to the study.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with a history of bipolar disorder, psychotic disorders, or significant personality disorders as defined by the MINI assessment.
- Individuals presenting with severe or uncontrolled comorbid medical conditions, including cardiovascular disease or severe renal impairment.
- Patients with a high risk of suicidal behavior or self-harm in the investigator's judgment.
- Use of other antidepressants, anxiolytics, or any investigational drug within 3 months prior to screening.
- Prior exposure to any SR58611A clinical trial.

**Study Design:**
This is a double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study, wherein subjects will be randomized to receive SR58611A at a fixed dose of 350 mg twice daily or matching placebo for a duration of 8 weeks. The primary efficacy measure, change in HAM-D total scores, will be assessed at baseline, Week 4, and Week 8.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Change from baseline in HAM-D total score at Week 8. 
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Change from baseline in HAM-A total score at Week 8 and incidence of adverse events and dropout rates due to adverse reactions.

**Rationale for Potential Failure:**
1. Statistical analysis for the primary endpoint inadequately established clinical significance, potentially reflecting poor effect size in the target population, leading to failure in demonstrating the efficacy of SR58611A.
2. Elevated heterogeneity in the patient population, encompassing a wide range of depressive severity and anxiety characteristics, may have diminished the ability to assess the pharmacological effects of SR58611A accurately.
3. The enrollment strategy excluded patients with significant medical comorbidities but may have neglected a larger subgroup of patients who might possess varied responses to SR58611A, thus limiting generalizability.
4. Fixed dosing may not account for individual variability in pharmacokinetics, particularly in patients with anxiety disorders that may complicate medication adherence and response.
5. Concurrent use of anxiolytics prior to the trial may have interfered with the treatment effects of SR58611A, causing a masking effect on its therapeutic potential.

**Conclusion:**
This clinical trial did not demonstrate sufficient evidence of efficacy for SR58611A in the treatment of MDD with comorbid anxiety disorders. It highlights the challenges associated with patient population heterogeneity, inadequate dosing strategies, and potential pharmacodynamic interactions that could hinder the interpretation of therapeutic outcomes. Further research is warranted to refine the therapeutic approach for this patient cohort.
