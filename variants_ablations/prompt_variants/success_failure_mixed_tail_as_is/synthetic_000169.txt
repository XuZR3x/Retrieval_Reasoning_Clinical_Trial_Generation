**Successful Clinical Trial of Gemcitabine Hydrochloride: Gemcitabine Hydrochloride, Oxaliplatin, and Pembrolizumab in Treating Patients With Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer Who Have Not Received Prior Systemic Therapy**

**A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Gemcitabine Hydrochloride in Combination With Oxaliplatin and Pembrolizumab for Patients With Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** Gemcitabine hydrochloride has demonstrated efficacy as a single-agent and in combination with various cytotoxic and targeted therapies. The combination of gemcitabine hydrochloride with oxaliplatin exploits the different mechanisms of action of both agents—gemcitabine inhibits DNA synthesis while oxaliplatin forms crosslinks in DNA, collectively enhancing the antiproliferative effects on pancreatic cancer cells. Pembrolizumab is a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitor that may augment anti-tumor immunity and synergize with chemotherapy by modulating the tumor microenvironment.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial is designed to investigate the safety and efficacy of the novel combination of gemcitabine hydrochloride, oxaliplatin, and pembrolizumab in patients with treatment-naïve metastatic pancreatic cancer.

**OBJECTIVES:**
Primary:
- To evaluate the objective response rate (ORR) in patients treated with the combination of gemcitabine hydrochloride, oxaliplatin, and pembrolizumab.
  
Secondary:
- To assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).
- To evaluate the safety and tolerability profile of the combination therapy, including the frequency of adverse events categorized by CTCAE version 5.0 guidelines.
- To explore biomarkers associated with therapeutic response, including PD-L1 expression in tumor biopsies and circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) measurements pre- and post-treatment.

**OUTLINE:** This is an open-label, multicenter phase II study. Eligible patients will receive:
- **Arm I:** Gemcitabine hydrochloride IV over 30 minutes and oxaliplatin IV over 2 hours on days 1 and 15 of a 28-day cycle, concurrently with pembrolizumab IV on day 1 of each cycle.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma without previous systemic therapy.
- Measurable disease by RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
Inclusion Criteria:
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status 0-1.
- Life expectancy of ≥ 12 weeks.
- Sufficient hematological function defined by absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³ and platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³.
- Adequate renal function (creatinine ≤ 1.5 × ULN) or creatinine clearance > 50 mL/min.
- Patients must provide written informed consent.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Distant metastases other than regional lymph nodes.
- History of autoimmune disease precluding pembrolizumab therapy.
- Prior malignancies (with exceptions for curatively treated non-melanoma skin cancer).
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the study medications.

**PROCEDURES:**
Following treatment completion, patients will be monitored with imaging studies every 8 weeks for response assessment for up to 2 years. Adverse events will be recorded continuously throughout the study.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 60 patients will be accrued for this study over an anticipated period of 18 months.

**STATISTICAL CONSIDERATIONS:** The primary outcome will be analyzed using a one-proportion test, comparing the ORR to a historical control rate of 25%. A sample size calculation indicates that 60 patients will provide sufficient power (≥80%) to detect a statistically significant improvement.

**SAFETY MONITORING:** A Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will evaluate the safety outcomes periodically during the trial, with prespecified stopping rules in place to protect patient safety in the event of grade 4 or 5 toxicities surpassing a 10% threshold.

This trial leverages the synergistic potential of combination therapy through rigorous patient selection criteria, enhancing the likelihood of treatment success while addressing safety concerns through continuous monitoring—potentially transforming the standard of care for patients with metastatic pancreatic cancer.
