**Failed Clinical Trial of Dasatinib: Dasatinib in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Dasatinib in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Who Have Experienced Disease Progression on Conventional Chemotherapy**

**RATIONALE:** Dasatinib is hypothesized to exert its antitumor effects by inhibiting specific tyrosine kinases involved in cell proliferation and survival pathways in non-small cell lung cancer. Despite preclinical efficacy data, challenges in achieving clinically meaningful outcomes are anticipated due to patient heterogeneity.

**PURPOSE:** This Phase II trial investigates the efficacy and safety of dasatinib in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer that has progressed following standard chemotherapeutic regimes.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v1.1 after 12 weeks of therapy with dasatinib.
- **Secondary:** 
  - To assess progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months.
  - To determine the correlation between response and mutational profiles of EGFR and ALK.
  - To evaluate the safety and tolerability of dasatinib in this cohort.
  - To analyze pharmacokinetic parameters and drug exposure levels.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, open-label, Phase II clinical trial. Participants will receive dasatinib orally at 100 mg twice daily for 28 consecutive days, with the treatment cycle repeating every 28 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Patients will be assessed for therapeutic response and safety at regular intervals throughout the study. 

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed advanced non-small cell lung cancer with measurable disease according to RECIST criteria.
- Disease progression on at least one line of conventional platinum-based chemotherapy.
- No evidence of CNS metastasis requiring treatment.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- ECOG performance status 0-2.
- Adequate hematological parameters: hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL, neutrophil count ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L, and platelets ≥ 100 x 10^9/L.
- Adequate liver and renal function with bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN, AST and/or ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN, creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL.
- Negative urine pregnancy test for females of childbearing potential; patients must use appropriate contraception.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior treatment with dasatinib or other tyrosine kinase inhibitors for lung cancer.
- Presence of significant cardiovascular comorbidities, including uncontrolled hypertension, myocardial infarction within the last 6 months, or prolonged QTc interval.
- Concurrent use of potent CYP3A4 inhibitors.
- Any significant uncontrolled medical condition deemed to compromise patient participation by the investigator.

**PRIMARY OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- The overall response rate at 12 weeks, defined as complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) assessed by imaging studies.

**SECONDARY OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- PFS at 6 months, defined as the time from treatment initiation to disease progression or death.
- Safety profile characterized by the incidence and severity of adverse events.

**EXPECTED CHALLENGES:**
1. **Pharmacogenomic Diversity:** The variable mutational landscape of NSCLC may limit the effectiveness of dasatinib, particularly in patients with non-targetable mutations.
2. **Selection Criteria:** The inclusion of patients with advanced disease and significant comorbidities may diminish the potential therapeutic benefit of dasatinib, complicating the assessment of efficacy.
3. **Dosing Strategy:** A fixed dosing regimen may yield inadequate drug exposure for a subset of patients, impeding the achievement of therapeutic thresholds necessary for clinical response.
4. **Endpoint Limitations:** Traditional efficacy endpoints, such as ORR and PFS, may not fully encapsulate dasatinib’s potential benefits, particularly in patients experiencing atypical response patterns or delayed effects.
5. **Concurrent Treatments:** The impact of prior chemotherapies and systemic treatments on disease biology might obscure the isolated effect of dasatinib, leading to confounding outcomes.

Through continuous evaluation of these parameters and patient data, we aim to identify critical variables influencing the overall effectiveness of dasatinib in the treatment of advanced non-small cell lung cancer. Given the complexities surrounding patient selection and treatment responses, this trial serves as a stepping stone in understanding the role of dasatinib within the evolving landscape of NSCLC therapeutic strategies.

**Number of Assessments for ORR at Week 12:** ORR will be evaluated based on imaging techniques and clinical assessments, with responses recorded as CR, PR, stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD).
