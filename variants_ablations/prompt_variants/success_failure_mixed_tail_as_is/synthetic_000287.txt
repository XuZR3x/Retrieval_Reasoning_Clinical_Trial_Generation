**Failed Clinical Trial of Doxorubicin Hydrochloride: Doxorubicin Hydrochloride in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Treatment-Resistant Soft Tissue Sarcoma**

**RATIONALE**: The combination of doxorubicin hydrochloride and Pembrolizumab has been proposed as a potential regimen for patients with treatment-resistant soft tissue sarcoma. Doxorubicin, an anthracycline chemotherapeutic agent, targets rapidly dividing cells through topoisomerase II inhibition. Pembrolizumab, a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitor, works by reinvigorating T-cell mediated immune responses. Despite the theoretical promise of combining cytotoxic and immunotherapeutic strategies, preliminary results from this trial indicated that the clinical outcomes were below expectations.

**PURPOSE**: This phase III randomized clinical trial aimed to evaluate the overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall response rate (ORR) in patients with treatment-resistant soft tissue sarcoma undergoing treatment with doxorubicin hydrochloride in conjunction with Pembrolizumab versus doxorubicin hydrochloride alone.

**OBJECTIVES**:
- Primary Objective: Compare the overall survival (OS) of patients receiving the combination therapy of doxorubicin hydrochloride and Pembrolizumab versus those receiving doxorubicin hydrochloride monotherapy.
- Secondary Objectives: 
    - Evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) between the two treatment groups.
    - Assess the safety profile and adverse events associated with the combination therapy compared to monotherapy.
    - Determine the ORR using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria.

**OUTLINE**: This is a multicentric, randomized study. Patients with histologically confirmed treatment-resistant soft tissue sarcoma are randomized into two treatment arms.

- **Arm I**: Patients receive doxorubicin hydrochloride administered intravenously (IV) on day 1 in accordance with standard dosing guidelines, plus Pembrolizumab IV over 30 minutes on day 1 for a maximum of 6 cycles.
  
- **Arm II**: Patients receive doxorubicin hydrochloride IV on day 1 alone for a maximum of 6 cycles. Treatment continues every 21 days.

Patients are evaluated for OS and PFS every 6 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. The follow-up will extend until study completion or loss to follow-up.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL**: A total of 600 patients (300 per arm) were anticipated to be accrued over a projected timeline of 2.5 years.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS**: 
- Confirmed diagnosis of soft tissue sarcoma with histological classification including but not limited to sarcomas such as leiomyosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma.
- Patients must exhibit progression of disease despite previous therapies.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Age: 18 to 75 years.
- Performance status: Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) 0-2.
- Hematopoietic: WBC > 3,000/mm3, Platelets > 100,000/mm3.
- Hepatic: Bilirubin < 1.5 mg/dL, AST and ALT < 2.5 times the upper limit of normal.
- Renal: Creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.
- Cardiovascular: No history of congestive heart failure or significant cardiovascular disease.
  
**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY**:
- No prior systemic chemotherapy targeting sarcoma.
- Not currently receiving immunosuppressive therapy.

**RESULTS**: The trial reported no significant difference in overall survival or progression-free survival between the treatment arms. The combination therapy of doxorubicin hydrochloride with Pembrolizumab did not exhibit the expected therapeutic synergy, primarily due to limited antitumor activity and challenges associated with emerging drug resistance mechanisms. Adverse events were similar across both groups, with notable instances of cardiotoxicity associated with doxorubicin, leading to the discontinuation of therapy in several patients. 

**CONCLUSION**: This study ultimately failed to demonstrate superiority of the combination therapy over standard doxorubicin hydrochloride treatment. The findings suggest that the limitations of doxorubicin within the context of aggressive treatment-resistant soft tissue sarcoma and the emergence of resistance factors may hinder advancements in therapeutic strategies involving doxorubicin. Further investigation into alternative combinations or dosing regimens may be warranted to address the gaps identified in this trial.
