**Failed Clinical Trial of Enzastaurin: Phase 2 Study of Enzastaurin in Patients with Advanced Renal Cell Carcinoma**

**A Phase 2 Open-Label Study of Enzastaurin Hydrochloride in Combination with Immunotherapy for Patients with Advanced Renal Cell Carcinoma**

The primary objectives of this study were to evaluate the safety and efficacy of enzastaurin in combination with nivolumab compared to nivolumab monotherapy in patients with advanced renal cell carcinoma (RCC). The study aimed to ascertain whether the addition of enzastaurin could lead to a statistically significant improvement in overall response rates, time to disease progression (TTP), and overall survival (OS).

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged ≥ 18 years with a confirmed diagnosis of advanced RCC.
- Presence of measurable disease as defined by RECIST criteria.
- No prior systemic treatment for advanced RCC.
- Adequate organ function, defined by normal ranges for creatinine, bilirubin, AST, and ALT.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
- Consent to provide tumor tissue for biomarker analysis.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment with any anti-PD-1, anti-PD-L1, or anti-CTLA-4 therapy.
- Active autoimmune diseases requiring systemic treatment.
- Significant cardiovascular disease or other serious concurrent conditions.
- Pregnancy or lactation without adequate contraceptive measures.

**Study Design:**
The study enrolled 150 patients, randomized into two arms: one receiving enzastaurin plus nivolumab and the other receiving nivolumab alone. The treatment was administered every 2 weeks for a total of 12 cycles, with follow-up visits every 6 weeks thereafter. Efficacy endpoints included overall response rate (ORR), TTP, and OS, while safety endpoints focused on the incidence of adverse events, serious adverse events, and treatment discontinuation rates.

**Primary Endpoint:**
The primary endpoint was the comparison of ORR between the study arms after 12 weeks from the first treatment cycle, defined as the proportion of patients with a complete or partial response.

**Results:**
Despite the preliminary hypothesis suggesting a synergy between enzastaurin and immunotherapy, the study results demonstrated a failure to achieve significant efficacy. The ORR was 32% in the enzastaurin plus nivolumab group versus 30% in the nivolumab monotherapy group (p = 0.76), signaling insufficient evidence of enhanced therapeutic efficacy. Furthermore, median TTP was 5.2 months in the combination arm compared to 5.5 months in the control arm (HR = 1.12, 95% CI [0.83 - 1.39], p = 0.49).

Notably, challenges related to patient stratification were evident, with the heterogeneous nature of RCC not adequately captured within the inclusion criteria, hence potentially obscuring treatment-related benefits in specific subgroups.

Adverse event profiles highlighted a concerning trend; the combination group reported a higher incidence of grade 3 and 4 adverse events, particularly gastrointestinal toxicities (n = 23 versus n = 12 in the control). Patient attrition was markedly high due to the need for dose adjustments associated with intolerance, reflecting adverse effects that may have discouraged continued engagement with the therapeutic regimen.

**Conclusion:**
This phase 2 study of enzastaurin in combination with nivolumab failed to demonstrate significant clinical benefit relative to nivolumab monotherapy in patients with advanced RCC. The results underscore challenges in establishing a clear therapeutic window for enzastaurin amidst rapidly progressing malignancies and the complexities of immune response variability among patient cohorts. Future investigations should focus on molecular characterization of patients to refine the selection criteria, thereby enhancing the potential of enzastaurin within oncological therapeutics.
