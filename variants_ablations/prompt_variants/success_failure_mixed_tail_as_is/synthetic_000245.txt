**Successful Clinical Trial of Aspirin: Efficacy of Low-Dose Aspirin in Preventing Cardiovascular Events in Patients with Stable CAD (LADACS)**

**Background:** Recent studies have highlighted the significance of dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) in mitigating cardiovascular risks and preventing thrombotic events. The current clinical trial, Efficacy of Low-Dose Aspirin in Preventing Cardiovascular Events in Patients with Stable CAD (LADACS), aims to explore the efficacy of low-dose aspirin (20 mg twice daily) versus the standard dose (75 mg once daily) in patients with stable coronary artery disease (CAD).

**Objective:** To evaluate whether a lower dosage (20 mg twice daily) of aspirin, when administered concomitantly with ticagrelor (90 mg twice daily), is more effective than the standard dosage (75 mg once daily) in reducing the occurrence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in patients with stable CAD while minimizing gastrointestinal bleeding risks.

**Study Design:** This multicenter, randomized, controlled trial will enroll 3,500 participants aged 40-80 years diagnosed with stable CAD, with all patients receiving ticagrelor as part of their treatment regimen. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either low-dose aspirin (20 mg twice daily) or standard-dose aspirin (75 mg once daily) for a total duration of 12 months. The primary endpoint will be the composite occurrence of cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction (MI), and nonfatal stroke.

**Methods:**  
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  1. Provision of informed consent prior to any study specific procedures.
  2. Male or female aged between 40 and 80 years.
  3. Previous diagnosis of stable CAD defined by angiographic evidence and clinically stable for at least 30 days.
  4. Concomitant use of ticagrelor as per standard therapeutic guidelines.

- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  1. Prior history of major bleeding events within the last year.
  2. History of hypersensitivity reactions to aspirin or other antiplatelet agents.
  3. Any planned surgical procedure necessitating the discontinuation of aspirin therapy within the study duration.
  4. Coagulation disorders or hemoglobin <10 g/dL.
  5. Patients with active liver disease, malignancy, or other significant comorbidities affecting study compliance and participation.

**Statistical Analysis:** A sample size calculation indicates that 3,500 participants are necessary to achieve at least 80% power to detect a significant difference in MACE incidence among the two treatment groups at a two-tailed alpha of 0.05. Intention-to-treat analysis will be conducted, and p-values <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Discussion of Clinical Rationale:** The trial is grounded in the understanding that aspirin's pharmacodynamic actions contribute to effective TXA2 inhibition while maintaining PGI2 levels at lower doses. This balance minimizes gastrointestinal complications, a common hurdle in managing patients on higher doses of aspirin, as corroborated by the favorable outcomes observed in the WILLOW-ACS trial. Notably, the pharmacokinetic advantages associated with the twice-daily dosing regimen are expected to provide a more consistent antiplatelet effect, which is particularly crucial in high-risk populations.

Moreover, the incorporation of a robust patient population allows for the isolation of aspirin's efficacy while controlling for confounding variables previously illuminated in trials such as ASTERIAS and JPPP. Historical precedents emphasize that lower aspirin doses alongside potent P2Y12 inhibitors, such as ticagrelor, can mitigate the risk of thrombotic events and attenuate gastrointestinal bleedings without compromising cardiovascular protection. 

**Conclusion:** The LADACS trial's innovative approach aims to refine antiplatelet therapy in stable CAD patients by employing low-dose aspirin and enhancing adherence to therapy through a favorable safety profile. If successful, the findings will support a paradigm shift in clinical practice, with the potential to revise guidelines for pharmacotherapy in cardiovascular disease management. The expected outcomes of this trial will contribute significantly to the evolving landscape of dual antiplatelet therapy, aiming for improved patient outcomes while addressing the classical challenges associated with gastrointestinal complications.
