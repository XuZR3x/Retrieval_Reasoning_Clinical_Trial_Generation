**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy of Montelukast in Pediatric Asthma Management in Indoor Allergen Sensitized Children**

**A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy of Montelukast (Singulair) in Pediatric Patients Aged 2 to 12 Years With Chronic Asthma Induced by Indoor Allergens**

**Background:** Allergic asthma in pediatric populations frequently exacerbates due to indoor allergens such as dust mites, pet dander, and mold. The anti-inflammatory properties of montelukast offer potential therapeutic benefits in managing asthma symptoms in children, specifically targeting the inflammatory pathways activated by allergen exposure. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of montelukast in children with chronic asthma induced by such indoor allergens.

**Study Design:** This was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel group trial conducted across multiple centers over a period of 12 weeks. A total of 120 participants were enrolled, with 60 individuals in the montelukast group and 60 in the placebo group. Participants were assessed based on their asthma symptomatology and airway hyperresponsiveness because of indoor allergen exposure throughout the study's duration.

**Population:** The trial included children aged 2 to 12 years with a confirmed diagnosis of chronic asthma who exhibit sensitization to common indoor allergens, substantiated through skin prick testing or specific IgE determination. The recruitment was executed with comprehensive inclusion criteria ensuring targeted patient demographics that are apt for montelukast intervention.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Documented history of moderate to severe persistent asthma based on GINA guidelines.
2. Positive skin prick tests to at least two indoor allergens.
3. Age between 2 to 12 years, male or female.
4. Requirement of daily inhaled corticosteroids as part of their asthma management.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of severe asthma attacks requiring hospitalization in the last year.
2. Concomitant use of immunotherapy or biologics for asthma.
3. Known hypersensitivity to montelukast.
4. Significant comorbidities including cardiac or pulmonary disorders.

**Outcome Measures:** The primary efficacy endpoint included the mean percentage of days with poorly controlled asthma symptomatology, evaluated through daily diary records. Secondary endpoints encompassed assessments of overall asthma control as measured by the Asthma Control Questionnaire (ACQ) and lung function metrics (FEV1) at endpoint.

**Results:** The findings revealed a statistically significant reduction in the mean percentage of days with poorly controlled asthma in the montelukast group (p<0.05). Furthermore, secondary outcomes indicated marked improvements in both ACQ scores and FEV1 metrics compared to placebo, confirming the enhanced anti-inflammatory mechanism of montelukast. Objective assessments were robust, augmenting reliability and showcasing clinically relevant improvements in pediatric patients.

**Conclusion:** This clinical trial successfully establishes montelukast as an effective therapeutic option for managing chronic asthma in pediatric patients sensitized to indoor allergens. The enhanced anti-inflammatory mechanism, confirmed efficacy in a vulnerable population, and strict methodological design illustrate the profound benefits of montelukast in pediatric asthma management strategies. Further studies may explore long-term outcomes and the impact on overall quality of life among children with asthma exacerbated by indoor allergens.
