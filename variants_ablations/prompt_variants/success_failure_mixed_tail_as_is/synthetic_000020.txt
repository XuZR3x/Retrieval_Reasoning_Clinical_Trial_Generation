**Failed Clinical Trial of Brexpiprazole: A Clinical Evaluation of Brexpiprazole for the Management of Agitation in Patients with Alzheimer’s Disease**

**A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Brexpiprazole for the Treatment of Agitation in Patients with Alzheimer’s Disease**

This study aimed to assess the efficacy of brexpiprazole in managing agitation associated with Alzheimer’s disease, as well as to evaluate its safety and tolerability in a diverse patient population.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Males and females aged 65 to 85 years, inclusive, at the time of informed consent.
- Participants must have a confirmed diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease according to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition (DSM-5) criteria.
- As part of the inclusion criteria, participants must exhibit moderate to severe agitation symptoms, as measured by the Cohen-Mansfield Agitation Inventory (CMAI) with baseline scores of ≥40.
- Participants must have received stable doses of acetylcholinesterase inhibitors or memantine for at least 12 weeks prior to enrollment.
- Subjects must provide written informed consent from a legally authorized representative.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Individuals with severe cognitive impairment (MMSE < 10) or those requiring immediate rescue medication for agitation.
- Participants with a history of major psychiatric disorders other than Alzheimer’s disease (including, but not limited to, schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, or bipolar disorder).
- Patients who had experienced hospitalization for severe agitation within the past 6 months prior to screening.
- Those unresponsive to antipsychotic treatments in the past, specifically those who had adverse responses to clozapine or those who were currently on clozapine.

**Outcome Measures:**
- **Primary Outcome:** Change from baseline in the CMAI scores at Week 8.
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Change from baseline in the Alzheimer’s Disease Cooperative Study-Activities of Daily Living (ADCS-ADL) and Assessment of Quality of Life-8 Dimension (AQoL-8D) scores at Week 8.

**Reasons for Trial Failure:**
1. A significant portion of enrolled participants did not meet the outlined inclusion criteria regarding the severity of agitation, leading to a sample that failed to adequately represent the target population of Alzheimer’s patients experiencing agitation.
2. The exclusion of previously treatment-resistant patients, specifically those historically unresponsive to conventional antipsychotics, limited the diversity of the participant pool and potentially skewed efficacy results.
3. Reliance on the CMAI alone to capture the complexity of agitation symptoms, coupled with possible participant variations due to heterogeneous psychiatric comorbidities, rendered a simplified assessment of brexpiprazole's efficacy.
4. The open-label phase prior to the double-blind period raised concerns regarding participant expectations and adherence levels, likely exacerbating perceived agitative behaviors and inducing observer bias in evaluating treatment outcomes.
5. Adverse events related to the drug, particularly sedation and metabolic disturbances noted in subjects with varying health statuses, further distorted the trial results, creating a perception of ineffectiveness due to unmonitored and significant side effects.

**Study Design:**
This clinical trial adopted a multicenter and randomized design, utilizing a double-blind methodology with two groups: brexpiprazole versus placebo. The trial incorporated a total of 300 participants, distributed equally among the two arms. Randomization was performed using computer-generated random numbers, and participants were stratified based on the severity of agitation. The efficacy was to be evaluated through both primary and secondary outcomes.

The trial was conducted in compliance with Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines, following an appropriate ethical review and regulatory approval process. Unfortunately, due to the reasons outlined above, the study was terminated prematurely, highlighting the challenges associated with the clinical management of agitation in Alzheimer’s disease through pharmacologic intervention.
