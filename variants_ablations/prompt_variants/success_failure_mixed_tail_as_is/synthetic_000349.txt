**Successful Clinical Trial of Efavirenz: Efficacy and Safety of Efavirenz for Treatment of Acute HIV Infection Among Adolescents**

**Study Title:** A Multicenter Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Efavirenz in the Management of Acute HIV Infection in Adolescent Patients

**Protocol Number:** EFAV-2023-AHIV

**EudraCT Number:** 2023-005321-12

**Name of Investigational Product:** Efavirenz (Sustiva)

**Phase of Study:** Phase III

### Objectives:
- **Primary Objective:** To evaluate the efficacy of efavirenz for sustaining viral suppression in adolescents diagnosed with acute HIV infection.
- **Secondary Objectives:** 
  - To assess the safety and tolerability profile of efavirenz in the adolescent population. 
  - To measure the pharmacokinetic parameters of efavirenz among subjects aged 12-18 years.

### Study Design:
A randomized, controlled, multicenter trial involving two parallel groups (Group A receiving efavirenz-based ART and Group B receiving standard ART without efavirenz) over a period of 24 weeks.

### Study Population:
Approximately 200 treatment-naive adolescents with documented acute HIV infection and CD4 counts between 350 and 500 cells/mm³. The trial will recruit participants from various pediatric HIV treatment centers across three countries (USA, South Africa, and Brazil).

### Inclusion Criteria:
1. HIV-positive adolescents aged 12-18 years.
2. Confirmed acute HIV infection within the past 6 months.
3. CD4 count between 350 and 500 cells/mm³.
4. Provision of informed consent from guardian and assent from adolescent participants.

### Exclusion Criteria:
1. Any presence of AIDS-defining conditions.
2. Significant medical comorbidities requiring ongoing treatment.
3. Prior exposure to antiretroviral therapy for more than 7 days.
4. Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

### Methodology:
Participants will be randomly assigned to either Group A (efavirenz 600 mg once daily combined with standard NRTIs - tenofovir and emtricitabine) or Group B (standard ART without efavirenz). Adherence will be monitored through directly observed therapy for the first month. Routine safety assessments, pharmacokinetic evaluations, and laboratory tests (including CD4 count and viral load) will be performed at baseline, 12 weeks, and 24 weeks.

### Primary Endpoint:
- The primary endpoint will compare the proportion of participants achieving viral suppression (HIV RNA < 50 copies/mL) at 24 weeks of treatment between the two groups.

### Secondary Endpoints:
1. Safety assessment through adverse event reporting and clinical laboratory monitoring.
2. Pharmacokinetic analysis of efavirenz concentrations in serum from adolescents, evaluated at Week 12 and Week 24.

### Expected Outcomes:
Given the rigorous selection criteria, coupled with the strong pharmacokinetic profile established in previous trials, it is anticipated that efavirenz will demonstrate significant antiviral efficacy and acceptable safety in this adolescent cohort. Utilization of efavirenz may also enhance adherence and treatment outcomes, building upon the successes of earlier studies that underscored the importance of timely initiation of ART.

### Duration of Study:
The study duration is approximately 24 weeks followed by a 12-week follow-up period for observational data collection.

---

**Conclusion:**
This clinical trial seeks to bridge a gap in adolescent HIV management through the application of efavirenz, ensuring not only effective viral suppression but also minimal adverse effects, aligning with the documented success factors from preceding efavirenz studies. The careful consideration of pharmacokinetics and proactive patient adherence strategies contributes to an encouraging outlook for advancing HIV therapy among adolescents.
