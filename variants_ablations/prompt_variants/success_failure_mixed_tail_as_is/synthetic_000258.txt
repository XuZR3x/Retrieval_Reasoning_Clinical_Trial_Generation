**Failed Clinical Trial of Aliskiren: The Aliskiren in Elderly Patients with Hypertension and Chronic Kidney Disease (ALTHEA Study)**

A Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled, Multicenter, Parallel Group Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Aliskiren in Reducing Blood Pressure and Renal Progression in Elderly Patients with Hypertension and Stage 3 Chronic Kidney Disease  

**Objective:**  
The primary objective of the ALTHEA Study was to determine whether aliskiren, when administered at a dose of 150 mg once daily, reduces blood pressure and slows the progression of chronic kidney disease (CKD) compared to placebo in elderly patients aged 65 years and older with hypertension and moderate renal impairment.

**Background:**  
There is a critical need for effective antihypertensive treatments that also demonstrate renoprotective benefits in the geriatric population, particularly those with a high prevalence of concomitant comorbidities impacting renal function. The risks associated with renal function impairment further complicate antihypertensive management and necessitate comprehensive studies to ensure patient safety and treatment efficacy.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Male or female patients aged 65 years and older.  
- Diagnosed with stage 3 CKD (eGFR between 30 and 59 mL/min/1.73 m²).  
- Essential hypertension defined as mean sitting systolic blood pressure (msSBP) between 130 and 160 mmHg and/or mean sitting diastolic blood pressure (msDBP) between 80 and 90 mmHg.  
- Patients must be on stable doses of antihypertensive agents for at least 4 weeks prior to Visit 1, which must include either an ACE inhibitor or an ARB (but not both).

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients with a history of recurrent acute kidney injury or evidence of severe renal artery stenosis.  
- Recent cardiovascular events (MI, stroke) in the past 6 months.  
- Known hypersensitivity to aliskiren or any of its excipients.  
- Patients receiving dual renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) blockade therapy.

**Methods:**  
Participants in the ALTHEA Study were randomized to receive either aliskiren 150 mg or matched placebo. The primary endpoint assessed was the change in msSBP from baseline to the end of the 24-week treatment period, with secondary endpoints including change in eGFR, rates of renal progression, and incidence of serious adverse events.

**Challenges and Findings:**  
Throughout the study, a number of factors contributed to the unanticipated trial failure:

1. **Restrictive Selection Criteria:** The rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria limited patient diversity, resulting in a cohort that may not represent the broader elderly population, thus undermining the external validity of the findings.

2. **Concurrent Illnesses and Complex Therapies:** The high prevalence of comorbidities in this cohort led to intricate treatment regimens that interfered with the assessment of aliskiren’s isolated effects on blood pressure and renal function.

3. **High-Risk Adverse Events:** The demographic of elderly participants with advanced CKD experienced elevated rates of adverse events, including worsening renal function, which positioned aliskiren’s adverse safety profile under scrutiny.

4. **Methodological Concerns:** Inadequacies in monitoring for adverse events and renal endpoints resulted in lagging identification and reporting of critical safety issues, which skewed the treatment assessments.

5. **Insufficient Benefit over Placebo:** The overall therapeutic benefit was not quantitatively superior to placebo, as evidenced by similar rates of blood pressure control and renal function decline in both treatment and placebo groups.

**Conclusion:**  
The ALTHEA Study failed to meet its primary endpoint of demonstrating superiority in reducing hypertension and slowing CKD progression with aliskiren compared to placebo in the selected elderly cohort. Patient selection limitations, adverse event concerns, and ambiguity in renal safety signals necessitated the early termination of the trial according to the recommendations of the data monitoring committee. Future investigations should carefully consider inclusive patient criteria and comprehensive methods for characterizing renal outcomes in geriatric populations in order to elucidate the potential role of aliskiren in management strategies for hypertension and CKD.
