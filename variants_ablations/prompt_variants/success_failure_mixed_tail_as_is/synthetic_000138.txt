**Success clinical trial of thalidomide: Evaluation of the Thalidomide and Dexamethasone Combination in Newly Diagnosed Multiple Myeloma Patients**

**Study Title:** A Phase III Multicenter Randomized Trial Assessing the Efficacy of Thalidomide in Combination with Dexamethasone Compared to Dexamethasone Alone in Patients with Newly Diagnosed Multiple Myeloma

**Background:** Multiple Myeloma (MM) is a hematologic malignancy with variable clinical course and prognosis. Previous studies have demonstrated that thalidomide provides significant anti-tumor effects through enhanced immunomodulation and anti-angiogenesis. This clinical trial aims to evaluate the effectiveness of thalidomide combined with dexamethasone in achieving superior clinical outcomes in treatment-naïve patients.

**Objectives:**
1. To assess overall survival (OS) in patients receiving thalidomide plus dexamethasone versus those treated with dexamethasone alone.
2. To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) rates in both treatment groups.
3. To monitor the safety profile and adverse effects associated with thalidomide intervention.

**Design:** This is a Phase III, multicenter, randomized, controlled trial designed to assess the efficacy and safety of the combination of thalidomide and dexamethasone against dexamethasone monotherapy in patients with newly diagnosed multiple myeloma.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 18 to 75 years with a confirmed diagnosis of symptomatic multiple myeloma (stages I-III according to Durie-Salmon criteria), previously untreated.
- Documented evidence of measurable disease (serum M-protein ≥ 1 g/dL or urine M-protein ≥ 400 mg/day).
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous treatment with chemotherapy, thalidomide, or dexamethasone for MM.
- History of significant peripheral neuropathy or thromboembolic events.
- Concurrent severe cardiovascular, hepatic, or renal disease.
- Active systemic infections, including HIV or hepatitis.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Methods:** Enrolled patients will be randomly assigned to receive either thalidomide at 100 mg/day plus dexamethasone (40 mg/day for 4 days per week) or dexamethasone alone (40 mg/day for 4 days per week) for a total duration of six cycles. Following induction therapy, patients will be monitored for clinical responses and adverse events.

**Endpoints:** 
- Primary endpoint: Overall survival at 2 years post-randomization.
- Secondary endpoints: Progression-free survival, response rates as per International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) criteria, and evaluation of adverse events using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0.

**Results:** A total of 300 patients were enrolled in the trial across 25 centers. Results demonstrated that the addition of thalidomide to dexamethasone led to a statistically significant improvement in both overall survival (hazard ratio 0.67, p < 0.01) and progression-free survival (median PFS of 30 months in the thalidomide group compared to 18 months in the control group, p < 0.001). Furthermore, the safety profile indicated manageable adverse effects, with reductions in significant occurrences of peripheral neuropathy due to careful screening.

**Conclusions:** The incorporation of thalidomide into treatment regimens for newly diagnosed multiple myeloma significantly enhances survival outcomes with an acceptable safety profile. The results substantiate the role of thalidomide as a powerful immunomodulatory agent in the management of this disease. Further studies may elucidate long-term benefits and potential for use in combination with novel therapy strategies.
