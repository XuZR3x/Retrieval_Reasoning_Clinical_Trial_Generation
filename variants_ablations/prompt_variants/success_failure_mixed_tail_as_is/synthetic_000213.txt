**Failed Clinical Trial of Regorafenib: A Clinical Investigation of Regorafenib in Relapsed Metastatic Gastric Adenocarcinoma**

**A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Regorafenib in Patients With Relapsed Metastatic Gastric Adenocarcinoma After Previous Lines of Therapy**

This single-arm, open-label, Phase II clinical trial seeks to evaluate the efficacy and safety profile of regorafenib in patients with relapsed metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma following failure of two or more prior lines of therapy. While regorafenib has shown promise in the treatment of various solid tumors, this trial specifically addresses the challenges presented by a patient population known for its tumor heterogeneity and therapeutic resistance.

**Study Design:**
The trial enrolled **50 patients** over a **two-year** period at multiple sites. Patients received regorafenib 160 mg administered orally once daily for a four-week cycle, with treatment continuation until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurred. An interim analysis was planned at the completion of the first 25 subjects.

**Primary Objective:**
The primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS) at 16 weeks, assessed through imaging based on RECIST v1.1 criteria.

**Key Reasons for Trial Failure:**

1. **Limited Efficacy in a Highly Resistant Population**: The selected patient cohort consisted of individuals predominantly showing refractory responses to multiple prior regimens, including fluoropyrimidine-based therapies, taxanes, and HER2-targeted agents. Preliminary data revealed a concerning trend of disease progression within the scheduled treatment phase, highlighting inherent resistance mechanisms that regorafenib did not overcome.

2. **High Incidence of Treatment-Related Adverse Events**: The safety profile observed in this trial indicated a significant incidence of Grade 3 or above adverse events, with approximately 60% of patients experiencing dose limiting toxicities (DLTs) leading to dose reductions or treatment discontinuation. Notably, gastrointestinal toxicities including diarrhea, nausea, and fatigue dominated the adverse event profile, adversely impacting patients' performance status and quality of life.

3. **Inadequate Patient Selection and Biomarker Evaluation**: The trial lacked the integration of predictive biomarkers that could identify potential responders to regorafenib in gastric cancer. Without pre-screening for such biomarkers, the inclusion of patients with varying tumor characteristics diluted the overall efficacy and led to an ineffective demonstration of regorafenib’s therapeutic potential.

4. **Insufficient Dosing Optimization**: The chosen dosing strategy of 160 mg without an initial lower dose or adaptive escalation model did not account for the diverse tolerability and metabolic profiles among patients with prior complex treatments. Given regorafenib's pharmacokinetics, a more strategic approach to dose optimization may have improved the safety and efficacy outcomes.

5. **Lack of Clinical Trial Retention and Compliance**: Patient retention proved challenging, with an attrition rate of approximately 45% attributed to treatment-related side effects and disease progression. Such high drop-out rates severely compromised statistical power and the ability to draw meaningful conclusions on regorafenib's effectiveness.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed relapsed metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma.
- Documented progression following two or more lines of systemic therapy.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0-1.
- Adequate organ function as per laboratory assessments.
- Informed consent obtained from all participants.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Presence of untreated, symptomatic central nervous system metastases.
- Uncontrolled hypertension or significant cardiovascular disease.
- A history of severe hypersensitivity to VEGF inhibitors or related compounds.
- Concurrent participation in any other clinical trial or use of investigational drugs within 28 days prior to enrollment.

**Results:**
At the conclusion of the trial, only 10% of patients achieved stable disease at the 16-week mark, with a cumulative PFS of less than 20%. The high incidence of adverse effects and limited clinical benefit underscored the lack of efficacy in this heavily pre-treated population.

**Conclusion:**
This clinical trial of regorafenib in relapsed metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma did not meet its primary endpoint due to factors related to patient selection, inadequate dosing strategies, and significant toxicity. Future studies in gastric cancer will require a more rigorous evaluation of patient characteristics and potential biomarkers to refine the therapeutic applications of regorafenib. Further investigation into alternative dosing strategies or combination therapies may provide more promising avenues for enhancing clinical outcomes in this challenging disease context.
