**Clinical Trial Report: Decitabine in Combination with Targeted Therapy for Patients with Mutated Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

**Trial Title:** A Phase II Multicenter Randomized Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Decitabine in Combination with Targeted Therapy in Adult Patients with Mutated Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)

**Objective:** This study aims to investigate the therapeutic efficacy of decitabine, when administered in conjunction with targeted therapies, in patients presenting with specific genetic mutations associated with acute myeloid leukemia. Moreover, it seeks to evaluate the safety profile of this combination treatment and its impact on overall survival rates and progression-free survival.

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial. Approximately 150 participants will be enrolled across five sites. Eligible patients will be assigned to either the experimental group receiving decitabine in combination with targeted agents based on their genetic profile or the control group receiving only standard supportive care.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults ≥ 18 years with a confirmed diagnosis of AML per WHO criteria.
2. Presence of specific genetic mutations (e.g., FLT3, IDH1/2, or NPM1) identified through genetic testing.
3. Performance status Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) 0-2.
4. Adequate hepatic (AST and ALT < 2.5 times the upper limit of normal) and renal function (creatinine < 1.5 mg/dL).
5. Signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior treatment with hypomethylating agents or any investigational agent within 30 days before the start of the study.
2. Severe uncontrolled cardiovascular disease or active viral/bacterial infections.
3. Pregnant or lactating females, or females of childbearing potential not using adequate contraceptive measures.

**Intervention:**
Participants in the experimental group will receive decitabine intravenously (20 mg/m²) daily for five consecutive days, combined with oral targeted therapy according to their mutation status. The targeted therapies include midostaurin for FLT3 mutations and ivosidenib or enasidenib for IDH1 and IDH2 mutations, respectively, administered at standard approved doses throughout the treatment cycles. The treatment will be repeated every 28 days for up to six cycles, contingent upon clinical benefit and tolerability.

**Endpoints:**
1. **Primary Endpoint:** Overall response rate (ORR) defined as Complete Response (CR) + Partial Response (PR) after three cycles of treatment, assessed by bone marrow biopsy and hematologic evaluation.
2. **Secondary Endpoints:** Overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and safety profiles measured by the incidence of adverse events (AEs) classified by Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

**Study Visits:** Participants will undergo clinical assessments and laboratory evaluations at baseline and at each cycle. Bone marrow aspirates will be performed at baseline, after three cycles, and at the final evaluation.

**Expected Outcomes:**
Due to prior studies highlighting suboptimal patient selection leading to heterogeneity, this trial will specifically target a genetically defined cohort to maximize response potential to decitabine. However, careful monitoring of toxicities will be pivotal, as a historical lack of adequate assessment of adverse events led to significant treatment interruptions in prior decitabine trials. 

**Trial Duration:** The study will last approximately 24 months, including follow-up for 12 months post-treatment.

**Hypothesis:** Improved outcomes will be observed in genetically characterized patients receiving decitabine in combination with targeted therapy compared to those receiving standard supportive care. However, due to previously identified factors impairing response rates—including compromised patient tolerance in certain populations—the anticipated impact may be limited.

**Significance:** The outcomes of this study may provide insights into optimizing intervention strategies for AML by aligning treatment with the underlying genetic profile of patients, although the historical factors contributing to the inability to achieve prior trial success must be meticulously addressed in this design. 

**Conclusion:** While the rationale for this trial seeks to learn from past failures of decitabine, including the recognition of a diverse patient population and managing side effects, there is a critical need for robust data to assess how genetic determinants can better predicate treatment response. The findings from this trial could reshape future approaches to therapeutic strategies in the management of AML.
