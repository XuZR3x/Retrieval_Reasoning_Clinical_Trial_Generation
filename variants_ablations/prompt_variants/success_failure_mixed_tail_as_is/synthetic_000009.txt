**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy and Safety of Vortioxetine in Adults With Major Depressive Disorder**

**Study Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Vortioxetine (Lu AA21004) in Adults With Major Depressive Disorder: A Phase 3, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Fixed-Dose Study Evaluating the Effects of Enhanced Dosing Regimens 

**Study Identifier:** NCT03518249

**Objective:** The primary objective of this clinical trial was to assess the efficacy and safety of vortioxetine (Lu AA21004) administered in elevated fixed doses of 20 mg and 25 mg once daily compared with placebo in adults diagnosed with major depressive disorder (MDD).

**Background:** Major depressive disorder remains a critical public health issue characterized by significant impairment in psychosocial functioning. Vortioxetine, a novel antidepressant, targets multiple serotonin receptors and shows promise in enhancing depressive symptomatology as measured by standardized scales.

**Methodology:**
- **Design:** This study utilized a prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design to minimize bias and derive valid efficacy conclusions based on robust methodology.
- **Duration:** The overall time for participant involvement was set at 14 weeks, encompassing 10 clinic visits and telephone follow-ups after 2 weeks post-trial completion.
- **Participants:** A total of 743 subjects aged 18-65 years were enrolled, all diagnosed with recurrent MDD according to DSM-IV-TR criteria, ensuring stringent inclusion for optimal therapeutic response evaluations.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Confirmed diagnosis of recurrent MDE as per DSM-IV-TR (classification code 296.3x) verified by the Structured Clinical Interview for DSM Disorders (SCID).
2. MADRS total score of 26 or greater at Screening and Baseline.
3. CGI-S score of 4 or greater at Screening and Baseline.
4. Participants must have experienced the current depressive episode for a minimum of 3 months.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of any psychiatric disorder other than MDD per DSM-IV-TR criteria.
2. Significant-risk individuals for suicide or those with recent self-harm ideation (score ≥5 on item 10 of MADRS).
3. Previous treatment with vortioxetine or investigational agents within the past 30 days.
4. Evidence of clinically significant medical conditions that could interfere with outcome assessments.

**Treatment Groups:**
- Group A: Vortioxetine 20 mg (n=247)
- Group B: Vortioxetine 25 mg (n=245)
- Group C: Placebo (n=251)

**Outcome Measures:**
1. Primary Endpoint: Change from Baseline in the MADRS total score, monitored using MMRM analysis for efficacy assessment.
2. Secondary Endpoints: Changes in CGI-S score, patient-reported outcomes via the Health Questionnaire-Short Form (SF-36), and tolerability as assessed by incidence of adverse events.

**Results:**
- The trial demonstrated significant reductions in MADRS scores in both treatment groups (20 mg: LS mean change -12.4; 25 mg: LS mean change -14.7) compared to placebo (-7.3), affirming the enhanced efficacy attributed to optimized dosing.
- The proportion of participants experiencing a response based on the predefined criteria (≥50% reduction in MADRS) was markedly higher in the treatment groups (20 mg: 60%, 25 mg: 65%) versus placebo (32%).
- Safety profiles were consistent with previous findings, revealing that adverse events were predominantly mild to moderate and transient, thereby confirming vortioxetine's tolerability.

**Conclusion:** This Phase 3 clinical trial substantiates the efficacy and safety of vortioxetine administered at higher fixed dosages in adults suffering from major depressive disorder. The incorporation of rigorous methodological frameworks, optimal subject selection criteria, structured dosage regimens informed by pharmacokinetic studies, and robust assessment instruments contribute to the substantial therapeutic advancements presented in these findings. This evidence positions vortioxetine favorably in the landscape of pharmacological interventions for MDD, advocating for its integration into treatment paradigms aimed at alleviating the burden of this chronic condition.
