**Failed Clinical Trial of Interleukin-2: A Phase II Study Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Interleukin-2 in Combination with Nivolumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Background**: This phase II trial was designed to evaluate the immunotherapeutic potential of interleukin-2 (IL-2) when administered in conjunction with the PD-1 inhibitor, nivolumab, in patients diagnosed with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Prior studies suggest that interleukin-2 promotes T-cell activation, potentially enhancing therapeutic responses to checkpoint inhibitors. However, mounting evidence demonstrates various challenges and factors that contributed to the trial's failure.

**Objectives**: The primary objective was to assess the objective response rate (ORR) to combined treatment at 12 weeks, while secondary endpoints included safety profiles, progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and identification of immune correlates.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Adults aged 18 years or older with histologically confirmed advanced NSCLC (Stage III or IV).
- Patients must be candidates for nivolumab treatment based on prior standard chemotherapy criteria.
- Measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Adequate organ function determined by laboratory values within acceptable limits prior to study initiation.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Previous exposure to PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors within 6 months prior to trial initiation.
- Active autoimmune diseases or disorders requiring systemic immunosuppressive therapy.
- Ongoing severe infections or uncontrolled medical conditions.
- Significant obstructive lung disease contributing to compromised respiratory functions (FEV1 < 50% predicted).

**Trial Design**: Participants were to receive subcutaneously administered IL-2 at a dose of 3.0 × 10^6 IU/m^2/day alongside standard intravenous administration of nivolumab (3 mg/kg) every two weeks for a duration of 12 weeks. The trial aimed to recruit a total of 80 patients.

**Outcome Measures**: 
- Primary Endpoint: Objective response rate defined by complete or partial response based on imaging assessments.
- Secondary Endpoints: Incidence of treatment-related adverse events, overall survival, progression-free survival, and analyses of pre- and post-treatment immune responses.

**Adverse Events and Challenges Identified**:
1. **Inadequate Patient Selection**: Although the study aimed to focus on advanced NSCLC patients, the stringent inclusion criteria eliminated individuals with critical therapeutic needs who may have benefited from the treatment, inadvertently restricting generalizability.
   
2. **Limited Synergistic Effect Observed**: Preliminary results indicated minimal to no enhancement of systemic immune responses with the combined regimen, contrary to the hypothesized synergistic mechanism proposed between IL-2 and nivolumab. Notably, objective responses in non-targeted lesions remained absent, highlighting a crucial gap in the expected systemic benefits.

3. **Immunosuppressive Microenvironment**: The highly immunosuppressive microenvironment associated with advanced NSCLC may have significantly undermined the efficacy of IL-2, which necessitates a competent immune milieu for successful action. This suggests that the combination therapy's effectiveness may be further compromised in patients with excessive tumor burden.

4. **Toxicity Management Issues**: There was an increased incidence of immune-related adverse events due to the combination strategy, resulting in several treatment discontinuations. Notably, the cumulative toxicities associated with both agents raised significant management concerns, leading to critical patient withdrawals.

5. **Lack of Robust Immune Correlates**: The trial did not yield sufficient immune correlates to support the efficacy of the combination therapy. The absence of biomarker data to predict responses further complicated any conclusions drawn from the objective response measurements.

**Conclusion**: The study failed to demonstrate a significant clinical benefit from the combination of IL-2 and nivolumab in patients with advanced NSCLC. The findings indicate a need for further refinement in patient selection and a reevaluation of therapeutic combinations to overcome the hurdles associated with treatment efficacy in this malignancy. Future research should focus on elucidating mechanisms underlying the interplay between IL-2 and checkpoint inhibitors in the context of immune regulation and tumor immunology.
