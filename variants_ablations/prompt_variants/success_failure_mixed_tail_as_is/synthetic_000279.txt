**Title:** Memantine Efficacy in Enhancing Cognitive Function in Chronic Pain Patients: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of memantine in improving cognitive function among patients experiencing chronic pain conditions, specifically those related to fibromyalgia. Secondary objectives include assessing the impact of memantine on pain intensity, sleep quality, and overall quality of life.

**Design:** This study employs a double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group design over a treatment duration of 12 weeks, with a total of 100 participants randomly assigned to receive either memantine or placebo.

**Study Population:** Patients aged 25-65 years, diagnosed with fibromyalgia based on the American College of Rheumatology criteria, and exhibiting clinically significant cognitive impairment (e.g., difficulties in attention, memory, and executive function as assessed by the Montreal Cognitive Assessment [MoCA]) will be included. Participants must have a baseline pain score of ≥ 4 on the Numeric Pain Rating Scale (NPRS).

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults aged 25-65 years.
2. Diagnosis of fibromyalgia as per ACR criteria.
3. Cognitive impairment defined as a MoCA score of < 26.
4. Baseline pain severity score of ≥ 4 on the NPRS.
5. Stable medication regimen for at least 3 months prior to screening.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of dementia or other neurodegenerative disorders.
2. Current psychiatric disorders or severe depression (HAM-D > 17).
3. History of substance abuse within the last year.
4. Significant renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min).
5. Prior adverse reactions to memantine.
6. Concomitant medication known to interact with memantine or affect cognitive function.

**Methods:** Participants will receive a titrated dose of memantine, beginning at 5 mg daily, increasing by 5 mg weekly up to a maximum of 20 mg daily. Cognitive function will be evaluated using the MoCA at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks. A longitudinal design will enable the collection of repeated measures across these time points to assess changes in cognitive function and pain intensity.

**Secondary Outcomes:** The study will also evaluate secondary outcomes including:
- Pain severity (NPRS).
- Sleep quality (Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index).
- Quality of life (Short Form 36 Health Survey [SF-36]).
- Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire (FIQ).

**Statistical Analysis:** Statistical analyses will be conducted using mixed-effects models to assess changes in cognitive and pain outcomes over time, while controlling for potential confounding variables. A p-value of < 0.05 will be deemed statistically significant.

**Ethics:** The study protocol has been approved by the institutional review board (IRB), and all participants will provide written informed consent prior to enrollment. The trial is registered under ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCTXXXXXXX.

**Results:** It is anticipated that this trial will demonstrate significant improvements in cognitive function in patients treated with memantine compared to the placebo group, alongside a notable decrease in pain severity and enhancement of quality of life metrics, owing to the robust selection criteria and comprehensive assessment strategy informed by prior successful memantine trials. 

**Conclusion:** This double-blind randomized controlled trial seeks to substantiate the therapeutic role of memantine in addressing cognitive impairments among chronic pain patients, providing a foundation for future research into the multi-faceted benefits of memantine in the context of chronic health challenges.
