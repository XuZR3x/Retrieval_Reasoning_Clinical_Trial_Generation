**A Phase II Study of Axitinib in Combination With Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor in Patients With Non-Clear Cell Renal Cell Carcinoma**

**Study Title:** A Phase II Study of Axitinib in Combination With Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor in Patients With Non-Clear Cell Renal Cell Carcinoma  

**Background:** Non-clear cell renal cell carcinoma (nccRCC) lacks standard treatment options and often exhibits resistance to existing therapies. Given that these tumors predominantly rely on angiogenesis mediated by vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), axitinib, a potent VEGFR inhibitor, presents a compelling therapeutic strategy. Additionally, the synergistic potential of combining axitinib with immune checkpoint inhibitors may enhance clinical outcomes, providing a dual mechanism of action against tumor progression.

**Objective:** To evaluate the efficacy and safety of axitinib administrated concurrently with an immune checkpoint inhibitor (level of melanoma-associated antigen A3, MAGE-A3) in patients with advanced nccRCC, previously untreated, and verify improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) compared to historical controls.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically confirmed nccRCC without clear cell component  
- Stage IV disease not amenable to curative surgery  
- No prior treatment with anti-VEGF or immunotherapy  
- Presence of measurable disease according to RECIST v1.1  
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0-1  
- Age ≥ 18 years with adequate organ function inclusive of:  
  - Hematology: Neutrophil count ≥ 1.5 x 10^9/L, Platelet count ≥ 100 x 10^9/L, Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL  
  - Hepatic function: Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN, AST, and ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN  
  - Renal function: Creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients with any prior systemic treatment for RCC  
- Clear cell histology  
- Uncontrolled hypertension (blood pressure >150/90 mmHg)  
- Significant cardiovascular disease  
- Impaired gastrointestinal functioning affecting drug absorption  
- Active central nervous system metastases  

**Methods:** This is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm phase II study. Eligible patients will receive axitinib at a dose of 5 mg daily, combined with the immune checkpoint inhibitor MAGE-A3, administered bi-weekly. Treatment will continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. The primary endpoint is PFS, assessed via imaging according to RECIST criteria every eight weeks. Secondary endpoints include overall response rate (ORR), overall survival (OS), and safety profile for combination therapy.

**Statistical Analysis:** An estimated sample size of 60 patients will be needed to achieve 80% power for detecting a significant improvement in PFS, with a one-sided significance level of 0.05. Survival analysis will be conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method, and efficacy will be compared against historical control data of single-agent therapies for nccRCC.

**Rationale:** 
1. The absence of recognized standard treatment options for nccRCC presents a critical clinical gap that axitinib may address.  
2. Previous studies indicate that VEGF antagonists can yield substantial clinical benefit in nccRCC, suggesting that axitinib will exhibit enhanced efficacy in this population.  
3. No prior clinical trials combining axitinib with checkpoint inhibitors specifically within the nccRCC cohort, constructing a unique framework with potential for improved outcomes.  
4. Preliminary evidence supports the superior activity of axitinib compared to other anti-angiogenic agents and aligns with the promise of immunotherapy, enhancing the therapeutic landscape for this malignancy.  
5. Observations from prior studies indicate improved efficacy of combination therapies in various cancer frameworks, reinforcing the need for such explorations in nccRCC.

**Conclusion:** This phase II study aims to establish the safety and efficacy of axitinib in conjunction with immune checkpoint inhibition in patients with nccRCC. The design leverages current gaps in treatment protocols and the pharmacological advantages of axitinib, potentially enhancing the standard of care for this challenging cancer subtype.
