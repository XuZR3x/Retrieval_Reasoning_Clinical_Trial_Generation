### Successful Clinical Trial of Doxorubicin Hydrochloride: Synergistic Efficacy of Doxorubicin, Bevacizumab, and Vorinostat in Treating Plaintiffs With Advanced Breast Cancer

**RATIONALE:** The therapeutic application of doxorubicin hydrochloride exhibits a potential for enhanced clinical outcomes when integrated into well-structured combination therapy involving targeted agents such as bevacizumab and epigenetic modulators like vorinostat. This trial investigates the overarching hypothesis that this multipronged approach may collectively amplify cancer cell cytotoxicity while ameliorating adverse effects through intensified targeting mechanisms.

**PURPOSE:** This Phase III trial aims to determine the efficacy and safety of doxorubicin hydrochloride in combination with bevacizumab and vorinostat versus doxorubicin monotherapy in patients with advanced or metastatic breast cancer.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- Primary: To compare the progression-free survival (PFS) rates between patients receiving combination therapy versus those treated with doxorubicin hydrochloride alone.
- Secondary: To evaluate overall survival, response rates, and the safety profile of the combination regimen relative to doxorubicin monotherapy.

**OUTLINE:** 
This is a randomized, double-blind, multicenter study. Patients are stratified according to tumor grade and receptor status (ER/PR positive vs. negative, HER2 positive vs. negative).

Patients are randomized into two treatment arms:
- **Arm I:** Patients receive doxorubicin hydrochloride IV at standard dosages on day 1 of each 21-day cycle, concurrently with bevacizumab IV administered at 15 mg/kg on day 1 of each cycle and vorinostat orally 400 mg administered daily for 21 days prior to each cycle of chemotherapy, repeating every 21 days for up to 6 cycles in the absence of disease progression.
  
- **Arm II:** Patients receive doxorubicin hydrochloride IV at the same dosage on day 1 and are followed within the same dosing schedule without the addition of bevacizumab or vorinostat.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** An anticipated enrollment of 350 patients across multiple sites.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:** 
- Confirmed diagnosis of locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer.
- Measurable disease with at least one lesion of ≥ 2 cm in longest diameter.
- No evidence of central nervous system metastases.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: 18 years and older.
- ECOG performance status: 0-2.
- Adequate hematologic function: WBC ≥ 3,000/mm³, ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³, and platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³.
- Hepatic and renal function within permissible limits.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Histologically confirmed breast cancer, with any receptor status.
- No prior anthracycline treatment.
- No concurrent use of other investigational agents or anti-cancer therapies.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:** 
- Presence of congestive heart failure grade II or greater.
- Significant uncontrolled comorbid conditions.
- Prior malignancies, with the exception of nonmelanoma skin cancers.

**ENDPOINT ASSESSMENT:** 
The primary endpoint is progression-free survival, assessed through regular imaging and clinical evaluations every 3 months for the initial year, transitioning to biannual assessments thereafter for 2 additional years. Secondary endpoints include overall survival determined from randomization to death from any cause and response rates defined by RECIST criteria.

**OUTCOMES:** 
Results indicated a statistically significant improvement in PFS for patients receiving the combination of doxorubicin, bevacizumab, and vorinostat, resulting in a median PFS of 12.5 months compared to 8.0 months observed in the control arm. Overall survival rates were also enhanced, demonstrating clinical relevance for the amended therapeutic approach, alongside manageable toxicity profiles principally comprising nausea and myelosuppression, which were effectively mitigated with supportive care.

### CONCLUSION:
The integration of bevacizumab and vorinostat into doxorubicin treatment protocols for advanced breast cancer not only heightens therapeutic efficacy but also provides a promising avenue for individualized cancer treatment strategies, demonstrating an essential advancement in clinical oncology practice.
