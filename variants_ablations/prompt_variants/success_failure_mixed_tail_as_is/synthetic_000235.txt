**Failed Clinical Trial of Sarilumab: Assessment of Long-Term Safety and Efficacy of SAR153191 (REGN88) in Patients with Ankylosing Spondylitis Post-Standard Therapy**

*Study Title: A Phase III Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial Evaluating the Long-Term Safety and Efficacy of Sarilumab (SAR153191) in Participants with Active Ankylosing Spondylitis Following Inadequate Response to Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)*

**Primary Objective:**  
To evaluate the long-term safety profile of Sarilumab (SAR153191) in participants with ankylosing spondylitis (AS) post inadequate response to standard NSAID treatment.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
1. To assess the efficacy of Sarilumab in improving clinical symptoms in ankylosing spondylitis, evaluated via the Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index (BASDAI).
2. To evaluate the proportion of participants achieving a 20% improvement in the Ankylosing Spondylitis Working Group (ASAS20) criteria.
3. To determine participant-reported outcomes and quality of life changes as measured by the Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Functional Index (BASFI).

**Study Duration:**  
Participants were to be enrolled for a maximum total duration of 52 weeks — including up to 8 weeks for screening and a 44-week treatment phase.

**Inclusion Criteria:** 
- Patients aged 18 to 75 years with a diagnosis of AS according to the modified New York criteria.
- Participants must have an inadequate response to at least two NSAIDs taken for at least 2 weeks each.
- Active disease (BASDAI score of ≥4) must be present at screening and baseline.

**Exclusion Criteria:** 
- Previous adverse events resulting in treatment discontinuation in any prior Sarilumab studies.
- Complete spinal fusion or any history of anti-TNF resistance, significantly limiting therapeutic options.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women or participants with significant comorbidities that could complicate safety assessments (e.g., other autoimmune diseases).

**Endpoints:**
- Primary Safety Endpoint: The percentage of participants experiencing any treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs).
- Primary Efficacy Endpoint: The proportion of participants achieving ASAS20 response criteria at Week 24.

**Results:**  
The trial faced significant challenges leading to its early termination. The results indicated:

1. **Adverse Event Profile:** A substantial percentage of participants (75%) experienced TEAEs, including serious infections and gastrointestinal complications, resulting in a safety signal that led to a high rate of treatment discontinuation (56%).
   
2. **Inefficacy Based on ASAS20 Criteria:** At the primary assessment point (Week 24), only 18% of participants achieved the ASAS20 criteria, which was statistically insignificant compared to the placebo group (20%).
   
3. **Population Specificity:** The study's eligibility criteria inadvertently excluded a breadth of AS participants who might have benefited, focusing too narrowly on those previously treated with limited NSAID options and hence diminishing generalizability and potential efficacy outcomes.

4. **Suboptimal Dosing Regimen:** The dosing regimen employed was based on previous trials but failed to achieve adequate therapeutic exposure, evidenced by pharmacokinetic analyses showing inadequate plasma concentrations in many participants.

5. **Disease Complexity:** There was considerable variability in disease presentation and response, with participants showing little to no improvement in disease activity scores despite receiving Sarilumab. This multivariate disease complexity highlighted challenges in measuring true clinical efficacy across a spectrum of responses over time.

In summary, the trial presented numerous challenges associated with the adverse event profile, inadequate treatment effectiveness, restrictive population criteria, ineffective dosing strategies, and the inherent complexity of ankylosing spondylitis. As a result, this trial demonstrates the difficulties faced in developing effective therapies for AS, and underscores the need for robust and adaptive clinical trial designs that can capture the multifaceted nature of the disease. 

**Conclusion:**  
The trial concluded that Sarilumab (SAR153191) was not safe or efficacious for the targeted population in light of significant adverse events, poor efficacy outcomes, and population limitations. Future investigations should consider revising therapeutic strategies and trial designs, including broader participant demographics and novel endpoints to accurately reflect real-world treatment outcomes in ankylosing spondylitis.
