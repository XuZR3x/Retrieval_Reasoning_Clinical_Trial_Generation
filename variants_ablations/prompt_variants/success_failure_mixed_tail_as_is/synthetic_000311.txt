**Successful Clinical Trial of Docetaxel: Docetaxel Combined with Pembrolizumab and Carboplatin as First-Line Treatment in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**A Phase II Multicenter Study of Docetaxel, Pembrolizumab, and Carboplatin in Chemotherapy-Naïve Patients with Unresectable Locally Advanced or Metastatic (Stage IIIB/IV) Non-Squamous Cell Histology Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer.**

The objective of this study was to assess the efficacy and safety of a combination therapy of docetaxel, pembrolizumab, and carboplatin as first-line treatment in patients diagnosed with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic non-squamous NSCLC. The hypothesis was based upon the concept of synergistic anti-tumor effects through the dual pathways of chemotherapy and immune checkpoint inhibition, with satisfactory preliminary evidence supporting the combination treatment in enhancing overall survival and progression-free survival.

The combination of docetaxel with pembrolizumab and carboplatin was determined to provide an innovative approach aimed at overcoming resistance mechanisms commonly observed with single-agent chemotherapy. This trial utilized a carefully selected study population, with rigorous inclusion criteria designed to ensure the enrollment of patients with measurable NSCLC and optimal performance status, facilitating reliable treatment effect assessments.

The treatment regimen involved administration of docetaxel at a dose of 75 mg/m², alongside pembrolizumab at 200 mg intravenously, and carboplatin administered with an area under the curve (AUC) of 5 over a 21-day treatment cycle. Participants who exhibited no progression would receive maintenance therapy comprising pembrolizumab until the indication of disease progression. Moreover, follow-up assessments scheduled every three months ensured proactive monitoring of patient wellbeing and disease status up to two years post-treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
To be eligible for enrollment, participants were required to meet all of the following criteria during screening:
1. Provided written informed consent prior to study participation.
2. Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of locally advanced or metastatic (stage IIIb/IV) non-squamous NSCLC.
3. Presence of measurable disease, defined by at least one lesion measurable in one dimension (longest diameter) of ≥ 1.0 cm via computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).
4. Treatment-naïve status, defined as no previous systemic chemotherapy for advanced disease.
5. Age ≥ 18 years, with an estimated life expectancy of ≥ 12 weeks.
6. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of 0, 1, or 2.
7. Sufficient renal function, as determined by creatinine clearance > 45 mL/min via Cockcroft-Gault formula.
8. Adequate hematologic parameters: absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500 cells/microL; platelet count ≥ 100,000 cells/microL; hemoglobin ≥ 9.0 g/dL.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
Patients were excluded from the trial if they presented with any of the following:
1. Receipt of prior systemic chemotherapy or immunotherapy for NSCLC.
2. Histological diagnosis of squamous cell NSCLC.
3. Concurrent uncontrolled cardiovascular conditions, including class III/IV heart failure or active arrhythmia.
4. Clinically significant liver or renal dysfunction as per predefined laboratory values.
5. Solid organ transplantation or evidence of additional malignancies (beyond curatively treated skin cancers).

**Outcome Measures:**
Primary endpoints included:
- Overall response rate (ORR) assessed using RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- Progression-free survival (PFS) as defined from registration to the first evidence of disease progression or death from any cause.

Secondary endpoints pursued included:
- Overall survival (OS) measured from the date of registration to death from any cause.
- Evaluation of safety and tolerability through reporting adverse events categorized based on CTCAE v5.0 criteria.

**Conclusion:**
This Phase II trial evaluated the combination of docetaxel, pembrolizumab, and carboplatin, resulting in significant improvements in ORR and PFS compared to historical controls. The study design ensured a homogeneous patient population and employed rigorous dosing regimens, contributing to the observed therapeutic benefits with manageable toxicity profiles. Comprehensive follow-up enabled effective tracking of long-term outcomes, contributing important data for future Phase III trials.
