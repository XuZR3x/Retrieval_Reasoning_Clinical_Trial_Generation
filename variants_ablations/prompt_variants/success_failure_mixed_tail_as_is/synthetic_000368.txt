**Failed Clinical Trial of Ticagrelor: Efficacy of Ticagrelor in Reducing Platelet Reactivity in Elderly Patients with Peripheral Artery Disease (E-TICAD)**

**Study Title**: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Assess the Efficacy of Ticagrelor in Reducing Platelet Reactivity and Improving Clinical Outcomes in Elderly Patients with Peripheral Artery Disease

**Objective**: The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of ticagrelor in reducing platelet reactivity and its impact on walking distance in elderly patients with peripheral artery disease (PAD). This trial specifically aims to clarify the relationship between treatment with ticagrelor, genetic variability, and comorbidities prevalent in an elderly population.

**Study Design**: This was a multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial designed to investigate the antiplatelet efficacy of ticagrelor in a demographic with unique genetic factors affecting drug metabolism and high prevalence of multiple underlying health conditions. The study was conducted across 15 centers with a target enrollment of 200 elderly participants (aged 65 years and older) with a confirmed diagnosis of PAD. 

**Intervention**: Participants were randomized in a 1:1 fashion to receive either ticagrelor (180 mg loading dose followed by 90 mg maintenance dose daily) or placebo for 12 weeks, while all subjects received aspirin 100 mg daily.

**Endpoint Selection**: The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in platelet reactivity, assessed by ADP-stimulated platelet aggregation after 12 weeks of treatment. Secondary endpoints included the change in 6-minute walk test (6MWT) distance and patient-reported outcomes related to quality of life.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
1. Written informed consent prior to any study-specific procedures.
2. Males and females aged 65 years or older.
3. Diagnosis of PAD, characterized by an ankle-brachial index (ABI) of ≤ 0.90.
4. Concurrent use of aspirin therapy without known contraindications.
5. No significant cognitive impairment impacting the ability to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
1. Any substantial comorbid conditions affecting pharmacodynamics or response (e.g. advanced chronic kidney disease, malignancy).
2. History of severe hypersensitivity to ticagrelor or related compounds.
3. Recent history of major bleeding or coagulopathy.
4. Use of medications known to interfere with CYP3A4 metabolism within one month prior to study participation. 
5. Participation in another clinical trial within 3 months prior to enrollment.

**Sample Size and Statistical Analysis**: The total sample size aimed to achieve a statistical power of 80% to detect a clinically significant reduction in platelet reactivity. Statistical analysis employed mixed-effects models to account for variability, particularly due to substantial comorbidities and potential genetic differences.

**Outcomes and Failures**: 
The study ultimately failed to demonstrate a significant reduction in platelet reactivity with ticagrelor compared to placebo at 12 weeks, predominantly due to the high variability and baseline comorbid conditions among the elderly participants. A substantial proportion of subjects exhibited genetic polymorphisms impacting the metabolism of ticagrelor, leading to unpredictable therapeutic responses and blunted antiplatelet effects. Furthermore, endpoint selection focusing on objective measures without adequate consideration of functional outcomes relevant to the elderly population resulted in inconclusive findings regarding improvement in walking distance.

Lack of adequate sample size was further exacerbated by high dropout rates related to concurrent medical issues, leading to insufficient power to draw definitive conclusions. The use of a primary endpoint that was not fully reflective of clinical relevance for quality of life also contributed to perceived ineffectiveness of ticagrelor in this specific population.

**Conclusion**: The E-TICAD trial elucidated critical considerations regarding the genetic, physiological, and health-related challenges faced by elderly patients with PAD. The findings highlight the need for tailored strategies that address both pharmacogenomic variability and the complexities of multicomorbidity within clinical trial frameworks involving antiplatelet agents like ticagrelor.
