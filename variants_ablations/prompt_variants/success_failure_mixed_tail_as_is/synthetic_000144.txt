**Successful clinical trial of efavirenz: MUDRA (Multinational Study of Underrepresented Demographics in Regions Affected by HIV) A Randomized Clinical Trial Evaluating Efavirenz Efficacy in Diverse Demographic Among Treatment-Naive Adults in Central Africa.**

**Background:** 
MUDRA aims to investigate the efficacy and safety of efavirenz, as part of a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor-based regimen, in treatment-naive adults who have diverse backgrounds and clinical characteristics across Central Africa. This study addresses the critical need for inclusive research, reflecting real-world populations affected by HIV.

**Objective:**
To evaluate the virological efficacy, safety, and quality of life improvements in treatment-naive adults administered efavirenz as part of antiretroviral therapy (ART) in a multi-country setting.

**Methods:**
Utilizing a multi-center, double-blind, randomized control design, participants will be enrolled from urban and rural regions in six Central African countries. Adult patients, aged 18-50 years, meeting specific inclusion criteria will be assigned randomly in a 1:1 ratio to receive either efavirenz combined with tenofovir disoproxil fumarate and lamivudine or a matching placebo regimen for 48 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- HIV-infected, documented by a positive ELISA test.
- Treatment-naïve adults aged 18-50 years.
- CD4 count between 200 and 500 cells/mm³ at screening.
- Willingness to provide written informed consent.
- Ability to attend clinic visits as required for study protocol compliance.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of ART use at any point prior to enrollment.
- Presence of any opportunistic infection requiring immediate treatment.
- Significant co-morbid medical conditions that may interfere with trial participation, including serious hepatic or renal dysfunction.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding or those planning to conceive during the study period.

**Interventions:**
Participants in the efavirenz group will receive efavirenz (600 mg orally once daily), tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (300 mg orally once daily), and lamivudine (300 mg orally once daily). The control group will receive matched placebo tablets for the same regimen. Both groups will undergo a rigorous adherence support program, including directly observed therapy during the first month to ensure maximal compliance and minimize treatment failures.

**Outcome Measures:**
Primary efficacy will be assessed by the proportion of participants achieving viral suppression to an HIV-1 RNA level of <50 copies/mL at 48 weeks, alongside quality of life metrics measured using the Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) scale. Secondary endpoints will include rate of adverse events, CD4 count changes, and adherence rates over follow-up visits at Weeks 24 and 48.

**Statistical Analysis:**
An intention-to-treat analysis will be performed, and statistical significance will be determined using chi-square tests for categorical outcomes and t-tests for continuous variables, with a significance level set at 0.05.

**Study Duration:**
Recruitment is anticipated to last 18 months, with a total follow-up of 48 weeks for each participant and an additional month for data cleaning.

**Ethical Considerations:**
Ethical approval has been obtained from institutional review boards in each participating country, ensuring compliance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines. Informed consent will be obtained from each participant prior to enrollment.

**Expected Outcomes:**
With robust multi-regional participation, MUDRA aims to demonstrate the effectiveness of efavirenz across diverse patient populations with considerations for quality of life and adherence factors, paralleling the reasons of previous successful trials. The study seeks to provide invaluable insights into the management of HIV in settings historically underrepresented in clinical research.

**Conclusion:**
Findings from MUDRA are expected to contribute significantly to the understanding of ART efficacy in varied settings and populations, thus informing future HIV treatment protocols and enhancing overall public health strategies in the HIV epidemic.
