**Failed Clinical Trial of Nintedanib:**  
*Phase II Study of Nintedanib as a Monotherapy in the Treatment of Recurrent Malignant Pleural Mesothelioma*  
*Evaluation of Efficacy, Safety, and Pharmacokinetics in Subjects Who Have Previously Underwent Systemic Therapy*

**Study Description:** 
This phase II clinical trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of nintedanib administered as a monotherapy in patients with recurrent malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM) who have previously received systemic therapy, including at least one platinum-based regimen. The trial particularly focused on the pharmacokinetic profiles and any interactions that may occur in this specific cohort, but ultimately did not demonstrate the anticipated therapeutic benefit.

**Primary Objective:**
To assess the overall response rate (ORR) in patients with recurrent, unresectable malignant pleural mesothelioma treated with nintedanib.

**Secondary Objectives:**
1. To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) at 4 months.
2. To assess overall survival (OS) outcomes.
3. To document the frequency and severity of adverse events.
4. To analyzemeasurements of pharmacokinetic parameters and drug-drug interactions.

**Design:** 
This was a multicenter, open-label, single-arm trial in which subjects received nintedanib orally at a dose of 200 mg bid for a treatment period of 28 days with subsequent cycles repeated in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**Inclusion Criteria:** 
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of unresectable recurrent malignant pleural mesothelioma.
- Patients must have received at least one prior systemic platinum-based chemotherapy.
- Measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1 criteria confirmed within 28 days prior to treatment initiation.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
- Adequate organ function as demonstrated by laboratory parameters.

**Exclusion Criteria:** 
- History of severe gastrointestinal disorders that preclude oral medication.
- Concurrent treatment with pirfenidone or other anti-cancer therapies, leading to potential complex pharmacokinetic interactions.
- Patients with known liver impairment exceeding Child-Pugh classification A, such as elevated ALT/AST, or bilirubin levels > 1.5 times the upper limit of normal.
- History of significant cardiac events (myocardial infarction or unstable angina within the last 6 months).
- Prior treatment with any vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) inhibitors, including nintedanib or similar agents.

**Outcome Measures:** 
- Primary Outcome: The proportion of participants achieving a complete or partial response to treatment evaluated at 12 weeks by imaging assessment.
- Secondary Outcomes: The calculation of PFS and OS using Kaplan-Meier analysis, the frequency of treatment-related adverse events as per CTCAE v5.0 criteria, and detailed pharmacokinetic profiling of nintedanib and its metabolites.

**Results:**
The trial was terminated prematurely due to failure to meet the primary endpoint. Only 12% of subjects exhibited an objective response (complete or partial response), which fell significantly short of the predetermined efficacy threshold. Additionally, substantial adverse events led to treatment discontinuation in 30% of participants, namely gastrointestinal complications and liver enzyme elevations, contributing to non-compliance. The lack of detecting significant pharmacokinetic interactions between nintedanib and the systemic therapies previously administered showcased the absence of established biomarkers to predict therapeutic outcomes. The stringent inclusion criteria further limited the representativeness of the patient population, thus reducing the study’s ability to show benefits in a more general MPM cohort affected by variations in tumor biology and prior treatment resistance.

**Conclusion:** 
This clinical trial illustrated critical challenges relevant to the pharmacokinetics of nintedanib, the appropriate identification of a representative patient population, and the need for further exploratory investigations on biomarkers for therapeutic prediction. Overall, the findings underscore the complexities inherent in treating malignant pleural mesothelioma with nintedanib and emphasize the necessity for future research avenues centered on optimizing treatment strategies in recurrent cases.
