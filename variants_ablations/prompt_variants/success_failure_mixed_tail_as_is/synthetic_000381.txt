**Successful clinical trial of qva149: Efficacy and Safety of QVA149 in Patients With Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease - The RESPOND Study** 

**A Multi-center, Randomized, Double-blind, Double-dummy, Parallel-group, Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of QVA149 (Indacaterol Maleate / Glycopyrronium Bromide) versus Tiotropium in Patients with Moderate to Severe Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD).**

**Objective:**  
This study aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of QVA149 in reducing the frequency of COPD exacerbations and improving lung function compared to the long-acting muscarinic antagonist, Tiotropium.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Written informed consent obtained prior to screening.  
- Male or female adults aged ≥ 40 years.  
- A confirmed diagnosis of moderate to severe COPD according to GOLD (2011) criteria.  
- Smoking history of at least 10 pack years.   
- Post-bronchodilator Forced Expiratory Volume in one second (FEV1) ≥ 30% and < 60% of the predicted normal value, with post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC (Forced Vital Capacity) ratio < 0.70 at screening.  
- Patients must have experienced at least one exacerbation requiring systemic glucocorticosteroids and/or antibiotics in the previous year.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Pregnant or lactating women.  
- Patients with any history of asthma, malignancies within the past 5 years, or significant cardiovascular disorders.  
- History of hypersensitivity to the components of trial medications.  
- Patients with any pulmonary disease other than COPD that could interfere with study outcomes.  
- Patients on stable COPD medications must have completed a washout period before screening.   

**Design:**  
The RESPOND study featured a 48-week duration, where subjects were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either QVA149 or Tiotropium. The study utilized a double-dummy technique to maintain blinding, ensuring that neither the patients nor the investigators were aware of the treatment assignments. 

**Primary Endpoint:**  
The primary outcome was the annual rate of moderate-to-severe COPD exacerbations over the study period. Secondary endpoints included trough FEV1 values collected at baseline and at multiple time points throughout the study, patient-reported outcomes including measures of symptom improvement, and overall health status assessed via the St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ).

**Results:**  
The results demonstrated that QVA149 significantly reduced the annual rate of COPD exacerbations by 26% compared to Tiotropium (p<0.001). Moreover, there was a pronounced improvement in trough FEV1, with mean increases of 101 mL with QVA149 compared to 62 mL with Tiotropium (p<0.001), reinforcing the hypothesis that the pharmacological synergy between Indacaterol Maleate and Glycopyrronium Bromide yields superior bronchodilation. Moreover, patient-reported outcomes reflected enhanced quality of life, with SGRQ scores improving significantly in the QVA149 group versus Tiotropium.

**Safety Assessments:**  
Adverse events were recorded and found to be comparable between both treatment groups, with no unforeseen safety signals identified throughout the study duration. Commonly reported adverse effects included headache and upper respiratory tract infections, which were consistent with previous findings associated with beta-agonists and anticholinergics.

**Conclusion:**  
The RESPOND study confirmed the superior efficacy of QVA149 over Tiotropium in reducing exacerbations and improving lung function in patients with moderate to severe COPD. These findings, supported by the robust design and well-defined inclusion criteria, provide compelling evidence for QVA149 as a frontline therapy in the management of COPD. This trial underscores the clinical relevance of QVA149 in delivering substantially improved outcomes for patients suffering from chronic pulmonary disease by leveraging the synergistic effects derived from its dual active ingredients.
