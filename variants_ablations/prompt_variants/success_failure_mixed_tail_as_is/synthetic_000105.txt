<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Evaluation of Bicalutamide with LHRH Agonist for Advanced Prostate Cancer: A Phase III Trial</Title>
    <Rationale>Advanced prostate cancer is often driven by androgen signaling, which is a critical factor in tumor proliferation. The combination of bicalutamide as an androgen receptor antagonist with luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) agonists is hypothesized to lead to enhanced suppression of testosterone levels and subsequent tumor growth inhibition. This trial aims to assess whether bicalutamide, synergistically combined with LHRH agonists, yields superior clinical outcomes over LHRH agonist monotherapy in advanced prostate cancer patients.</Rationale>
    <Purpose>This randomized, multicenter phase III trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of bicalutamide when administered alongside LHRH agonists in patients with advanced prostate cancer. The primary objective is to determine overall survival rates and progression-free survival in comparison to LHRH therapy alone.</Purpose>
    
    <ConditionsForPatientEligibility>
        <Condition>Histologically confirmed diagnosis of adenocarcinoma of the prostate with evidence of advanced, non-metastatic disease (cT3, N0) or metastatic disease (M1).</Condition>
        <Condition>Aged 18 years or older with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.</Condition>
        <Condition>Patients must have a serum PSA level greater than 5.0 ng/mL.</Condition>
        <Condition>Eligible patients must have adequate hematologic (WBC count ≥ 3,000/µL, platelets ≥ 75,000/µL) and hepatic function (total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL, transaminases < 2.5 times ULN).</Condition>
        <Condition>Patients must be willing to provide informed consent and adhere to study assessments.</Condition>
    </ConditionsForPatientEligibility>

    <ConditionsForPatientIneligibility>
        <Condition>Prior histologically confirmed prostate carcinoma treatments with chemotherapy or hormonal therapy within 6 months prior to randomization.</Condition>
        <Condition>History of severe hypersensitivity to bicalutamide or any of its components.</Condition>
        <Condition>Presence of active, uncontrolled infections or significant comorbidity that might preclude participation in the study.</Condition>
        <Condition>Patients with symptomatic brain metastases are excluded.</Condition>
    </ConditionsForPatientIneligibility>

    <TrialDesign>Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either bicalutamide at 150 mg orally once daily combined with an LHRH agonist or LHRH agonist monotherapy for a duration of 24 months. Efficacy will be evaluated through progression-free survival rates at 12, 24, and 36 months, with longitudinal follow-ups for overall survival assessed by Kaplan-Meier methods. Quality of life will also be monitored through validated questionnaires.</TrialDesign>

    <PrimaryEndpoints>
        <Endpoint>Overall survival defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause.</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Progression-free survival defined as the time until disease progression or death.</Endpoint>
    </PrimaryEndpoints>

    <SecondaryEndpoints>
        <Endpoint>Assessment of changes in PSA levels from baseline to 12 months.</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Comparison of quality of life metrics utilizing the EORTC QLQ-C30 questionnaire pre-treatment and at 6, 12, and 24 months post-treatment.</Endpoint>
    </SecondaryEndpoints>

    <FollowUp>Patients will be followed every 3 months for the first 2 years and every 6 months thereafter until death or loss to follow-up.</FollowUp>

    <ExpectedOutcome>It is anticipated that the integration of bicalutamide with LHRH agonist therapy will be associated with significantly greater overall survival and an improved PSA response when compared to LHRH agonist therapy alone. This trial's design benefits from a strict inclusion attendance policy and methodical longitudinal follow-up protocols, which are expected to yield comprehensive insights into treatment efficacy.</ExpectedOutcome>
</ClinicalTrial>
