**Efficacy of Flexible Insulin Dosing Schedule to Enhance Glycemic Control in Adults with Type 1 Diabetes**

Inadequate glycemic control remains a significant challenge in the management of type 1 diabetes. This clinical trial aims to assess the impact of a flexible insulin dosing schedule, tailored to individual lifestyle and dietary patterns, on glycemic control in adult patients with type 1 diabetes. The hypothesis posits that personalized insulin dosing, when combined with an educational intervention, will result in significant improvements in both fasting and postprandial blood glucose levels over a 24-week period compared to a standard fixed dosing approach.

**Study Design:**  
This randomized controlled trial will be conducted across multiple outpatient centers, enrolling a total of 300 participants aged 18 to 65 years. Participants will be assigned to either the intervention group, receiving the flexible insulin dosing schedule, or the control group, adhering to a standard three-injection regimen. The flexible group will have their insulin doses adjusted based on carbohydrate intake, physical activity levels, and continuous glucose monitoring feedback to tailor to their daily routines.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Clinical diagnosis of type 1 diabetes for at least 1 year  
- Aged 18 to 65 years  
- HbA1c levels between 7.0% and 10.0%  
- Ability to utilize continuous glucose monitoring technology  
- Consent to participate in the educational component of the trial  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Co-existing significant endocrine disorders  
- Prior history of severe hypoglycemia unawareness  
- Current participant in another clinical trial  
- Changes in diabetes medication regimen within the past three months  

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
The primary outcome measure will be the change in HbA1c levels from baseline to 24 weeks, with secondary outcomes including the frequency of hypoglycemic events, insulin dosage adjustments, and patient-reported quality of life metrics assessed through standardized questionnaires.

**Study Procedures:**  
Both groups will receive continuous glucose monitoring devices throughout the intervention, enabling real-time glucose tracking and reporting. The flexible dosing group will additionally attend bi-weekly educational sessions focusing on carbohydrate counting, insulin titration, and lifestyle modifications, contributing to the integrity of the inclusion criteria while minimizing confounding variables that may arise from uncontrolled dietary practices. 

**Data Collection:**  
Comprehensive blood glucose data will be logged using the continuous glucose monitoring systems, with a focus on capturing variations in glycemic control, particularly after meals, to establish a robust correlation between individualized insulin regimens and improved diabetes management outcomes. 

**Expected Outcomes:**  
It is anticipated that participants in the flexible insulin dosing group will exhibit marked improvements in both fasting and postprandial glycemic levels, supported by the structured monitoring protocol. The use of targeted insulin education and real-time feedback will facilitate optimal dosing adjustments, contributing to achieving predefined glycemic targets more effectively than the control group.

This trial underscores the importance of personalized treatment plans in diabetes management and seeks to provide evidence that a flexible dosing schedule can significantly enhance patient outcomes in adults with type 1 diabetes.
