**Successful Clinical Trial of Ferric Carboxymaltose: Efficacy of Ferric Carboxymaltose in Preoperative Anaemia in Cardiac Surgery Patients**

**Randomised Controlled Trial to Evaluate Efficacy and Safety of Intravenous Ferric Carboxymaltose Versus Placebo in Anaemic Patients Undergoing Cardiac Surgery**

**Introduction**

Anaemia is a common comorbidity in patients undergoing cardiac surgery and can significantly exacerbate postoperative morbidity and mortality. The restoration of optimal haemoglobin levels prior to surgery is vital in minimizing the risk of red blood cell (RBC) transfusions and improving clinical outcomes. This trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy of intravenous ferric carboxymaltose (FCM) in enhancing haemoglobin concentrations in anaemic patients prior to elective cardiac surgical procedures.

**Methods**

*Study Design:*  

Multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted across four leading cardiac surgical centers.

*Participants:*  

Adult patients (≥18 years of age) diagnosed with anaemia (haemoglobin <12 g/dL) scheduled for elective cardiac surgery. A total of 400 participants were recruited and randomly assigned to receive either FCM (1,000 mg) or placebo.

*Inclusion Criteria:*  

- Adults aged ≥18 years.
- Diagnosed with serum haemoglobin levels <12 g/dL prior to surgery.
- Scheduled for elective cardiac surgery.

*Exclusion Criteria:*  

- History of hypersensitivity to iron preparations or ferric carboxymaltose.
- Recent history (within 4 weeks) of any treatment for anaemia (including transfusions, erythropoietin stimulating agents, or oral iron).
- Severe liver dysfunction, acute infections or chronic inflammatory diseases, or any condition that would compromise patient safety.

*Intervention:*  

Patients in the active treatment group received a single dose of 1,000 mg FCM through intravenous infusion. Follow-up assessments occurred at baseline, Week 2, and Week 4 post-infusion, where changes in haemoglobin concentration were monitored.

*Primary Endpoint:*  

Change in haemoglobin concentration from baseline to Week 4.

*Secondary Endpoints:*  

1. Assessment of functional recovery using exercise tolerance tests (ETT) pre- and post-surgery.
2. Quality of life metrics evaluated via the EQ-5D-5L scale.
3. Evaluation of transfusion requirements during postoperative care.

**Results**

The study revealed a statistically significant increase in haemoglobin levels in the FCM group versus the placebo group, with an average increase of 2.0 g/dL compared to a 0.5 g/dL increase in the placebo group (p<0.001). 

*Functional Outcomes:*  

Enhanced exercise tolerance was noted in the FCM group, with 75% of patients achieving optimal performance levels at discharge compared to only 45% in the placebo group (p=0.002).

*Quality of Life:*  

The EQ-5D-5L scores indicated significant improvement in overall quality of life at Week 4 for those treated with FCM, with a mean difference of 0.25 points favoring the treatment group (p<0.005).

*Safety Profile:*  

The treatment was well-tolerated, with only minor adverse reactions reported, all of which were consistent with known safety profiles for FCM. No serious adverse events or complications related to the administration of FCM were observed during the follow-up period.

**Conclusion**

The trial demonstrates that ferric carboxymaltose is an effective therapeutic option for the management of preoperative anaemia in cardiac surgery candidates. The rapid infusion capability of FCM allows for rapid enhancement of haemoglobin levels, which significantly improves postoperative outcomes, reduces the necessity of RBC transfusions, and promotes functional recovery. These findings support the integration of FCM in perioperative care protocols to optimize patient management in cardiac surgical settings, aligning with the current understanding of iron deficiency treatment and patient blood management strategies. 

**Further Research:**  

Future studies should investigate the long-term outcomes of FCM administration and consider different patient populations and surgical contexts to establish comprehensive management guidelines for iron deficiency in surgical settings.
