<clinical_trial>
    <title>Combination of Temozolomide and Targeted Therapy in Treating Patients with Advanced Neuroendocrine Tumors: A Phase II Study</title>
    <design>Randomized, open-label, multicenter study</design>
    <objective>
        The primary objective is to evaluate the efficacy of temozolomide in combination with targeted therapy compared to temozolomide alone in patients with advanced neuroendocrine tumors (NETs).
    </objective>
    <background>
        Neuroendocrine tumors are a heterogeneous group of neoplasms with varying degrees of malignancy. Temozolomide has demonstrated promising activity against certain subtypes of NETs when used as monotherapy. This study aims to enhance therapeutic response by integrating temozolomide with established targeted agents, addressing the need for more effective treatment regimens in advanced NET cases.
    </background>
    <primary_objective>
        I. To determine progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months using Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria among patients treated with temozolomide combined with targeted therapy versus temozolomide alone.
    </primary_objective>
    <secondary_objectives>
        I. To assess overall survival (OS) rates after 12 months of treatment.
        II. To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) based on RECIST criteria for both treatment arms.
        III. To analyze the safety profile and toxicity associated with the combined regimen.
        IV. To investigate the correlation of methyl guanine methyltransferase (MGMT) promoter methylation status with therapeutic outcomes.
    </secondary_objectives>
    <outline>
        Patients will be randomized into two arms: 
        <arm_a>
            Patients will receive temozolomide orally (PO) at a dose of 150 mg/m² daily for 5 days every 28 days in combination with a targeted therapy agent, which will be defined according to the patient's tumor characteristics.
        </arm_a>
        <arm_b>
            Patients will receive temozolomide PO at the same regimen of 150 mg/m² daily for 5 days every 28 days as monotherapy.
        </arm_b>
        Follow-up assessments will occur every 3 months for a total of 24 months post-treatment initiation, focusing on tumor responses and adverse events.
    </outline>
    <inclusion_criteria>
        - Histologically confirmed advanced neuroendocrine tumor.
        - Measurable disease according to RECIST v1.1.
        - Patients must have an ECOG performance status of 0-2.
        - Must have received prior systemic therapy, allowing for a controlled exploration of treatment efficacy.
        - Ability to comply with follow-up.
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
        - Previous treatment with temozolomide or the specific targeted therapy agent in question.
        - Patients with documented uncontrolled central nervous system metastases.
        - Any concurrent malignancy except for adequately treated non-melanoma skin carcinoma or in situ carcinoma.
        - Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
        - Patients who have had major surgery or infection within 4 weeks of randomization.
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <progression_free_survival>
        Progression-free survival is defined as the time from randomization until documented disease progression or death from any cause, evaluated through serial imaging and clinical assessments every 8 weeks for the first 6 months and then every 12 weeks thereafter.
    </progression_free_survival>
    <expected_outcomes>
        The hypothesis is that the combination of temozolomide and targeted therapy will offer a statistically significant improvement in PFS compared to temozolomide alone due to the potential synergistic effect and ability to overcome chemoresistance, as supported by previous studies indicating enhanced effectiveness in specific patient populations with advanced neuroendocrine tumors.
    </expected_outcomes>
</clinical_trial>
