**Failure Clinical Trial of Infliximab: Efficacy and Safety of Infliximab in the Management of Chronic Idiopathic Urticaria (CIU) Non-Responsive to Standard Therapy**

**RATIONALE**: Chronic idiopathic urticaria (CIU) poses therapeutic challenges, with many patients exhibiting insufficient response to conventional antihistamines or corticosteroids. Infliximab, a chimeric IgG1 monoclonal antibody targeting tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), was hypothesized to modulate the immune-mediated processes underlying CIU. Despite existing evidence suggesting TNF-α's role in urticaria-related inflammation, the present trial failed to validate infliximab’s efficacy in this context.

**PURPOSE**: This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of infliximab compared to a placebo in patients with CIU who remained symptomatic despite optimal treatment with antihistamines.

**OBJECTIVES**: 
- Evaluate the change in urticaria activity score over a 12-week treatment period.
- Assess the frequency and severity of adverse events related to infliximab therapy.
- Determine patient-reported outcomes encompassing quality of life indices at baseline and study conclusion.
- Establish predictive biomarkers correlating with clinical response to infliximab treatment.

**OUTLINE**:  The study comprised a multicenter, randomized, double-blind design:
- **Part A**: Patients received either infliximab (5 mg/kg IV at weeks 0, 2, and 6) or placebo.
- **Part B**: Post-treatment assessments were done to evaluate changes in urticaria activity score and quality of life measures.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS**: 
- Patients aged 18-65 with a clinical diagnosis of CIU of at least six months duration, refractory to high-dose antihistamines and corticosteroids.
- CIU diagnosed according to standard criteria with a daily itch severity score >6 on a 10-point Likert scale.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS**: 
- Adult patients (ages 18-65).
- Informed consent signed prior to study participation.
- Absence of significant comorbidities influencing chronic urticaria outcomes.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA**: 
- Patients with known hypersensitivity to infliximab or any murine proteins.
- History of malignant neoplasms within the last five years.
- Previous treatment with biologic agents targeting TNF-α.
- Active infections or chronic inflammatory conditions unrelated to CIU.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY**: 
- Strict washout periods mandated prior to trial initiation, excluding corticosteroids and biologic therapies within 3 months of enrollment.
- Ongoing stable antihistamine therapy permitted, but not required.

**RESULTS**: 
The trial enrolled 150 participants, equally distributed among treatment and placebo arms. Therapeutic evaluation showed no significant improvement in urticaria activity scores between the infliximab and placebo groups (mean change of -1.2 vs -1.1, p=0.36). Patient-reported outcomes and quality of life indices exhibited no clinically meaningful differences post-treatment. Adverse effects, particularly infusion reactions and serious infections, raised concerns about infliximab tolerability, leading to diminished adherence and premature discontinuation in some participants.

**CONCLUSION**: The trial results suggest that infliximab does not confer beneficial effects for patients suffering from CIU refractory to standard treatments. Insufficient therapeutic efficacy, compounded by adverse safety concerns and inadequate patient selection parameters likely contributed to the study's failure. The findings highlight the need for further research into alternative therapeutic agents and approaches in the context of this complex immune-mediated condition.
