**Successful Clinical Trial of Cyclophosphamide:**  
**A Study of Cyclophosphamide With Multi-epitope Peptide Vaccine and Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients With Advanced Stage Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Phase 2 Trial of Cyclophosphamide Combined With a Multi-epitope Peptide Vaccine and Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients With Advanced Stage Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

This Phase II clinical trial evaluates the safety, immunogenicity, and clinical efficacy of cyclophosphamide when administered in combination with a multi-epitope peptide vaccine and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in patients with advanced stage non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The objective of this trial is to explore whether the synergistic effects of the combined modalities can enhance therapeutic outcomes in a patient population with limited treatment options.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**

I. To assess the safety profile of the treatment regimen, specifically the incidence of adverse events (AEs) attributable to the combination of cyclophosphamide, multi-epitope peptide vaccine, and ICIs.

II. To determine the immunogenic response elicited by the multi-epitope peptide vaccine as measured by the frequency of Activated T cells specific for the targeted tumor antigens post-treatment.

III. To evaluate overall response rates (ORR) and progression-free survival (PFS) compared to historical controls in patients treated with standard chemotherapy alone.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**

I. To assess the impact of cyclophosphamide on the tumor microenvironment, particularly in terms of regulatory T cell (Treg) depletion, and consequent increases in tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) populations.

II. To characterize expression changes in immune checkpoint molecules on TILs before and after treatment.

III. To correlate baseline and post-treatment immunological markers with clinical outcomes, potentially providing additional prognostic insights.

**OUTLINE:**

Patients will receive cyclophosphamide orally (PO) at a fixed dose on days 1-3. Concurrently, patients will receive a multi-epitope peptide vaccine targeting defined neoantigens intradermally (ID) on day 1 and ICIs (PD-1 or CTLA-4 inhibitors) will be integrated into the regimen commencing on day 7 with subsequent doses every 3 weeks for a total of 6 cycles, until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occur.

After study treatment, patients will be followed every 3 months for 2 years to capture long-term efficacy and safety data.

**Inclusion Criteria:**

- Histologically confirmed advanced stage non-small cell lung cancer (Stage IIIB or IV) resistant to prior systemic therapy.
- At least one measurable lesion per RECIST criteria.
- ECOG Performance Status 0-1.
- Adequate organ function as defined by standard laboratory parameters.

**Exclusion Criteria:**

- Any prior treatment with ICIs or previous immunotherapeutic modalities.
- Presence of autoimmune diseases or history of significant allergic reactions to study medications.
- Active infection requiring systemic therapy.
- Known history of severe cardiac or pulmonary disorders that may compromise treatment safety.

**OUTCOMES MEASUREMENT:**

The primary outcome will focus on the safety evaluation based on the CTEP Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 for AEs graded 3-5, evaluated throughout the study duration. 

Immunological responses will be assessed through peripheral blood and tissue biopsy analyses to quantify antigen-specific T cell populations.

Clinical outcomes such as ORR will be determined based on imaging studies assessed at baseline, 8 weeks, and upon disease progression, utilizing the modified RECIST guidelines.

This trial aims to affirm the hypothesis that the incorporation of cyclophosphamide, in conjunction with targeted vaccine therapy and ICIs, modulates the immune landscape effectively, thereby enhancing anticancer efficacy and redefining the therapeutic strategy for a challenging cohort in advanced NSCLC.
