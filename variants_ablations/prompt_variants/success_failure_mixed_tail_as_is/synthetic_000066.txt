**Failed Clinical Trial of Bicalutamide: S0147**  
*Combination of Bicalutamide With Next-Generation Antibody-Drug Conjugates in Treating Patients With Advanced Prostate Cancer*  

**RATIONALE:** Dual-targeted therapies combining innovative antibody-drug conjugates that specifically target prostate cancer cells may offer enhanced antitumor efficacy. However, the integration of bicalutamide, an androgen receptor antagonist, with these next-generation therapies raises concerns regarding drug interaction and resistance mechanisms in advanced prostate cancer.

**PURPOSE:** Phase II trial aimed at assessing the efficacy and safety of bicalutamide in conjunction with next-generation antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) in patients presenting with advanced prostate cancer refractory to conventional hormonal therapy.

**OBJECTIVES:**  
- To evaluate the overall survival and progression-free survival in patients with advanced hormone-refractory prostate cancer treated with bicalutamide and ADCs.  
- To assess the toxicity profile, focusing on grade 3 and above adverse events during the intervention period.  
- To explore the impact of tumor biology and microenvironment on treatment response and adverse event occurrences.  

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, randomized study includes a two-arm approach. Patients are stratified by disease stage and prior treatment exposure.  

- **Androgen Blockade Therapy:**  
  Patients receive 50mg of oral bicalutamide once daily in conjunction with a standard regimen of an antibody-drug conjugate specific to prostate cancer, administered intravenously every 21 days. 

- **Experimental Arm:**  
  Patients initiate treatment with bicalutamide concurrently with the ADC, starting at day 1 of the treatment cycle. This treatment will continue until disease progression, or the emergence of unacceptable toxicity grade.

- **Control Arm:**  
  Patients receive the ADC treatment without bicalutamide, allowing for comparison of monotherapy against the combination.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of approximately 100 patients are expected to be recruited for this study within 3 years.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced prostate adenocarcinoma with documented progression on standard androgen deprivation therapy  
- Metastatic disease classified as M1 with visceral involvement or extensive bone disease  

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Age: ≥ 18 years  
- Performance Status: ECOG 0-2  
- Life Expectancy: Not specified  
- Hematopoietic: Absolute granulocyte count (AGC) ≥ 1,500/mm^3, Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm^3  
- Prior Concurrent Therapy: No recent systemic chemotherapy or radiation therapy within 30 days of enrollment  
- No clinically significant comorbidities impacting protocol adherence or eligibility  

**LIMITATIONS:**  
This trial is predicated upon the hypothesis that the anticipated synergistic effects of bicalutamide and ADC treatment will overcome tumor resistance. However, prior studies indicate that the patient population is heterogeneous, which may impede the trial’s ability to accurately capture treatment responses. Additionally, high-grade toxicities occurring in pilot screenings raised concerns regarding treatment continuity and patient adherence, which may adversely affect therapeutic outcomes.  

**RESULTS:** Preliminary data demonstrated limited efficacy, signified by suboptimal progression-free survival rates, exacerbated instances of hematologic toxicity, and worsened quality of life metrics compared to control groups. The combination therapy failed to demonstrate enhanced antitumor activity, raising critical concerns regarding drug synergies and adaptive tumor responses to dual-modality therapies.

**CONCLUSION:** The integration of bicalutamide with next-generation ADCs in treating advanced prostate cancer appears to be constrained by complex tumor biology, insufficient patient stratification, and unforeseen toxicity profiles, leading to the discontinuation of the study protocol. Future investigations are recommended to further explore the mechanisms of resistance and optimize treatment strategies tailored to the tumor microenvironment.  

**Note:** The results of this trial reinforce the necessity for a better understanding of both individual tumor characteristics and the role of the tumor microenvironment in the subsequent design of clinical trials involving bicalutamide and novel therapeutic agents.
