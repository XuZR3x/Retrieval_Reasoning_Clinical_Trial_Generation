<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Evaluation of Riociguat (Adempas, BAY63-2521) in Enhancing Digital Blood Flow During Cold Stress in Patients with Systemic Sclerosis-Associated Raynaud's Phenomenon</Title>
    <Design>Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled, Multi-center Study</Design>
    <Objective>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of riociguat in improving digital blood flow in patients suffering from systemic sclerosis-associated Raynaud's phenomenon during induced cold exposure. Secondary objectives will include assessing the safety profile and pharmacokinetic properties of riociguat after repeated doses.</Objective>
    
    <Methods>
        <InclusionCriteria>
            <Criterion>Adults aged 18-70 years diagnosed with systemic sclerosis and Raynaud's phenomenon for at least 2 years.</Criterion>
            <Criterion>Patients experiencing at least 2 episodes of vasospastic attacks per week despite standard protective measures.</Criterion>
        </InclusionCriteria>
        <ExclusionCriteria>
            <Criterion>Patients currently taking therapies that may interfere with digital blood flow, including calcium channel blockers, PDE5 inhibitors, or nitrates.</Criterion>
            <Criterion>History of severe hypertension (systolic BP > 160 mmHg) or contraindications to heart failure medications.</Criterion>
        </ExclusionCriteria>
        
        <StudyPopulation>140 patients with a confirmed diagnosis of systemic sclerosis associated with Raynaud's phenomenon.</StudyPopulation>

        <Endpoints>
            <PrimaryEndpoint>Change in digital blood flow measured by laser Doppler flowmetry following exposure to cold before and after administration of riociguat.</PrimaryEndpoint>
            <SecondaryEndpoints>Safety profile assessed by adverse events tracking and pharmacokinetic parameters derived from blood samples collected at baseline and 12 hours post-dose.</SecondaryEndpoints>
        </Endpoints>
    </Methods>

    <Results>
        <Outcome>Patients receiving riociguat exhibited a statistically significant increase in digital blood flow during cold exposure compared to placebo, with an average increase of 35% (p < 0.01). Furthermore, no serious adverse events were reported, and the drug was well tolerated across both treatment groups.</Outcome>
        <SafetyObservations>No significant differences in adverse events were observed when compared to placebo, corroborating the safety profile established in previous studies.</SafetyObservations>
    </Results>

    <Conclusion>This trial successfully demonstrates the potential of riociguat as a therapeutic agent for improving symptoms in patients with systemic sclerosis-associated Raynaud's phenomenon, supported by a robust methodology that mitigated potential biases, and well-defined clinical endpoints. Future studies may further elucidate long-term outcomes and optimal dosing regimens in this patient population.</Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
