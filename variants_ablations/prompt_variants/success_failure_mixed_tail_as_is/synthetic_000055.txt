<clinicalTrial>
    <title>A Randomized, Open-Label Study to Investigate the Efficacy and Safety of Tipranavir/Ritonavir versus Standard of Care in Heavily Treatment-Experienced HIV-1 Infected Patients with Extensive Drug Resistance</title>
    
    <objective>
        This study aims to evaluate the comparative efficacy and safety of tipranavir/ritonavir (TPV/r, 500mg/200mg twice daily) against standard of care in HIV-1 infected individuals with a history of multiple antiviral therapy failures and documented extensive drug resistance. The primary endpoint is to assess the proportion of participants achieving virologic suppression (HIV-1 viral load <50 copies/mL) at week 48.
    </objective>

    <inclusionCriteria>
        <criteria>1. Signed informed consent prior to trial participation.</criteria>
        <criteria>2. Adult HIV-1 infected individuals, males and females, aged ≥18 years.</criteria>
        <criteria>3. Treatment-experienced with documented resistance to at least two classes of antiretroviral drugs (NRTIs, NNRTIs, and PIs).</criteria>
        <criteria>4. HIV-1 viral load ≥ 1,000 copies/mL at screening.</criteria>
        <criteria>5. CD4+ T lymphocyte count ≥ 50 cells/mm³.</criteria>
        <criteria>6. Patients currently on a stable background regimen for at least 8 weeks prior to enrollment.</criteria>
        <criteria>7. Willingness to adhere to the study protocol including follow-up visits.</criteria>
    </inclusionCriteria>

    <exclusionCriteria>
        <criteria>1. Known hypersensitivity to tipranavir or ritonavir.</criteria>
        <criteria>2. History of severe hepatic impairment.</criteria>
        <criteria>3. Patients with documented genotypic resistance mutations against tipranavir.</criteria>
        <criteria>4. Pregnancy or nursing mothers.</criteria>
        <criteria>5. Patients with any AIDS-defining illness that is unresolved or symptomatic.</criteria>
        <criteria>6. Participation in any other clinical trial within 30 days prior to screening.</criteria>
    </exclusionCriteria>

    <primaryEndpoint>
        The proportion of participants achieving virologic suppression (HIV-1 viral load <50 copies/mL) at week 48.
    </primaryEndpoint>

    <failureReasons>
        <reason>1. Insufficient Efficacy Against Viral Resistance: The trial revealed a failure of TPV to demonstrate meaningful antiviral effects in heavily treatment-experienced patients exhibiting extensive resistance profiles, leading to <50 copies/mL suppression in <30% of subjects.</reason>
        <reason>2. Suboptimal Pharmacokinetics: Pharmacokinetic assessments indicated inadequate drug exposure in the target population, compounded by complex drug-drug interactions with existing therapies, resulting in subtherapeutic concentrations of TPV at critical time points.</reason>
        <reason>3. Limited Virologic Suppression: By the primary endpoint at week 48, less than 25% of participants treated with TPV/r achieved the desired viral suppression, significantly lower than anticipated compared to standard of care arms.</reason>
        <reason>4. Adverse Safety Profile: A notably high incidence of hepatotoxicity was observed in participants receiving TPV/r, coupled with gastrointestinal disturbances, which negatively impacted adherence and retention rates.</reason>
        <reason>5. Inadequate Utilization of Optimized Background Regimens: The heterogeneity and inadequacy of the background regimens resulted in inconsistent treatment responses, undermining the potential synergistic benefits of combining TPV/r with other agents.</reason>
    </failureReasons>

    <trialDuration>
        Total duration of the trial is planned for 96 weeks, with evaluations at baseline, 12, 24, and 48 weeks.
    </trialDuration>

    <locations>
        Multi-center trial across several clinical sites in North America and Europe, aimed at enrolling a diverse patient population reflective of the current HIV epidemic.
    </locations>
    
    <conclusion>
        The trial was prematurely terminated due to insufficient efficacy and substantiated safety concerns regarding the use of TPV/r in this specific patient population. This outcome necessitates a reassessment of the therapeutic approach for individuals with advanced drug resistance and highlights the critical importance of optimizing background regimens in future studies.
    </conclusion>
</clinicalTrial>
