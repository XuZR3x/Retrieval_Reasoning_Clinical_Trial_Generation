**Successful Clinical Trial of CC-5013: Phase II Multicenter Study Evaluating Efficacy and Safety of CC-5013 Combined with Low-Dose Dexamethasone in Patients with Relapsed and Refractory Multiple Myeloma**

**Protocol Title:** A Phase II Multicenter, Open-Label Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of CC-5013 in Combination with Low-Dose Dexamethasone in Patients with Relapsed and Refractory Multiple Myeloma

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of CC-5013 combined with low-dose dexamethasone in subjects with relapsed and refractory multiple myeloma. The secondary objective includes the assessment of the safety profile of the combination therapy.

**Study Design:** This is a Phase II, multicenter, open-label trial. The trial will consist of a treatment phase followed by a follow-up phase. Screening procedures will occur within 28 days prior to treatment initiation.

**Treatment Phase:** Eligible subjects will receive CC-5013 in 28-day cycles along with low-dose dexamethasone weekly. Study visits will occur every 4 weeks, and comprehensive hematologic and myeloma paraprotein laboratory assessments will occur biweekly during the first 3 cycles and then every 4 weeks thereafter.

**Follow-Up Phase:** All subjects will be monitored for survival, treatment-related adverse events, and post-treatment interventions regardless of the reason for discontinuation from the treatment phase.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Subjects must understand and voluntarily sign an informed consent document.
2. Age ≥ 18 years at the time of signing informed consent.
3. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of relapsed and refractory multiple myeloma.
4. Documented evidence of disease progression following at least two prior lines of therapy, including a minimum of one proteasome inhibitor and one immunomodulatory drug.
5. Measurable levels of myeloma paraprotein in serum (≥ 0.5 g/dL) or urine (≥ 0.2 g in a 24-hour collection).
6. ECOG performance status score of 0, 1, or 2 (Appendix II).
7. Ability to adhere to the study visit schedule and other protocol requirements.
8. Women of childbearing potential must have a negative serum or urine pregnancy test within 7 days of enrollment.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Pregnant or lactating females.
2. Any serious medical condition or psychiatric disorder that places the subject at significant risk from study participation.
3. Presence of central nervous system metastases.
4. Known active infections.
5. Significant laboratory abnormalities as defined by the following:
   - ANC < 1,000 cells/mm³ (1.0 x 10⁹/L)
   - Platelet count < 75,000/mm³ (75 x 10⁹/L)
   - Serum creatinine > 2.5 mg/dL (221 μmol/L)
   - Total bilirubin > 2.0 mg/dL (34 μmol/L)
6. Prior use of CC-5013 or any of the study drugs within 28 days prior to treatment initiation.
7. Prior history of grade ≥ 3 allergic reactions to thalidomide or desquamating rashes while under treatment with thalidomide.

**Endpoints:** 
- The primary efficacy endpoint is the overall response rate (ORR), defined as the percentage of subjects that achieve at least a partial response (≥ 50% reduction in myeloma paraprotein).
- Secondary endpoints include the duration of response, progression-free survival, overall survival, and the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events.

**Statistical Analysis:** Statistical analysis will be performed using predefined statistical methods to compare the outcomes of subjects treated with CC-5013 and low-dose dexamethasone against historical controls. Multivariate analyses will account for demographics and baseline characteristics to evaluate treatment efficacy.

**Conclusion:** This Phase II study aims to rigorously evaluate the safety and efficacy of CC-5013 in combination with low-dose dexamethasone in a well-defined patient population. The robust design emphasizes comprehensive safety monitoring and the collection of timely efficacy data to assess the potential of CC-5013 in treating relapsed and refractory multiple myeloma. The multicenter approach enhances generalizability and supports the goal of informing future therapeutic strategies in this challenging disease state.
