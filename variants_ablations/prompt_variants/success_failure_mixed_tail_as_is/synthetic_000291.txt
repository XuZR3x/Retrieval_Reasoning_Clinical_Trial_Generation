**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy and Safety of Eszopiclone in Patients with Comorbid Primary Insomnia and Major Depressive Disorder**

**Study Title**: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Eszopiclone in Patients with Comorbid Primary Insomnia and Major Depressive Disorder

**Background and Objectives**:  
Insomnia is a prevalent condition that can significantly affect quality of life and is often comorbid with major depressive disorder (MDD). This study aims to assess the efficacy and safety of eszopiclone in a patient population with both primary insomnia and MDD, recognizing the impact of poor sleep on mood and overall health.

**Study Design**:  
This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. A total of 320 subjects meeting the inclusion criteria will be enrolled. Subjects will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either eszopiclone (3 mg) or placebo at bedtime for a duration of 12 weeks. The primary endpoint will be the change from baseline in total sleep time (TST) as measured using patient logs, while secondary endpoints will include changes in Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D) scores, sleep latency, and overall quality of life assessed by the Quality of Life Enjoyment and Satisfaction Questionnaire (Q-LES-Q).

**Study Population**:  
**Inclusion Criteria**:  
- Male and female subjects aged 18 to 65 years.  
- A confirmed diagnosis of primary insomnia and major depressive disorder per DSM-5 criteria.  
- Reported sleep duration of less than 6 hours per night on average over the previous month.  
- Subjects must be in generally good health, excluding the conditions of interest.

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
- History of substance abuse or dependence.  
- Any significant uncontrolled medical condition (including uncontrolled diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, or severe hepatic impairment).  
- Recent history (within 3 months) of initiating or changing antidepressant medications, or ongoing psychotherapy specifically directed at depression.  
- Pregnant or nursing women; women of childbearing potential must use contraception.

**Methods**:  
The trial will consist of a screening period, followed by the 12-week treatment phase, and a subsequent 2-week follow-up for safety evaluation. The treatment will be administered in a double-blind manner to ensure both participant and investigator bias is minimized. 

**Endpoints**:  
- Primary Endpoint: Change from baseline in Subject-Reported Total Sleep Time (TST) as averaged from weeks 4 to 12.  
- Secondary Endpoints: Change from baseline in HAM-D scores, sleep latency, and scores from the Q-LES-Q.

**Assessment Parameters**:  
Metrics will include both subjective measures (patient-reported logs of sleep time and quality) and clinically relevant scales for depression (HAM-D). Longitudinal assessment through regular follow-up will ensure comprehensive evaluation of treatment effects and safety profiles. 

**Duration**:  
The study will involve up to 9 visits, with an overall duration of participation for each subject of approximately 16 weeks.

**Anticipated Outcomes**:  
It is hypothesized that eszopiclone will demonstrate significant improvement in subjective sleep measures, reduced depressive symptoms, and enhanced quality of life, leading to a favorable safety profile without significant adverse sensations related to withdrawal or rebound insomnia.

---

This trial design incorporates a focused inclusion and exclusion criteria, rigorous methodologies to reduce bias, and clinically relevant endpoints that emphasize patient-reported outcomes related to both insomnia and comorbid depressive symptoms. The anticipated data will contribute to expanding eszopiclone’s treatment indications and provide a robust safety and efficacy profile in this specific patient population.
