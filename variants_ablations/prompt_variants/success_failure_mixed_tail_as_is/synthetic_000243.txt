**Failed Clinical Trial of Galantamine: Evaluating Galantamine's Efficacy in Chronic Fatigue Syndrome**  
*An Open-Label, Multicenter Trial of Galantamine in Patients with Chronic Fatigue Syndrome*  

The purpose of this study was to evaluate the efficacy of galantamine in improving cognitive and functional impairments in adults diagnosed with Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (CFS). This open-label trial aims to determine the treatment's effect on fatigue levels, cognitive performance, and overall quality of life across a nine-month treatment period. It was hypothesized that galantamine would lead to significant improvements in measures of fatigue and cognitive functioning.

The study, comprising multiple tertiary care centers, included patients aged 18-65 who met the Diagnostic Criteria for CFS. Galantamine was initiated at a dose of 4 mg/day, which was to be titrated based on individual tolerability and clinical response to a maximum dose of 24 mg/day by week 12. Primary endpoints were assessed using the Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS) and the Cognitive Assessment Screening Scale (CASS) at baseline and after 36 weeks of intervention. Secondary endpoints included evaluations of quality of life measured by the Short Form Health Survey (SF-36) and patient-reported outcomes.

Despite the promising preclinical findings and earlier anecdotal reports of galantamine's benefit in cognitive enhancement, results from this exploratory phase III trial did not support significant improvements in the primary endpoints when compared to baseline measures. The observed attrition rate was notably high, with fewer than 30% of enrolled participants completing the study. High dropout rates were indicative of both suboptimal adherence and adverse events, leading to a dilution of any potential efficacy signals. 

A comprehensive review of patient profiles indicated a lack of robust patient selection criteria. The inclusion of patients exhibiting a wide variety of baseline cognitive deficits resulted in considerable heterogeneity, thus potentially masking the drug effect. Furthermore, many participants reported concurrent medication use that could have interacted adversely with galantamine, further complicating the outcomes. 

**Study Design:**  
A multicenter, open-label trial was designed to last for 36 weeks, with an initial recruitment period spanning from March 2021 to December 2022.  

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Age 18-65 years  
- Diagnosis of Chronic Fatigue Syndrome as per the Fukuda Criteria  
- Ability to provide informed consent  
- Presence of cognitive complaints defined by the CASS scoring at least 10 points  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Assessment of significant psychiatric diagnoses (e.g., major depression, anxiety disorders) requiring pharmacologic treatment  
- History of neurodegenerative diseases, including dementia and other related cognitive disorders  
- Presence of significant medical comorbidities limiting compliance  
- Current treatment with any cholinesterase inhibitors or similar pharmacological agents  
- Females who are pregnant or lactating  

**Efficacy Measures:**  
Primary endpoints included changes in fatigue measured by the FSS and cognitive performance evaluated by the CASS. Secondary endpoints comprised the quality of life assessed via the SF-36 scale and patient-reported outcomes. Longitudinal assessments were made thrice: at baseline, midpoint (18 weeks), and endpoint (36 weeks).

**Conclusion:**  
The study failed to demonstrate a significant therapeutic effect of galantamine in patients with Chronic Fatigue Syndrome regarding cognitive enhancement and reduction of fatigue. The limitations noted included inadequate duration of treatment, with assessments failing to encompass the potential chronicity of cognitive deficits, alongside the inadequacy of robust efficacy measures, as subjective fatigue and cognitive instruments might not have fully captured the complexities of symptomatology observed in the population. Adverse events reported were primarily gastrointestinal and exacerbation of fatigue, resulting in high dropout rates, revealing the necessity for a reevaluation of the pharmacological intervention in this condition. Further research focusing on more homogeneous patient populations, longer treatment periods, and improved adherence strategies is warranted to elucidate the role of galantamine in treating CFS.
