**Failed Clinical Trial of Ruxolitinib: Feasibility of Ruxolitinib Combined with Standardized Chemotherapy Regimen in Advanced Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC)**

A Phase 2, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Ruxolitinib in Combination with Chemotherapy in Patients with Advanced Triple-Negative Breast Cancer

**Objective:**  
The objective of this study was to investigate the efficacy and safety of ruxolitinib in combination with a standard chemotherapy regimen (docetaxel and carboplatin) in patients with advanced triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). The primary endpoint was overall response rate (ORR) after 16 weeks of treatment.

**Study Design:**  
This investigational study was designed as a multicenter, randomized, double-blind trial consisting of a safety run-in followed by a randomized phase. Following the initial safety evaluation, patients were randomly assigned to receive either ruxolitinib in combination with standard chemotherapy or placebo in conjunction with chemotherapy. Treatment cycles consisted of 21-day intervals.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced triple-negative breast cancer (Stage IIIB, Stage IV, or recurrent).
- At least one measurable lesion according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Life expectancy of at least 12 weeks.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
- Adequate hematological, renal, and hepatic function, as per protocol-specified laboratory parameters at screening.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Previous treatment with ruxolitinib or other JAK inhibitors.
- Known activating mutations of the PIK3CA or PTEN genes.
- Active central nervous system metastases.
- Uncontrolled medical conditions that may interfere with treatment compliance or evaluation of response.
- Current or previous history of any other malignancy within the last five years, except nonmelanoma skin cancers or localized solid tumors that require no further intervention.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
The primary endpoint was the overall response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of participants who achieved a complete or partial response according to RECIST 1.1 after 16 weeks of treatment. 

**Adverse Events Monitoring:**  
The study monitored the incidence of adverse events (AEs) associated with both ruxolitinib and the standard chemotherapy regimen, with a particular emphasis on those leading to treatment discontinuation or dose reductions.

**Results:**  
Preliminary results demonstrated that the addition of ruxolitinib did not significantly improve ORR in the treatment arm compared to the placebo group (ORR of 25% versus 22%, respectively, p=0.56). Notably, pharmacodynamic interactions between ruxolitinib and the standard chemotherapy agents led to diminished efficacy and a concerning rate of adverse events, including severe neutropenia and anemia, which resulted in increased dose modifications. Furthermore, the inclusion of participants with an ECOG performance status of 1 raised concerns regarding tolerability and treatment adherence, directly impacting the outcome measures of the trial.

The limited eligibility criteria resulted in a cohort that may not accurately reflect the heterogeneity of the TNBC population, ultimately compromising the generalizability of the trial results. The study relied heavily on ORR as the primary endpoint; however, the multifactorial nature of outcomes in advanced breast cancer undermined the ability to assess the specific contribution of ruxolitinib to overall therapeutic efficacy.

**Conclusion:**  
This Phase 2 clinical trial of ruxolitinib in combination with a standard chemotherapy regimen in advanced triple-negative breast cancer was terminated due to a lack of efficacy and significant safety concerns. The results highlighted the necessity for meticulous patient monitoring and the consideration of pharmacodynamic interactions in future clinical designs aimed at integrating targeted therapies with conventional chemotherapy approaches. Further research is warranted to explore alternative strategies for optimizing treatment outcomes in this patient population.
