<clinicalTrial>
    <trialTitle>Evaluation of Prednisone in the Management of Eosinophilic Esophagitis: A Randomized Placebo-Controlled Trial</trialTitle>
    <primaryObjective>The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy of prednisone as an adjunct therapy for patients diagnosed with eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE), with a specific focus on evaluating clinical symptoms and pathological improvements.</primaryObjective>
    <secondaryObjectives>To determine the effects of prednisone on eosinophilic infiltration as determined by histological analysis, and to monitor any potential adverse effects associated with corticosteroid therapy.</secondaryObjectives>
    <hypothesis>Prednisone treatment will lead to significant improvements in clinical and histopathological outcomes in patients with eosinophilic esophagitis compared to placebo.</hypothesis>
    
    <studyDesign>This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving adult patients (ages 18-65) confirmed to have EoE according to the latest diagnostic criteria. Patients will be randomized to receive either prednisone or a placebo for a duration of 12 weeks.</studyDesign>
    
    <inclusionCriteria>
        <criteria>Adult patients aged 18 to 65 with a confirmed diagnosis of eosinophilic esophagitis based on histological evidence of eosinophilic infiltration (≥15 eosinophils/high-power field).</criteria>
        <criteria>Patients must report symptoms related to esophageal dysfunction, including dysphagia, food impaction, and/or chest pain.</criteria>
        <criteria>Patients must provide written informed consent to participate in the study.</criteria>
    </inclusionCriteria>
    
    <exclusionCriteria>
        <criteria>Patients with prior corticosteroid therapy within three months of enrollment.</criteria>
        <criteria>Patients with known malignancies of the gastrointestinal tract.</criteria>
        <criteria>Presence of comorbidities positively impacting esophageal motility or inflammation, such as inflammatory bowel disease.</criteria>
        <criteria>Patients with known immunocompromised states, current infections, or contraindications to corticosteroid use.</criteria>
    </exclusionCriteria>
    
    <expectedOutcomes>The primary measure of efficacy will be the change in frequency and severity of symptom scores evaluated using a validated esophageal symptom assessment tool. Secondary measures will include esophageal biopsy results evaluated by pathologists for eosinophil count and patient-reported outcomes regarding quality of life.</expectedOutcomes>
    
    <rationale>Insufficient evidence in the current literature suggests that corticosteroids, while beneficial in acute inflammatory processes, may not provide sustainable efficacy in chronic conditions such as eosinophilic esophagitis. The unrepresentative patient selection criteria, particularly concerning symptom chronicity and previously inadequate treatment histories, could lead to a cohort with unmanageable symptoms, thereby adversely affecting the perceived success of the prednisone intervention.</rationale>
    
    <dosingAndAdministration>Patients randomized to the prednisone group will receive an initial dose of 40 mg per day for four weeks, tapering down to 20 mg per day for the following four weeks, while the placebo group will receive a matching placebo for the entire duration.</dosingAndAdministration>
    
    <confoundingVariables>Potential confounding factors could include ongoing dietary interventions, previous history of anti-inflammatory therapies, or co-administration of esophageal dilatation procedures that may not align with the corticosteroid treatment timeline, thereby introducing variability into outcome measures.</confoundingVariables>
    
    <anticipatedResults>Given the pathophysiological nature of eosinophilic esophagitis and issues seen in previous trials, including inadequate dosing and inappropriate patient selection, it is hypothesized that treatment failure will likely occur. The anticipated outcome may reveal no statistically significant improvement in both clinical and histopathological endpoints when compared to placebo, leading to a conclusion that prednisone may not serve as an effective therapy for this patient population.</anticipatedResults>
</clinicalTrial>
