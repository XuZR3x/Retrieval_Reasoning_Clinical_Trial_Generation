**Failed clinical trial of everolimus: Multicenter Phase II Study of Everolimus for Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Follicular Lymphoma**

**EudraCT Number:** 2023-018456-16

This is a multicenter, open-label, uncontrolled Phase II study aimed to evaluate the clinical activity and safety profile of everolimus in patients diagnosed with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma (FL) following at least one prior systemic therapy.

**Rationale:** Previous data on the efficacy of everolimus in indolent non-Hodgkin lymphoma, particularly follicular lymphoma, remains inconclusive, thus limiting the ability to predict treatment outcomes based on available preclinical evidence. The hypothesis posited that inhibiting the mTOR pathway could enhance the antitumor activity in this cohort, necessitating an exploration of potential clinical benefits.

**Objectives:**
- Primary objective: To assess the overall response rate (ORR) defined as the sum of complete remission (CR) and partial remission (PR) within the study population.
- Secondary objectives: To evaluate safety and tolerability, duration of response (DR), and progression-free survival (PFS) across all participants enrolled in the study.

**Inclusion Criteria:** 
1. Adults ≥ 18 years of age with a histologically confirmed diagnosis of follicular lymphoma, relapsed or refractory after at least one prior systemic regimen.
2. Documented measurable disease according to the International Working Group (IWG) criteria.
3. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
4. Adequate organ function, defined as: 
   - Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³
   - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
   - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 x upper limit of normal (ULN)
   - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN
5. Written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Presence of transformed or aggressive lymphoma.
2. Known history of CNS involvement by lymphoma.
3. Prior treatment with mTOR inhibitors or investigational agents within the last 30 days.
4. Serious comorbid conditions that could compromise participation (e.g., active infections, significant cardiac disease).
5. Pregnant or lactating women, or women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.

**Intervention:** Participants will receive oral everolimus administered daily (10 mg once a day) for a 28-day cycle, with treatment continuation until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.

**Projected Accrual:** Approximately 120 patients are targeted for enrollment over a 24-month period.

**Outcome Measures:**
- ORR will be calculated based on the number of participants achieving CR or PR assessed via imaging techniques following IWG criteria.
- Safety and tolerability will be characterized by monitoring adverse event frequency, severity, and impact on quality of life.

**Results and Rationale for Failure:**
Despite enrollment of the target patient population, the trial encountered critical issues leading to failure. 

1. **Insufficient Preclinical Evidence:** The study highlighted a lack of definable preclinical data that could appropriately guide treatment expectations, leading to unexpected ORR outcomes.

2. **Patient Population Heterogeneity:** The enrolled cohort exhibited significant diversity in prior therapies, disease stage, and molecular characteristics, resulting in challenges in data interpretation regarding differential responses.

3. **Limited Molecular Targeting:** The absence of biomarker-driven patient selection hampered the trial's ability to identify patients who could potentially benefit from mTOR inhibition versus those with tumors driven by alternative pathways.

4. **Suboptimal Drug Administration Strategy:** The predetermined dosing regimen of everolimus failed to achieve adequate therapeutic levels, contributing to limited efficacy and suboptimal response rates.

5. **Adverse Safety Profile and Tolerability Issues:** A notable incidence of significant treatment-related adverse effects curtailed patient adherence to the protocol, precipitating early terminations that adversely skewed efficacy assessments.

In conclusion, this Phase II trial elucidated the complexities surrounding the use of everolimus in a markedly heterogeneous population of patients with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma, underscoring the necessity for refined eligibility criteria, robust preclinical validation, and adaptive strategies focusing on biomarker-driven patient selection for enhancing clinical trial success.
