**Successful Clinical Trial of Adalimumab: Assessing Long-Term Efficacy and Safety of Sustained Adalimumab Therapy in Patients with Moderate to Severe Psoriasis**

**A Phase III, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial Evaluating the Long-Term Efficacy and Safety of Adalimumab in Patients with Moderate to Severe Plaque Psoriasis**

**Study Overview:**
This study aims to evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety of adalimumab (Humira®), an anti-TNF-α monoclonal antibody, in patients diagnosed with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis, utilizing a robust clinical design to mitigate bias and ensure validity of results. 

**Objectives:**
- **Primary Objective:** To determine the efficacy of adalimumab in achieving at least a 75% reduction from baseline in the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) score, compared to placebo at week 24.
- **Secondary Objectives:** 
  - To assess the long-term safety profile of continuous adalimumab therapy.
  - To evaluate Quality of Life (QoL) improvements using the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) at 12, 24, and 48 weeks.

**Methodology:**
This is a Phase III, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial. A total of 120 subjects between 18 to 75 years, meeting the established diagnostic criteria for moderate to severe plaque psoriasis, will be recruited. Patients with a baseline PASI score of ≥12 will be included.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Male or female aged 18-75 years at the time of signing the informed consent form.
2. Diagnosis of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis for a minimum of 6 months.
3. Patients are eligible if they have not achieved adequate control with conventional therapy (topical agents or phototherapy) in the prior 12 weeks.
4. Ability to provide informed consent and comply with protocol requirements.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Previous treatment with any biologic agents for psoriasis within 6 months prior to screening.
2. History of hypersensitivity to adalimumab or any of its excipients.
3. Serious or uncontrolled concomitant medical conditions.
4. Active infection or history of recurrent severe infections.

**Randomization & Treatment:**
Eligible participants will be randomized (1:1) to receive either adalimumab (80 mg initially followed by 40 mg every other week) or placebo subcutaneously for 24 weeks. After the initial 24-week treatment phase, all participants will receive active adalimumab treatment for an additional 24 weeks.

**Outcomes:**
The primary efficacy endpoint is the proportion of subjects achieving at least a 75% reduction in PASI score (PASI 75) at week 24 versus placebo. Secondary endpoints include changes in PASI score from baseline to weeks 12, 24, and overall QoL using DLQI. Safety assessments will include the incidence of adverse events, laboratory tests, and serious adverse event monitoring throughout the study duration.

**Data Quality Assurance:**
Stringent monitoring procedures will be implemented to maintain data integrity. All clinical sites will undergo rigorous auditing by clinical research organizations and adhere to Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines. Randomization codes will be maintained in sealed envelopes until the completion of the study to ensure blinding.

**Statistical Analysis:**
For the primary efficacy endpoint, a chi-square test will be used to compare the proportions of PASI 75 achievers between the adalimumab and placebo groups. A two-sided significance level of 0.05 will be applied.

**Sample Size Determination:**
To detect a significant difference in PASI 75 response rates, a sample size of 120 patients (60 per group) will afford 90% power with a 5% significance level. Allowing for a dropout rate of 20%, the target enrollment will be 150 patients.

**Ethics and Compliance:**
This trial protocol has been approved by the institutional review boards of all participating sites. Written informed consent will be obtained from all patients before initiation of participation in the study.

**Monitoring and Safety Assessments:**
Participants will undergo monitoring at baseline, weeks 12, 24, and 48, with assessments of clinical response, adverse events, laboratory assessments, and patient-reported outcomes. Incidence rates of adverse events will be recorded and analyzed per body system.

**Conclusion:**
This study is designed to robustly ascertain the long-term efficacy and safety of adalimumab in patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis. The successful implementation of extensive clinical monitoring, defined inclusion/exclusion criteria, and well-established methodologies will contribute to the trial's objectives, ultimately providing critical data on adalimumab's therapeutic potential in this patient population.
