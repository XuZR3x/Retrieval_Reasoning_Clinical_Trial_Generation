**Failed Clinical Trial of Candesartan Cilexetil: Candesartan and Efficacy in Acute Ischemic Stroke Management**

**Study Title:** Candesartan Cilexetil in Acute Ischemic Stroke Management: A Randomized, Placebo-Controlled, Phase III Trial (CASIM)

**Purpose:** This trial was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the angiotensin receptor blocker, candesartan cilexetil, in patients presenting with acute ischemic stroke and elevated blood pressure, in an effort to elucidate its role in neurologic outcomes and mortality.

**Hypothesis:** Administration of candesartan cilexetil in the acute phase of ischemic stroke will yield: 1. An absolute risk reduction of 7% in the incidence of composite outcomes comprising disability or death at 90 days relative to placebo. 2. A relative risk reduction of 20% in the occurrence of subsequent vascular events (including recurrent stroke, myocardial infarction, or vascular death) within six months compared to placebo.

**Background:** The management of hypertension during acute ischemic stroke warrants careful consideration, as recent guidelines advocate for the maintenance of elevated blood pressures to optimize cerebral perfusion. Previous trials have indicated possible benefits of candesartan; however, the inconsistent findings raise concerns about the robustness of evidence concerning its clinical utility. The ACCESS trial suggested potential efficacy but was underpowered, leading to ambiguities in clinical practice and recruitment enthusiasm for CASIM.

**Study Design:** This was a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial with a target enrollment of 3,000 patients. Eligible patients presenting within 24 hours of symptom onset and with systolic blood pressure ≥150 mm Hg were randomized to receive either candesartan cilexetil (4-16 mg once daily) or matching placebo for 10 days, with follow-ups at baseline, 30 days, and 90 days.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years and older with a confirmed diagnosis of acute ischemic stroke.
- Systolic blood pressure ≥150 mm Hg at presentation.
- Proficiency in providing informed consent or surrogate consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Stroke deemed unsuitable for thrombolytic therapy.
- Patients with significant renal impairment (creatinine clearance <30 mL/min).
- Known contraindications to angiotensin receptor blockers.
- History of significant hemorrhagic stroke or severe comorbid conditions impacting life expectancy.
- Pregnancy or lactation.

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
- Proportion of patients achieving a Disability Rank Scale (modified Rankin Scale) of 0-2 at 90 days.
- Incidence of the composite endpoint of all-cause mortality, recurrent stroke, and myocardial infarction within 180 days.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
- Changes in blood pressure over the treatment period.
- Evaluation of adverse events potentially related to candesartan therapy.

**Trial Conduct:** CASIM faced logistical challenges pertaining to patient recruitment. The controversy surrounding the necessity of intensive blood pressure control during the acute phase of stroke led to reluctance among enrolling physicians. This skepticism and competing clinical priorities significantly impacted the desired enrollment figures and trial feasibility.

**Results:** Ultimately, the trial was terminated prematurely after enrollment of only 1,200 participants. Data analysis indicated no statistically significant differences in primary or secondary outcomes between the treatment and control groups (p>0.05). The trial's failure could be attributed to five primary factors: 

1. **Insufficient Evidence Base:** Previous data lacked statistical strength, affecting recruitment enthusiasm among both clinicians and participants.
  
2. **Controversy Surrounding Blood Pressure Management:** The prevailing beliefs regarding the risks associated with lowering blood pressure during acute strokes severely impaired recruitment efficacy.
  
3. **Clinical Heterogeneity of the Population:** Patient heterogeneity, reflective of a wide range of underlying conditions, made it difficult to delineate the therapeutic effect of candesartan from natural variability in patient outcomes.

4. **Rigid Follow-up Protocols:** Stringent follow-up schedules (30 days, 90 days) led to poor adherence and high dropout rates, effectively diminishing statistical power.

5. **Unfavorable Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** The interplay of candesartan with other prevalent comorbidities undermined adherence and introduced risks of adverse events, dampening any potential positive outcomes.

**Conclusion:** CASIM underscores a notable instance of failure in elucidating the role of candesartan cilexetil in acute ischemic stroke management, revealing the complexities entrenched in patient recruitment and therapeutic expectations within this clinical domain. Further research is needed to elucidate the appropriate context for the utilization of candesartan within this patient population. The trial was coordinated by the University Hospital Oslo in collaboration with various centers across Scandinavia. Financial contributions originated from public health sources and AstraZeneca, which also provided drug supplies for the investigation.
