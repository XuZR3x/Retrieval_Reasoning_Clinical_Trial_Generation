Failed clinical trial of pregabalin: Efficacy of Pregabalin in Patients with Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy

A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of Pregabalin (150-600 mg/day) for the Management of Pain in Patients with Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy

The objective of this study was to assess the efficacy of pregabalin for pain relief in individuals diagnosed with diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN). The trial aimed to determine if pregabalin could produce statistically significant reductions in pain scores compared to a placebo group, within the context of a flexible dosing regimen.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Patients aged 18 years or older with a clinical diagnosis of type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus for a minimum duration of 5 years.
- Positive identification of diabetic peripheral neuropathy confirmed by clinical examination and nerve conduction studies.
- A baseline average pain score of ≥ 4 on the 11-point Numeric Rating Scale (NRS), utilizing a daily pain assessment over the preceding week.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Presence of significant comorbidities that could affect pain perception, including chronic pain syndromes or neurological disorders other than DPN.
- Current use or recent discontinuation (within 3 months) of other neuropathic pain medications, including but not limited to gabapentin, tricyclic antidepressants, or opioids.
- Creatinine clearance < 50 mL/min; history of major psychiatric disorders affecting compliance and pain reporting.

The primary endpoint was the change in mean pain score as measured by the NRS over the 12-week treatment period. Daily assessments were conducted to gather pain scores ideally suited for analysis towards understanding the analgesic efficacy of pregabalin versus placebo.

Results:
The study was halted after an interim analysis revealed insufficient statistical power to demonstrate significant differences between the treatment groups. Mean pain score reductions in the pregabalin cohort failed to outperform placebo, reflecting an inherent limitation of trial design whereby the chosen sample size likely lacked appropriate justification based on anticipated effect sizes or variance within the target population. Furthermore, the constrained demographics restricted recruitment to a specific subset of patients, diminishing the generalizability of outcomes.

Discussion:
Several critical factors contributed to the termination of the trial. There was a notable underestimation of the psychological components influencing patient-reported pain scores, including mood disorders often associated with diabetic conditions. Concurrently, an adjustment for concomitant medication usage was not feasible due to the study's design, potentially leading to confounded efficacy outcomes.

Conclusions:
This clinical trial elucidates the complexity in assessing the efficacy of pregabalin in a patient population characterized by variability in pain perception and treatment history. While pregabalin is known for its analgesic properties, this undertaking highlights how combined patient characteristics, inadequate control measures, and study design limitations can cumulatively undermine potential success. Further investigations with expanded inclusion criteria and robust statistical frameworks may be warranted to explore pregabalin's true capability in managing diabetic peripheral neuropathy.
