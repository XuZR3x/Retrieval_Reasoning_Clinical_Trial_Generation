**Failed clinical trial of avastin: A Study of Avastin in Combination With CAR-T Cell Therapy in Patients With Relapsed/Refractory Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma**

**A Phase II Study to Evaluate Avastin in Combination with CAR-T Cell Therapy for Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma**

**Primary Objective**

To assess the efficacy of Avastin administered in conjunction with Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy, measured by the overall response rate (ORR) in subjects with relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL).

**Study Design**

This is an open-label, single-arm Phase II study. Eligible participants will receive CAR-T cell therapy followed immediately by Avastin infusion. Participants will be monitored for clinical response and adverse events over a 12-month period.

**Inclusion Criteria**

- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of relapsed or refractory DLBCL
- Age ≥ 18 years
- Prior treatment with one or more lines of systemic therapy, which may include chemotherapy, targeted agents, or immunotherapy
- Measurable disease using RECIST criteria
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2
- Adequate organ function, including:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,000 cells/µL
  - Platelets ≥ 75,000 cells/µL
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL
  - Total bilirubin < 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN)
- Signed informed consent and compliance with study requirements

**Exclusion Criteria**

- Prior CAR-T therapy or anti-angiogenic agents, including Avastin
- Active central nervous system involvement
- Prior history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to Avastin
- Cardiovascular instability, including New York Heart Association (NYHA) Grade II or greater congestive heart failure within 6 months
- Existing uncontrolled hypertension (systolic blood pressure > 150 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure > 100 mmHg)
- Significant comorbidities that may interfere with treatment or compliance
- History of hypercoagulable states, including thrombotic events

**Treatment Regimen**

Participants will undergo leukapheresis to harvest T cells for CAR-T therapy, followed by the administration of the engineered CAR-T cells. Avastin will be administered at a dosage of 15 mg/kg intravenously on day 7 post CAR-T infusion. Treatment will continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal from the study.

**Endpoints**

- **Primary endpoint:** Overall Response Rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) according to the Lugano Classification.
- **Secondary endpoints:** Incidence and severity of adverse events (AEs) related to therapy, duration of response (DoR), and progression-free survival (PFS).

**Statistical Analysis**

The hypothesis underlying the investigation projects a 30% ORR as the historical benchmark for combination therapies. Efficacy analyses will be conducted using the rules of intention-to-treat. Kaplan-Meier estimates will evaluate PFS and DoR.

**Potential Reasons for Trial Failure**

1. **Insufficient Patient Selection:** Although enrollment criteria aimed to target patients with confirmed DLBCL, the underlying heterogeneous nature of relapsed cases may have resulted in varying responses to Avastin, particularly in those with complex prior treatment histories and comorbid conditions that adversely influenced treatment outcomes.

2. **Inadequate Treatment Regimen Compatibility:** The proposed combination of CAR-T therapy and Avastin may not have yielded the anticipated synergistic effects. The presence of immunosuppressive effects inherent in Avastin could potentially mitigate CAR-T cell function, thereby compromising overall efficacy in a disease inherently resistant to treatment.

3. **Elevated Toxicity Rates:** The trial encountered a notably elevated incidence of serious adverse events, specifically cardiovascular events and thromboembolic complications. The combined toxicity profiles of Avastin and CAR-T therapy may have contributed to an unacceptable rate of treatment discontinuation, directly impacting the success of measurable outcomes.

4. **Ineffective Endpoints for Evaluation:** The reliance on ORR as a primary endpoint may have failed to capture relevant clinical benefit regarding long-term survival or improvement in quality of life, thereby misaligning with the therapeutic objectives of both Avastin and CAR-T approaches.

5. **Tumor Microenvironment Resistance:** The existence of a suppressive tumor microenvironment, characterized by immune escape mechanisms and hypoxic conditions, could have thwarted the potency of CAR-T cell therapy when combined with Avastin, leading to deficiencies in the expected clinical benefits from the intervention.

In conclusion, this study aims to explore the efficacy of Avastin in conjunction with CAR-T cell therapy; however, potential failures may arise from the aforementioned critical aspects that could hinder the therapeutic objectives. The outcomes will provide valuable insights into principles underlying combination therapies in relapsed or refractory lymphomas.
