**Erlotinib Hydrochloride in Combination with Tazemetostat for Treatment-Resistant Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: A Phase II Study**

**RATIONALE**: This phase II trial investigates the efficacy and safety of erlotinib hydrochloride combined with tazemetostat, an EZH2 inhibitor, in patients with stage IIIB or IV non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have developed resistance following prior therapies. The mechanistic synergy of erlotinib targeting the EGFR signaling pathway with the pathway modulation instantiated by tazemetostat is hypothesized to enhance tumor suppression and improve clinical outcomes.

**PURPOSE**: The primary objective is to evaluate the objective response rate (ORR) of erlotinib hydrochloride combined with tazemetostat in patients with treatment-resistant stage IIIB or IV NSCLC. This study also aims to establish an understanding of the pharmacodynamics of this combination and assess potential pharmacokinetic interactions.

**OBJECTIVES**:  
**Primary**: To determine the objective tumor response (complete response [CR] + partial response [PR]) in patients receiving erlotinib and tazemetostat.

**Secondary**: 
- To assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).
- To evaluate the safety profile of the combination therapy, focusing on dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs).
- To analyze tumor biopsies for biomarkers of response, including EGFR mutations and EZH2 expression.

**OUTLINE**: This is a randomized, open-label, multicenter study. Following screening and baseline assessments, eligible patients will be randomized into two treatment arms. 

- **Arm I**: Patients will receive oral erlotinib hydrochloride 150 mg daily on days 1-28 and oral tazemetostat 800 mg once per day on days 1-28. Courses will repeat every 28 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
  
- **Arm II**: Patients will receive oral erlotinib hydrochloride 150 mg daily as in Arm I, with an additional cohort receiving tazemetostat in a lower escalation scenario to identify optimal dosage. 

Patients will be evaluated for response and safety every 4 weeks via imaging studies and clinical assessment, with post-treatment follow-up conducted at 60 and 120 days.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL**: A total of 150 patients will be enrolled over an estimated 12-month period.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of stage IIIB or IV NSCLC.
- Confirmed resistance to at least one prior platinum-based or targeted therapy, preserving performance status of ECOG 0-2.
- Documentation of available tumor tissue for genetic analysis.
- Adequate hematological parameters and organ function indicators as outlined [(ANC ≥ 1,500 cells/mm³; platelets ≥ 100,000 cells/mm³; creatinine ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal)].
- Written informed consent capability.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Active CNS metastases requiring treatment.
- Previous treatment with anti-EGFR agents or EZH2 inhibitors.
- Significant comorbidities posing medical risks as per operational medical judgment (e.g. cardiac conditions defined by NYHA class III or IV).
- Patients with unresolved toxicity from prior systemic therapy beyond grade 1, except alopecia.
- Concurrent participation in other investigational drug studies.

**SAFETY MONITORING**: A Safety Monitoring Committee will oversee the collection of adverse events systematically, utilizing CTCAE v5.0 criteria for toxicity. Early protocol-defined stopping rules will be established for any severe adverse reactions, ensuring patient safety while maximizing therapeutic efficacy.

**ENDPOINTS**: Primary endpoints include overall ORR as defined by RECIST criteria and safety assessment through incidence of adverse effects leading to treatment discontinuation. Secondary endpoints encompass OS and PFS metrics alongside pharmacokinetic evaluations to analyze drug interaction profiles between erlotinib hydrochloride and tazemetostat.

**TRIAL REGISTRATION**: This study will be registered within clinical trial databases, with adherence to the ethical principles underlying clinical research.

This trial is expected to yield significant insights into the combinatorial therapeutic approach for treatment-resistant NSCLC, directly leveraging mechanistic synergy to overcome resistance and enhance patient prognosis.
