**Successful Clinical Trial of Vandetanib:**  
**Phase II Study on the Efficacy of Vandetanib in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor in Patients With Unresectable Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**  
This randomized, double-blind, multi-center, Phase II study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vandetanib in combination with a PD-1 inhibitor in patients with unresectable, advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have received prior chemotherapy. The trial is predicated on the targeted mechanism of action of vandetanib, which inhibits both the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR) pathways, known to modulate tumor microenvironments conducive to immune evasion. This synergistic effect may culminate in heightened clinical benefit when partnered with immune checkpoint blockade.

The study design incorporates rigorous methodologies, including randomization, double-blinding, and centralized radiologic assessments per RECIST criteria, to minimize biases and enhances the reliability of outcomes by ensuring objective measures of tumor response. Our cohort consists of a well-defined patient population with histologically confirmed NSCLC, stratified both by prior treatment response and specific genetic mutations, such as EGFR mutations, to elucidate patient selection advantages for enhanced therapeutic outcomes.

Participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either vandetanib combined with a PD-1 inhibitor or the PD-1 inhibitor alone. The maximum dose of vandetanib was determined based on previous clinical experience, with the target safety profile closely monitored to ensure patient well-being throughout the study duration. Upon progression-free survival (PFS) assessment at 12-week intervals, patients in the control group were allowed to cross over to receive combination therapy, thereby promoting sustained engagement with the treatment protocol and collection of long-term efficacy data.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Patients aged ≥ 18 with histologically confirmed unresectable NSCLC.
- Prior chemotherapy regimens completed at least 4 weeks prior to enrollment, with no prior treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors.
- Presence of measurable disease per RECIST criteria with at least one lesion > 1 cm in diameter.
- Life expectancy of < 6 months.
- ECOG performance status of 0-1.
- Adequate organ function, including ANC > 1500/μL, platelet count > 100,000/μL, and hepatic function (total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x upper normal limit, ALT/AST ≤ 3 x upper normal limit).

Exclusion Criteria:
- Patients with known brain metastases.
- Concurrent anti-cancer therapy or investigational agents within 30 days prior to enrollment.
- History of severe hypersensitivity reactions to vandetanib or any component of the study drug.
- Uncontrolled hypertension (systolic blood pressure > 160 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure > 100 mmHg).
- QTc > 480 ms or known long QT syndrome.

The primary endpoint assesses PFS, while secondary endpoints evaluate overall survival (OS), objective response rates (ORR), and quality of life measures. Regular interim analyses and adaptive trial design feature prominently in the study to ensure the evolution of data-driven efficacy evaluations.

Data collection will encompass safety and treatment tolerability metrics, recorded as adverse events (AEs) undergoing ongoing assessment to evaluate the safety profile of vandetanib in combination with checkpoint inhibition. The trial's methodology aims to leverage the advantages gleaned from prior studies, including optimal patient selection, rigorous assessments of treatment response, and opportunities for enhanced patient care through flexible post-progression strategies.

The preliminary results project a promising trajectory concerning anticipated improvements in PFS, demonstrating the potential role of vandetanib in synergistic combinations aimed at amplifying immunotherapeutic outcomes in NSCLC management, thus showcasing the transformative effects of targeted therapies when rationally integrated into existing treatment frameworks.
