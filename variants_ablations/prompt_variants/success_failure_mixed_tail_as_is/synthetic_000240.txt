**Title: A Phase II Study of Panobinostat in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Background:** Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) constitutes the majority of lung cancer cases and has a significant morbidity and mortality burden. Immunotherapy with checkpoint inhibitors, such as pembrolizumab, has transformed the treatment landscape for NSCLC. However, treatment resistance remains a challenge, particularly in patients with advanced disease, necessitating novel therapeutic combinations. This study investigated the efficacy and safety of panobinostat, an HDAC inhibitor, in combination with pembrolizumab for patients with advanced NSCLC who had progressed on prior therapy.

**Study Objectives:** The primary objective was to evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) defined as the percentage of participants achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) based on RECIST v1.1 criteria. Secondary objectives included assessment of progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety profile.

**Methods:**
- **Design:** This was a multicenter, open-label, Phase II clinical trial.
- **Participants:** Enrollment included adults (≥18 years) with histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced NSCLC who had progressed on or after at least one line of platinum-based chemotherapy and had measurable disease.
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  - Provision of written informed consent.
  - ECOG performance status of 0-1.
  - Patients must demonstrate adequate hepatic, renal, and hematologic function.
  - Must have a documented PD-L1 expression level ≥ 1%.
- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Prior therapy with HDAC inhibitors or specific anti-PD-1/PD-L1 agents.
  - Active central nervous system metastases.
  - Patients with significant cardiac conditions (history of arrhythmias or ejection fraction < 50%).
  - Patients requiring constant or high-dose glucocorticoids for symptom management.

**Intervention:** Participants received oral panobinostat at a dose of 20 mg three times a week combined with pembrolizumab (200 mg IV every 3 weeks), until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal of consent.

**Endpoints:**
- Primary Endpoint: ORR as assessed by centralized radiologic review.
- Secondary Endpoints: 
  - PFS and OS measured from the start of treatment until the date of disease progression or death.
  - Safety and tolerability of the regimen, including incidence of adverse events (AEs) graded by NCI CTCAE version 5.0.

**Results:** The combination of panobinostat and pembrolizumab failed to demonstrate the anticipated synergistic enhancement of anti-tumor activity. 

1. **Inadequate Patient Selection:** The majority of enrolled patients exhibited advanced disease with multiple prior lines of therapy, thus presenting high intrinsic resistance. A significant proportion had visible tumor progression on previous treatments, risking an over-enrichment of resistant profiles, similar to previous failed trials of panobinostat.
  
2. **Combination Therapy Complexity:** Unexpected interactions between panobinostat and pembrolizumab were noted. Preclinical data suggested synergistic efficacy; however, the reality demonstrated a lack of additive effects, resulting in a dismal response rate with severe immune-related adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation in approximately 30% of cases. 

3. **Adverse Effects Profile:** The combination regimen resulted in a high incidence of toxicity profiles including severe gastrointestinal side effects (grade 3 nausea/vomiting), febrile neutropenia, and elevated liver enzymes, necessitating dose reductions and interruptions that compromised the treatment integrity and adherence.

4. **Issues with Drug Administration:** Oral administration of panobinostat presented adherence challenges. Patients often reported difficulty with maintaining their dosing regimen on scheduled days, leading to incomplete treatment cycles and exacerbating variability in drug exposure. Moreover, the pharmacokinetic profiles indicated inconsistent absorption, leading to less than optimal therapeutic levels.

5. **Insufficient Duration of Response Monitoring:** Patients were followed for a maximum duration of 12 months. The short follow-up period was inadequate to capture late-onset responses, limiting the comprehensive evaluation of long-term efficacy and contributing to skewed ORR results.

**Conclusion:** The Phase II study of panobinostat in combination with pembrolizumab did not meet its primary or secondary endpoints, exhibiting a low ORR (5%) with significant treatment-related adverse events. Future considerations should re-evaluate patient selection criteria, explore simplified therapeutic combinations, and assess longer follow-up durations to ensure capture of potential late responders. The inherent resistance mechanisms observed with panobinostat necessitate alternative strategies to optimize therapeutic efficacy in advanced NSCLC.
