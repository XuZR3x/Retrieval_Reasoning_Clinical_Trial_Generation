**Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Liraglutide in Overweight Patients with Type 2 Diabetes and Co-Morbid Hypertension: A 24-Week Multicentric, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial

**Background:** Liraglutide has consistently demonstrated advantages in glycemic control and weight management among patients with type 2 diabetes. Despite previous investigations predominantly focusing on glycemic outcomes, the current study aims to elucidate liraglutide's impact on hypertension and its potential mechanisms, thereby enhancing our understanding of its therapeutic profile across multiple dimensions of metabolic syndrome.

**Hypothesis:** It is hypothesized that overweight patients with type 2 diabetes receiving liraglutide will exhibit significant reductions in systolic blood pressure and HbA1c levels compared to those receiving placebo, with concurrent improvements in body weight and quality of life metrics assessed through validated patient-reported outcome measures. 

**Study Design:** This is a 24-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving multiple centers. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either liraglutide or matching placebo in conjunction with their current antihypertensive regimen.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Male and female patients aged 30–75 years with a diagnosis of type 2 diabetes as per ADA criteria.
2. HbA1c levels between 7.0% (53 mmol/mol) and 10.0% (86 mmol/mol).
3. Body Mass Index (BMI) ≥ 27 kg/m² and < 40 kg/m².
4. Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP) ≥ 130 mmHg and ≤ 180 mmHg.
5. Provision of written informed consent prior to participation.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of type 1 diabetes or secondary diabetes.
2. Current treatment with GLP-1 receptor agonists, DPP-4 inhibitors, or insulin within 3 months prior to enrollment.
3. Significant cardiovascular events within the last 6 months.
4. Known chronic pancreatitis or previous episodes of acute pancreatitis.
5. Renal impairment with eGFR < 30 ml/min/1.73 m².

**Endpoints:**
- Primary Endpoint: Change in HbA1c from baseline to 24 weeks, assessed bi-monthly.
- Secondary Endpoints: Change in SBP, body weight, and patient-reported outcomes (assessed via the EQ-5D scale) from baseline to 24 weeks.

**Results:** Preliminary analyses indicate that liraglutide treatment led to a statistically significant reduction in HbA1c (mean reduction of 1.5% vs. 0.3% in placebo, p < 0.001) and SBP (average decrease of 8 mmHg vs. an increase of 0.5 mmHg in placebo, p < 0.001). Additionally, participants demonstrated a mean weight loss of 4.2 kg compared to a gain of 0.5 kg in the placebo arm.

**Discussion:** The assessment of multi-faceted endpoints in this trial strengthens the understanding of liraglutide's benefits beyond glycemic control, particularly in managing hypertension and associated cardiovascular risk factors. The combination of comprehensive study design, targeted patient selection, and robust outcome measures corroborates the initial hypothesis. 

**Conclusion:** Liraglutide proves to be a valuable adjunct therapy in managing not only hyperglycemia but also hypertension in overweight patients with type 2 diabetes, showcasing its potential as an integral component in diabetes management protocols aimed at comprehensive metabolic health improvement.

**Keywords:** Liraglutide, Type 2 Diabetes, Hypertension, Randomized Trial, Patient-Reported Outcomes, Multicentric Study.
