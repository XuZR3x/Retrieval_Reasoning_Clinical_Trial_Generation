<report>
  <trial>
    <title>A Phase II Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Corifollitropin Alfa in Inducing Monofollicular Growth in Women with WHO Group II Anovulatory Infertility (Study ID: P05715)</title>
    <objective>The primary objective of this trial is to assess the efficacy of corifollitropin alfa in inducing monofollicular growth in women diagnosed with WHO Group II anovulatory infertility and to evaluate its safety profile during the ovulation induction process.</objective>
    <design>A Phase II, Open-Label, Non-Randomized Clinical Trial involving two stages: Stage I comprising a preliminary assessment of safety and efficacy in a small cohort (n=10), followed by Stage II, an expanded cohort (n=30) aimed at further evaluating the treatment's response and adverse events. The study will closely monitor key efficacy endpoints such as monofollicular response, alongside overall safety assessments.</design>
    <inclusionCriteria>
      <criterion>Women aged 18-40 years with a diagnosis of WHO Group II anovulatory infertility.</criterion>
      <criterion>Body Mass Index (BMI) within the range of 18-30 kg/m^2.</criterion>
      <criterion>Normal serum levels of Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH) and Estradiol at screening.</criterion>
      <criterion>Willingness to provide informed consent and participate throughout the duration of the study.</criterion>
    </inclusionCriteria>
    <exclusionCriteria>
      <criterion>History of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome or pre-existing endocrine abnormalities.</criterion>
      <criterion>Presence of ovarian cysts or malignancies as confirmed via ultrasound.</criterion>
      <criterion>Any clinically relevant laboratory abnormalities as determined during screening.</criterion>
      <criterion>Pregnancy or lactation.</criterion>
      <criterion>History of drug or alcohol abuse within the past 12 months.</criterion>
    </exclusionCriteria>
    <endpoints>
      <efficacyEndpoint>The primary efficacy endpoint is the rate of monofollicular growth defined as observation of one follicle ≥18 mm with no other follicles ≥15 mm at the administration of hCG.</efficacyEndpoint>
      <safetyEndpoint>Secondary safety endpoints include the assessment of adverse events, immunogenicity evaluation via antibody formation against corifollitropin alfa, and monitoring of pregnancy outcomes.</safetyEndpoint>
    </endpoints>
    <results>The trial faced substantial challenges attributed to the design and methodology, leading to inconclusive results. The open-label nature precipitated significant bias in outcome interpretation. The restricted sample size and niche target population resulted in a lack of statistical power to validate findings, thus questioning the reliability of the identified treatment effects. Furthermore, comprehensive monitoring for potential adverse pregnancy outcomes and neonatal complications was inadequate, limiting insight into the long-term safety of the intervention.</results>
    <conclusion>Results indicate that the study's design issues, including insufficient sample sizes, uncontrolled conditions, and narrow eligibility criteria, were significant contributors to the trial's inability to demonstrate a clinically meaningful efficacy. The data collected reiterated the necessity for more robust, well-controlled trials with extensive participant demographics and valid safety outcomes to ascertain the true potential of corifollitropin alfa in enhancing reproductive success in women with anovulatory infertility.</conclusion>
  </trial>
</report>
