**Failed Clinical Trial of Sorafenib Tosylate: Sorafenib in Treating Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**  
A Phase II Study of Sorafenib Tosylate for Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

The objective of this phase II clinical trial was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of sorafenib tosylate in patients diagnosed with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Sorafenib may inhibit tumor growth through the attenuation of tumor angiogenesis and the disruption of cellular signaling pathways associated with proliferation. Given the limited therapeutic options for advanced NSCLC, this trial aimed to explore the potential of sorafenib when administered as a single agent for patients with progressive disease.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To assess the objective response rate (ORR) in patients with advanced NSCLC treated with sorafenib tosylate.  
II. To characterize the toxicity profile and tolerability of sorafenib in this patient population.

**OUTLINE:**  
This multicenter, open-label study involved patients receiving oral sorafenib tosylate twice daily on Days 1-28 of each treatment cycle. Treatment cycles would continue in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:**  
A total of 50-100 patients were anticipated to be accrued over a period of 12 months.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Histologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer (adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, or large cell carcinoma).
- Staged IIIB or IV disease, based on the AJCC classification prior to enrollment.
- Patients must have measurable disease as defined by RECIST criteria, with at least one unidimensionally measurable lesion greater than or equal to 20 mm by conventional techniques or greater than or equal to 10 mm by CT scan.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate renal function (creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min) and liver function (bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal, AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal).
- At least 4 weeks since major surgery, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy, and recovered from any acute side effects.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Patients with brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease.
- Active cardiac disease or uncontrolled hypertension.
- Previous treatment with any anti-angiogenic agent or experimental therapies within 30 days prior to study entry.
- Concurrent malignancies, except those with a sufficient disease-free interval (≥5 years).

**RESULTS:**  
- **Limited Objective Response Rates:** The trial demonstrated an objective response rate of only 12%, which fell significantly below the clinically meaningful threshold established prior to study commencement. This inadequacy in response substantially diminished the justification for further therapeutic advancement involving sorafenib.
  
- **Substantial Toxicity Profiles:** The incidence of adverse effects was pronounced, with significant rates of grade 3 or higher toxicities including hypertension, fatigue, and skin rash reported. Approximately 30% of participants experienced intolerable side effects that necessitated dose interruptions and early treatment discontinuations.
  
- **Ineffective Mechanistic Synergy:** Although initial hypotheses suggested that sorafenib would effectively inhibit angiogenesis through its mechanism of action, the trial revealed no significant pharmacodynamic correlation with markers of vascular endothelial growth factor expression in tumor tissue, indicating poor target engagement.

- **Inadequate Patient Accrual:** The trial was hindered by challenges in enrolling patients, ultimately achieving an accrual of only 45 patients, reflective of insufficient clinical equipoise regarding the potential benefits of sorafenib in advanced NSCLC.

- **Poor Disease Characteristics:** The complexity of the patient's disease characteristics, including a high proportion of patients with extensive pleural effusion and prior chemoresistance, compounded the ineffectiveness of sorafenib thereby exacerbating treatment resistance and lowering the observed clinical outcomes.

Based on these findings, the study concluded that sorafenib tosylate did not demonstrate sufficient clinical efficacy in this setting and was accompanied by a substantial adverse effect burden, leading to the cessation of the trial.
