**Failed clinical trial of ldk378: Phase II Study of LDK378 in Patients With Advanced ALK-rearranged Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Who Have Previously Experienced Treatment Failure/Resistance**

**A Phase II, Open-label Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of LDK378 in Patients With Advanced ALK-rearranged Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Who Have Previously Exhibited Resistance to Crizotinib and/or Alectinib**

**Study Objectives:**  
The primary objective of this study is to assess the objective response rate (ORR) of LDK378 in patients with advanced ALK-rearranged non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have previously experienced treatment resistance to first-line ALK inhibitors such as crizotinib or alectinib. Secondary objectives include evaluation of progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), safety, and tolerability of the intervention in this patient population.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of ALK-rearranged stage IIIB or IV NSCLC.  
- Patients must have experienced documented disease progression following treatment with crizotinib and/or alectinib.
- Patients must possess measurable disease per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Age ≥ 18 years at the time of consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients with symptomatic central nervous system (CNS) metastases.
- Previous therapy with other investigational ALK inhibitors aside from crizotinib and alectinib.
- Significant cardiac dysfunction or a recent cardiac event (within 6 months).
- Documented history of interstitial lung disease or pulmonary complications related to previous therapies.
- Any contraindications related to compounds in LDK378 formulation.

**Treatment Protocol:**  
LDK378 will be administered at a dose of 750 mg orally once daily continuously until disease progression as determined by RECIST 1.1 criteria, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal.

**Sample Size:**  
Given the anticipated 15% response rate based on prior studies with ALK inhibitors in similar cohorts, a sample size of 60 evaluable patients will be required.

**Endpoints:**  
The primary endpoint is the ORR defined as the proportion of subjects with a best overall response of complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as evaluated by independent radiological assessment. Secondary endpoints include PFS, OS, safety and tolerability assessed via the incidence of adverse events (AEs) including dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs).

**Study Outcomes:**  
1. **Insufficient Target Population:** The cohort represented a heterogeneous population with varied resistance mechanisms to prior therapies, resulting in an overall unfavorable response rate that fell short of the therapeutic efficacy anticipated.
  
2. **Dose-limiting Toxicities (DLTs) Observed:** The study noted a concerning incidence of DLTs, predominantly comprising liver enzyme elevations and gastrointestinal disturbances, significantly impacting treatment adherence and leading to frequent dose adjustments.

3. **Suboptimal Combination Efficacy:** The investigational single-agent use of LDK378 did not yield synergistic interaction effects, limiting the clinical benefit and raising doubts about the inherent effectiveness of the agent in heavily pre-treated patients who may have developed compensatory resistance pathways.

4. **Limited Biomarker Correlation:** Although patients were chosen based on ALK rearrangement status, predictive biomarker assessments did not correlate with observed ORR, highlighting the potential inadequacies of current screening methodologies for this pathway in the selected demographics.

5. **Strict Exclusionary Criteria:** The stringent eligibility criteria severely limited patient recruitment, affecting the generalizability of study findings and subsequently leading to a diminished sample size which further impacted statistical power.

Overall, the study was terminated early due to an inability to demonstrate meaningful efficacy, combined with high rates of adverse events suggesting a poor risk-benefit ratio in the investigated patient cohort. Future studies need to reassess the pharmacologic approach and the incorporation of comprehensive biomarker profiling to identify subsets of patients more likely to benefit from LDK378 therapy.
