**Failed clinical trial of itacitinib: Evaluation of Itacitinib in Combination With Various Chemotherapeutic Regimens in Advanced Malignant Neoplasms**

**Official Title:** A Phase 2 Study of Itacitinib (INCB039110) in Combination With Standard Chemotherapeutic Agents in Patients With Advanced Malignant Neoplasms

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study was to assess the safety and efficacy of itacitinib when used in combination with various standard chemotherapeutic regimens for patients with advanced malignancies. Despite initial hypotheses suggesting potential benefits, the study was terminated due to a lack of clinically significant efficacy coupled with an unacceptable safety profile, characterized by dose-limiting toxicities.

**Study Design:** This multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial aimed to evaluate the combination of itacitinib with established therapies, including carboplatin, paclitaxel, and 5-fluorouracil. The study was designed in two parts: Part 1 focused on dose-finding and safety, while Part 2 sought to compare the overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) of subjects receiving itacitinib in combination with standard therapy versus placebo.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced malignant neoplasm.
2. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0 to 2.
3. Patients must have received no more than one prior systemic chemotherapy regimen.
4. Life expectancy of ≥ 12 weeks.
5. Age ≥ 18 years.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of severe allergic reactions to any JAK inhibitors or related compounds.
2. Known central nervous system metastases or significant hemorrhagic diathesis.
3. Presence of ≥ Grade 2 neuropathy or other significant concurrent medical conditions.
4. Prior treatment with any investigational agents within 30 days prior to enrollment.
5. Uncontrolled comorbidities including major organ dysfunction, cardiovascular disorders, or infectious diseases.

**Endpoints:**
- Primary Endpoint: Overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months.
- Secondary Endpoints: Safety and tolerability, incidence of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs), and assessment of pharmacokinetic interactions with chemotherapeutic agents.

**Results:** The trial experienced numerous challenges, leading to its premature termination. The combination of itacitinib with standard chemotherapeutic regimens did not yield statistically significant improvements in OS or PFS metrics when compared with placebo administration. The observed efficacy parameters exhibited inadequate therapeutic benefit, necessitating a reevaluation of the therapeutic approach.

Dose-limiting toxicities were reported at higher-than-anticipated rates, highlighting concerns with the safety profile of itacitinib in this patient population. Notably, serious adverse events, including grade 3 and 4 toxicities related to hematologic parameters and organ function, resulted in a reassessment of the maximum tolerated dose (MTD), eliminating the potential for further exploration of promising dosing regimens.

Additionally, the demographic limitations of the included patient population, particularly the narrow inclusion criteria focusing primarily on patients with a single prior treatment, led to an insufficiently diverse cohort. This exclusion strategy potentially limited the generalizability of the findings and resulted in skewed efficacy results observed in the clinical trial.

**Conclusion:** Due to the insufficient efficacy demonstrated in survival metrics, coupled with the notable safety concerns identified during the trial, the sponsor elected not to proceed with further phases of the study. The clinical trial of itacitinib in combination with established chemotherapeutic regimens ultimately failed to meet key therapeutic objectives, prompting a reevaluation of its role in the treatment of advanced malignancies. Further research is warranted to define the therapeutic landscape of itacitinib or explore alternative combinations that may yield more favorable outcomes.
