<ClinicalTrial>
    <TrialID>CTC-2023-001</TrialID>
    <Title>Impact of Cilostazol on Angiographic Outcomes Post Percutaneous Coronary Intervention in Patients with Diabetes Mellitus</Title>
    <Description>
        This double-blind, randomized, multicenter trial aims to investigate the efficacy of cilostazol when used in conjunction with dual antiplatelet therapy in patients with diabetes mellitus undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with drug-eluting stents (DES). The study seeks to evaluate the effects of cilostazol on angiographic endpoints, specifically neointimal hyperplasia and in-stent late loss, compared to standard dual antiplatelet therapy alone.
    </Description>
    <Purpose>
        The primary objective is to determine whether cilostazol significantly reduces neointimal hyperplasia after DES implantation in patients with diabetes compared to placebo, thereby supporting cilostazol's established role in enhancing stent performance in high-risk populations.
    </Purpose>
    <Design>
        This is a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled study. Patients will be randomly assigned to receive either cilostazol (100 mg twice daily) in addition to aspirin (75 mg daily) and clopidogrel (75 mg daily) or a placebo alongside the standard dual antiplatelet therapy for a duration of 12 months.
    </Design>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>1. Patients aged between 40 and 75 years diagnosed with diabetes mellitus.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>2. Patients with documented ischemic heart disease requiring PCI.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>3. De novo coronary lesions with a diameter stenosis ≥ 50% and lesion length ≥ 25 mm.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>4. Patients eligible for DES implantation, with no previous stenting in the target vessel.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>1. Active bleeding diathesis or known hypersensitivity to cilostazol or related medications.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>2. Patients with recent acute coronary syndromes or myocardial infarction within the past month.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>3. Any significant comorbidities that impair life expectancy, including chronic renal or hepatic failure.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>4. History of hemiplegia or significant cerebral hemorrhage.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <Endpoints>
        <PrimaryEndpoint>In-stent late loss assessed via angiography at 12 months.</PrimaryEndpoint>
        <SecondaryEndpoints>
            <SecondaryEndpoint>1. Composite of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) including death, myocardial infarction, and target lesion revascularization.</SecondaryEndpoint>
            <SecondaryEndpoint>2. Incidence of in-stent and in-segment restenosis at 12 months.</SecondaryEndpoint>
            <SecondaryEndpoint>3. Assessment of quality of life using the EQ-5D scale.</SecondaryEndpoint>
        </SecondaryEndpoints>
    </Endpoints>
    <SafetyMonitoring>
        Adverse events will be closely monitored throughout the study. All participants will undergo regular follow-up evaluations, including laboratory tests and physical examinations, to assess safety profiles and medication tolerability.
    </SafetyMonitoring>
    <DataCollection>
        Data will be collected through site visits at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months post-intervention, with angiographic assessments performed at the 12-month milestone.
    </DataCollection>
    <ExpectedOutcomes>
        Given the established efficacy of cilostazol in treating neointimal hyperplasia and the comprehensive evaluation of cardiovascular outcomes, we anticipate significant improvement in angiographic findings and reduction in overall adverse cardiovascular events in the cilostazol group compared to the control group. This clinical trial aligns with previous successful studies and emphasizes cilostazol's potential as a standard adjunct therapy for patients with diabetes undergoing PCI.
    </ExpectedOutcomes>
</ClinicalTrial>
