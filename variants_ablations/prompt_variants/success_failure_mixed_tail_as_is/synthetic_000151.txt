**Failed clinical trial of arsenic trioxide: Arsenic Trioxide for Treatment-Resistant Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)**

A Phase II Study of Arsenic Trioxide in Patients With Treatment-Resistant Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)

**RATIONALE**: Arsenic trioxide has previously demonstrated some efficacy in hematologic malignancies; however, its applicability in treatment-resistant acute myeloid leukemia remains uncertain. This study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of arsenic trioxide in patients with refractory AML. Despite the theoretical capacity of the drug to induce apoptosis and differentiation in leukemic cells, factors such as inadequate targeting of leukemic stem cells and hematological toxicity may impair clinical outcomes.

**PURPOSE**: The purpose of this Phase II study is to assess the safety, maximum tolerated dose, and efficacy of arsenic trioxide in participants with treatment-resistant acute myeloid leukemia, with an emphasis on survival rates and hematological recovery.

**OBJECTIVES**:
- To ascertain the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of arsenic trioxide in participants with treatment-resistant AML.
- To evaluate the incidence of complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as defined by standard hematologic criteria.
- To monitor adverse effects, specifically hematological toxicity and cardiotoxicity, as primary safety measures.

**OUTLINE**: This is a multi-center, open-label, single-arm study. Participants will receive arsenic trioxide intravenously over 1 to 2 hours at varying doses for 28 days, followed by a four-week interval for response evaluation. Dose escalation will occur until the MTD is reached, defined as the highest dose at which no more than one out of six patients experiences dose-limiting toxicity.

**NUMBER OF PARTICIPANTS**: Total of 50 eligible participants will be enrolled across multiple institutions over an 18-month period.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Histologically confirmed acute myeloid leukemia (AML) with refractory or relapsed disease following at least two lines of prior therapy.
- Patients must exhibit persistent disease characterized by clinical progression or elevated blast counts.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS**:
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status ≤ 2.
- Life expectancy of >3 months.
- Hematologic inclusion criteria: 
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) > 1,000/mm³.
  - Platelet count > 50,000/mm³, provided that bleeding risk and active infections are controlled.
- Patients must not present with any pre-existing cardiac disease that could exacerbate the potential for QTc interval prolongation.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA**:
- Confirmed diagnosis of treatment-resistant AML.
- Informed consent provided prior to study commencement.
- Women of child-bearing potential must provide a negative pregnancy test and agree to adequate contraception.
  
**EXCLUSION CRITERIA**:
- History of significant cardiovascular events (e.g., myocardial infarction, congestive heart failure) within the last six months.
- Presence of active infections or other uncontrolled medical conditions.
- QTc interval exceeding 450 ms at baseline.
- Patients who received any investigational agents or chemotherapy within 28 days prior to enrollment.

**ENDPOINTS**:
- Primary endpoint: Number of patients achieving CR/PR as assessed by bone marrow biopsy and cytogenetic studies.
- Secondary endpoints: Time to progression, overall survival rate, and quantification of hematological adverse events including rates of cytopenias and transfusion dependency.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL**: A total of 50 patients will be accrued for this study over approximately 18 months.

**SAFETY ASSESSMENT**: Adverse events will be monitored meticulously, focusing on hematological toxicity, cardiotoxicity indications (including Torsades de Pointes), and other Serious Adverse Events (SAEs) throughout the course of the study.

Unfortunately, this trial was terminated early due to multiple factors. These included the ineffectiveness of arsenic trioxide in effectively targeting the leukemia stem cell population and inadequate dosing adjustments which led to significant incidences of prolonged cytopenias and adverse hematological effects. Despite initial optimism regarding the potential of arsenic trioxide, limitations in appropriate patient selection and unacceptable toxicity profiles ultimately resulted in suboptimal therapeutic outcomes and a failure to meet primary efficacy endpoints.
