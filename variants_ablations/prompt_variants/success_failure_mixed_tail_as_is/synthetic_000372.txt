**Successful Clinical Trial of Fentanyl: Efficacy and Safety of Fentanyl Buccal Tablet in the Management of Breakthrough Pain in Cancer Patients**

**FT-021-IM**  
**A Double-blind, Randomized, Placebo-controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy of Fentanyl Buccal Tablets in Patients Experiencing Breakthrough Pain Due to Cancer**

**Primary Objectives:**  
- To evaluate the efficacy of fentanyl buccal tablets titrated at doses of 100 µg, 200 µg, and 400 µg in achieving significant pain relief compared to placebo in patients with cancer-related breakthrough pain.  
- To assess the long-term safety profile of fentanyl buccal tablets in this population.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
- To analyze the relationship between background analgesic therapy and the efficacy of the titrated fentanyl buccal tablets.  
- To investigate patient-reported satisfaction and quality of life improvements following treatment with fentanyl buccal tablets.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Patients who have provided informed consent according to local requirements prior to any trial-related activities.  
2. Adults aged ≥18 years diagnosed with any form of cancer, experiencing breakthrough pain.  
3. Patients must be on a stable regimen of opioids for background pain management for at least one month, with an equivalent dose of 60-500 mg oral morphine/day or transdermal fentanyl 25-200 µg/hour.  
4. Stable background pain intensity, averaging ≤4 on an 11-point Numeric Rating Scale (NRS), managed with background opioids.  
5. Patients must experience at least three breakthrough pain episodes weekly that last for more than 15 minutes and require additional analgesia.  
6. Patients can utilize buccal drugs appropriately and must have a negative pregnancy test if applicable.  
7. Patients must agree to utilize adequate contraceptive precautions throughout the trial period.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Patients with a history of substance use disorder or drug dependency.  
2. Pregnant or nursing females.  
3. Patients with significant neurological or psychiatric disorders affecting data collection.  
4. Patients with severe hepatic impairment, as judged by the investigator.  
5. Patients who have undergone facial radiotherapy within the last six months.  
6. History of hypersensitivity to fentanyl, other opioids, or any excipients pertaining to fentanyl buccal tablets.  
7. Patients currently enrolled in other investigational studies involving the use of investigational drugs or devices.

**Methodology:**  
This clinical trial utilized a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled design, comprised of a methodologically rigorous patient recruitment strategy with a focus on creating a homogeneous sample. The dosing of fentanyl buccal tablets was systematically adjusted based on individual patient response, carefully monitored through validated pain assessment metrics including NRS and VAS, reaffirming the statistical significance of anesthesia achieved with fentanyl at multiple dose levels.  

In the long-term safety evaluation, adverse events were recorded and assessed to establish fentanyl’s tolerability within the cancer patient demographic, emulating criteria of prior success from existing trials. Concomitantly, pharmacokinetic data regarding fentanyl absorption and metabolism were gathered to further validate the safety profile of this innovative formulation.

**Endpoints:**  
The primary efficacy endpoint was demonstrated through measurable improvements in pain relief, defined as a minimum 30% reduction in pain intensity from baseline following administration of fentanyl buccal tablets. Secondary endpoints encompassed the frequency of breakthrough pain and patient-reported outcomes assessing overall satisfaction and quality of life throughout the treatment protocol.

**Conclusion:**  
The clinical findings confirmed the hypothesis that fentanyl buccal tablets provide superior analgesic efficacy with a promising safety profile for cancer patients experiencing breakthrough pain, marking this trial as a pivotal advancement in palliative oncology care. The composite outcomes derived from rigorous analysis point towards a paradigm shift in breakthrough pain management strategies within this sensitive cohort. Thus, this clinical trial establishes a new therapeutic option for improved end-of-life care.
