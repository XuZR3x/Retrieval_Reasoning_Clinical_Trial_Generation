**Title: Efficacy and Safety of Telbivudine in Patients with Chronic Hepatitis B Related Liver Disease: A Randomized Controlled Trial**

**Study Design: Randomized, Double-Blind, Multicenter Study Evaluating Telbivudine Versus Placebo in Patients With Compensated Liver Disease Due to Chronic Hepatitis B Virus Infection**

**Objective:**  
To assess the efficacy and safety of telbivudine compared to placebo in the treatment of patients with compensated liver disease secondary to chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection.

**Background:**  
Chronic hepatitis B infection often leads to significant liver morbidity, including cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. While there are various treatment regimens available, there is a need for effective antiviral therapies that not only suppress viral replication but also confer improvements in clinical outcomes. Telbivudine has demonstrated promising efficacy in prior studies, establishing a basis for further investigation into its therapeutic benefits in liver disease.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Male or female adults aged 18-75 years.
- Documented chronic HBV infection (HBV DNA PCR positive).
- Compensated liver disease, defined as Child-Turcotte-Pugh score ≤ 6.
- Willingness to provide written informed consent and comply with the study protocol.
- Negative HBeAg status with ALT levels at least 1.5 times the upper limit of normal OR positive HBeAg status with transaminase elevations.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Pregnancy or lactation, or childbearing potential without effective contraception.
- Significant co-infection with hepatitis C virus (HCV), hepatitis D virus (HDV), or HIV.
- Prior exposure to antiviral therapies for HBV within the last year.
- Any serious concurrent disease or condition that could interfere with study participation.
- Renal function impairment as evidenced by an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 30 mL/min.
- Known hypersensitivity to telbivudine or formulation components.

**Methodology:**  
A total of 300 participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either telbivudine (600 mg/day) or placebo for a duration of 48 weeks. Routine laboratory evaluations including HBV DNA levels, ALT, as well as monitoring for adverse events will occur at baseline, week 12, week 24, week 36, and week 48. Patients will also enter a 24-week post-treatment follow-up period to assess the durability of response and any long-term safety concerns.

**Endpoints:**  
- The primary endpoint is the proportion of patients achieving a complete virologic response defined as HBV DNA < 20 IU/mL at week 48.
- Secondary endpoints include clinical improvement assessed through changes in ALT levels and Child-Turcotte-Pugh scoring, as well as the proportion of patients reporting adverse events.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Data will be analyzed using intention-to-treat principles. Primary efficacy outcomes will be analyzed utilizing a chi-squared test for between-group comparisons, while secondary endpoint assessments will examine changes from baseline with paired t-tests and ANOVA, as deemed appropriate by the nature of the data.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
It is anticipated that telbivudine will demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in HBV viral load compared to placebo while exhibiting a favorable safety profile. By utilizing well-defined inclusion criteria, multicenter recruitment, and rigorous monitoring of safety and efficacy, the study aims to expand the current understanding of the benefits of telbivudine in patients with chronic hepatitis B, thus bolstering its position as a viable treatment option in this patient population. Robust follow-up assessments post-treatment are expected to yield insights into long-term benefits and safety profiles, ultimately advancing the management of compensated liver disease due to chronic HBV.
