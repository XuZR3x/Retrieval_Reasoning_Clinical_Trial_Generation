**Title: A Phase II Study Evaluating Subcutaneous Decitabine in Combination with Supportive Care for Patients with High-Risk Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS)**

**Study Design**: This is a multicenter, open-label, randomized Phase II trial designed to investigate the safety and efficacy of subcutaneous decitabine in conjunction with supportive care in patients diagnosed with high-risk MDS, classified as IPSS Intermediate-2 to High-Risk. 

**Primary Objective**: To evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete remission (CR), partial remission (PR), and hematologic improvement (HI) post-treatment with decitabine.

**Methodology**: 
Patients will be enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either decitabine (20 mg/m²) administered subcutaneously on Days 1-5 of a 28-day cycle, or to a control group receiving supportive care only. The trial is estimated to enroll 150 patients across 10 clinical sites.

**Efficacy Assessment**: 
Efficacy will be assessed using robust internationally recognized criteria, namely the Modified International Working Group Response Criteria. The primary endpoint will be the ORR at 12 weeks, while secondary endpoints will include overall survival (OS), time to response, duration of response, and quality of life measures evaluated via the EORTC QLQ-C30 scale. 

**Patient Population**: 
Participants eligible for enrollment must fulfill the following inclusion criteria:
1. Adults aged 18 years or older.
2. Diagnosis of high-risk MDS as per the IPSS classification.
3. ECOG performance status of 0-2.
4. Adequate organ function: renal (creatinine < 2 times ULN), hepatic (ALT & AST ≤ 2 times ULN, bilirubin < 2 times ULN).
5. Signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
Patients will be excluded if they:
1. Are currently pregnant or breastfeeding.
2. Have previously received decitabine or other azanucleotide treatments.
3. Have had significant comorbid conditions or uncontrolled infections.
4. Are currently enrolled in other investigational studies.

**Endpoints**:
Primary endpoint will be overall response rate at 12 weeks defined as the proportion of patients achieving CR, PR, or HI. Secondary endpoints include OS, which will be analyzed through Kaplan-Meier survival curves, as well as side-effect profiles recorded via Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

**Statistical Analysis**:
The trial will employ an intent-to-treat approach, with response rates analyzed using Chi-Square tests and survival analysis performed using the log-rank test for OS comparison between treatment arms. The analysis will be deemed significant at a p-value less than 0.05.

**Rationale**:
The use of subcutaneous administration of decitabine allows for improved patient compliance and a favorable toxicity profile, particularly with regard to hematologic parameters. The selection of high-risk MDS patients provides a focused application of decitabine’s hypomethylating effects, where low toxicity (less severe neutropenia and thrombocytopenia) can potentially translate to significant therapeutic responses. Furthermore, comprehensive response assessment through established criteria ensures robust evaluation of treatment outcomes.

**Conclusion**:
This study intends to leverage the advantageous administration route and patient population targeting of decitabine, thereby aiming to substantiate its role in improving the clinical outcomes for patients with high-risk MDS, supplementing existing treatment modalities. The effectiveness and tolerability highlighted by this trial may reaffirm decitabine as a cornerstone therapeutic option within this patient demographic.
