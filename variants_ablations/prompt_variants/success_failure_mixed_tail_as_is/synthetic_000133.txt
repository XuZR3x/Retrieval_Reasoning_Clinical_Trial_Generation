**Study of Temozolomide in Combination with Novel Targeted Therapy for Patients with Recurrent Glioblastoma Multiforme**

**Phase II Study of Temozolomide Combined with a Novel EGFR Inhibitor in Patients with Recurrent Glioblastoma Multiforme (GBM) Who Exhibit Low O6-Methylguanine Methyltransferase (MGMT) Expression**

The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of temozolomide in combination with a novel epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) inhibitor in patients with recurrent glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) exhibiting low MGMT expression levels. This innovative approach aims to exploit the unique biological markers to improve therapeutic outcomes in this otherwise poor prognostic group while leveraging the established efficacy of temozolomide.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
Participants will be included based on the following criteria:
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of recurrent glioblastoma multiforme as per World Health Organization (WHO) criteria.
- Documented low MGMT expression (MGMT: beta-actin ≤0.2) assessed via immunohistochemistry or Western blot.
- Prior treatment with at least one line of therapy, which may include temozolomide, radiotherapy, or immunotherapy.
- Age ≥18 years and ≤80 years.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0, 1, or 2.
- Adequate hematologic function measured by: absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥1.5 x 10^9/L; platelet count ≥100 x 10^9/L; hemoglobin ≥90 g/L.
- Patients must provide written informed consent and adhere to contraceptive measures if applicable.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
Participants meeting any of the following conditions will be excluded:
- History of prior hypersensitivity to temozolomide or related alkylating agents.
- Clinically significant cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or significant arrhythmias.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding; women of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test prior to enrollment.
- Presence of active, uncontrolled infections, or any condition requiring prolonged hospitalization.
- Previous treatment with investigational agents within 28 days prior to enrollment.
- Concurrent treatment with any chemotherapy or investigational drugs during the trial period.

**Clinical Response Assessment:**
Efficacy will be measured using the following criteria:
- Objective Response Rate (ORR): defined as the percentage of participants achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) according to the Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology (RANO) criteria.
- Progression-Free Survival (PFS): defined as the time from the start of treatment until the first observation of disease progression or death from any cause.
- Overall Survival (OS): defined as the time from randomization to death.

**Treatment Regimen:**
Patients will receive oral temozolomide at 75 mg/m² daily for 21 days in conjunction with the EGFR inhibitor dosed according to the manufacturer’s guidelines, beginning on Day 1. Treatment cycles will be repeated every 28 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Tumor assessments will be conducted every two cycles via MRI to evaluate treatment efficacy.

**Rationale for Study Design:**
The selection of participants based on low MGMT expression allows for a focused application of temozolomide, enhancing the likelihood of clinical response in a population with historically poor outcomes. By marrying a traditional chemotherapeutic regimen with a targeted therapy approach, the trial aims to build on synergies identified in previous research relating to timing and method of drug administration. The robust definitions for clinical endpoints align with contemporary approaches to cancer treatment assessment, fostering confidence in outcomes while considering the durability of responses in the context of recurrent malignancy. Extended follow-ups will be critical in evaluating the long-term implications of this combined treatment strategy and provide insight for future clinical exploration.

**Projected Accrual:**
A total of 60 patients will be accrued for this study over a period of 2 years, with continuous monitoring and evaluation of safety and efficacy outcomes throughout the trial duration.
