<clinicalTrial>
    <name>Candesartan Intervention in Heart Failure Patients: A Multicenter, Randomized, Control Study</name>
    <objective>
        To evaluate the efficacy of candesartan in reducing cardiovascular morbidity and improving quality of life in patients with heart failure and preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF).
    </objective>
    <design>
        This study is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted across multiple centers. The duration of the study will be 36 weeks, with follow-up assessments at 12, 24, and 36 weeks. Participants will be randomized to receive either candesartan or placebo, with a primary focus on clinical outcomes assessing heart failure symptoms and biomarkers.
    </design>
    <inclusionCriteria>
        - Adults aged 60 years and older
        - Clinically diagnosed HFpEF based on echocardiographic criteria
        - NYHA Functional Class II-III heart failure
        - A documented history of hypertension (systolic BP 130-180 mmHg)
    </inclusionCriteria>
    <exclusionCriteria>
        - Patients with chronic kidney disease (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²)
        - Recent hospitalization for heart failure (within the past 30 days)
        - History of hypersensitivity to angiotensin II receptor blockers
        - Active malignancy or severe comorbidities that may interfere with study outcomes
    </exclusionCriteria>
    <primaryOutcome>
        The primary outcome will be the change in Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ) scores from baseline to 36 weeks, indicating quality of life improvements associated with treatment.
    </primaryOutcome>
    <secondaryOutcomes>
        - Percent change in NT-proBNP levels at 36 weeks
        - Time to first cardiovascular hospitalization due to heart failure exacerbation
        - Change in NYHA functional class at 36 weeks
    </secondaryOutcomes>
    <studyRationale>
        The design incorporates carefully defined inclusion criteria to ensure that participants have HFpEF and concurrent hypertension, optimizing the target population likely to benefit from candesartan therapy, aligning with successful outcomes observed in previous studies. Utilizing an extended treatment duration of 36 weeks allows for comprehensive evaluation of therapeutic effects on biomarkers and quality of life measures. 
        Moreover, the study rigorously excludes patients with conditions that might confound the findings, thereby enhancing result validity. The focus on established surrogate markers, such as NT-proBNP levels and quality of life assessments, provides clear measures to determine the intervention's efficacy and reinforce the clinical benefits of candesartan in HFpEF.
    </studyRationale>
    <expectedResults>
        We hypothesize that participants receiving candesartan will demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in MLHFQ scores compared to placebo. Additionally, we aim to show a notable reduction in NT-proBNP, indicating a decrease in heart failure severity and lower rates of hospitalization, thus confirming candesartan’s role in managing heart failure with preserved ejection fraction.
    </expectedResults>
</clinicalTrial>
