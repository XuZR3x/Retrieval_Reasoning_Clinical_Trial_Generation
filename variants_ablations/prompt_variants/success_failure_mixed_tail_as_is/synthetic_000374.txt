**Failed clinical trial of montelukast sodium: Montelukast Sodium in Adult Asthma with Comorbid Allergic Rhinitis (MK-0476-450)**

A Multicenter, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Randomized, Parallel-Group Study to Evaluate the Clinical Effect of Montelukast Sodium in Adults with Persistent Asthma and Comorbid Allergic Rhinitis

This trial was designed to assess the efficacy of montelukast sodium in improving respiratory function and quality of life in adult patients with persistent asthma who also suffer from concurrent allergic rhinitis. Despite the rationale, the study failed to demonstrate significant clinical benefits.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male and female patients aged 18 to 55 years with a physician-confirmed diagnosis of persistent asthma.
- Patients must have a diagnosis of allergic rhinitis supported by positive skin allergen tests to common aeroallergens.
- Participants were required to exhibit a baseline FEV1 value between 60-80% of predicted normal.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with any acute or chronic lung disorders, other than allergic asthma.
- The presence of any significant cardiac, hepatic, or renal disorders.
- History of a recent asthma exacerbation leading to hospitalization within the past 3 months.

**Primary Outcome Measures:**
1. Change in Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 Second (FEV1) at Week 12, assessed as the percent change from baseline.
2. Patient-reported outcomes related to asthma control and quality of life over the course of the 12-week treatment period.

**Secondary Outcome Measures:**
- Total use of rescue bronchodilators over the entire study period.
- Incidence of asthma exacerbations requiring systemic corticosteroids.

**Results:**
Analysis revealed no statistically significant change in FEV1 between the montelukast sodium and placebo groups at the 12-week endpoint, leading to insufficient evidence to support the efficacy of the intervention in this specific population. The absence of a significant difference in primary endpoint outcomes was exacerbated by considerable variability in treatment response due to the inadequacy of population specificity. The study sample lacked stratification based on asthma phenotypes and comorbid conditions, which may have obscured the true effects of montelukast sodium.

Moreover, the study faced limitations including an insufficient duration to capture chronic therapeutic benefits, with many patients likely not experiencing an adequate treatment window to observe the impacts on inflammatory processes involved in asthma.

Placebo responses were pronounced, indicating a significant placebo effect, particularly in subjective outcomes such as health-related quality of life, confounding the efficacy assessment of montelukast sodium. The reliance on FEV1 as a singular primary endpoint restricted the comprehensive evaluation of treatment effects, potentially undermining the perceived clinical relevance of the findings.

Given the limitations in trial design—encompassing patient demographics, treatment duration, and the methodological inconsistencies—the overall outcomes of MK-0476-450 exemplify the complexities surrounding the clinical evaluation of montelukast sodium in diverse asthma populations. Consequently, these factors culminated in the lack of observed efficacy, classifying the trial as unsuccessful.
