**Successful Clinical Trial of Lubiprostone: Lubiprostone in Treating Opioid-Induced Constipation**

A Phase 3, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate Efficacy and Safety of Lubiprostone in Adult Participants With Opioid-Induced Constipation

**Abstract**: This clinical trial aims to assess the efficacy and safety of lubiprostone in patients suffering from opioid-induced constipation (OIC). Participants will receive either lubiprostone or placebo to evaluate changes in spontaneous bowel movements (SBMs) while closely monitoring gastrointestinal safety parameters throughout the 12-week study period.

**Objectives**: 
1. To evaluate the change in the frequency of SBMs per week when comparing lubiprostone to placebo.
2. To assess the safety and tolerability of lubiprostone over the course of the study, focusing on treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs).

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
- Medically confirmed diagnosis of opioid-induced constipation (OIC), compliant with the Rome IV Diagnostic Criteria for Functional Gastrointestinal Disorders.
- Age 18 years or older at the time of randomization.
- Participants must have been on a stable dose of opioids for at least the preceding 4 weeks.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
- Any gastrointestinal (GI) disorder excluding OIC that impacts motility or defecation.
- Conditions resulting in untreated fecal impaction at screening.
- History of surgeries affecting GI tract structure or function.

**Study Design**: 
This study features a robust design incorporating multicenter collaboration with established clinical sites to ensure diverse recruitment and data collection. A total of 300 participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either 24 mcg of lubiprostone taken orally twice daily (BID) or matched placebo. Rigorous double-blinding practices will shield both participants and investigators from study medication assignments, thereby reducing bias in outcomes.

**Safety Monitoring**: 
Longitudinal safety assessments will occur at designated intervals throughout the trial, with an emphasis on collecting data regarding TEAEs experienced during the treatment duration. Safety evaluations will continue through a 14-day follow-up period post-treatment cessation.

**Participant Engagement and Record Keeping**: 
To enhance treatment adherence and ensure precise data collection, participants will be asked to meticulously log their SBMs daily utilizing provided diaries. Parameters to be recorded within these diaries include frequency, stool consistency, and difficulties experienced during defecation, akin to previous successful studies.

**Endpoints**: 
The primary efficacy outcome will be defined as the change from baseline in weekly SBM frequency from randomization to Week 12. Secondary endpoints will encompass patient-reported outcomes related to constipation severity and satisfaction with treatment.

**Trial Duration**: 
The projected duration for participant involvement is approximately 14 weeks, encompassing a screening phase, 12 weeks of treatment, and follow-up evaluations.

This well-structured clinical trial, employing comprehensive study design and targeted patient population strategies, anticipates confirming the efficacy of lubiprostone in alleviating opioid-induced constipation while ensuring patient safety and adherence through simplified dosing and supportive engagement methods.
