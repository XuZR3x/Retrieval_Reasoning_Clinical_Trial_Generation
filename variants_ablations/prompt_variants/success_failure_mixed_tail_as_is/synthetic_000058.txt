**Title**: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study of Aripiprazole in the Reduction of Aggressive Behaviors in Adult Patients With Intermittent Explosive Disorder (IED)

**Objective**: This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of aripiprazole in reducing the frequency and severity of aggressive outbursts in adult patients diagnosed with Intermittent Explosive Disorder (IED).

**Study Design**: This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial with two parallel groups. A total of 120 adult participants will be enrolled and assigned to receive either aripiprazole with a flexible dosing regimen (10 to 30 mg/day) or matching placebo over a period of 12 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Males and females aged 18 to 65 years.
- Meets DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for Intermittent Explosive Disorder, confirmed by the Diagnostic Interview Schedule (DIS).
- History of recurrent impulsive aggressive behavior resulting in destruction of property or physical injury to others.
- Clinical Global Impressions-Severity (CGI-S) score ≥ 4 at screening visit.
- Signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- History of acute or chronic psychiatric disorders other than IED (e.g., schizophrenia, major depressive disorder).
- Previous treatment with aripiprazole yielding no therapeutic benefit.
- Current use of medications contraindicated with aripiprazole or affecting metabolic profiles significantly.
- Major medical comorbidities, including severe cardiovascular disease or hepatic dysfunction.
- Pregnancy, breastfeeding, or lack of appropriate contraception for females.

**Intervention**:
Participants will be randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to receive either aripiprazole or placebo. Dosing will commence at 10 mg and be titrated to a maximum of 30 mg based on clinical response and tolerance, emphasizing the tailored and flexible dosing strategy that capitalizes on aripiprazole's unique pharmacological profile.

**Outcomes**:
The primary efficacy outcome will be measured through the Aggression Questionnaire (AQ) derived scores and the frequency of aggressive outbursts recorded via self-reported diaries over the 12 weeks. The secondary outcomes will include assessments of tolerability and safety, utilizing the Systematic Assessment for Treatment Emergent Events (SAFTEE), the Clinical Global Impressions-Improvement (CGI-I) scale, and adverse event monitoring.

**Safety Measures**:
Routine safety assessments including vital signs, weight, metabolic parameters (glucose and lipid profiles), and abnormalities detected through physical examinations will be conducted at baseline, week 6, and week 12. Participants will receive thorough counseling regarding potential side effects, emphasizing the favorable safety profile of aripiprazole known from previous literature.

**Statistical Analysis**:
Statistical analysis will be conducted using intention-to-treat principles. Comparison of baseline and post-treatment AQ scores will be analyzed using mixed-effects models to account for repeated measures, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05. Safety data will be summarized descriptively.

**Data Management**:
Data will be analyzed and managed by a designated statistical team to ensure confidentiality and integrity. The study will be monitored by an independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) to oversee participant safety and data integrity throughout the trial duration.

**Protection of Human Subjects**:
Approval will be sought from the relevant institutional review boards. Comprehensive informed consent will be obtained from participants following a thorough discussion of the trial procedures and potential risks.

**Expected Impact**:
This innovative trial is anticipated to elucidate the therapeutic benefits of aripiprazole specific to the management of aggressive behaviors in IED, thus broadening the clinical applications of aripiprazole beyond established psychiatric conditions while potentially addressing comorbid metabolic concerns prevalent in aggressive patients.

**Location**: The study will be conducted at multiple investigational sites across outpatient psychiatric facilities specializing in mood and impulse control disorders.

**Conclusion**:
We hypothesize that aripiprazole's unique pharmacodynamic properties may significantly ameliorate aggression in patients with IED, enhancing overall patient wellbeing and reinforcing the multidimensional therapeutic potential of aripiprazole in clinical practice.
