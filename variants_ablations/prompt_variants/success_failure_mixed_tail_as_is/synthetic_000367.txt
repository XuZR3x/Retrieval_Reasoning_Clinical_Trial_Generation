**Failed Clinical Trial of Prednisone: Efficacy of Prednisone in Treatment-Resistant Allergic Rhinitis in Adults**

**A Multi-Center Phase III Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy of Prednisone in Patients with Treatment-Resistant Allergic Rhinitis**

Background: Allergic rhinitis is a prevalent condition that significantly diminishes the quality of life among affected individuals. While intranasal corticosteroids are the gold standard for managing moderate to severe allergic rhinitis, some patients report treatment resistance. This trial aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of oral prednisone in patients with treatment-resistant allergic rhinitis, hypothesizing that systemic corticosteroid therapy could provide additional symptomatic relief.

**Study Design:**
This multi-center, double-blind, randomized controlled trial included participants aged 18-65 years who exhibited persistent symptoms of allergic rhinitis despite appropriate therapy with intranasal corticosteroids and oral antihistamines. A total of 200 patients were randomly assigned to one of two groups: oral prednisone (starting dose of 40 mg/day tapered over 14 days) or placebo.

**Primary Outcomes:** 
The primary endpoint was the change in the Total Nasal Symptom Score (TNSS) from baseline to week 4 of treatment. Secondary endpoints included Quality of Life measures using the Rhinoconjunctivitis Quality of Life Questionnaire (RQLQ) and the assessment of systemic corticosteroid side effects.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult males and females aged 18-65 years
- Diagnosis of allergic rhinitis based on clinical history and skin-prick testing
- Prior inadequate response to at least two treatment modalities including intranasal corticosteroids and oral antihistamines
- Written informed consent provided

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of hypersensitivity to corticosteroids
- Concurrent use of systemic steroids in the past 30 days
- Significant co-morbidities such as uncontrolled diabetes, severe hypertension, or infections
- Pregnancy or lactation
- Presence of other primary causes of rhinitis

**Results:**
Despite stringent inclusion criteria aimed at capturing treatment-resistant individuals and controlling for confounders, the study faced notable challenges. The statistical power of the trial was limited by uneven randomization, resulting in a small treatment cohort. Variability in symptomatology associated with differing allergic triggers diminished the homogeneity of the participant group, leading to inconclusive results. Clinical assessment at week 4 revealed no statistically significant differences in TNSS between the prednisone and placebo groups (p = 0.312). Conversely, many participants experienced exacerbation of underlying conditions, leading to higher reported adverse events related to corticosteroid therapy.

**Conclusion:** 
The present trial failed to demonstrate a superiority of oral prednisone over placebo in the management of treatment-resistant allergic rhinitis. Primary factors contributing to the inconclusiveness of findings included inadequate control of confounding variables related to the diverse nature of allergic phenotypes and inadequate statistical power to reveal significant differences. Furthermore, the study did not utilize standardized measures for tracking long-term outcomes or patient-reported effects, limiting the interpretation of its potential utility. As such, these findings suggest that systemic corticosteroid therapy may not provide additional benefits to patients with treatment-resistant allergic rhinitis, necessitating further research to identify more effective treatment strategies.
