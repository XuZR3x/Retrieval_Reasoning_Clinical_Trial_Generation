**Successful Clinical Trial of TKI258: Efficacy and Safety of TKI258 in Patients With Advanced Colorectal Cancer**

A Phase II Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of TKI258 in Patients With Advanced Colorectal Cancer Refractory to Standard Treatment

This multi-center, randomized, double-blind study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of TKI258 in patients with advanced colorectal cancer who have progressed despite standard therapy. The design employs robust methodologies, including clear therapeutic indications with a targeted approach to a specific, unmet medical need, ensuring diverse patient demographics reflective of the advanced colorectal cancer population. 

The study involves a comparison between TKI258 and a placebo, where eligible patients will be administered 500 mg of TKI258 daily according to a 5 days on/2 days off regimen for 12 weeks. The primary endpoint is progression-free survival (PFS) evaluated by RECIST 1.1 criteria. Secondary endpoints include overall survival (OS), safety assessments (adverse events monitoring), and pharmacokinetic evaluations, utilizing relevant parameters such as AUC and Cmax to ensure accurate measurement of drug bioavailability. 

Inclusion Criteria: 
1. Patients with histopathologically confirmed advanced colorectal cancer, with metastatic disease, who have failed at least one line of standard systemic therapy.
2. ECOG performance status (PS) of 0 or 1.
3. Patients must meet protocol-specified laboratory values, including renal and hepatic function parameters.

Exclusion Criteria: 
1. Presence of any active brain metastases.
2. Uncontrolled concurrent medical conditions that could compromise patient safety or confound efficacy outcomes.
3. Patients who have received chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or investigational drugs within 4 weeks prior to randomization.
4. Pregnant or breast-feeding women, and fertile males or females not compliant with strict contraceptive measures.

Throughout the study, comprehensive pharmacokinetic data will be collected and analyzed in relation to safety signals, providing insight into the therapeutic window of TKI258. The study design's rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria ensure the recruitment of a homogenous and well-characterized patient population, thereby enhancing the reliability of efficacy outcomes. 

Moreover, a thorough assessment of adverse events will be conducted, with particular attention to dose-limiting toxicities, to establish a safety profile congruent with earlier phase studies. 

Investigator-initiated assessments including quality of life questionnaires will supplement clinical data, further enriching this trial's ability to relay findings pertinent not only to drug effectiveness but also to the impacts on patient experience and care.

This clinical trial is powered to provide essential data to support the therapeutic potential of TKI258 in treating patients with refractory advanced colorectal cancer, contributing to expanded treatment options for a population with significant unmet clinical needs. The outcome will potentially facilitate the establishment of TKI258 as a novel addition to treatment regimens in this patient cohort.
