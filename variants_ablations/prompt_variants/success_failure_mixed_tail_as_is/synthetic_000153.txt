**Successful Clinical Trial of Fluorouracil: Neoadjuvant Fluorouracil with Immunotherapy in Stage III Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** The use of fluorouracil in conjunction with immunotherapy is founded on enhancing immune-mediated tumor cell lysis, which may synergistically complement the cytotoxic effect of fluorouracil. The neoadjuvant strategy allows for early intervention in patients with stage III non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), potentially leading to improved pathologic complete response rates prior to surgical resection.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aims to study the efficacy of neoadjuvant fluorouracil combined with a PD-1 inhibitor in patients with stage III NSCLC, assessing pathologic response, overall survival (OS), and progression-free survival (PFS).

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** To determine the pathologic complete response (pCR) rate in patients with stage III NSCLC after neoadjuvant treatment with fluorouracil and PD-1 inhibitor.
- **Secondary:** To evaluate the overall survival and progression-free survival rates in this patient population.
- **Exploratory Analyses:** To correlate immune-related gene expression signatures from pre-treatment biopsy samples with treatment response and survival outcomes.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, randomized, open-label study involves two arms:
- **Arm A:** Patients receive fluorouracil IV continuously for 4 weeks, combined with the PD-1 inhibitor administered every two weeks for a total of two doses, preceding surgical intervention.
- **Arm B:** Patients undergo standard-of-care treatment with platinum-based chemotherapy for 4 weeks, followed by the same surgical and adjuvant therapy as Arm A.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 120 patients will be enrolled across multiple institutions over a projected time frame of 24 months.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed primary non-small cell lung carcinoma (adenocarcinoma or squamous cell carcinoma)
- Clinical stage III, suitable for neoadjuvant treatment as determined by imaging studies (CT, PET scan)
- No prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy for NSCLC

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: ≥ 18 years
- Performance Status: Karnofsky ≥ 70
- Adequate organ function: 
  - Hematologic: ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
  - Hepatic: Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times upper limit of normal (ULN), AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 times ULN
  - Renal: Creatinine ≤ 1.2 mg/dL

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Valid informed consent
- Measurable disease based on RECIST 1.1 criteria
- No evidence of distant metastasis

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- History of prior malignancy (within 5 years, except for non-melanoma skin cancers)
- Concurrent serious medical conditions preventing participation
- Pregnancy or lactation, or failure to comply with contraception requirements.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- **Pathological Complete Response (pCR):** Defined as absence of residual invasive carcinoma in the resected specimens.
- **Overall Survival (OS):** Time from randomization to death from any cause.
- **Progression-Free Survival (PFS):** Time from randomization to disease progression or death from any cause.

**TRIAL DETAIL EXAMPLE:**
Patients are randomized into two arms with stratification based on tumor histology and performance status. In Arm A, participants receive continuous intravenous fluorouracil at a dose of 1,000 mg/m²/day alongside nivolumab, administered at doses of 3 mg/kg every two weeks. Patients undergo surgical resection 4-6 weeks post-treatment completion. 

**EXPECTED IMPACT:** By utilizing a neoadjuvant regimen with fluorouracil and immunotherapy, this trial is anticipated to enhance the pCR rates significantly, thereby improving OS and PFS in stage III NSCLC, supporting the hypothesis of immune system activation in conjunction with chemotherapeutic agents.

**ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS:** This study will adhere to ethical standards outlined by the Declaration of Helsinki and will obtain approval from relevant Institutional Review Boards (IRBs). All patients will receive comprehensive information regarding potential risks and benefits of participation. 

Through rigorous patient selection, a focus on molecular correlates of response to therapy, and the incorporation of innovative treatment strategies, this clinical trial seeks to redefine the therapeutic landscape for patients afflicted with stage III NSCLC.
