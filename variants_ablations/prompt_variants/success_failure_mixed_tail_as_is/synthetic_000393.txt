**Clinical Trial of Donepezil: Cognitive Preservation Study in Alzheimer's Disease**

**A Randomized, Multicenter, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Donepezil for Cognitive Preservation in Early Alzheimer's Disease**

The Neurodegenerative Disorders Research Consortium is conducting a pivotal clinical trial aimed at evaluating the efficacy of donepezil for cognitive preservation in patients with early-stage Alzheimer's disease (AD). This study aims to provide insights into enhancing cognitive function and delaying the progression of clinical symptoms using a well-characterized patient population.

**Study Objectives:**
1. To assess the cognitive efficacy of donepezil in patients diagnosed with early-stage AD compared to placebo over a 24-month period.
2. To evaluate secondary outcomes related to the quality of life, overall functioning, and caregiver-reported outcomes.

**Study Design:**
This trial is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study involving 900 participants diagnosed with early-stage AD, aged between 60 and 85 years, recruited from 40 specialized memory clinics across North America.

**Primary Endpoint:** 
The change in cognitive function, as measured by the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive (ADAS-Cog), from baseline to 24 months.

**Secondary Endpoints:** 
1. Changes in quality of life, assessed by the Quality of Life in Alzheimer's Disease (QoL-AD) scale.
2. Daily functioning, evaluated using the activities of daily living inventory (ADCS-ADL).
3. Caregiver burden, measured by the Zarit Burden Interview.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Participants must have a diagnosis of probable or possible AD according to the NINCDS-ADRDA criteria.
2. A Mini-Mental State Examination score between 18 and 26.
3. Evidence of gradual cognitive decline over at least 6 months.
4. Availability of a reliable caregiver to assist with monitoring compliance and attending study visits.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Presence of other neurodegenerative disorders (e.g., Parkinson’s, frontotemporal dementia).
2. Significant psychiatric conditions that may interfere with the study (e.g., major depression).
3. History of hypersensitivity to cholinesterase inhibitors.
4. Clinical evidence of uncontrolled medical conditions that may confound results.

**Recruitment and Screening:**
Participants will undergo a comprehensive screening process, including neuropsychological testing, medical history review, and diagnostic imaging where necessary. Recruitment is anticipated to occur over a 12-month period.

**Intervention and Randomization:**
Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either donepezil (10 mg daily) or placebo for a total duration of 24 months. Dosing will initiate at 5 mg for the first month, increasing to 10 mg thereafter, contingent on tolerability. 

**Follow-Up Assessments:**
Participants will be evaluated at baseline and then at 3-month intervals for the first year, followed by semi-annual checks. Cognitive assessments will be conducted at each visit to monitor treatment effects and participant safety.

To enhance compliance, caregivers will receive educational sessions regarding the importance of adherence and management of potential side effects. Throughout the duration of the trial, strategies will be implemented to maintain high retention rates, including regular follow-ups and counseling sessions.

**Statistical Analysis:**
An intention-to-treat analysis will be utilized to assess cognitive outcomes, with a significance level set at p<0.05. Multiple regression analyses will evaluate the effects of donepezil on primary and secondary endpoints while controlling for potential confounding variables.

In summary, this clinical trial seeks to gather robust evidence on the cognitive benefits of donepezil in early AD while delivering high-quality, patient-centered care by ensuring strategic recruitment and supportive follow-up methods. The findings from this study promise to contribute significantly to the existing body of knowledge regarding interventions for Alzheimer's disease.
