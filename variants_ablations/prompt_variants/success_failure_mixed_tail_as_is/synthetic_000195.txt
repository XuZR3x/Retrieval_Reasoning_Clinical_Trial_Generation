<ClinicalTrial>
  <Title>Evaluation of Itacitinib in Combination with Standardized Supportive Care in Patients With Myelofibrosis: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study</Title>
  <Objective>This study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of itacitinib in patients with myelofibrosis when administered alongside standardized supportive care.</Objective>
  
  <StudyDesign>
    <Type>Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled</Type>
    <Phases>Phase 2</Phases>
    <Endpoints>
      <Primary>Proportion of participants achieving > 50% reduction in total symptom score as measured by the Myelofibrosis Symptom Assessment Form (MFSAF) v3.0 by Week 24.</Primary>
      <Secondary>Assessment of safety parameters including adverse events, changes in hematologic profiles, and quality of life measures using validated tools.</Secondary>
    </Endpoints>
  </StudyDesign>
  
  <InclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>Patients aged 18-80 diagnosed with Primary Myelofibrosis (PMF), confirmed via bone marrow biopsy.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Patients must exhibit moderate to severe disease symptoms (MFSAF score ≥ 8) at screening.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Documented history of at least one prior systemic therapy for myelofibrosis.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Hematologic stability defined as hemoglobin ≥ 8 g/dL, platelet count ≥ 50 x 10^9/L, and absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1.0 x 10^9/L at baseline.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Patients must provide informed consent and be capable of adhering to study requirements.</Criterion>
  </InclusionCriteria>
  
  <ExclusionCriteria>
    <Criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding women, and individuals unable to comply with contraception regulations.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Patients with liver function tests exceeding 3 times the upper limit of normal or significant renal impairment.</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Individuals with active infections requiring systemic treatment or history of malignancies within the last five years (other than treated skin cancers).</Criterion>
    <Criterion>Severe comorbidities that could interfere with the study outcomes or patient safety.</Criterion>
  </ExclusionCriteria>
  
  <Methodology>
    <Population>Participants will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either itacitinib or placebo.</Population>
    <Monitoring>A dose escalation protocol will be used to determine the optimal dosing while minimizing adverse effects. Participants will be monitored bi-weekly for clinical safety and efficacy through laboratory tests and symptom assessment interviews.</Monitoring>
    <SupportiveCare>All participants will receive standardized supportive care, including symptom management and nutritional support, to ensure optimal trial conditions.</SupportiveCare>
  </Methodology>
  
  <Duration>Participants will be enrolled and monitored for a total duration of 24 weeks, with follow-up assessments extending to 36 weeks.</Duration>
  
  <OutcomeMeasure>
    <Description>The primary outcome measure will evaluate the efficacy of itacitinib by assessing the symptom burden reduction and the corresponding improvement in quality of life of participants. Safety will be assessed through incidence and severity of adverse events, as well as laboratory abnormalities.</Description>
  </OutcomeMeasure>

  <Rationale>The success of this trial is attributed to the strict inclusion criteria, ensuring the appropriate identification of individuals diagnosed with myelofibrosis. By employing a rigorous assessment of safety and efficacy parameters, the study design is predictive of greater therapeutic response due to its ability to minimize bias and focus on clinically relevant outcomes. Adequate patient monitoring, paired with a defined dose escalation protocol, further heightens the probability of achieving a successful response while maintaining a commitment to participant safety. Additionally, the engagement with patients demonstrating moderate to severe symptomatology enhances the likelihood of meaningful clinical outcomes, thereby improving the overall success of the trial.</Rationale>
</ClinicalTrial>
