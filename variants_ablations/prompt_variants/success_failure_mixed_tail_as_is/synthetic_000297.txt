**Successful Clinical Trial of Everolimus: Phase III Study Evaluating Everolimus in Combination With Tacrolimus and Corticosteroids in De Novo Liver Transplant Recipients**

**A Phase III, Randomized, Multi-Center, Open-Label Trial to Evaluate the Safety and Efficacy of Everolimus in Combination With Tacrolimus and Corticosteroids in De Novo Liver Transplant Recipients**

**Study Overview**

The purpose of this study is to assess the safety and efficacy of everolimus in conjunction with tacrolimus and corticosteroids compared to a standard immunosuppressive regimen consisting solely of tacrolimus and corticosteroids in de novo adult liver transplant recipients. The primary objective is to demonstrate non-inferiority in renal function at 12 months post-transplantation, as measured by estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), while secondary endpoints will examine composite outcomes of graft survival, acute rejection incidence, and patient safety profiles.

**Inclusion Criteria**
- Adult males or females aged 18 to 70 years.
- Recipients of de novo orthotopic liver transplantation from either deceased or living donors.
- Patients who provide written informed consent and are able to comply with protocol requirements.
- Women of childbearing potential must have a negative serum pregnancy test within 7 days prior to enrollment and must agree to use a highly effective contraceptive method throughout the study period.

**Exclusion Criteria**
- Patients with prior liver transplantation or contraindications for tacrolimus/everolimus.
- Evidence of active hepatic malignancy at the time of transplantation.
- Recipients receiving immunosuppressive therapy not specified in this protocol.
- Pregnancy or lactation during the study period.
- Laboratory parameters indicative of active infection or serious underlying medical conditions such as significant cardiovascular, pulmonary, or infectious diseases that would preclude participation.

**Endpoints**
- The primary endpoint will be the comparison of mean eGFR at 12 months post-transplantation between treatment arms using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) formula.
- Secondary endpoints will include the rate of acute rejection episodes requiring treatment through month 12, overall graft survival, and the incidence of adverse events classified by severity per the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0.

**Methodology**
This trial is designed as a randomized, open-label, multi-center approach to enhance statistical power and generalizability of results. Utilization of rigorous inclusion criteria aims to create a homogeneous patient population, thus minimizing confounding variables and facilitating a clearer interpretation of direct treatment effects, aligning with previously identified success factors in everolimus trials. The assessment of renal outcomes will employ the well-established CKD-EPI eGFR calculation, thus providing an objective measure of renal function that permits correctly attributing any observed effects to the intervention versus standard care.

**Statistical Analysis**
The statistical analyses will employ an ANCOVA model adjusting for baseline covariates including donor age and type, with significance set at p < 0.05 for primary outcomes. Safety data will be analyzed descriptively and reported as incidence rates, with overall efficacy trajectories graphically represented to inform clinical practice.

**Study Design**
- Investigational Drug: Everolimus (Certican)
- Comparator: Tacrolimus + Corticosteroids (Control)
- Total Sample Size: Approximately 600 participants across multiple international transplant centers.

**Results Anticipation**
It is hypothesized that the addition of everolimus to the immunosuppressive regimen will result in superior renal function outcomes compared to the standard regimen, thereby correlating with previously demonstrated pharmacokinetic benefits of everolimus in reducing CNI-related nephrotoxicity while maintaining adequate immunosuppression and decreasing rejection risk through effective therapeutic modulation of T-cell activity.

**Conclusion**
This phase III trial is poised to provide pivotal evidence regarding the role of everolimus in liver transplantation protocols, responding strategically to earlier successes in renal transplantation while addressing challenges observed in previous lung transplant studies that have faced limitations due in part to inadequate patient population homogeneity and mechanistic understanding of drug interactions. Should positive outcomes emerge, this investigation can significantly advance immunosuppressive strategies in liver transplant care, potentially enhancing patient outcomes and graft longevity in this critical clinical setting.
