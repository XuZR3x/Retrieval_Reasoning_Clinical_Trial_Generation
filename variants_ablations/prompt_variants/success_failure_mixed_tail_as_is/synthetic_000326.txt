<clinical_trial>
    <title>Aprepitant for the Management of Acute Nausea and Vomiting in Patients Undergoing Chemotherapy: A Phase III, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind Study</title>
    
    <study_id>2023-APRMN-002</study_id>
    
    <abstract>
        This study evaluates the efficacy and safety of aprepitant, when administered as part of an anti-emetic regimen, in the prevention of acute chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV) in patients scheduled to receive emetogenic chemotherapy. The study utilizes a robust, double-blind, placebo-controlled design to ensure the integrity of the findings.
    </abstract>
    
    <objectives>
        <primary>
            To determine the efficacy of aprepitant in reducing the incidence of acute vomiting episodes in patients receiving moderately to highly emetogenic chemotherapy.
        </primary>
        <secondary>
            To assess the tolerability profile of aprepitant in combination with standard anti-emetics (ondansetron and dexamethasone) and its impact on patient quality of life (QoL) following chemotherapy treatment.
        </secondary>
    </objectives>
    
    <study_design>
        <design>Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Multicenter</design>
        <goals>
            <goal>To achieve high statistical power for efficacy outcomes through careful study design and patient stratification based on baseline characteristics.</goal>
            <goal>To maintain rigorous data collection protocols to ensure high quality and completeness of data across all sites.</goal>
        </goals>
    </study_design>
    
    <methods>
        <inclusion_criteria>
            <criterion>Patients with a confirmed diagnosis of cancer, chemotherapy-naive, scheduled to receive moderately to highly emetogenic chemotherapy.</criterion>
            <criterion>Karnofsky Performance Status ≤ 60.</criterion>
            <criterion>Age ≥ 18 years and able to provide informed consent.</criterion>
        </inclusion_criteria>
        <exclusion_criteria>
            <criterion>Patients scheduled for concurrent radiotherapy.</criterion>
            <criterion>Known hypersensitivity to aprepitant or any components of the regimen.</criterion>
            <criterion>Patients with presence of symptomatic brain metastases.</criterion>
            <criterion>Use of strong CYP3A4 inducers or inhibitors within the period of the study.</criterion>
        </exclusion_criteria>
        <randomization>
            Patients will be randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either aprepitant or placebo, alongside standard anti-emetic therapy of ondansetron and dexamethasone.
        </randomization>
    </methods>
    
    <outcomes>
        <primary_outcome>
            The primary outcome will be the proportion of patients experiencing no vomiting during the first 120 hours post-chemotherapy initiation.
        </primary_outcome>
        <secondary_outcomes>
            <outcome>Measurement of acute nausea severity using a numeric rating scale.</outcome>
            <outcome>Assessment of rescue medication use and patient-reported QoL measures at baseline and at the end of the chemotherapy cycle.</outcome>
            <outcome>Comparison of adverse events between both groups to evaluate safety profiles.</outcome>
        </secondary_outcomes>
    </outcomes>
    
    <statistical_analysis>
        <description>Statistical analysis will employ intention-to-treat principles to account for patient dropouts. Efficacy will be assessed using chi-square tests for categorical variables and ANOVA for continuous variables. A p-value of less than 0.05 will be considered significant.</description>
    </statistical_analysis>
    
    <projected_accrual>
        A total of 850 patients are projected to be enrolled across multiple sites over a 12-month recruitment period.
    </projected_accrual>
    
    <patient_engagement>
        Emphasis on establishing a patient-centered approach will be initiated through educational sessions regarding the management of nausea and vomiting, thereby enhancing adherence to the treatment protocol as seen in previous successful trials.
    </patient_engagement>
    
    <conclusion>
        This trial aims to build upon the clinical efficacy of aprepitant demonstrated in prior studies by leveraging a stringent methodology and a well-defined patient population, with the goal of significantly improving acute CINV management in oncology.
    </conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
