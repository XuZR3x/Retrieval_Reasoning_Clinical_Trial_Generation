**Failed Clinical Trial of Donepezil: PREDICT Study - A Randomized Controlled Trial of Donepezil in Patients with Mild Cognitive Impairment**

The primary objective of the PREDICT Study was to evaluate the effectiveness, safety, and tolerability of donepezil in patients diagnosed with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) as classified by the DSM-5 criteria. Given the complexities associated with cognitive decline, this trial aimed to establish whether donepezil would provide cognitive improvement, behavioral modification, and an enhancement in quality of life as compared to placebo over a 12-month period.

However, due to recruitment challenges, particularly the mismatch between the demographic needs of the study and the dwindling eligible population willing to partake in cognitive research, the trial faced significant impediments. The planned enrollment of 300 patients was not met, and by the end of the recruitment phase, only 57 subjects had consented to participate. This insufficiency necessitated a cessation of the trial's progression on March 30, 2021. There were no documented safety or efficacy concerns with donepezil that contributed to this decision, as the trial was potentially promising from a pharmacological standpoint.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients aged 50 to 80 years meeting the diagnostic criteria for MCI, characterized by a notable cognitive decline that does not severely interfere with activities of daily living.
2. The presence of a reliable informant who could report on the patient's cognitive and functional status.
3. Consent to participate in the study and provide regular follow-up information.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. A history of major medical conditions affecting cognition (e.g., stroke, traumatic brain injury) other than MCI.
2. Coexisting psychiatric disorders requiring active treatment with psychotropic medications.
3. Recent use of other acetylcholinesterase inhibitors or NMDA antagonists.
4. Presence of significant comorbid conditions impacting cognitive assessment such as uncontrolled diabetes or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

**Outcome Measures:**
1. Change from baseline in the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) scores over 12 months, applying a longitudinal framework to evaluate cognitive change.
2. Secondary measures included the Neuropsychiatric Inventory (NPI) and the Alzheimer's Disease Cooperative Study-Activities of Daily Living (ADCS-ADL).

In reviewing the underpinning rationale behind recruitment failure, several key factors were identified. The subset population specificity posed difficulties, as the MCI cohort's cognitive profiles often varied considerably in declines from participant to participant, making it challenging to ascertain a coherent treatment effect from donepezil. Furthermore, environmental and social factors significantly impacted participant engagement; multiple inquiries revealed hesitance related to the stigma surrounding cognitive decline and the burden placed on caregivers, further leading to decreased enrollment enthusiasm.

The limitations in evaluation methods were also duly noted—the chosen assessment instruments did not adequately capture subtle cognitive changes in the MCI population, lending uncertainty to the ordinary metrics of improvement anticipated from pharmacotherapy.

Thus, while donepezil remains an important agent in the selective enhancement of cognitive function in Alzheimer's and related disorders, the inability to successfully recruit an adequate subject pool precipitated the unraveling of the PREDICT Study's ambitions.
