**Title:** A Phase II Study of Panobinostat in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Patients with Refractory Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

**Abstract:**
This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of panobinostat in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with refractory non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The trial was designed based on the rationale that the synergistic effects of histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibition and immune checkpoint blockade could enhance therapeutic responses in an immunologically privileged tumor microenvironment often seen in NSCLC. Despite robust preclinical data supporting this combination, the trial was prematurely terminated due to limited efficacy and an unfavorable toxicity profile.

**Study Design:**
This was a multicenter, open-label, single-arm Phase II trial. Eligible participants received panobinostat at a dose of 20 mg orally three times per week in conjunction with pembrolizumab administered every three weeks. Treatment continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or up to a maximum of 12 months. The primary endpoints were overall response rate (ORR) evaluated by RECIST v1.1 criteria, and safety, measured by the incidence of treatment-related adverse events (AEs).

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed NSCLC.
- At least one prior line of systemic therapy for advanced or metastatic disease.
- Patients who have progressed on or after platinum-based doublet chemotherapy and have a documented PD-L1 expression level of ≥ 1%.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function, as defined by the following laboratory values: 
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1.5 x 10⁹/L
  - Platelets ≥ 100 x 10⁹/L 
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN
- Female patients of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test and agree to use effective contraception.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with any HDAC inhibitors.
- Active central nervous system (CNS) metastases or any history of uncontrolled seizure disorder.
- Unresolved diarrhea ≥ CTCAE grade 2, or any significant gastrointestinal dysfunction affecting drug absorption.
- Concurrent systemic corticosteroid therapy exceeding prednisone 10 mg/day or other immunosuppressive agents.
- Known hypersensitivity to either panobinostat or pembrolizumab or any excipient of the study drugs.

**Results:**
The study enrolled 60 patients; however, the planned interim analysis revealed limited efficacy. The ORR was only 12% with complete response (CR) rates being negligible. There was a notable incidence of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) including severe fatigue, diarrhea, and thrombocytopenia, which limited dose escalations: over 40% of patients experienced at least one DLT. These toxicities resulted in significant interruptions in treatment schedules, adversely affecting patient compliance and contributing to an overall poor therapeutic index.

**Discussion:**
The disappointing results from this trial can be attributed to several intertwined factors. Firstly, the limited efficacy in a heavily pre-treated population led to an inadequate therapeutic impact of panobinostat, suggesting the biological heterogeneity of NSCLC may preclude the hoped-for responsiveness to HDAC inhibition. Furthermore, the pharmacodynamic response, essential for panobinostat's action, was likely suboptimal in this patient cohort, compromised by prior treatments that altered tumor epigenetics.

Moreover, the toxicity profile was particularly concerning, with treatment-related adverse events leading to significant patient morbidity and necessitating close monitoring and periodic dose adjustments. In addition, the trial was further hampered by the restrictive inclusion criteria which may have led to a selection bias, adversely affecting the generalizability of the findings.

**Conclusion:**
The combination of panobinostat and pembrolizumab in patients with refractory NSCLC demonstrated insufficient clinical efficacy and an unfavorable safety profile, leading to the premature termination of this Phase II trial. Future investigations may benefit from reevaluating the indications for panobinostat's use, focusing on less refractory disease settings and optimizing combination strategies through careful preclinical evaluation.
