Failed clinical trial of peginterferon alfa-2a [pegasys]: A Study of PEGASYS (Peginterferon Alfa-2a (40KD)) in Adult Patients With Chronic Hepatitis C and Active Coexisting Liver Conditions. A Multicenter, Open-label, Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of PEGASYS in Adult Patients With Chronic Hepatitis C Virus Infection and Active Coexisting Liver Conditions 

This multicenter, open-label, single-arm study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of peginterferon alfa-2a (PEGASYS) in patients with chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection, who also present with active coexisting liver conditions, including alcoholic liver disease and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). The anticipated enrollment is 300 individuals who will receive PEGASYS at a dose of 180 micrograms weekly for 48 weeks, followed by a 24-week treatment-free follow-up period. 

The primary endpoint will be the sustained virological response (SVR) defined as the proportion of individuals with undetectable HCV RNA at 24 weeks post-treatment. Secondary outcomes include safety assessments through adverse event monitoring and laboratory values. The anticipated duration of study participation for each patient will be approximately 12 months.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Adult patients, 18 years and older;
- Confirmed chronic hepatitis C infection, any genotype;
- Clinically significant active liver disease conditions defined as alcoholic liver disease or NAFLD;
- Positive serum HCV RNA levels (>500 IU/mL).

Exclusion Criteria:
- Evidence of decompensated liver disease or patients with Child-Pugh score >6;
- History or evidence of any other significant liver disease apart from HCV infection;
- Prior therapy with any interferon-based regimen or nucleos(t)ide analogs for hepatitis C within the last 6 months;
- Renal impairment with a calculated glomerular filtration rate (GFR) <30 mL/min.

Expected Outcomes:
The anticipated outcome is to assess the efficacy of PEGASYS in a complex and heterogeneous patient population burdened with liver conditions that are known to influence treatment tolerance and response dynamics. The design takes into account the baseline characteristics of coexisting liver disease, reflecting real-world clinical scenarios which may impact treatment adherence and viral clearance outcomes.

Historically, trials focusing on chronic hepatitis C patients with concurrent liver diseases have faced challenges due to the confounding nature of these coexisting conditions. Such factors may lead to reduced treatment responsiveness and increased adverse effects as observed in previous clinical research, thus informing the necessity for improved patient stratification and therapeutic customization moving forward. 

The study will contribute to a better understanding of the limitations in employing peginterferon alfa-2a within more complex patient populations, addressing evidenced-based gaps in current management protocols and informing future therapeutic strategies.

Completion of the study will reveal critical insights into the barriers to achieving positive treatment outcomes in real-world settings plagued by chronic hepatitis C and comorbid liver conditions. The anticipated timeline for trial completion is expected to span over 18 months, including analysis and dissemination of findings among the medical community.
