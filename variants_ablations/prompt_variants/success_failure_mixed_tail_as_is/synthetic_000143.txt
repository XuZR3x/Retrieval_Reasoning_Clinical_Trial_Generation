**Successful Clinical Trial of Azacitidine: Azacitidine in Combination with Obinutuzumab for Patients with Hypomethylated Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) and Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS)**

**RATIONALE:** Previous studies have demonstrated that azacitidine functions as a potent hypomethylating agent, which can restore the expression of tumor suppressor genes silenced by DNA methylation. Furthermore, the inclusion of a monoclonal antibody like obinutuzumab, which targets CD20 and enhances immune-mediated cell death, may facilitate the eradication of leukemic cells exhibiting hypomethylation patterns. This synergistic approach can enhance overall therapeutic efficacy, particularly in a patient population with advanced hypomethylated AML and MDS.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial investigates the safety and efficacy of azacitidine in combination with obinutuzumab in patients with hypomethylated AML or high-risk MDS. The primary objective is to evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) to the combination therapy.

**OBJECTIVES:**
*Primary* 
- Determine the overall response rate (ORR) to azacitidine and obinutuzumab in patients with hypomethylated AML or high-risk MDS.

*Secondary*
- Assess the duration of response (DOR), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS).
- Evaluate the safety profile and dose-limiting toxicities associated with the combination therapy.
- Investigate changes in the epigenetic landscape of leukemic cells pre- and post-treatment via next-generation sequencing and DNA methylation analysis.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, open-label, randomized phase II trial. Patients receive intravenous obinutuzumab on days 1, 8, and 15 of the first treatment cycle while simultaneously receiving azacitidine subcutaneously on days 1-5. Treatment cycles are repeated every 28 days until disease progression or intolerable toxicity occurs.

Cohorts of patients will be monitored for clinical responses using the International Working Group criteria, with serial assessments of bone marrow blasts and peripheral blood counts. Longitudinal follow-up will provide insight into the long-term benefits of the treatment regimen, particularly in relation to measures of epigenetic modulation and immunologic changes.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:** 
- Diagnosis of hypomethylated AML meeting World Health Organization (WHO) criteria or high-risk MDS based on International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS) criteria.
- Patients must exhibit a high hypomethylation index, as determined by genomic profiling.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:** 
- Life expectancy ≥ 6 months
- ECOG performance status 0-2 
- Age 18-75 years 
- Negative pregnancy test for women of childbearing potential; effective contraception required.
- No serious medical conditions or laboratory abnormalities that would interfere with study participation.
- No prior therapies with azacitidine or obinutuzumab.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- More than 28 days since any previous anticancer therapies, including investigational agents.
- No history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to monoclonal antibodies.

**EVALUATION OF OUTCOMES:** 
Response evaluations will occur at the end of each cycle with assessments of ORR, DOR, and PFS calculated using Kaplan-Meier methods. A hypothesis-testing approach will be employed based on whether the treatment achieves ORR significantly greater than the historical baseline rates of respective therapies in this population.

**SIGNIFICANCE:** 
This study addresses the unmet needs in patients with hypomethylated AML and MDS by combining azacitidine's epigenetic reprogramming effects with obinutuzumab's immune-mediated cytotoxic potential. The translational research component will expand our understanding of epigenetic modifications and their relevance to clinical outcomes. Establishing this combination's safety and efficacy could lead to innovative treatment strategies for this challenging patient demographic.
