**Failed Clinical Trial of Maraviroc: Multicenter Randomized Study to Evaluate the Efficacy of Maraviroc in Comparison to Dolutegravir/Abacavir/Lamivudine in Treatment-Naïve Adults with CCR5-Tropic HIV-1**

**Study Design:**
This multicenter, double-blind, randomized clinical trial aimed to compare the efficacy of maraviroc, administered twice daily, to a fixed-dose combination of dolutegravir, abacavir, and lamivudine, also administered once daily, in antiretroviral-naïve patients infected with CCR5-tropic HIV-1. The primary objective was to assess virologic response as measured by plasma HIV-1 RNA levels at Week 48.

**Study Termination:**
On March 15, 2022, the trial was terminated following an interim analysis by an independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC). The DMC reported statistically significant differences in virologic outcomes, indicating that maraviroc was inferior to the comparator arm in achieving the primary endpoint (plasma HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL at Week 48). Furthermore, participants in the maraviroc arm demonstrated inadequate viral load reduction, particularly those with higher baseline viral loads, aligning with previously noted pharmacological limitations of maraviroc.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age ≥ 18 years at the time of screening.
- Documented HIV-1 infection with confirmed CCR5-tropic strains as verified by a randomized tropism assay.
- Plasma HIV-1 RNA ≥ 1,000 copies/mL measured during screening.
- CD4 count ≥ 200 cells/mm³ at screening.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Any prior exposure to antiretroviral therapy for more than 14 days.
- Documented resistance to any components of the reference regimen (dolutegravir, abacavir, or lamivudine) based on genotypic testing.
- Evidence of CXCR4-using virus detected during the randomized tropism assay.

**Primary Endpoint:**
The primary endpoint was the proportion of participants achieving plasma HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL at Week 48. The efficacy was assessed in accordance with the FDA’s Missing, Switch, Discontinuation, and Death (MSDF) Snapshot algorithm, categorizing participants with missing data, switches to prohibited regimens, or treatment discontinuation as failing to meet the endpoint.

**Secondary Endpoint:**
Secondary endpoints included the change from baseline in CD4 counts, safety assessments, and adverse event monitoring. Despite a lack of significant safety concerns, logistical challenges including low enrollment rates contributed to difficulty in adhering to the planned sample size, further affecting the overall power of the trial.

**Data Findings:**
The initial analysis indicated that only 45% of participants in the maraviroc treatment arm achieved the primary endpoint compared to 78% in the dolutegravir/abacavir/lamivudine arm. The DMC expressed significant concerns regarding the operational execution of the study, particularly the reliance on tropism assessment which may have limited the trial's generalizability and application in diverse HIV populations.

**Conclusion:**
The combination of poor virologic response, operational inefficiencies, and participant enrollment challenges ultimately resulted in the early termination of this clinical trial of maraviroc. Future studies incorporating a broader patient demographic and those exploring alternative tropism assays or combinatory treatment regimens may be warranted to optimize the therapeutic potential of maraviroc in the management of CCR5-tropic HIV-1 infections.
