Failed clinical trial of insulin degludec: A Comprehensive Assessment of Insulin Degludec Efficacy in Diverse Patient Populations with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (BEGIN™: DIVERSITY)

This trial was conducted globally, including Europe, North America, and Asia, to evaluate the safety and efficacy of insulin degludec (IDeg) in a broad patient population with type 2 diabetes mellitus. 

**Study Design**: A multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial over a period of 52 weeks.

**Objectives**: The primary objective was to investigate the change in Glycosylated Hemoglobin (HbA1c) from baseline in patients treated with insulin degludec compared to those remaining on their current regimen, inclusive of various background anti-diabetic therapies. 

**Inclusion Criteria**:
- Diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus for at least 12 months.
- Patients aged 30-70 years, with a diversified demographic representation including different ethnic backgrounds.
- HbA1c level between 7.5-11.0% (inclusive), allowing for a broader eligibility range.
- Body Mass Index (BMI) up to 50.0 kg/m^2, accommodating overweight and obese patients.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
- Previous use of long-acting insulin analogs for more than two months in the past year.
- Significant uncontrolled comorbid conditions, including cardiovascular diseases, defined within the past year as heart failure Class III or IV or history of myocardial infarction.
- Pregnancy or intention to become pregnant, as well as previous participation in any trials involving GLP-1 receptor agonists or additional anti-diabetic therapies within three months of enrollment.
- Individuals displaying recurrent severe hypoglycemia in the preceding year or exhibiting hypoglycemic unawareness.

**Endpoints**:
- Primary Endpoint: Change in HbA1c from baseline at 52 weeks.
- Secondary Endpoint: Change in body weight from baseline at 52 weeks, alongside safety assessments including the incidence of hypoglycemic events.

**Results**: The trial encountered significant challenges leading to failure in achieving its primary endpoint of reducing HbA1c. Factors contributing to this outcome included:

1. **Stringent and Broader Inclusion Criteria**: While the intention was to enhance population diversity, the broadening of eligibility led to higher variability in clinical responses among participants, hence obscuring effectiveness signals for insulin degludec.

2. **Complexity of Multi-Drug Regimen**: Extensive background medications including oral anti-diabetic agents' interactions with insulin degludec posed difficulties in isolating the effect of the drug, thereby complicating outcome measures and leading to inconclusive results regarding efficacy.

3. **Divergent Pharmacodynamic Responses**: Notably, disparities in metabolic responses across the varied ethnic groups in the trial led to uneven glycemic control outcomes, which directly affected the overall efficacy observed.

4. **Patient Heterogeneity**: The wide spectrum of comorbidities in the enrolled population contributed to variable treatment responses. Many participants suffered from conditions that can significantly influence insulin sensitivity, thereby impacting the assessment of insulin degludec's efficacy.

5. **Administration Frequency Misalignment**: The mandated injection schedule, requiring evening administration with inflexible dosing, did not accommodate individual patient preferences or lifestyles, leading to suboptimal adherence to the treatment regimen, ultimately exacerbating glycemic levels.

In conclusion, the anticipated therapeutic improvements with insulin degludec were not realized in this multifaceted clinical trial due to these critical factors that limited participant adherence and the ability to assess treatment effects meaningfully across a demographically varied patient population.
