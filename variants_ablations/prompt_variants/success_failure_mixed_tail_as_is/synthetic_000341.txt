**Successful clinical trial of warfarin: Warfarin Efficacy and Safety in Afro-Caribbean Patients With Atrial Fibrillation (WEASPAF)**

A Phase 3, Randomized, Double-Blind, Active-Controlled, Multicenter Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Warfarin for Stroke Prevention in Afro-Caribbean Patients With Nonvalvular Atrial Fibrillation

**BACKGROUND**: Atrial fibrillation (AF) significantly contributes to stroke risk among diverse populations, yet there is a limited understanding of the efficacy of anticoagulation therapy, particularly warfarin, within Afro-Caribbean populations. Given the distinct genetic, environmental, and clinical characteristics of this demographic, the WEASPAF trial aimed to assess the effectiveness and safety of warfarin in preventing stroke and system embolism in Afro-Caribbean patients diagnosed with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation.

**DESIGN NARRATIVE**: This trial was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, active-controlled study conducted across twelve clinical sites in the Caribbean, including Jamaica, Barbados, and Trinidad. A total of 500 patients aged ≥ 50 years with a confirmed diagnosis of nonvalvular atrial fibrillation and at least one additional risk factor for stroke were enrolled. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either warfarin or a standard comparator, apixaban, with treatment duration set for 18 months. Strict inclusion criteria ensured a focus on patients with certain baseline characteristics such as hypertension, diabetes, and a history of transient ischemic attacks.

The rigorous methodology employed multicenter randomization and double-blinding to minimize bias and improve the reliability of trial outcomes. The primary endpoint measured was the incidence of stroke and systemic embolism, with secondary endpoints including all-cause mortality and serious adverse events. Comprehensive monitoring protocols involved regular INR assessments to ensure appropriate dosing and minimize the risk of bleeding complications.

**RESULTS**: The trial achieved conclusive results, demonstrating that warfarin provided superior protection against strokes compared to apixaban, particularly among the Afro-Caribbean population. The incidence of stroke in the warfarin cohort was significantly lower, recorded at 1.2% over the 18-month period compared to 3.5% in the apixaban group (p<0.001). Furthermore, adverse events, including major bleeding episodes, were adequately managed with a well-monitored INR, highlighting the favorable safety profile of warfarin in this population.

**CONCLUSION**: The WEASPAF trial substantiates the long-term efficacy of warfarin in the prevention of stroke in Afro-Caribbean patients with atrial fibrillation. The study reaffirms the importance of personalized anticoagulation therapy, taking into account genetic and demographic factors affecting drug metabolism and response. Enhanced understanding of these parameters will further inform clinical guidelines and optimize treatment approaches for high-risk populations.

Patients diagnosed with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation who exhibit one or more risk factors including age ≥ 65, prior cerebrovascular events, and significant comorbidities were effectively targeted, ensuring a homogenous study cohort that strengthens the external validity of the findings. The integration of long-term follow-up and rigorous safety assessments additionally contributes to the establishment of an evidence-based framework for future anticoagulation strategies.

This successful clinical trial will guide clinicians in optimizing anticoagulation therapy for Afro-Caribbean patients, fostering improved outcomes in the management of atrial fibrillation and reducing the burden of stroke in this high-risk demographic.
