**Successful clinical trial of dapagliflozin: Impact of Dapagliflozin on Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes**

**A 36-week, International, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Phase III Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Dapagliflozin in the Management of Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes.**

**Purpose:**  
This clinical study aims to evaluate the effects of dapagliflozin on diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) outcomes in individuals diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes. The study hypothesizes that dapagliflozin will demonstrate significant improvements in nerve conduction velocity and neuropathic symptoms compared to placebo. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adult patients aged 18 to 75 years.
- Diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes for at least 5 years.
- HbA1c levels between 7.0% and 10.0% despite stable treatment with metformin, sulfonylureas, or lifestyle management. 
- Presence of symptomatic diabetic peripheral neuropathy, defined by scores on the Neuropathy Symptom Score (NSS) of ≥3.
- Baseline assessment indicating measurable impairment in nerve conduction.
- BMI <35 kg/m².

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- History of diabetic ketoacidosis.
- Participation in any other clinical trials within 30 days prior to enrollment.
- History of significant cardiovascular disease or severe hypertension.
- Renal impairment as assessed by eGFR <60ml/min/1.73m².
- Current administration of SGLT-2 inhibitors, GLP-1 analogs, or any neuroprotective agent.
- Active infection requiring antibiotics or hospitalization for infection.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
To evaluate the adjusted mean change in nerve conduction velocity (NCV) from baseline to week 36 between dapagliflozin and placebo groups.

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
1. Change in Neuropathy Symptom Score from baseline to week 36.
2. Changes in quality of life measured by the EQ-5D scale.
3. Safety evaluation through the number of adverse events and incidence of hypoglycemia.

**Study Design:**  
This study employs a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design involving two parallel groups. Participants will receive either dapagliflozin (10 mg daily) or a matching placebo in addition to their existing diabetes management regimen. 

**Rationale:**  
Dapagliflozin's mechanism as an SGLT-2 inhibitor may enhance metabolic parameters, leading to improved glycemic control and subsequently reducing the impact of hyperglycemia on peripheral neural tissues. The successful results of previous trials indicate a correlation between dapagliflozin's efficacy in glucose regulation and its potential benefits in neuropathic symptom amelioration. Careful inclusion criteria ensure the study population is sufficiently homogeneous concerning DPN severity and diabetes management, maximizing the relevance of observed outcomes. 

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Dapagliflozin is anticipated to significantly improve nerve conduction velocity and diabetic neuropathic symptoms compared to placebo, supporting its role as an adjunctive therapy for patients with Type 2 diabetes suffering from diabetic peripheral neuropathy. The thorough assessment of safety, particularly concerning hypoglycemic incidents and tolerability, will bolster understanding of dapagliflozin's clinical applicability in this patient demographic.

**Conclusion:**  
This trial is positioned to provide robust evidence on the role of dapagliflozin in managing diabetic peripheral neuropathy within the context of Type 2 diabetes care, potentially influencing future therapeutic strategies and patient management protocols.
