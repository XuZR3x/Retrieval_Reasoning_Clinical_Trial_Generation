**Failed Clinical Trial of Apixaban: Assessment of Apixaban in Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease for Thromboembolic Prevention**

**Purpose:**  
The goal of this clinical trial was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of apixaban in reducing the incidence of thromboembolic events in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) stages 3-4, while assessing the risk of hemorrhagic complications associated with anticoagulation therapy. Given the complex interplay of CKD pathophysiology and anticoagulation pharmacodynamics, this trial aimed to elucidate the potential benefits and risks of apixaban in this high-risk population.

**Design:**  
This trial was a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized controlled trial (RCT). A total of 500 patients with an established diagnosis of chronic kidney disease (CrCl 15-60 mL/min) and at high risk for thromboembolic complications were enrolled and randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either apixaban 5 mg bid or placebo for a duration of 12 months. Randomization was performed using a computer-generated allocation sequence, ensuring concealed allocation.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adults aged 18 years and older with chronic kidney disease stages 3 or 4.  
- Patients with a clinical indication for antithrombotic therapy due to at least one of the following: history of thromboembolic event, atrial fibrillation, or venous thromboembolism.  
- Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study protocol requirements.   
  
**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients requiring systemic anticoagulation for established conditions.  
- Severe current cardiovascular events that may confound the results (e.g., recent myocardial infarction or stroke within the last 3 months).  
- Hemoglobin <10 g/dL or platelet count <100,000/μL.  
- History of active or significant bleeding tendencies or skin lesions.  
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding, or inadequate contraception in women of childbearing potential.  

**Primary Endpoint:**  
The primary outcome was the incidence of composite thromboembolic events, defined as the occurrence of symptomatic deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, or ischemic stroke. Secondary endpoints included the incidence of major and clinically relevant non-major bleeding events.

**Results:**  
Despite robust enrollment efforts, the trial faced significant adherence challenges. Only 60% of patients randomized to apixaban maintained compliance, highlighting the inadequacies of patient education pertaining to the potential risks and benefits of the treatment. Notably, adverse events necessitating discontinuation of therapy were reported during the initial screening phase, resulting in an underrepresentation of those with more severe diseases.

Further complicating the assessment was the emergence of major hemorrhagic complications in patients receiving apixaban, leading to prematurely elevated concerns regarding its safety profile. Due to the unique pathophysiology of chronic kidney disease affecting pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics, the investigational arm experienced a greater than anticipated incidence of gastrointestinal bleeding compared to placebo. 

Furthermore, the primary endpoint was not met, as the incidence of thromboembolic events did not show a significant reduction in the apixaban group compared to placebo. This raises concerns regarding the sufficiency of therapeutic levels attained, which may have led to inadequate efficacy in this specific population. 

**Conclusion:**  
The trial failed to demonstrate a significant reduction in thromboembolic events utilizing apixaban as a preventive measure in patients with chronic kidney disease stages 3-4, primarily due to insufficient adherence, increased risk of hemorrhagic complications, and limitations in patient recruitment owing to stringent inclusion criteria. This outcome underscores the complexity of managing anticoagulation in the CKD population and necessitates further studies to explore alternative strategies and potential modifications to existing therapeutic protocols to optimize patient outcomes.
