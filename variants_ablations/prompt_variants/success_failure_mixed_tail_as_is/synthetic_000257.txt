**Successful clinical trial of curcumin: Evaluation of Curcumin Supplementation on Neurological Recovery Post-Stroke**

**Study Design:**

A 12-month, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy of curcumin supplementation in improving neurological recovery in post-stroke patients. The primary objective is to assess the impact of curcumin on functional recovery, cognitive performance, and microstructural changes in brain imaging.

**Participants:**

Eighty post-stroke patients aged 45-80 years, diagnosed with ischemic stroke within 2 to 6 months prior to enrollment, will be recruited. Participants will be sourced from multiple rehabilitation centers following strict inclusion and exclusion criteria.

**Procedure:**

Participants will be randomly assigned to one of two treatment groups: curcumin (Curcumin Forte®, 500 mg twice daily) or placebo. The study will consist of regular evaluations at baseline, 3, 6, and 12 months post-treatment initiation. Advanced neuroimaging techniques, including diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) using MRI, will be performed at baseline and at the end of the study to observe changes in white matter integrity associated with treatment. Functional assessments will include the Fugl-Meyer Assessment (FMA) for motor recovery and the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) for cognitive function.

**Hypotheses:**

1. Patients receiving curcumin will demonstrate superior functional recovery compared to the placebo group after 12 months, as measured by FMA scores.
2. Curcumin will significantly improve cognitive performance scores on MoCA compared to placebo.
3. DTI will reveal enhanced microstructural integrity (measured by fractional anisotropy) in curcumin-treated patients compared to placebo.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age between 45-80 years.
- Diagnosis of ischemic stroke confirmed by CT or MRI.
- Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study protocols.
- National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score between 4-15 at baseline.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of hemorrhagic stroke or other significant neurological disorders.
- Major psychiatric disorders or cognitive impairment prior to stroke.
- Current use of anti-inflammatory medications or supplements exceeding dietary sources of curcumin.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

**Outcome Measures:**

The primary outcome will be functional recovery as assessed by the FMA. Secondary outcomes will include cognitive improvements measured by MoCA, correlating with DTI findings. Blood samples will be collected at baseline and 12 months to analyze circulating inflammatory markers, providing insight into curcumin's potential mechanisms of action.

**Rationale and Expected Outcomes:**

This trial harnesses established diagnostic criteria for participant selection and utilizes advanced imaging techniques to monitor structural changes correlated with functional and cognitive recovery. The double-blind, placebo-controlled design will mitigate biases and enhance the statistical validity of our findings. The longitudinal follow-up will allow for the detection of gradual improvements.

Given curcumin's anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective properties, we postulate its supplementation will facilitate recovery post-stroke, potentially transforming therapeutic strategies in stroke rehabilitation. The alignment with precision medicine is strengthened through the ongoing assessment of inflammatory markers, which may help tailor interventions based on patient-specific responses.

The anticipation of establishing curcumin's utility as an adjunct therapeutic agent in stroke recovery reaffirms its promise beyond dietary significance, marking a crucial advancement in clinical neurology. The findings from this trial may serve to lay the groundwork for larger-scale studies and future clinical applications.
