**Failed Clinical Trial of Carboplatin: Combination Therapy in Treating Patients with Stage IV Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**A Phase II Study of Carboplatin and Gemcitabine in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**RATIONALE:** Chemotherapeutic agents such as carboplatin and gemcitabine are utilized to target the proliferation of tumor cells in non-small cell lung cancer. The hypothesis posited that synergy between these two agents could enhance tumor cell apoptosis and improve overall survival in patients with advanced disease. However, the implementation of combination therapy may harbor limitations, potentially leading to suboptimal outcomes.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of carboplatin and gemcitabine in a population diagnosed with stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objective:** To determine the overall response rate (complete and partial responses) in patients treated with the carboplatin and gemcitabine regimen.
- **Secondary Objectives:**
  - To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) using radiological assessments.
  - To assess overall survival (OS) rates post-therapy.
  - To document the toxicity profile associated with this combination treatment.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter, non-randomized study comprises adult patients with histologically confirmed stage IV NSCLC. Participants will receive carboplatin intravenously on day 1 in tandem with gemcitabine on day 1 and day 8 of a 21-day cycle. Treatment will continue for a maximum of 6 cycles or until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** The total sample size estimated for enrollment is 150 patients, with an anticipated duration of accrual spanning 18 months.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer, stage IV.
- Documented measurable disease per RECIST criteria.
- Patients must have either not received prior systemic chemotherapy for advanced disease or have progressed following previous treatment.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: ≥ 18 years.
- Performance status: Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) 0-2.
- Adequate bone marrow, liver, and renal function indicated by: 
  - Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm^3.
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm^3.
  - Creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Prior history of hypersensitivity reactions to carboplatin or gemcitabine.
- Uncontrolled comorbidities such as cardiovascular disease or severe pulmonary dysfunction.
- Concurrent malignancies or recent treatment with investigational drugs.

**RESULTS:**
Preliminary results indicated an inadequate overall response rate of 12%, significantly below anticipated thresholds. There was a high incidence of dose-limiting toxicities, notably febrile neutropenia and renal impairment, that intersected with participant tolerability, leading to premature discontinuation of therapy in 30% of enrolled individuals. 

**SAFETY CONCERNS:** The toxicity profile revealed that the combination significantly elevated events of severe hematological toxicities, notably thrombocytopenia, alongside increases in renal adverse effects. Utilization of both agents concomitantly yielded a markedly higher incidence of peripheral neuropathy than previously considered acceptable.

**CONCLUSIONS:** This clinical trial of carboplatin and gemcitabine for advanced non-small cell lung cancer demonstrated unfavorable outcomes due to inadequate response rates, elevated toxicity profiles, and complications from patient selection criteria that included a diverse cohort with variable tumor biology. The combination failed to provide a sufficient synergistic effect, ultimately leading to its cessation. Further investigations are warranted to refine patient selection and explore novel dosing strategies for enhanced therapeutic efficacy.
