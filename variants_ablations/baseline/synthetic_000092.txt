Failed clinical trial of ranibizumab: Ranibizumab for Choroidal Neovascularization in Myopic Macular Degeneration

Objective: The primary aim of this Phase III, randomized, open-label trial is to assess the efficacy and safety of intravitreal ranibizumab (0.5 mg) in patients with myopic choroidal neovascularization (mCNV). The study hypothesizes that anti-VEGF therapy with ranibizumab can reduce the progression of visual impairment associated with mCNV.

Background: Myopic degeneration is characterized by an elongation of the eyeball leading to structural changes in the retina, including the development of choroidal neovascularization. Elevated VEGF levels facilitated by the hypoxic state of the tissue are implicated in the disease's pathogenesis. Despite evidence from prior trials asserting the effectiveness of ranibizumab in other forms of neovascularization, the translation of these results to mCNV remains inconclusive.

Design: This multicenter trial intends to enroll 250 subjects aged 18 years and older diagnosed with mCNV. Each subject will receive intravitreal injections of 0.5 mg ranibizumab at baseline, followed by additional injections at 30 and 60 days, determined by the presence of residual activity assessed through OCT and visual acuity measurements. Treatment duration is set for a total of 6 months, with a primary endpoint of mean change in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) assessed via Early Treatment Diabetes Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letters at 6 months.

Inclusion Criteria: 
- Documented diagnosis of mCNV 
- Significant visual impairment defined by a BCVA of ≤ 70 ETDRS letters
- Written informed consent provided by the subject
- Age between 18 and 80 years

Exclusion Criteria:
- Previous treatment with anti-VEGF within 3 months prior to enrollment
- Other ocular conditions that may affect visual acuity outcomes (e.g., advanced glaucoma, significant cataracts)
- Systemic diseases contraindicating ranibizumab usage (e.g., active infections, hypertension poorly controlled)
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding

Outcome Measures: 
Primary: Mean change in BCVA from baseline to 6 months.
Secondary: Changes in central retinal thickness, safety assessments (including ocular and systemic adverse events), and quality of life using the National Eye Institute Visual Function Questionnaire (NEI VFQ-25).

Study Status: Study enrollment failed to meet the projected target of 250 subjects, with only 120 patients recruited across all sites. Contributory factors included an inadequate sample size (limiting statistical power), lack of a placebo or control group for comparison, and participant dropout due to adverse systemic reactions not adequately monitored in the study design. Furthermore, considerable variability in individual responsiveness to ranibizumab due to the heterogeneous pathophysiological landscape of myopic degeneration resulted in an inconclusive outcome. Consequently, the study was terminated prior to achieving any substantial data collection, leading to the inability to evaluate the expected efficacy of ranibizumab treatment in this specific patient population.

Conclusion: The absence of rigorous enrollment strategies, a randomized control arm, and comprehensive systemic safety measures contributed to the failure of this clinical trial, underscoring the challenges inherent in applying anti-VEGF therapies across varied ocular pathologies. Further research is warranted to address these concerns and optimize therapeutic protocols for patient populations with myopic degeneration.
