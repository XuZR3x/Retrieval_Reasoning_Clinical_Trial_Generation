**Successful clinical trial of enzalutamide: A Phase 2 Study of Enzalutamide Combined with Atezolizumab in Patients with Androgen Receptor-Positive, Metastatic Urothelial Carcinoma**

**A Phase 2 Open-Label Study of Enzalutamide in Combination with Atezolizumab in Patients with Metastatic Urothelial Carcinoma**

The objective of this study is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of enzalutamide in combination with atezolizumab, an anti-PD-L1 monoclonal antibody, in patients diagnosed with androgen receptor-positive metastatic urothelial carcinoma (mUC). The rationale for this combination therapy arises from the potential for enhanced immunologic responses by leveraging the androgen receptor blockade alongside immune checkpoint inhibition.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of metastatic urothelial carcinoma with tumor expression of androgen receptors (≥ 1+ by immunohistochemistry).
- At least one measurable lesion as defined by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST 1.1).
- Patients must have received no more than one prior line of systemic therapy for metastatic disease, with prior platinum-based chemotherapy permitted if completed at least six months prior to enrollment.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function as defined by normal parameters for hepatic, renal, and hematological criteria.
- Patients must agree to use effective contraception throughout the trial.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Known history of hypersensitivity to enzalutamide or atezolizumab.
- Current or prior treatment with other immune checkpoint inhibitors.
- History of seizures or conditions that may predispose to seizures.
- Active brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease.
- Concurrent malignancies requiring active treatment.
- Major surgery within 4 weeks prior to trial initiation.
- Previous exposure to androgen receptor antagonists outside of study context.

**Primary Endpoint:**
- Objective response rate (ORR) defined as the percentage of participants achieving a complete or partial response to treatment, as assessed by RECIST 1.1 criteria after cycle 4 (12 weeks).

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Duration of response (DoR) as calculated from the first documentation of response until documented progression.
- Safety profile assessed through the occurrence of adverse events (AEs) per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.
- Progression-free survival (PFS) defined as the time from enrollment until disease progression or death.

**Treatment Regimen:**
Participants will receive enzalutamide at a fixed dose of 160 mg orally once daily alongside atezolizumab at 1200 mg administered via intravenous infusion every 21 days until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or withdrawal from the study.

**Expected Outcomes:**
This study aims to capitalize on the synergistic potential of enzalutamide and atezolizumab to improve treatment responses in patients with advanced urothelial carcinoma. Continuous monitoring of efficacy via imaging and biomarker assessments will provide robust insights into treatment scalability, helping to establish optimal therapeutic pathways for AR-positive mUC patients.

**Adverse Events Assessment:**
All treatment-related adverse events will be documented throughout the study duration, with a focus on those classified as grade 3-4 toxicities that necessitate intervention or discontinuation of therapy. Data collected will contribute significantly to our understanding of the combined therapeutic safety profiles. 

**Anticipated Impact:**
Incorporating comprehensive monitoring methodologies, such as circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) and specific biomarkers for treatment response, aims to elucidate the underlying mechanisms and afford personalized treatment options for urothelial carcinoma, thereby enhancing treatment paradigms in a highly heterogeneous disease landscape.

In conclusion, this study presents an innovative approach that interlinks enzalutamide with immunotherapeutic strategies, paving the way for potential advancements in managing androgen receptor-positive metastatic urothelial carcinoma.
