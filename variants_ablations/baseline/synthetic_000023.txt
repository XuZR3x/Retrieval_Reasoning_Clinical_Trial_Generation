**Failed Clinical Trial of Cilostazol: Investigating the Efficacy of Cilostazol for Pain Management in Patients with Peripheral Artery Disease (CAD-PAD Study)**  

**Phase IV, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Cilostazol in Relieving Intermittent Claudication Symptoms in Patients with Peripheral Artery Disease**  

**Objective**: The primary objective of this clinical trial is to evaluate the efficacy of cilostazol in managing symptoms of intermittent claudication in patients diagnosed with moderate to severe peripheral artery disease (PAD). This study aims to assess if cilostazol can enhance walking distance and improve overall quality of life compared to placebo.  

**Trial Design**: This study was designed as a double-blind, multicenter, randomized controlled trial, comparing cilostazol against a matching placebo.  

**Participants**: The study targeted 500 patients diagnosed with intermittent claudication secondary to stable PAD. Eligible participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either cilostazol 100 mg (twice daily) or placebo for a duration of 12 weeks.  

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
- Adults aged between 40 and 75 years.  
- Documented diagnosis of symptomatic PAD with a Rutherford class of 2 or 3.  
- Ability to ambulate independently for at least 10 meters.  
- No recent history of major vascular surgery (within 6 months).  
- Signed informed consent from participants prior to study enrollment.  

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
- Recent acute limb ischemia within 6 months.  
- History of hemorrhagic stroke or significant bleeding diathesis.  
- Chronic renal dysfunction (creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL).  
- Current use of any medication contraindicated with cilostazol, including significant antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy.  
- Pregnancy or lactation.  
- History of hypersensitivity to cilostazol.  
- Concurrent participation in other interventional studies.  

**Outcome Measures**:  
- Primary Outcome Variable: Change in the 6-minute walk test distance from baseline to the end of the treatment period.  
- Secondary Outcome Variables:  
  - Quality of Life as assessed by the Walking Impairment Questionnaire (WIQ).  
  - Incidence of adverse events including serious cardiovascular complications.  
  - Assessment of claudication onset time.  

**Results**: Initial findings demonstrated an alarming rate of adverse effects including increased incidence of gastrointestinal bleeding. Patients reported heightened discomfort during application due to the adverse effects, significantly leading to reduced adherence. Specifically, a 40% increase in drop-out rates was noted relative to the projected estimates, indicating potential inadequacies in patient engagement strategies.

Pharmacokinetic evaluations indicated that the administered cilostazol dose of 200 mg/day did not achieve the anticipated therapeutic blood levels necessary for optimal clinical outcomes. Patient selection was criticized as heavily biased, primarily including Caucasian subjects from urban settings. This narrow demographic representation restricted the ability to generalize results across other PAD populations characterized by varying comorbidities and demographic backgrounds.

Variability in trial execution, primarily attributed to inconsistent adherence to the study protocol, compounded the anomaly observed in the results. Concurrent medication regimens—particularly the use of aspirin—were inadequately controlled and monitored, further adding to confounding variables that undermined the data integrity.

**Conclusion**: In conclusion, this study failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in walking distance or quality of life for patients treated with cilostazol compared to placebo, leading to the cessation of development of cilostazol for this indication. The results shed light on the crucial need for more inclusive patient selection strategies, optimized dosing regimens, and enhanced adherence monitoring in future clinical trials assessing cilostazol’s efficacy in managing cardiovascular conditions.
