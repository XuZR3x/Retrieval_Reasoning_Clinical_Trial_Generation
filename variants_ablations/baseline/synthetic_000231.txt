```xml
<clinical_trial>
    <study_title>Evaluation of Avastin (Bevacizumab) in Conjunction with Enhanced Dosage Chemotherapy in Patients with Recurrent High-Grade Glioma</study_title>
    <trial_identifier>NCT04567834</trial_identifier>
    <phase>II</phase>
    <design>Multi-Center, Randomized, Open-Label</design>
    <objective>The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the efficacy of Avastin in combination with an optimized high-dose chemotherapy regimen in prolonging progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with recurrent high-grade glioma. Secondary objectives included safety assessment and overall survival (OS) evaluation.</objective>
    
    <population>
        <inclusion_criteria>
            - Adults aged 18 years and older
            - Histologically confirmed recurrent high-grade glioma (WHO grades III and IV)
            - Measurable disease via MRI, with a defined longest diameter
            - ECOG performance status of 0-2
            - Adequate hematologic and organ function
        </inclusion_criteria>
        
        <exclusion_criteria>
            - Previous systemic treatment with Avastin
            - Any concurrent anti-angiogenic therapy
            - History of any major cardiovascular events in the past 6 months
            - New York Heart Association (NYHA) Grade II or greater heart failure
            - Pregnant or lactating women
            - Evidence of active bleeding diathesis or coagulopathy
        </exclusion_criteria>
    </population>
    
    <methodology>
        <treatment>
            <arm>
                <name>Avastin Plus High-Dose Chemotherapy</name>
                <details>Participants received 15 mg/kg Avastin intravenously every 3 weeks in combination with a high-dose chemotherapy regimen of temozolomide at 200 mg/m² daily for 5 consecutive days, supplemented with dose-dense radiation therapy.</details>
            </arm>
            <arm>
                <name>Control - High-Dose Chemotherapy Alone</name>
                <details>Participants received the high-dose chemotherapy regimen without Avastin.</details>
            </arm>
        </treatment>
        
        <randomization>
            - Randomized 1:1 to either treatment arm
        </randomization>
        
        <followup>
            - Participants were assessed every 6 weeks for clinical response and toxicity for a total duration of 12 months.
        </followup>
    </methodology>

    <outcomes>
        <primary_endpoint>
            - The primary endpoint was PFS calculated from the date of randomization until disease progression or death.
        </primary_endpoint>
        <secondary_endpoints>
            - Safety profile assessment through incidence of adverse events including but not limited to hypertension, gastrointestinal perforations, and neurological deterioration.
            - Overall survival measured at 12 and 24 months.
        </secondary_endpoints>
    </outcomes>

    <results>
        <primary_outcome>
            - Analysis revealed no statistically significant difference in PFS between treatment arms, suggesting failed enhancement of therapeutic effects with the addition of Avastin.
        </primary_outcome>
        <safety_findings>
            - Increased incidence of adverse events was documented in the Avastin plus chemotherapy cohort, particularly hypertension and instances of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage leading to treatment discontinuation for some participants.
        </safety_findings>

    <conclusion>
        - The study hypothesized that combining Avastin with optimized high-dose chemotherapy to achieve improved efficacy might improve PFS in recurrent high-grade glioma. However, outcomes indicate limited efficacy of Avastin in this patient population, alongside a concerning adverse safety profile that negatively impacted patient compliance and overall treatment viability.
    </conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
```
