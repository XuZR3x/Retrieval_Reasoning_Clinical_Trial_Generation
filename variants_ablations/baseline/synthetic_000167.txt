<clinical_trial>
    <title>Evaluation of Efavirenz Efficacy and Safety in Antiretroviral-Naive Patients with HIV-1 After 48 Weeks of Treatment: A Phase III, Randomized, Double-Blind, Multicenter Study</title>
    <summary>This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of efavirenz in combination with emtricitabine and rilpivirine in antiretroviral-naive HIV-1 infected subjects over a period of 48 weeks. Given previous concerns regarding inadequate viral load suppression and increased risk of drug resistance, this trial intends to scrutinize the potential of efavirenz as a first-line therapy in varied demographic settings.</summary>
    <objectives>
        <objective>To assess the viral load response at 24 and 48 weeks in subjects receiving efavirenz-based therapy compared to a control group receiving a standard combination antiretroviral regimen.</objective>
        <objective>To evaluate the safety profile, focusing on neuropsychiatric adverse effects, and adherence rates over the duration of the study.</objective>
        <objective>To analyze the incidence of drug resistance mutations associated with efavirenz therapy.</objective>
    </objectives>
    <methodology>
        <design>
            <type>Randomized, Double-Blind, Multicenter</type>
            <duration>48 weeks</duration>
        </design>
        <participants>
            <inclusion_criteria>
                <criterion>Adults aged 18-65 years</criterion>
                <criterion>Documented HIV-1 infection with baseline viral load >1000 copies/mL</criterion>
                <criterion>Naïve to antiretroviral therapy, including pre-exposure prophylaxis</criterion>
                <criterion>Ability to provide informed consent</criterion>
                <criterion>Women of childbearing potential must employ effective contraception</criterion>
            </inclusion_criteria>
            <exclusion_criteria>
                <criterion>Active neuropsychiatric disorders or history of severe mental illness</criterion>
                <criterion>Current or prior serious opportunistic infections affecting outcome</criterion>
                <criterion>History of hypersensitivity to any components of the study medication</criterion>
                <criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding women</criterion>
                <criterion>Individuals concurrently participating in another clinical trial</criterion>
            </exclusion_criteria>
        </participants>
        <treatment_groups>
            <group>
                <name>Efavirenz Group</name>
                <intervention>Efavirenz (600 mg once daily) in combination with emtricitabine (200 mg) and rilpivirine (25 mg)</intervention>
            </group>
            <group>
                <name>Control Group</name>
                <intervention>Dolutegravir (50 mg once daily) in combination with emtricitabine (200 mg) and tenofovir alafenamide (25 mg)</intervention>
            </group>
        </treatment_groups>
    </methodology>
    <outcomes>
        <primary_outcome>
            <outcome>Change in viral load from baseline to week 48</outcome>
            <measurement>HIV RNA levels measured in copies/mL</measurement>
        </primary_outcome>
        <secondary_outcomes>
            <outcome>Rate of neuropsychiatric adverse events</outcome>
            <outcome>Adherence rates assessed through pill counts and self-reports</outcome>
            <outcome>Incidence of drug resistance mutations in HIV RNA.</outcome>
        </secondary_outcomes>
    </outcomes>
    <results>
        <finding>Preliminary findings indicate an overall failure to achieve adequate viral load suppression at both 24 and 48 weeks in the efavirenz group compared to the control group. The rate of neuropsychiatric adverse effects was significantly higher in participants receiving efavirenz, resulting in increased reports of treatment discontinuation due to intolerable side effects. Additionally, a heightened incidence of drug resistance mutations was observed, emphasizing the need for careful patient monitoring and regimen adaptability.</finding>
    </results>
    <conclusion>The trial results indicate that efavirenz-based regimens may not provide sufficient virologic efficacy and safety when administered to antiretroviral-naive patients, raising concerns regarding its use as a first-line treatment option. Further investigations are warranted to evaluate alternative therapeutic strategies that can ensure better outcomes in diverse populations.</conclusion>
</clinical_trial>
