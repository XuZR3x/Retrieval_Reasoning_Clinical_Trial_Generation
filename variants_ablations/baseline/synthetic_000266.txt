**Failed clinical trial of dapagliflozin: Efficacy and Safety Assessment of Dapagliflozin in Older Adults with Type 2 Diabetes and Comorbidities in Primary Care**

**Multi-Center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Dapagliflozin in Older Adults with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus and Multiple Comorbidities**

The primary objective of this clinical trial was to assess the efficacy and safety of dapagliflozin in managing glycemic control among older adults diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus and presenting with multiple comorbid conditions, including but not limited to cardiovascular disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and renal impairment. Despite the anticipated benefits, the trial was terminated prematurely due to overwhelming evidence of ineffectiveness and safety concerns.

In this multicenter trial, eligible participants aged 65 and older, diagnosed with type 2 diabetes and having an inadequate glycemic control status, were randomly allocated to receive dapagliflozin or placebo over a 24-week intervention period. Participants underwent a one-week lead-in phase receiving placebo prior to randomization. The study aimed to evaluate the primary endpoint of adjusted mean change from baseline in HbA1c at 24 weeks, along with secondary outcomes including incidence rates of adverse events, such as hypoglycemia, urinary tract infections, and overall quality of life assessments.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Males and females, aged ≥65 years, diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- Documented history of inadequate glycemic control (HbA1c ≥7.5%).
- Presence of comorbid disorders such as cardiovascular disease, COPD, or chronic kidney disease.
  
**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Significant renal dysfunction defined as estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <45 mL/min.
- Recent hospitalization for any cardiovascular events or severe respiratory exacerbations within the last three months.
- Patients with life-threatening malignancies undergoing active treatment.
- Severe hepatic impairment described as AST/ALT > 3 times ULN.

The trial faced multifaceted challenges primarily linked to the aforementioned design flaws, ineffective patient selection, and adverse safety profiles observed in the treatment group. Patients recruited exhibited significant heterogeneity due to the presence of advanced comorbid conditions that rendered dapagliflozin less effective in controlling glycemia, as anticipated by the trial's hypotheses.

Initial interim analysis revealed suboptimal glycemic outcomes, with the dapagliflozin group demonstrating comparatively trivial changes in HbA1c at 24 weeks vis-a-vis placebo (mean change: -0.2% vs. -0.1%; not statistically significant). Moreover, increased reports of adverse events were noted in the dapagliflozin cohort, with hypoglycemic episodes occurring at an incidence rate exceeding 20%, particularly in those concurrently on other glucose-lowering agents or glucocorticoid medications due to other chronic conditions.

Additionally, safety concerns arose regarding the heightened risk of urinary tract infections and hypotension amongst the dapagliflozin-treated participants, further exacerbated by their existing cardiovascular and renal conditions. The unfavorable risk-to-benefit ratio prompted the ethics committee to recommend premature cessation of the trial.

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint of change from baseline in HbA1c was assessed through centrally analyzed blood samples at screening, week 12, and at week 24. Secondary endpoints included safety outcomes such as the incidence of hypoglycemic events, urinary tract infections, and quality of life metrics evaluated through validated questionnaires.

In conclusion, the trial's objective to establish dapagliflozin's value in a population characterized by significant comorbidities and advanced age remains unfulfilled, solely attributable to the high prevalence of adverse effects alongside inadequate efficacy. Subsequent clinical guidelines will require re-evaluation in the context of SGLT2 inhibitor usage among older adults with complex medical histories, underscoring the necessity for meticulous patient selection and treatment duration to delineate potential therapeutic benefits.
