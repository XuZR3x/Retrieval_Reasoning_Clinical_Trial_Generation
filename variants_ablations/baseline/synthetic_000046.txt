**Title:** Evaluating the Long-term Effect of Aspirin in Combination with P2Y12 Inhibitors on Patency and Clinical Outcomes After Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) in Patients with Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS): A Multicenter Randomized Trial (CAPITAL-PCI)

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the long-term efficacy and safety profile of a dual antiplatelet regimen consisting of aspirin (81 mg daily) in combination with ticagrelor (90 mg twice daily) versus ticagrelor monotherapy alone in preserving vein graft patency and improving clinical outcomes in patients who underwent PCI following ACS.

**Background:** The current literature substantiates the critical role of dual antiplatelet therapy in post-PCI settings, as demonstrated by prior successful trials showing enhanced outcomes with TIC and ASP combinations over monotherapies. However, there remains a gap in knowledge regarding the durability of aspirin's antiplatelet effects when used in conjunction with the more potent tiagrelor and the optimal role of lower-dose aspirin in terms of safety and efficacy. This trial is designed to elucidate these aspects and further support clinical decision-making in the context of antiplatelet therapy.

**Study Design:** This will be a multicenter, randomized, open-label trial. A total of 650 eligible patients aged 18 to 80 years, with a diagnosis of ACS who have successfully undergone PCI, will be recruited across multiple cardiology centers. Patients will be randomly assigned to one of two groups in a 1:1 ratio:
- Group A: Aspirin 81 mg once daily in combination with ticagrelor 90 mg twice daily for 12 months.
- Group B: Ticagrelor 90 mg twice daily alone for 12 months.

Primary efficacy endpoints will be assessed at 12 months via multislice computed tomography angiography (MSCTA) for vein graft patency rates. Secondary endpoints will include rates of major adverse cardiac events (MACE), stent thrombosis, and bleeding incidents, evaluated through clinical follow-up and adverse effect monitoring.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Provision of informed consent prior to any study-specific procedures
- Adult patients (≥18 years) with confirmed ACS undergoing PCI
- Ability to comply with the study protocol

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Absolute contraindications to aspirin or ticagrelor
- Anticipated need for major surgery that would necessitate cessation of antiplatelet therapy during the study period
- Active malignancy or other significant comorbidities with a limited life expectancy (<1 year)
- Prior history of gastrointestinal bleeding or other bleed diathesis within the last year
- Participation in other investigational trials within the last 30 days

**Endpoints:**
- Primary endpoint: Vein graft patency assessed through MSCTA, defined as FitzGibbon grade A (stenosis <50%).
- Secondary endpoints: Clinical outcomes including rates of MACE (comprising cardiac death, myocardial infarction, and urgent revascularization), incidence of stent thrombosis, and bleeding events as categorized by the Bled score.

**Statistical Considerations:** With a one-year vein graft patency rate assumption of 85% in the combination therapy group (aspirin + ticagrelor) and 75% in the monotherapy group, a total of 650 patients provides 80% power to detect statistical significance at a two-sided alpha level of 0.05.

**Conclusion:** The CAPITAL-PCI trial is designed to rigorously evaluate the clinical efficacy of aspirin as a vital component in dual antiplatelet therapy alongside ticagrelor in patients post-PCI for ACS. Given the robust methodology and the essential endpoints, the study aims to substantiate the role of aspirin in enhancing vein graft patency while assessing the safety profile in the context of potent P2Y12 inhibition, contributing significantly to the existing body of knowledge and clinical practice.

**Anticipated Outcomes:** The successful completion of this trial may reinforce the importance of tailored antiplatelet regimens and reevaluate aspirin’s long-standing position as a cornerstone therapy in contemporary antithrombotic strategies following ACS interventions.
