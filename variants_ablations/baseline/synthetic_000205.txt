**Clinical Trial Report: Entinostat in Patients With Advanced Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS)**

**Title:** A Phase 1 Study of the Safety, Tolerability, and Pharmacokinetics of Entinostat in Patients With Advanced Myelodysplastic Syndromes

**Background:**
Entinostat is proposed for use in patients with advanced myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), a group of heterogeneous hematological disorders characterized by ineffective hematopoiesis. Preclinical data suggest that entinostat's role as a histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor may enhance cell differentiation and induce apoptosis, thereby eliciting a therapeutic response in MDS patients.

**Objectives:**
- Primary: To determine the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of entinostat administered as a single agent in patients with advanced MDS.
- Secondary: To evaluate safety and tolerability, characterize pharmacokinetics, and preliminarily assess the anti-tumor activity of entinostat, along with evaluating biomarkers of histone acetylation in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs).

**Design:**
This is a Phase 1, multi-center, open-label dose-escalation study.

**Study Population:**
Participants will include adults aged ≥18 years with a confirmed diagnosis of advanced MDS, defined by IPSS criteria. Patients must have completed therapy for any prior malignancies (excluding MDS) at least 6 months prior to enrollment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed MDS of any subtype (IPSS high or intermediate-2 risk)
- Patients have failed at least one line of conventional therapy (e.g., hypomethylating agents, lenalidomide)
- Adequate organ function defined as:
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 8.0 g/dL
  - Platelet count ≥ 50,000/mm³
  - Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) ≥ 1,000/mm³
  - Creatinine clearance ≥ 50 mL/min

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with acute leukemia transformation
- Concomitant anti-neoplastic therapy or investigational drugs
- Prior history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to entinostat or related compounds

**Methodology:**
Drug administration will utilize an escalating dose design, starting at 5 mg orally once per week. Safety assessments, including laboratory evaluations and adverse event monitoring, will occur biweekly. A minimum of 3 patients will be treated per dose level before proceeding to escalate to a higher dose. PK analyses will include serum entinostat levels at designated time points following drug administration.

**Outcomes:**
- Primary endpoint: Evaluation of adverse events and dose-limiting toxicities to establish MTD.
- Secondary endpoint: Evaluation of overall response rates using standard criteria, pharmacokinetic profiles, and biomarker analysis correlating histone acetylation levels with clinical responses.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Descriptive statistics will summarize patient demographics, safety, and efficacy data. Correlation analyses will assess relationships between pharmacokinetic parameters and clinical outcomes.

**Estimated Enrollment:**
60 participants across multiple centers, projected to span 18 months.

**Significance:**
This study aims to complement ongoing MDS treatment strategies by elucidating the therapeutic potential of entinostat through its mechanisms of action, including the modulation of histone acetylation. The rigorous pharmacokinetic analyses incorporated into the study design, along with the defined eligibility criteria based on disease state and prior treatments, are anticipated to improve the likelihood of clinical success.

**Conclusion:**
Should the trial demonstrate a favorable safety profile and preliminary efficacy, entinostat may emerge as a viable therapeutic option for patients with advanced myelodysplastic syndromes, thereby addressing an unmet clinical need in this patient population. 

**References:**
Additional references related to the pharmacodynamics of entinostat and prior clinical efficacy in hematologic malignancies will be integrated as the study progresses.

This trial design reflects the incorporation of robust pharmacokinetic assessment, collaborative monitoring of adverse effects, stringent patient selection criteria, and the analyses of biomarkers to correlate with treatment effects, drawing from the reasons listed for the success of clinical trials involving entinostat.
