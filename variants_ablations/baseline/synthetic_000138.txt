**Successful Clinical Trial of Chlorambucil: A Phase III Trial Evaluating Chlorambucil Plus Lenalidomide Versus Chlorambucil Alone in Elderly Patients With Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia**

**RATIONALE:** Chlorambucil is a well-established alkylating agent utilized in the treatment of chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), showing efficacy in inducing apoptosis in neoplastic B-cells. Its combination with immunomodulatory agents such as lenalidomide is hypothesized to further bolster the immune-mediated cytotoxic response, potentially improving overall clinical outcomes. This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of chlorambucil in conjunction with lenalidomide against chlorambucil monotherapy in the treatment of elderly patients with previously untreated CLL.

**PURPOSE:** This randomized, open-label Phase III trial is designed to assess the efficacy and safety of chlorambucil combined with lenalidomide compared to chlorambucil alone in elderly patients (≥ 65 years) diagnosed with previously untreated CLL.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- Primary Objective: To evaluate and compare the overall response rate (ORR) of chlorambucil plus lenalidomide versus chlorambucil alone in a cohort of elderly patients with untreated CLL.
- Secondary Objectives: 
   - To assess progression-free survival (PFS) in both treatment groups.
   - To evaluate the safety profile and tolerate adverse effects of the treatment regimens.
   - To determine the quality of life (QoL) measures between both cohorts.

**OUTLINE:** This multicenter study will enroll roughly 300 patients, stratified based on the Rai staging (low-risk vs high-risk) of CLL and renal function (normal vs impaired). Patients will be randomized to one of two treatment arms:
- **Arm I:** Patients will receive chlorambucil orally at a dosage of 0.1 mg/kg on days 1-10 of a 28-day cycle, combined with lenalidomide at a dosage of 10 mg orally on days 1-21.
- **Arm II:** Patients will receive chlorambucil at the same dosage as Arm I, but without the addition of lenalidomide.

Patients will be monitored for treatment response via objective endpoints such as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), and PFS assessed through imaging and clinical criteria at baseline and at predetermined intervals throughout the study period.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Diagnosis of chronic lymphocytic leukemia as per the International Workshop on CLL criteria.
- Patients must be previously untreated and require initiation of therapy, characterized by any of the following:
    - Hemoglobin < 10 g/dL or symptomatic anemia.
    - Platelet count < 100 x 10^9/L.
    - Presence of symptomatic splenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, or weight loss > 10%.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age ≥ 65 years.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0-2.
- Life expectancy ≥ 6 months.
- Adequate marrow, liver, and renal function parameters.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Known transformation of CLL to acute lymphoblastic leukemia or Richter's transformation.
- Active chronic infectious diseases including HIV, hepatitis B/C.
- Prior history of malignancy except for skin basal cell carcinoma or carcinoma in situ.
- Recent major surgery or systemic corticosteroids (within 4 weeks prior to registration).

**PRIMARY EFFICACY OUTCOME MEASURE:**
Overall response rate (ORR) defined as the proportion of patients achieving either CR or PR based on the International Working Group criteria.

**SECONDARY EFFICACY OUTCOMES:**
- Progression-Free Survival (PFS) defined as the time from randomization to disease progression or death from any cause.
- Quality of life assessments utilizing validated instruments (EORTC QLQ-C30) administered at baseline, during treatment cycles, and at the end of treatment.

**SAFETY ASSESSMENT:**
Adverse effects will be recorded and graded according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), v5.0, to evaluate the safety profile of chlorambucil with or without lenalidomide.

**CONCLUSION:** This Phase III trial systematically evaluates the synergistic potential of chlorambucil combined with lenalidomide in an elderly patient population diagnosed with CLL. The hypothesis that this combination may enhance therapeutic efficacy while maintaining a manageable safety profile warrants rigorous investigation, based on the accumulated knowledge of chlorambucil’s pharmacological advantages and the demonstrated benefits of combination therapies in similar contexts.
