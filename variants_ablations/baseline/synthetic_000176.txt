Successful clinical trial of tki258: Efficacy and Tolerability of TKI258 in Combination with Immunotherapy in Patients with Advanced Solid Tumors

A Phase II, Open-Label, Multi-Center Study to Evaluate the Efficacy, Safety, and Pharmacodynamic Markers of TKI258 Administered in Combination with Immunotherapeutic Agents in Adult Patients with Advanced Solid Tumors

This is an open-label, multi-center, Phase II study designed to assess the efficacy, safety, and pharmacodynamic response of TKI258 in combination with established immunotherapeutic regimens in adult patients with advanced solid tumors who have progressed on standard therapies or for whom no curative options remain. The study aims to elucidate the potential synergistic effects of targeted therapy and immunotherapy while evaluating pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic parameters. 

Inclusion Criteria:
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of an advanced solid tumor (excluding hematologic malignancies) that has progressed despite standard therapy, or for which no standard therapy exists.
- Patients must be 18 years of age or older with an ECOG performance status of 0 or 1.
- Adequate organ function as evidenced by pre-specified laboratory parameters.
- Patients must provide written informed consent and agree to use effective contraception during the study.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Patients with untreated or symptomatic brain metastases.
- Concurrent treatment with other investigational agents or immunotherapy in the past 28 days.
- Severe and/or uncontrolled comorbid conditions that would compromise the safety of the study participants.
- Patients who are pregnant, breast-feeding, or planning to conceive during the study.
- History of other malignancies within the last 5 years, except for non-melanoma skin cancer or localized prostate cancer.

The study will monitor the pharmacokinetic parameters Cmax and AUClast for TKI258 when administered in combination with immunotherapy to determine the bioavailability and absorption characteristics. Comprehensive exclusion criteria will mitigate confounding variables, ensuring a focused evaluation of TKI258's efficacy. 

Adverse events will be meticulously recorded from the outset of treatment to assess the safety profile of TKI258 in conjunction with immunotherapeutic agents. The open-label design facilitates real-world applicability, enhancing participant engagement and the reliability of outcomes. Treatment response will be evaluated according to RECIST 1.1 criteria, focusing on tumor response rates and progression-free survival.

This study leverages the successful methodologies established in previous trials that emphasized well-defined patient populations, robust study designs, and extensive pharmacokinetic monitoring to substantiate the therapeutic potential of TKI258 in advanced solid tumors amid standard treatment failure. The anticipated results will contribute to an enhanced understanding of TKI258's role within a combined treatment paradigm and delineate its safety and efficacy profile in a challenging patient population.
