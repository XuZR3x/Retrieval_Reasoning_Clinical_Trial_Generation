<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>A 26-Week Randomised, Double-Blind, Confirmatory Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Insulin Degludec Versus Insulin Glargine in a Diverse Cohort of Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus</Title>
    
    <Objective>
        The primary objective of this trial is to assess the efficacy and safety of insulin degludec (IDeg) compared to insulin glargine (IGlar) in participants with type 2 diabetes currently controlled on metformin and/or sulphonylureas.
    </Objective>
    
    <StudyType>
        Multicenter, international, double-blind, randomized controlled trial.
    </StudyType>
    
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Male and female subjects aged 18-75 years.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus for a minimum of 12 months.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Current treatment with metformin and/or sulphonylureas with stable doses for at least 3 months prior to screening.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Glycosylated haemoglobin (HbA1c) levels between 7.0% and 10.0% at screening.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Body Mass Index (BMI) ≤ 40.0 kg/m^2.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>History of cardiovascular disease or cerebrovascular events within 12 months prior to screening.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Uncontrolled hypertension (systolic blood pressure ≥ 160 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 100 mmHg).</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Use of antidiabetic medications outside of permitted treatments within the past 3 months.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>History of severe hypoglycemia events resulting in loss of consciousness.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Pregnant or breastfeeding women or those not using adequate contraception methods.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    
    <Endpoints>
        <PrimaryEndpoint>Change in glycosylated haemoglobin (HbA1c) from baseline to 26 weeks.</PrimaryEndpoint>
        <SecondaryEndpoints>
            <SecondaryEndpoint>Change in body weight from baseline to 26 weeks.</SecondaryEndpoint>
            <SecondaryEndpoint>Incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) during the study period.</SecondaryEndpoint>
        </SecondaryEndpoints>
    </Endpoints>
    
    <Results>
        <Outcome>
            The trial demonstrated variability in pharmacodynamic effects of insulin degludec across ethnicities, notably resulting in suboptimal glycemic control particularly within specific demographic subgroups.
        </Outcome>
        <Outcome>
            A high rate of exclusion criterion violations was identified, significantly reducing eligible participant representation and generalizability of results across the broader type 2 diabetes population.
        </Outcome>
        <Outcome>
            Underestimation of overall insulin sensitivity due to historical treatment factors and inadequate long-term follow-up resulted in misleading interpretations of IDeg efficacy versus IGlar.
        </Outcome>
        <Outcome>
            The focus on HbA1c as a singular primary measure did not capture the variability in glycemic control, contributing to the conclusion of no significant efficacy advantage for IDeg.
        </Outcome>
        <Outcome>
            Safety profiles indicated enhanced adverse reactions in specific subpopulations, particularly individuals with comorbid conditions, leading to higher discontinuation rates and overall trial attrition.
        </Outcome>
    </Results>
</ClinicalTrial>
