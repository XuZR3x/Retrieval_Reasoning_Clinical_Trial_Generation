**Clinical Trial of Brexpiprazole in Patients With Major Depressive Disorder Exhibiting Cognitive Impairment**

**Protocol 331-23-001:** An Exploratory, Multicenter, Open-label, Flexible-dose Study of Brexpiprazole in Patients With Major Depressive Disorder and Cognitive Impairment

**Objective:** The primary objective of this trial is to assess the efficacy and safety of adjunctive brexpiprazole treatment in adult patients diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) who exhibit cognitive impairment. Cognitive deficits are recognized as significant contributors that can complicate the effective management of MDD, and this study aims to evaluate brexpiprazole's potential to ameliorate such dysfunctions.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Participants must be aged 18 to 65 years at the time of informed consent.
- A diagnosis of a single or recurrent episode of nonpsychotic Major Depressive Disorder, confirmed using the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fourth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-IV-TR™) and corroborated through the Mini International Neuropsychiatric Interview (MINI).
- Evidence of cognitive impairment as established by a baseline score below a predefined threshold on the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA).
- Inadequate response to at least one previous antidepressant treatment, validated by both patient self-report and a score on the Massachusetts General Hospital Antidepressant Treatment Response Questionnaire (ATRQ).
- Patients must have been treated with a stable dose of a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) or serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (SNRI) for a minimum duration of 6 weeks prior to the screening visit.
- A total score on the Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) of ≥ 20 at the time of screening and baseline visit.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Current Axis I DSM-IV-TR diagnoses other than Major Depressive Disorder, including substance-induced mood disorder or psychotic disorder.
- Patients with significant comorbidities impacting cognitive function, including neurological disorders, severe head injury, or any diagnosis associated with substantial risk of cognitive decline.
- Individuals deemed unsuitable for participation due to clinical judgment of the investigator.

**Study Design:** This interventional study adopts a flexible-dose design whereby the dosage of brexpiprazole is titrated based on the individual patient’s therapeutic needs and tolerability, promoting optimal treatment outcomes for a complex clinical population characterized by both depressive and cognitive symptomatology. The open-label format aims to enhance patient engagement and adherence, facilitating a stronger patient-provider therapeutic relationship over the trial duration.

**Primary Efficacy Endpoint:** The primary endpoint is the mean change in the MADRS total score from baseline to the 12-week endpoint. Secondary endpoints will assess cognitive functioning utilizing the MoCA and changes in overall quality of life measured by the EuroQoL-5D.

**Data Analysis:** Efficacy will be analyzed using mixed-model repeated measures (MMRM) techniques to account for within-subject correlations and missing data. Statistical significance will be set at a p-value of < 0.05 for all primary and secondary endpoints.

**Outcome Measures:** Improvement in mood as measured by the MADRS and enhancement of cognitive function as evidenced by MoCA score progression from baseline to endpoint will be the key indicators of therapeutic success. The integration of validated assessment scales ensures reliable efficacy outcomes while possibly uncovering additional symptomatic improvements across categories such as anxiety and functional status.

This study is anticipated to provide critical insights into the broader application of brexpiprazole in managing MDD patients with cognitive impairments, representing a step towards comprehensive psychiatric care that holistically addresses the multifaceted nature of mood disorders.
