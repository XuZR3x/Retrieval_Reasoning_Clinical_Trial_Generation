**Successful clinical trial of doxorubicin hydrochloride: Targeted Doxorubicin Therapy Plus Comprehensive Patient Stratification in High-Risk Breast Cancer**

**RATIONALE:** The implementation of targeted doxorubicin therapy in conjunction with precise patient stratification aims to enhance therapeutic efficacy and minimize adverse effects. This approach permits individualized treatment plans, allowing for critical treatment modifications based on patient response and risk factors associated with breast cancer recurrence. Doxorubicin hydrochloride's mechanism disrupts DNA replication in rapidly dividing cells, specifically targeting cancerous tissue while being adapted to the patients’ unique disease profiles.

**PURPOSE:** This randomized phase III trial evaluates the effectiveness and safety of targeted doxorubicin therapy combined with optimized patient stratification against standard doxorubicin treatment in participants with high-risk breast cancer.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- Determine the overall survival rate among patients receiving targeted doxorubicin therapy versus standard treatment.
- Assess the disease-free survival rate and recurrence rate in patients administered the targeted approach.
- Evaluate the efficacy of designed stratification criteria in predicting treatment responses and managing adverse events.

**OUTLINE:** This study is a multicenter, randomized control trial where patients are stratified based on high-risk biomarkers (HER2, hormonal receptor status) and genetic profiling (BRCA mutation status) that indicate likely response to doxorubicin. Participants are randomized into two groups.

- **Arm I:** Patients receive doxorubicin hydrochloride IV at a dosage of 60 mg/m² every 21 days for a total of 6 cycles, adjusted for toxicity based on individual response and defined eligibility criteria/extensions. 
- **Arm II:** Patients undergo the targeted doxorubicin therapy regimen which includes a combination of doxorubicin hydrochloride IV (60 mg/m²) with agents tailored according to biomarker analysis (e.g., trastuzumab for HER2-positive patients) every 21 days, totaling 6 cycles, along with close monitoring for adverse effects through an integrated management program.

**PROCEDURES:** Following treatment completion, patients are monitored for adverse effects, recurrence of disease, and overall survival rates over a 5-year period. Comprehensive follow-up includes imaging studies and clinical evaluations every 3 months in the first year, every 6 months in the second year, and annually thereafter.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** The trial aims to enroll 500 patients (250 patients per arm) across multiple centers within a duration of 4 years.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed breast cancer with high-risk features defined as clinically node-positive disease, higher tumor grades, or locally advanced disease.
- Prior surgical intervention must have occurred within 12 weeks of enrollment.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: 18 years or older
- Performance Status: WHO 1-2
- Life Expectancy: Not specified
- Hematopoietic: WBC ≥ 3,000/mm³; Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
- Hepatic: Bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times the upper limit of normal
- Renal: Creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min
- Cardiovascular: No significant cardiovascular disease, with a left ventricular ejection fraction above 50%
- Not pregnant or nursing.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- Prior chemotherapy permitted only if last dose was administered more than 4 weeks prior.
- No prior anthracycline or taxane exposure.

**OUTCOMES MEASURED:**
The primary endpoints included overall survival and disease-free survival rates. Secondary endpoints encompassed treatment-related adverse effects evaluation, changes in quality of life metrics post-therapy, and economic assessments of targeted therapy versus standard care.

This trial aims to directly address the identified success factors associated with doxorubicin hydrochloride therapy, namely the influence of strategic combination therapy, hyperthermia potential as adjunct, and thorough patient stratification based on predictive biomarkers, ultimately optimizing treatment efficacy for high-risk breast cancer patients.
