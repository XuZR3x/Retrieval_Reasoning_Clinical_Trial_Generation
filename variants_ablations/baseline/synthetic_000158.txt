**Successful Clinical Trial of Temozolomide: Temozolomide Combined With Anti-Angiogenic Therapy for Newly Diagnosed Glioblastoma Multiforme**

**Study ID:** P05803 AM1  
**Status:** Completed  
**Phase:** II

**RATIONALE:** The combination of temozolomide (TMZ) with an anti-angiogenic agent has shown promising potential in disrupting tumor vascularization, thus enhancing drug delivery to the tumor site. This trial aims to capitalize on the synergistic effects of TMZ and anti-angiogenic therapy in newly diagnosed glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) patients, potentially improving therapeutic outcomes.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of concurrent temozolomide and anti-angiogenic therapy in patients with newly diagnosed GBM. The primary hypothesis is that the addition of anti-angiogenic therapy to TMZ will enhance overall survival compared to historical controls receiving temozolomide monotherapy.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** To assess the overall survival rate at 12 months after initiating treatment with TMZ and anti-angiogenic therapy in patients with newly diagnosed GBM.
- **Secondary:** To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) at 6 months, assess response rates as determined by MRI, and monitor adverse events associated with the combined regimen, including cumulative toxicity.

**OUTLINE:** This study will recruit patients who receive concurrent treatment with temozolomide (150 mg/m² daily for 5 days every 28 days) and a specified anti-angiogenic agent (dosage determined based on previous dosage safety studies) during the initial treatment cycle. Patients will undergo MRI assessments after every two cycles to monitor treatment responses and disease progression.

**ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA:**
- Patients must be aged 18 years or older with a histologically confirmed diagnosis of GBM (WHO grade IV).
- Patients must have a Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) of ≥ 70%.
- Eligible patients must not have undergone prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy for glioma.
- Laboratory criteria must include adequate hematologic and organ function, defined as follows:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mcL
  - Platelets ≥ 100,000/mcL
  - Serum creatinine within institutional upper limit of normal (ULN)
  - Total bilirubin < 1.5 times ULN
  - AST and ALT < 2.5 times ULN
- Women of childbearing potential must test negative for pregnancy and adhere to acceptable contraceptive measures during the study.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- History of allergy or severe reaction to temozolomide or any anti-angiogenic agents under study.
- Presence of significant cardiovascular, hepatic, or renal illnesses that would preclude safe participation.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding patients.
- Patients receiving concurrent anticancer therapies, including investigational agents within 30 days of study enrollment.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- Progression-free survival at 6 months as measured by MRI.
- Overall survival at 12 months, assessed via clinical follow-up and imaging.
- Evaluation of adverse events, graded according to NCI CTCAE version 5.0.

**SAMPLING METHODOLOGY:**  
Patients will be stratified based on MGMT promoter methylation status, which has been shown to impact treatment outcomes. This stratification allows for a targeted analysis of treatment efficacy, particularly for tumors with known resistance pathways.

**MONITORING AND REPORTING:**  
Regular follow-up will ensure meticulous recording of clinical outcomes and safety profiles, with interim analyses scheduled upon reaching predetermined recruitment milestones.

**EXPECTED IMPACT:**  
This trial is expected to corroborate the hypothesis that the incorporation of anti-angiogenic therapy with temozolomide can significantly improve therapeutic outcomes in patients with newly diagnosed GBM, as demonstrated by previous pharmacokinetic studies indicating enhanced drug delivery and potential modulation of tumor microenvironment. By providing a clear understanding of this combination therapy, we aim to establish a new standard of care for glioblastoma patients.
