**Failed Clinical Trial of Arsenic Trioxide: Arsenic Trioxide in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Phase II Study of Arsenic Trioxide in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Advanced NSCLC**

**RATIONALE:** The potential synergistic effect of arsenic trioxide, a cytotoxic agent, with pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody, was hypothesized to enhance immune-mediated tumor eradication in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). This trial aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of this combination therapy given the current challenges in managing patients exhibiting primary or acquired resistance to checkpoint inhibitors.

**PURPOSE:** This Phase II trial was designed to evaluate the overall response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and safety profile of arsenic trioxide in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced NSCLC who had previously received one or more lines of systemic therapy.

**OBJECTIVES:**

**Primary Objective:**
- To determine the overall response rate (defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR)) in patients with advanced NSCLC following treatment with arsenic trioxide and pembrolizumab.

**Secondary Objectives:**
- To assess progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in this patient population.
- To evaluate the safety and tolerability of the combination regimen, with a particular focus on the incidence of immune-related adverse events.

**OUTLINE:** Patients will receive a regimen consisting of arsenic trioxide administered intravenously at a dose of 0.25 mg/kg on days 1-5 every 28 days, alongside pembrolizumab administered at 200 mg IV on day 1 of each cycle. Treatment will continue until radiographic disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or up to 24 cycles.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Histologically confirmed, advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), Stage IIIB or IV.
2. Evidence of progressive disease per RECIST v1.1 criteria following at least one prior treatment regimen.
3. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2.
4. Adequate organ function, defined as: 
   - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN
   - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN (or ≤ 5 x ULN in patients with liver metastases)
   - Creatinine ≤ 1.5 x ULN or creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min
5. Patients must have a life expectancy of ≥ 3 months.
6. Provision of written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients with a history of autoimmune disease or requiring systemic corticosteroids.
2. Prior treatment with arsenic trioxide or pembrolizumab.
3. Controlled central nervous system (CNS) metastases are permitted; however, active or symptomatic CNS metastases are excluded.
4. Known hypersensitivity to arsenic trioxide, pembrolizumab, or their components.
5. Significant cardiovascular conditions, including history of myocardial infarction, unstable angina, or ECG abnormalities such as a prolonged QT interval exceeding 450 msec.
6. Pregnancy or lactation.

**PRIMARY OUTCOME MEASURES:**
Objective Response Rate (CR + PR) according to RECIST v1.1 criteria over the course of treatment.

**SECONDARY OUTCOME MEASURES:**
1. Progression-Free Survival (PFS) defined as time from treatment initiation to disease progression or death.
2. Overall Survival (OS) measured from treatment initiation until patient death or last follow-up.
3. Assessment of treatment-related toxicities including immune-mediated adverse events, graded according to CTCAE v5.0.

**FINDINGS:** The trial was terminated prematurely after an interim analysis revealed inadequate efficacy in terms of both response rates and survival outcomes. The combination regimen failed to show a significant improvement in tumor response in the heavily pre-treated cohort, with only 10% of patients achieving a response, signifying an inability to adequately target the critical signaling pathways involved in NSCLC within this population. Furthermore, safety profiles indicated a concerning frequency of dose-limiting toxicities, including severe dermatitis and hepatic impairment, which necessitated treatment discontinuation in a subset of participants. 

**CONCLUSION:** The trial underscored the challenges associated with the integration of arsenic trioxide into treatment protocols for NSCLC, particularly in individuals with advanced disease. Factors such as inadequate drug synergy, heightened toxicity, and a poorly defined therapeutic window contributed to the failure of this clinical intervention, indicating that further investigation into alternative combination strategies or treatment schedules may be warranted to enhance clinical efficacy. 

**Patient Follow-up:** Enrolled patients will receive follow-up evaluations to assess long-term outcomes and monitor post-trial health states despite the early termination. 

This clinical trial serves as a crucial reminder of the complexities involved in cancer therapeutics and the necessity of rigorous evaluation in advancing treatment options effectively.
