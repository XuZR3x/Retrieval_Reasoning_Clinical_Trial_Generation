**Title:** A Phase II Study of Erlotinib Hydrochloride in Combination with Bevacizumab for Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

**RATIONALE:** Erlotinib hydrochloride, as a selective tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), has shown promise in inhibiting tumor growth. Bevacizumab, a monoclonal antibody that inhibits vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), may enhance the efficacy of erlotinib by disrupting tumor angiogenesis, thereby improving drug delivery and facilitating therapeutic outcomes in advanced NSCLC.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aims to evaluate the clinical efficacy and safety of erlotinib hydrochloride in conjunction with bevacizumab in patients diagnosed with advanced stage IIIB or IV non-small cell lung cancer, focusing on progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary Objectives:**
  1. To determine the progression-free survival in patients receiving the combination of erlotinib hydrochloride and bevacizumab compared to historical controls using erlotinib monotherapy.
  
- **Secondary Objectives:**
  1. To assess overall survival rates in patients on the combination therapy.
  2. To evaluate the objective response rate (complete response [CR] + partial response [PR]) per RECIST criteria.
  3. To assess the safety and toxicity profile of the combination treatment, with a focus on dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs).
  4. To perform pharmacogenetic analysis of EGFR mutation status and measure plasma VEGF levels to correlate with treatment response.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm phase II study. Eligible participants will receive erlotinib hydrochloride orally once daily and bevacizumab intravenously every 21 days. Treatment continues until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

- **Treatment Regimen:**
  - **Erlotinib Hydrochloride:** 150 mg orally once daily.
  - **Bevacizumab:** 15 mg/kg intravenously on days 1 and 22 of each 42-day cycle.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 120 patients will be accrued for this study over 18 months.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed stage IIIB or IV NSCLC (adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, or large cell carcinoma).
- Measurable disease according to RECIST criteria.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age ≥ 18 years old.
- ECOG performance status 0-2.
- Life expectancy ≥ 12 weeks.
- Adequate hematologic function: Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³, hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL.
- Creatinine within normal limits or creatinine clearance ≥ 60 mL/min.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Previous treatment with EGFR inhibitors or bevacizumab.
- Other malignancies within the past 3 years (excluding treated non-melanoma skin cancers).
- Major surgery within the last 4 weeks.
- Uncontrolled hypertension or significant cardiovascular disease.
- Active pulmonary disease or history of interstitial lung disease.

**STATISTICAL ANALYSIS:** Progression-free survival, overall survival, and response rates will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Differences between groups will be evaluated using the log-rank test, with significance set at p < 0.05.

**OUTCOMES MEASUREMENTS:** 
- **Primary Endpoint:** Progression-free survival at 6 months.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Overall survival rates, objective response rates, and comprehensive safety profiles, assessed using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

**FOLLOW-UP:** Patients will be monitored for disease progression and toxicities throughout the treatment duration, with follow-up evaluations occurring every 6 weeks for a year post-treatment.

The integration of individualized pharmacogenetic assessments into the study design will enhance precision medicine, correlating EGFR mutation status with therapeutic efficacy, and potentially tailor future treatment strategies for patients with advanced NSCLC. This study is expected to reinforce the clinical benefits of erlotinib hydrochloride in combination with bevacizumab, offering a prospective standard of care for patients confronting the challenges of advanced lung cancer.
