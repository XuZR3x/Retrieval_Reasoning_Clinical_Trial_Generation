Successful clinical trial of infliximab: Evaluation of Infliximab Efficacy in Patients with Moderate to Severe Psoriatic Arthritis A Phase III, Multicenter, Double-blind, Randomized, Placebo-controlled Study to Determine the Efficacy and Safety of Infliximab (REMICADE®) in Patients with Moderate to Severe Psoriatic Arthritis This study aims to evaluate the safety and therapeutic efficacy of infliximab (Remicade) in patients diagnosed with moderate to severe psoriatic arthritis who are not adequately managed by conventional disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs). Psoriatic arthritis can significantly impair functional capacity and quality of life, necessitating alternative treatments for those with suboptimal responses to traditional therapies. Infliximab, a chimeric monoclonal antibody that targets tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), has been proposed as a viable treatment option due to its mechanism of action in inhibiting inflammatory pathways associated with psoriatic arthritis. Patients demonstrating inadequate clinical response after a minimum of six months on DMARDs will be enrolled. This multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involves a systematic evaluation of two doses of infliximab (3 mg/kg and 5 mg/kg) administered at weeks 0, 2, and 6 after randomization, with subsequent follow-ups to assess sustained clinical improvement. Key efficacy measures will include the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) response rates, which quantify improvements in joint tenderness and swelling, as well as patient-reported outcomes related to health-related quality of life. Safety monitoring will be rigorously conducted throughout the study duration. Inclusion Criteria: - Aged 18 years or older with a documented diagnosis of moderate to severe psoriatic arthritis - Must have had stable doses of DMARDs like methotrexate or sulfasalazine for at least three months prior to screening - Presence of at least five swollen and five tender joints and a physician global assessment score of 3 or higher (on a 0-10 scale) at screening - Demonstrable skin lesions as assessed by the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) Exclusion Criteria: - Previous exposure to infliximab or other TNF inhibitors - History of serious infections, including active tuberculosis or opportunistic infections - Significant comorbidities that may confound clinical assessment, including malignancies in the last five years - Women who are pregnant, nursing, or planning to conceive during the study - Use of systemic steroids within four weeks prior to screening The primary endpoint will be the proportion of patients achieving ACR20 response at week 14, defined as at least a 20% improvement in tender and swollen joint counts as well as patient-reported outcomes. Secondary endpoints will include ACR50 and ACR70 response rates at week 14, changes in PASI scores, and adverse events related to infliximab administration. This robust study design, which leverages a carefully defined patient cohort, standardized efficacy assessments, and stringent exclusion criteria, is anticipated to affirm the therapeutic role of infliximab in the management of psoriatic arthritis and enhance the overall understanding of treatment effectiveness in this challenging patient population.
