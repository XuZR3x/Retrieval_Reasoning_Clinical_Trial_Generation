**Study Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Low-Dose Decitabine in Combination with Venetoclax in Patients with Acute Myeloid Leukemia: A Multicenter Randomized Phase II Trial  

**RATIONALE:**  
Decitabine, a hypomethylating agent, has shown promise in altering the epigenetic landscape of leukemia cells, potentially enhancing sensitivity to other therapies such as venetoclax, a BCL-2 inhibitor. This trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the combination in respect to overall survival and complete remission rates in patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Historically, discordant results have been noted due to issues such as insufficient patient selection and tolerability challenges.  

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVE:**  
To assess the overall survival (OS) of patients with untreated AML receiving low-dose decitabine combined with venetoclax versus standard supportive care.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
- To evaluate the complete remission (CR) rates in both treatment arms.  
- To determine progression-free survival (PFS) in participants receiving the combination therapy.  
- To characterize the adverse event profile associated with decitabine and venetoclax in the study cohort.  
- To investigate potential predictors of response based on pre-treatment cytogenetic and molecular profiles.  

**STUDY DESIGN:**  
This is a randomized, open-label, multicenter clinical trial involving approximately 200 eligible patients diagnosed with previously untreated AML. Participants will be stratified based on age (<60 vs ≥60 years) and cytogenetic risk (favorable vs intermediate vs adverse).  

**STUDY DRUGS:**  
- **Decitabine**: Administered intravenously at a dose of 20 mg/m2 over 1 hour on Days 1-5 of each 28-day cycle.  
- **Venetoclax**: Administered orally at a starting dose of 400 mg daily commencing on Day 6 of the cycle, continued throughout therapy.  

**STUDY TREATMENT REGIMEN:**  
Eligible patients will be randomized into two arms:  
- **Arm A**: Receive low-dose decitabine plus venetoclax.  
- **Arm B**: Receive standard supportive care only.  

**STUDY VISITS:**  
Patients will attend outpatient clinics for treatment administration, monitoring, and assessment at baseline, then on Days 14, 28, and every other month thereafter for up to 1 year or until disease progression or intolerable toxicity occurs. Each visit will include physical examinations, vital signs assessments, laboratory studies for complete blood counts, and monitoring of organ function.  

**EXPECTED DURATION OF STUDY:**  
Expected study duration is approximately 2 years for patient accrual, followed by a minimum of 12 months of follow-up for survival analysis.  

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of AML according to WHO criteria (>20% blasts).  
- Participants aged 18 years or older.  
- ECOG performance status 0-2.  
- Adequate organ function: bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN, creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL.  
- Written informed consent obtained.  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Previous treatments for AML.  
- Active central nervous system involvement.  
- Significant uncontrolled comorbidities or psychiatric disorders.  
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.  

**EXPECTED OUTCOMES:**  
The primary endpoint is overall survival, defined as the duration from randomization to death from any cause, whereas secondary endpoints include the rate of complete remission (CR) and progression-free survival (PFS) measured from treatment initiation to first documented disease progression or death.  

**RESULTS:**  
Preliminary results demonstrated that decitabine combined with venetoclax resulted in insufficient clinical efficacy in the targeted patient population. The combination failed to show a statistically significant improvement in overall survival compared to standard supportive care. Furthermore, the treatment was associated with a high incidence of hematological toxicities, non-hematological adverse effects, and increased discontinuation rates of therapy, echoing previous observations of tolerability issues with decitabine. The heterogeneity in cytogenetic profiles among participants diluted the treatment effect, suggesting discrepancies in patient selection were a significant factor impacting outcomes.  

**CONCLUSION:**  
This randomized phase II clinical trial fails to validate the combination of low-dose decitabine with venetoclax as an effective treatment for AML sufferers. The results align with previous studies pointing to challenges in dosing regimens and patient inclusivity, ultimately reinforcing the need for further research to define optimal therapeutic approaches for this patient population.
