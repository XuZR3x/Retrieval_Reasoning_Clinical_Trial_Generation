**Failed Clinical Trial of Vorinostat: Phase II Clinical Trial of Vorinostat in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Phase II Clinical Trial of Vorinostat in Combination with Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Background** - Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) represents the predominant subtype of lung malignancies and is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally. Despite advancements in surgical resection and systemic therapy, the prognosis remains poor, particularly in advanced stages of the disease. Immune checkpoint inhibition has emerged as a significant breakthrough in oncology; however, a substantial proportion of patients fail to achieve durable responses. Vorinostat, a histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor, has demonstrated the ability to enhance antitumor immunity by modulating the tumor microenvironment. This study aimed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of vorinostat in combination with a checkpoint inhibitor in patients with advanced NSCLC.

**Objectives**  
1. To assess the overall response rate (ORR) of vorinostat combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors in patients with advanced NSCLC.  
2. To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) among treatment cohorts.  
3. To validate potential biomarkers of response, including histone acetylation levels and tumor mutational burden (TMB).

**Design**  
This multicenter, open-label, Phase II study enrolled up to 100 patients diagnosed with advanced NSCLC who had progressed on standard therapies. Vorinostat was administered orally on days 1-21 of a 28-day cycle alongside the immune checkpoint inhibitor.

**Eligibility**  
- Histologically confirmed stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.  
- Documented progression following at least one line of systemic therapy.  
- Measurable disease per RECIST v1.1 criteria (≥1 measurable lesion).  
- Age ≥18 years with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.  
- Adequate hematologic, renal, and liver function parameters.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
1. Patients must have stage IV NSCLC with measurable disease as defined by RECIST.  
2. Patients must be ≥18 years old with ECOG performance status scores of 0-1, indicating full function without major restrictions.  
3. Patients must have received at least one prior systemic therapy for advanced disease.  
4. Patients must provide written informed consent to participate.  

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
1. Any prior history of treatment with HDAC inhibitors including vorinostat; an active infection requiring systemic therapy.  
2. Significant comorbidities precluding participation, including uncontrolled hypertension, cardiac arrhythmias, or severe psychiatric disorders.  
3. Patients with known CNS metastases unless treated and stable for at least four weeks.  
4. Pregnant or lactating women, along with those of childbearing potential not employing effective contraception.  

**Clinical Trial Results**  
The trial's primary endpoint of ORR showed suboptimal results with an overall response rate of only 11%, significantly lower than the anticipated expectations based on preclinical data. Furthermore, the median PFS was 2.5 months, and the median OS was 5.9 months, well below the threshold defined for success in similar studies. 

**Reasons for Failure**  
1. **Inadequate Mechanistic Synergy**: Initial data suggested that vorinostat could potentiate immune checkpoint blockade; however, the anticipated mechanism of action failed to manifest, resulting in minimal improvements in responses. The supposed synergistic effects on immune modulation were not realized in the context of an established tumor microenvironment.  

2. **Limited Patient Population Diversity**: The trial predominantly recruited patients with specific histological subtypes and prior treatment regimens, diminishing the generalizability of the data and potentially biasing the efficacy results based on a selected patient cohort.  

3. **Suboptimal Dosing Strategy**: The initial dosing regimen of vorinostat was determined without adequate pharmacokinetic understanding, leading to potential underdosing which may have limited systemic exposure necessary for therapeutic effects when combined with the immune checkpoint inhibitor.  

4. **Compromised Biomarker Validation**: Although the trial aimed to assess predictive biomarkers such as histone acetylation and TMB, there were challenges in collecting consistent samples. Variability in histone modification responses among the patient cohort rendered these biomarkers ineffective for predicting treatment outcomes.  

**Conclusion**  
The futility of this clinical trial underscores several aspects that necessitate reevaluation. Future studies are encouraged to adopt more comprehensive inclusion criteria, optimize dosing protocols, and emphasize robust biomarker development to better establish predictive relationships and mechanism-driven hypotheses before initiating larger phase trials. An understanding of the underlying biology of synergy between vorinostat and immune checkpoint inhibition remains crucial for navigating potential therapeutic avenues in advanced NSCLC.
