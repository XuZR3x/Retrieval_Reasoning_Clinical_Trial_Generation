<clinicalTrial>
    <title>Lamotrigine Efficacy in Depression Prevention: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study of Lamotrigine in Patients with High-Risk History of Bipolar Depression</title>
    <objective>This trial aims to evaluate the long-term efficacy of lamotrigine in preventing recurrence of depressive episodes in individuals with a documented history of bipolar disorder, particularly those with recurrent depressive episodes despite previous treatment. The study hypothesis proposes that lamotrigine's pharmacological profile enhances mood stabilization through its unique mechanism of action.</objective>
    
    <methodology>
        <design>This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study over a 12-month period.</design>
        <participants>Eligible participants will include adults aged 18-65 years with a confirmed diagnosis of bipolar disorder (I or II) and a history of recurrent depressive episodes. A history of at least three depressive episodes in the past five years is required for inclusion.</participants>
        <randomization>Participants will be randomly assigned to either the lamotrigine (200 mg/day) or matching placebo group using a stratified randomization scheme to ensure balanced distribution across treatment arms.</randomization>
        <protocol>Participants will receive their assigned intervention daily for 12 months, with clinical evaluations and standardized self-reported questionnaires assessing mood and functionality every three months.</protocol>
    </methodology>

    <assessments>The primary endpoint will be the time to the first depressive episode as defined by the DSM-5 criteria. Secondary endpoints will include changes in scores on the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS) and Quality of Life Enjoyment and Satisfaction Questionnaire (Q-LES-Q) over the study period.</assessments>

    <inclusionCriteria>
        <criterion>- Age between 18 and 65 years</criterion>
        <criterion>- Documented diagnosis of bipolar disorder I or II</criterion>
        <criterion>- History of recurrent depressive episodes (≥ 3 episodes in the last 5 years)</criterion>
        <criterion>- Currently experiencing stable mood without imminent risk of acute mania or hypomania</criterion>
        <criterion>- Providing informed consent</criterion>
    </inclusionCriteria>
    
    <exclusionCriteria>
        <criterion>- Diagnosis of any other major psychiatric disorder (e.g., schizophrenia)</criterion>
        <criterion>- Current substance abuse or dependence</criterion>
        <criterion>- History of severe allergic reactions to lamotrigine or related compounds</criterion>
        <criterion>- Severe hepatic or renal impairment</criterion>
        <criterion>- Pregnant or breastfeeding</criterion>
    </exclusionCriteria>

    <outcomes>The study anticipates that treatment with lamotrigine will demonstrate a statistically significant prolongation of the time to first depressive episode compared to placebo, thereby corroborating its established efficacy in mood stabilization and prevention of depressive symptoms within the context of bipolar disorder treatment.</outcomes>

    <conclusion>The study is expected to highlight the advantageous incorporation of lamotrigine into clinical practice for the long-term management of bipolar disorder, emphasizing its role in reducing depressive episodes through not only its monotherapy capabilities but potentially improved outcomes in combination therapy with agents such as divalproex.</conclusion>
</clinicalTrial>
