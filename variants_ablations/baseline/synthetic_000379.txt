**Failed Clinical Trial of Pregabalin: Efficacy of Pregabalin in Patients with Post-Operative Neuropathic Pain Following Major Abdominal Surgery**

**Study Objective:**  
This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of pregabalin (300 mg/day) in managing post-operative neuropathic pain in patients following major abdominal surgery, hypothesizing that pregabalin would significantly reduce neuropathic pain compared to placebo.

**Background:**  
Post-operative neuropathic pain is a prevalent complication that can arise after major abdominal surgery, significantly impairing patients' quality of life. Despite prior studies demonstrating efficacy of pregabalin in various neuropathic pain settings, its application in post-operative pain management remains inadequately characterized. 

**Study Design:**  
This was a multicenter trial conducted across multiple surgical centers, involved 200 patients aged 18 years and older who had undergone major abdominal surgery (e.g., colectomy, gastrectomy, pancreatic surgery). Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either pregabalin (300 mg/day) or matching placebo for a duration of 8 weeks post-surgery. The primary endpoint was the change in neuropathic pain intensity, assessed using the Numerical Rating Scale (NRS), measured weekly over the study duration.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Male and female subjects aged 18 years or older.  
- Undergoing major abdominal surgery with expected post-operative pain management.  
- Reported neuropathic pain symptoms, with an NRS rating of ≥4 at the end of the first post-operative week.  
- Willingness to comply with treatment protocol and follow-up assessments.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- History of significant psychiatric disorders, chronic pain syndromes unrelated to surgery, or prior treatment with pregabalin or gabapentin.  
- Current use of centrally acting analgesics or other neuropathic pain medications.  
- Patients with a surgical history indicating potential complications that could confound pain assessment, such as complications from reoperation.  
- Known allergies to pregabalin or its components.  

**Results:**  
The study was terminated after an interim analysis at the 4-week mark revealed no statistically significant difference in NRS scores between the pregabalin and placebo groups. In fact, the mean reduction in pain scores did not exceed the predefined minimal clinically relevant difference (MCRD). 

Despite rigorous inclusion criteria, the diverse patient cohort demonstrated notable heterogeneity in pain etiology post-surgery, complicating pain assessment. Many participants presented with non-neuropathic pain related to post-operative healing, thereby obscuring the potential analgesic effects of pregabalin. 

Further, the low placebo response observed in this cohort limited the apparent efficacy of pregabalin, leading to challenges in demonstrating superiority over placebo. Patient-reported outcomes also indicated that comorbid conditions such as anxiety disorders and ongoing opioid therapy could have influenced the perception of both pregabalin and placebo efficacy.

This trial highlighted the pharmacokinetic interactions of pregabalin with opioid medications routinely prescribed post-operatively, which may further undermine its effectiveness in such complex pain scenarios, ultimately leading to an insufficient separation from placebo outcomes.

**Conclusion:**  
The failure of this trial underscores the limitations of pregabalin in specific post-operative settings. The findings suggest that pregabalin’s efficacy may not extend effectively to managing neuropathic pain resulting from surgical interventions. Future investigations should consider patient selection, the presence of comorbid pain syndromes, and tailored treatment approaches to enhance the application of pregabalin in diverse pain contexts.
