**Failed Clinical Trial of S-1: Phase II Study of S-1 in Advanced Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma**

**TITLE:** An Open-Label, Non-Randomized, Multicenter, Phase II Study of S-1 in Chemotherapy-Naïve Patients With Advanced or Metastatic Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma 

**PURPOSE:** This study aims to assess the efficacy and safety of S-1, a novel oral fluoropyrimidine agent, in patients with advanced or metastatic esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) who have not previously received chemotherapy. Given the poor overall survival rates and limited treatment options for advanced ESCC, the investigation of S-1's efficacy may provide valuable insights into potential therapeutic avenues.

**RATIONALE:** S-1 consists of tegafur, gimeracil, and oteracil, which together potentially enhance the pharmacokinetic profile of 5-FU while mitigating gastrointestinal toxicities. Despite promising preclinical data, clinical studies have demonstrated inadequate overall tumor response rates, thus prompting the current investigation into S-1's efficacy for this patient cohort.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Participants must provide written informed consent.
2. Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced or metastatic esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, not amenable to curative surgical resection or radiotherapy.
3. Must have measurable disease defined by RECIST criteria.
4. Patients aged 18 years or older.
5. A Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) ≥ 70%.
6. Life expectancy of at least 12 weeks.
7. Normal organ function defined as:
   - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).
   - Total serum bilirubin ≤ 1.5 times ULN.
   - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³, platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³, and hemoglobin ≥ 9.0 g/dL.
  
**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
1. Previous treatment with any anticancer therapies, including any investigational agents.
2. History of significant allergic reactions to study agents.
3. Active gastrointestinal disorders that might affect oral ingestion and drug absorption.
4. Other serious comorbidities that could jeopardize patient safety or study outcomes.
5. Pregnant or lactating women, or those who refuse to use effective contraception methods during participation.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- Primary: Evaluate the overall tumor response rate (ORR) for participants treated with S-1.
- Secondary: Assess the safety profile, including the incidence of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs), and correlate biomarker expression levels (e.g., RRM1, TS, DPD) with treatment response.

**METHODOLOGY:** This study involves a non-randomized, open-label design across multiple centers. Eligible participants will receive S-1 monotherapy, dosed as per body surface area, administered orally for three weeks followed by a one-week rest period to assess for toxicity and efficacy.

**RESULTS:** The trial was halted due to multiple factors leading to evident failure:
1. **Poor Tumor Response:** The obtained ORR fell significantly below the clinically significant benchmarks, reflecting the inherent resistance of ESCC to S-1.
2. **Suboptimal Biomarker Correlation:** Insufficient predictive value of RRM1 and others correlated with poor tumor response, supporting the existence of additional resistance mechanisms inherent in this malignancy.
3. **Safety Concerns:** A considerable fraction of participants experienced intolerable DLTs, such as significant gastrointestinal toxicities, leading to early treatment discontinuation.
4. **Limitations of Non-Randomized Design:** The absence of a comparator group rendered biases relating to patient selection and potential confounding factors, clouding the reliability of the results.
5. **Inefficacious Combination Options:** Attempted combinations with other agents failed to exhibit synergism, compounding the ineffectiveness observed from S-1 monotherapy.

**CONCLUSION:** In conclusion, the Phase II study of S-1 for advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma yielded disappointing outcomes across multiple facets, including efficacy, safety, and predictive biomarkers. Future investigations may necessitate a focus on identifying more efficacious combination regimens, as well as rigorous molecular profiling to better elucidate the underlying mechanisms of chemoresistance in advanced esophageal cancers.

**TRIAL STATUS:** The study has officially been classified as a failure and will not progress to further phases. Participants will be informed about alternative therapeutic options and monitored per institutional protocols.
