**Title:** A Phase 2, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Enzalutamide in Combination with Docetaxel in Chemotherapy-Naïve Patients with Metastatic Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy of enzalutamide as an adjunct therapy to docetaxel in improving overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in chemotherapy-naïve patients diagnosed with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) compared to placebo in conjunction with docetaxel.

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, phase 2, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, adaptive design trial.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed adenocarcinoma of the prostate without neuroendocrine differentiation or small cell features.
- Evidence of bone metastatic disease confirmed by imaging studies, with at least two lesions verifiable through radiological assessment.
- Patients must be receiving standard androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) at study initiation through LHRH agonists or surgical castration.
- Progressive disease as evidenced by sequentially rising prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels, defined as two consecutively increasing PSA values obtained at least 1 week apart.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 to 2.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with chemotherapy or any investigational agents affecting androgen signaling or hormone synthesis.
- Known brain metastases or any history of central nervous system disorders increasing seizure risk.
- Severe cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled co-morbid conditions that pose a risk during participation as per investigator discretion.
- Active visceral metastasis or any history of other malignancies within the past 5 years (except for non-melanoma skin cancers).

**Intervention:** Eligible participants will be randomized (2:1 ratio) to receive either enzalutamide 160 mg orally once daily plus standard docetaxel (75 mg/m^2 every 3 weeks) or placebo plus standard docetaxel. Treatment will continue until the occurrence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Overall survival (OS) at 1-year follow-up.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Progression-free survival (PFS), time to PSA progression, and safety profile assessed through the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) based on National Cancer Institute-Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) guidelines.

**Statistical Analysis:** A total of 300 patients will be enrolled, with an anticipated 150 participants in the enzalutamide group and 75 in the placebo group. Data will be analyzed using Kaplan-Meier methods for survival endpoints, and the log-rank test will be utilized to compare OS and PFS between treatment arms.

**Mechanistic Rationale:** Leveraging the robust mechanism of action of enzalutamide as a non-steroidal anti-androgen, this study seeks to elucidate the potential for synergistic effects when combined with docetaxel, given its known efficacy in targeting mCRPC, thereby enhancing therapeutic outcomes through dual inhibition of androgen signaling pathways and direct tumor cytotoxicity.

**Safety Monitoring:** Continuous safety assessments will be performed at each visit, with particular attention to treatment-related toxicities and submission of unanticipated problems according to institutional review board guidelines.

**Expected Outcomes:** We hypothesize that the addition of enzalutamide to docetaxel will significantly extend OS and PFS compared to docetaxel alone. Rigorous patient selection based on previous therapeutic ineligibility and comprehensive monitoring will serve to optimize the trial's efficacy evaluation, thus reinforcing the established rationale for the combination therapy in mCRPC treatment paradigms.

**Registration:** This trial is registered with the ClinicalTrials.gov identifier [NCTXXXXX].

**Conclusion:** The study is anticipated to provide critical insights into the treatment strategy for chemotherapy-naïve patients with mCRPC and may establish enzalutamide's role in extending survival through enhanced combination therapy, thereby aligning with the rationale that both tumor biology and patient selection are paramount for therapeutic success.
