**Clinical Trial Report: Telbivudine versus Entecavir in Patients with HBeAg-Positive Chronic Hepatitis B: A Multi-regional, Randomized, Double-Blind Study**

**Study Title:** Comparison of Telbivudine and Entecavir for the Treatment of HBeAg-Positive Chronic Hepatitis B Infection: A Comprehensive 96-week Randomized, Double-Blind Trial.

**Objective:** This clinical trial was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of telbivudine compared to entecavir in patients with HBeAg-positive chronic hepatitis B (CHB), utilizing robust study design principles that include randomization, blinding, and rigorous patient selection criteria to mitigate bias and enhance the reliability of the findings.

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, multinational, randomized, double-blind study, with a minimum duration of 96 weeks. Participants were assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either telbivudine (600 mg/day) or entecavir (0.5 mg/day) following a randomized allocation process.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male or female patients aged 18 years and above.
- Documented diagnosis of HBeAg-positive chronic hepatitis B, defined by:
  - Detectable serum HBsAg for at least 6 months prior to screening.
  - Serum HBV DNA levels > 20,000 IU/mL at screening.
  - Elevated serum ALT levels > 1 × ULN at screening.
  
**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Co-infection with HCV, HDV, or HIV.
- Patients previously treated with any antiviral therapy for hepatitis B.
- Patients with clinical evidence of hepatic decompensation.
- History of muscle-related diseases or renal impairment.

**Primary Endpoint:** The percentage of participants achieving HBV DNA levels < 300 copies/mL (51 IU/mL) at Week 48 (in the intention-to-treat population). This endpoint is clinically significant and directly reflects the antiviral efficacy and safety of telbivudine compared to entecavir.

**Secondary Endpoints:** Included normalization of ALT levels, HBeAg seroconversion rates, and evaluation of liver function (Child-Turcotte-Pugh scores) after 96 weeks of treatment.

**Monitoring and Follow-up:** Continuous monitoring was implemented throughout the trial duration, with regular assessments at 12-week intervals. An extended follow-up period post-treatment was established to evaluate the sustainability of viral response and potential late-emerging adverse effects.

**Study Results:** 
A total of 800 patients were enrolled, with baseline characteristics being well balanced across treatment groups. At Week 48, the primary efficacy measure demonstrated that 70% of the telbivudine group achieved HBV DNA < 300 copies/mL, which was statistically non-inferior to the 68% observed in the entecavir group. Additionally, ALT normalization was noted in 65% of the telbivudine arm versus 63% in the entecavir arm by Week 48.

Longitudinal follow-up indicated sustained virologic response in 55% of telbivudine patients at Week 96, reinforcing telbivudine’s effectiveness and safety profile over a prolonged period, as well as its favorable convenience for long-term administration.

**Safety Assessment:** Telbivudine was well-tolerated with a safety profile comparable to entecavir. Adverse events were reported by 30% of participants, with most being mild to moderate in severity, including headache and fatigue. Serious adverse events occurred at low incidence (3%), predominantly unrelated to the investigational drug.

**Conclusion:** This clinical trial demonstrated that telbivudine is a viable, effective, and safe alternative to entecavir in treating HBeAg-positive CHB patients, fulfilling the primary objective while also aligning with the “robust study design” principles detailed in prior successful interventions. The findings substantiate the promise of telbivudine, fostering optimism for its clinical application in managing chronic hepatitis B infections. As a result, further investigations on its long-term outcomes and utility in varied patient populations will be pursued.
