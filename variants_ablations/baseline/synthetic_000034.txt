**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy of Thalidomide in the Management of Chronic Cough in Patients with Interstitial Lung Disease**

**Background**  
Chronic cough is a debilitating symptom prevalent in various forms of interstitial lung disease (ILD), significantly impairing patients' quality of life. Prior studies have suggested that thalidomide possesses anti-inflammatory properties capable of modulating cytokine levels and reducing neurogenic inflammation, rendering it a candidate for cough suppression within this patient population. The present clinical trial aims to evaluate the effectiveness of thalidomide in alleviating chronic cough associated with ILD through its well-documented mechanisms of action, including immunomodulation and synergistic enhancement when combined with standard supportive therapies.

**Objective**  
To assess the efficacy of thalidomide in reducing the frequency and severity of chronic cough in patients diagnosed with interstitial lung disease, utilizing standardized cough assessment tools.

**Study Design**  
This Phase III, multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial will include a total of 200 participants diagnosed with varying forms of interstitial lung disease characterized by persistent cough for a duration exceeding 8 weeks. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either thalidomide (starting dosage 50 mg/day, escalating to a maximum of 200 mg/day based on therapeutic response) or an inactive placebo for a total duration of 24 weeks.

**Methods**  
The primary endpoint will evaluate the reduction of chronic cough severity, quantified through the Cough Quality of Life Questionnaire (CQLQ) and the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) for cough. Secondary endpoints will include quality-of-life assessments captured through the St. George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ), as well as the monitoring of adverse effects via the NCI CTCAE criteria.

Participants will undergo a rigorous monitoring schedule, including baseline assessments, periodic evaluations at weeks 4, 12, and 24, and follow-up at week 30 after trial completion to assess long-term effects and any late-onset adverse reactions.

**Inclusion Criteria**  
- Adults aged 40 to 75 years
- Confirmed diagnosis of any form of interstitial lung disease with a chronic cough lasting longer than 8 weeks
- Baseline forced vital capacity (FVC) greater than 40% predicted
- Adequate hepatic and renal function (AST/ALT < 3 × upper limit of normal, creatinine clearance > 30 mL/min)

**Exclusion Criteria**  
- Significant comorbidities interfering with cough severity evaluation (e.g., active malignancies, severe cardiovascular or renal diseases)
- Prior exposure to thalidomide or other immunosuppressive therapies in the last 6 months
- Current pregnancy or lactation
- Known hypersensitivity to thalidomide

**Statistical Analysis**  
Efficacy will be analyzed using an intention-to-treat approach. Differences in outcomes between the thalidomide group and placebo will be assessed using appropriate statistical methods including ANOVA and chi-square tests, where relevant, with p-values < 0.05 considered statistically significant. 

**Expected Outcomes**  
Given the robust evidence supporting thalidomide’s anti-inflammatory effects, it is hypothesized that administrative use will yield a significant reduction in cough severity as measured through CQLQ and VAS. Moreover, it is anticipated that symptom relief will contribute positively to overall quality of life parameters measured by SGRQ.

**Safety Monitoring**  
An independent data monitoring committee will oversee safety assessments throughout the study, with stringent guidelines established for managing adverse events associated with thalidomide exposure, which historically include peripheral neuropathy and sedation.  Adequate patient education will be provided to mitigate risks associated with teratogenicity.

**Conclusion**  
This trial represents a critical effort to leverage thalidomide's anti-inflammatory properties to improve patient quality of life in chronic cough due to interstitial lung disease. Given the anticipated results from prior studies regarding thalidomide’s efficacy and safety profile, success in this clinical trial would pave the way for further investigations into its therapeutic roles across other chronic cough-related disorders.
