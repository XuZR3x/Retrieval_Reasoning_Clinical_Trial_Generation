**Failed Clinical Trial of Sitagliptin: Evaluation of Sitagliptin's Efficacy in Controlling Postprandial Glucose Levels in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes and Moderate Obesity**

**Objective:**  
To analyze the efficacy of sitagliptin in controlling postprandial glucose levels in overweight patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus, focusing on potential long-term physiological adaptations and the variances in pharmacogenetic responses.

**Hypothesis:**  
Sitagliptin administration will significantly lower postprandial glucose levels compared to placebo over a treatment period of six weeks. However, it is anticipated that the limited duration of treatment may hinder the comprehensive observation of pharmacodynamic stability and responder variability in clinical outcomes.

**Design:**  
A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolling 40 subjects diagnosed with type 2 diabetes and a body mass index (BMI) ranging from 28 to 35 kg/m². Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either sitagliptin (100 mg daily) or placebo for six weeks, followed by a washout period and crossover to the alternate treatment.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Diagnosis of type 2 diabetes mellitus (HbA1c 7.0% - 9.0%)  
- Age between 30 and 65 years  
- BMI between 28 and 35 kg/m²  
- Patients must be naïve to DPP-4 inhibitors and not on any anti-diabetic medications for three months prior to the study  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- History of significant diabetes-related complications including severe neuropathy or nephropathy  
- Concurrent use of medications that may affect blood glucose or interfere with sitagliptin (e.g., corticosteroids, other hypoglycemics)  
- Severe hepatic dysfunction or significant chronic disease states which may exaggerate metabolic dysregulation and impede outcome assessment  
- Pregnant or lactating women, or those not using effective contraception

**Endpoints:**  
Primary endpoint will measure the change in postprandial glucose levels following a standardized meal in the last week of the trial compared to baseline measurements. Secondary endpoints will assess glucagon levels and the incretin response before and after the intervention. 

**Results:**  
Post-treatment analysis indicated no statistically significant difference in postprandial glucose control between the sitagliptin and placebo groups. The limited duration of intervention possibly precluded the observation of longer-term metabolic adaptations and their impact on glycemic control, as predicted. Additionally, the exclusion criteria may have limited sample diversity, which hampered the trial's generalizability to a broader population suffering from type 2 diabetes and obesity.

**Discussion:**  
The findings suggest that the pharmacokinetic interactions between sitagliptin and typical concomitant therapies prevalent among the trial participants may have further diminished its efficacy—an aspect not sufficiently addressed by the study design. Inter-individual variability in patient responses due to pharmacogenetic differences was noted but not measured, which is likely to have contributed to the inconsistency in glucose-lowering effects observed within a homogenous treatment group.

**Conclusion:**  
The study was unable to demonstrate the expected efficacy of sitagliptin in managing postprandial glucose levels in the targeted demographic. This could be attributed to both a short treatment duration and restrictive participant selection criteria, illustrating the need for future investigations that encompass a more extended treatment timeline to fully elucidate sitagliptin's effects in diverse patient populations suffering from type 2 diabetes. Evaluating the pharmacodynamics beyond a six-week period may yield critical insights into the therapeutic potential of DPP-4 inhibitors under varied metabolic conditions.
