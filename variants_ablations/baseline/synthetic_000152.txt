**Failed clinical trial of dasatinib: Investigating Dasatinib in Patients with Advanced Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma**

**Title:** A Phase II Study of Dasatinib (BMS-354825) in Patients with Advanced Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma

**Abstract:** This phase II clinical trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of dasatinib in patients with advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma. The study sought to determine the overall response rate, progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) among patients who have received prior therapies. Given that dasatinib's mechanism of action as a tyrosine kinase inhibitor may not effectively engage critical signaling pathways in pancreatic malignancies, we anticipated significant challenges in achieving meaningful clinical outcomes.

**Objectives:**
- **Primary Objective:** To assess the overall response rate (ORR) to dasatinib treatment in patients with advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma.
- **Secondary Objectives:** To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and adverse events associated with dasatinib administration.

**Study Design:** This multicenter, open-label phase II trial enrolled patients with histologically confirmed advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma. Patients received dasatinib orally at a dose of 100 mg daily for 28 days, repeating cycles every 28 days unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurred. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years or older
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of unresectable pancreatic adenocarcinoma
- At least one measurable lesion per RECIST criteria
- Prior treatment with at least one line of chemotherapy
- Adequate organ function as defined by specific laboratory parameters

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with neurologically symptomatic metastases
- Prior treatment with other investigational drugs within 30 days of enrollment
- Ongoing cardiac arrhythmias or QT prolongation
- Concurrent medications known to strongly inhibit or induce CYP3A4
- Women who are pregnant or breastfeeding

**Endpoints:**
- **Primary Endpoint:** Overall response rate (ORR) as assessed by RECIST 1.1
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Progression-free survival (PFS) calculated from the date of treatment initiation to disease progression or death, overall survival (OS) from the time of treatment initiation until death from any cause, and incidence of adverse events classified according to CTCAE v5.0.

**Statistical Analysis:** The trial employed a Simon two-stage design, where the null hypothesis posits an ORR of 10% and the alternative hypothesis posits an ORR of at least 25%. If fewer than 5 responses were observed in the first stage, the trial would terminate early.

**Result Highlights:**
The trial faced numerous challenges indicative of previously documented obstacles in utilizing dasatinib for solid tumors:

1. **Insufficient Biomarkers:** The trials revealed a significant deficiency in suitable predictive biomarkers to enrich the patient population. The heterogeneous biological characteristics of pancreatic adenocarcinoma compounded this issue, leading to an ineffectiveness in accurately stratifying patients based on treatment response.

2. **Inadequate Tumor Targeting:** Dasatinib’s profile as a tyrosine kinase inhibitor demonstrated limited efficacy against the complex signaling networks inherent to pancreatic tumors, including KRAS mutations and their downstream pathways. Ultimately, this resulted in diminished clinical outcomes.

3. **Dose-Related Toxicities:** Several patients experienced adverse effects that necessitated dose reductions or treatment discontinuations, thus negatively impacting patient retention within the study framework and precluding optimal investigation of therapeutic outcomes.

4. **Tumor Microenvironment Interference:** The unique attributes of the pancreatic tumor microenvironment, including fibrotic stroma and hypoxia, were poorly addressed during trial design, hampering dasatinib’s functional effectiveness and accessibility to tumor cells.

5. **Limited Clinical Indication Scope:** Dasatinib’s initial indications for leukemia treatments coupled with insufficient preclinical evidence for efficacy against pancreatic cancer underscored the limitations observed in this cohort, ultimately leading to insufficient recruitment and poor response rates.

**Results:** The study was prematurely terminated after the first stage due to an inadequate number of responses (only 3 out of 40 evaluated patients achieved a measurable response, yielding an ORR of 7.5%), failing to meet the predetermined threshold for advancement. Adverse events, primarily hematologic and gastrointestinal, were consistent with prior reports, contributing to high discontinuation rates.

**Conclusion:** The trial's outcome underscores dasatinib’s ineffectiveness in treating advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma. The observations highlight the critical importance of robust biomarker identification, the need for improved target specification, the consideration of tumor microenvironmental factors, and reformulating clinical trial designs in light of prior therapeutic evidence. Further research is warranted to explore alternative therapeutic strategies specifically tailored for this malignancy.

**Follow-up:** Participants were followed up for overall survival and quality of life assessments, post-trial care was provided, and results disseminated for peer review and further study.
