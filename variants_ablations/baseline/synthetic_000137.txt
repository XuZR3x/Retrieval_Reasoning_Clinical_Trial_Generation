**Hydrocortisone for Enhancing Recovery in Chronic Pain Patients Undergoing Rehabilitation: A Pragmatic, Randomized, Double-Blind Study of Hydrocortisone vs. Placebo**

**Summary:** This clinical trial is designed to investigate the efficacy of low-dose hydrocortisone on recovery outcomes in patients with chronic pain undergoing rehabilitation. The hypothesis posits that hydrocortisone, by addressing inflammation and potentially altering neuroplastic changes associated with chronic pain, can facilitate improvements in pain levels, functional capacity, and overall quality of life. A meticulous approach is employed, recognizing that chronic pain is mediated by complex pathways, thus necessitating a multifaceted evaluation.

**Design:** This is a pragmatic, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study conducted over a span of six months, comparing the effects of hydrocortisone to placebo in a cohort of chronic pain rehabilitation patients. 

**Setting:** The study will be executed at the Pain Management Center, University Hospital of [REDACTED], [REDACTED] country.

**Hypothesis:** It is hypothesized that low-dose hydrocortisone will lead to significant improvements in pain control and functional status when compared to placebo, likely due to its anti-inflammatory effects and influence on pain processing pathways.

**Specific Aims:**
1. To evaluate the impact of hydrocortisone supplementation on self-reported pain levels, as measured by the Numeric Pain Rating Scale (NRS), in patients with chronic pain undergoing rehabilitation.
2. To assess changes in functional capacity through validated instruments such as the Oswestry Disability Index and the Short Form Health Survey (SF-36).
3. To conduct a thorough analysis of both physical and psychosocial recovery outcomes in relation to treatment, employing comprehensive psychological assessments of mood and anxiety.

**Methodology:**
- **Study Design:** Pragmatic, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled.
- **Duration:** 6 months.
- **Sample Size:** A total of 120 participants (60 in each group, based on power calculations from previous studies).
- **Intervention:** Patients will be randomized to receive either low-dose hydrocortisone (0.1 mg/kg body weight/daily) or placebo for the duration of the study alongside standard rehabilitation therapy.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Adults aged 18-65 years.
2. Diagnosed with chronic pain (>6 months) due to non-specific musculoskeletal disorders.
3. Enrolled in a rehabilitation program.
4. Provide informed consent to participate.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of significant psychiatric disorder (e.g., severe depression, psychosis).
2. Current systemic corticosteroid therapy or use of immunosuppressants.
3. Chronic conditions such as Cushing's Syndrome or uncontrolled diabetes.
4. Hypersensitivity to hydrocortisone.

**Outcome Measures:**
- Primary: Change in average pain score (NRS) from baseline to 6 months.
- Secondary: Functional abilities (Oswestry Disability Index score) and health-related quality of life (SF-36).
- Exploratory: Assessment of psychological well-being using validated questionnaires (e.g., Beck Anxiety Inventory).

**Data Analysis:**
- Statistical comparisons will be performed using mixed-model ANOVA to account for repeated measures over time.
- Post hoc analyses will determine treatment effects and significance (p<0.05).
