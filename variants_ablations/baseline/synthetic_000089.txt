**Failed Clinical Trial of Plerixafor: Plerixafor in Combination with Azacitidine for the Treatment of Acute Myeloid Leukemia in Older Adults**

**Phase I/II Study of Plerixafor and Azacitidine in Patients Aged ≥70 Years with Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) and Adverse Prognostic Factors**

The objective of this clinical research study is to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of the combination of plerixafor and azacitidine in treating older adults diagnosed with acute myeloid leukemia (AML) exhibiting at least two adverse prognostic factors. The study was designed to explore whether plerixafor could potentiate the effects of azacitidine through enhanced stem cell mobilization and chemotherapy efficacy.

**Study Drugs:**
Plerixafor functions as a CXCR4 antagonist, aiming to disrupt the interaction between leukemic cells and the bone marrow microenvironment, facilitating improved mobilization of hematopoietic stem cells. Azacitidine is a hypomethylating agent effective in alleviating the epigenetic silencing of tumor suppressor genes in AML.

**Study Groups:**
Eligible participants, aged ≥70 years, were assigned to receive either a regimen of plerixafor in conjunction with azacitidine or azacitidine alone in a 1:1 randomization. The plerixafor dosing schedule was positioned to align closely with the azacitidine administration.

**Study Drug Administration:**
- Administration of plerixafor was scheduled for Days 1-5, with a subcutaneous injection 6–12 hours prior to azacitidine infusion on Days 2-6 throughout each treatment cycle. 
- Azacitidine was administered intravenously on Days 2–6 of each 28-day cycle.

**Primary Objectives:**
To assess the overall response rates (complete remission and partial remission) in patients receiving plerixafor combined with azacitidine compared to azacitidine alone.

**Secondary Objectives:**
- To evaluate the mobilization efficiency of CD34+ cells in both treatment arms.
- To compare toxicities experienced by patients in both study groups to ascertain tolerability and safety metrics.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Age ≥70 years.
2. Diagnosis of untreated AML based on World Health Organization (WHO) criteria, with unfavorable prognostic factors that include adverse cytogenetics.
3. Prior history of insufficient stem cell mobilization characterized by low CD34+ counts (<4 x 10^6/kg) in prior treatment efforts.
4. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–2.
5. Signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior treatment for AML with intensive chemotherapy or other investigational agents.
2. Diagnosed with acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL).
3. Significant cardiac or renal comorbidity potentially compromising drug metabolism.
4. Active systemic infections that would impede the study outcomes.

**Results:**
The study was terminated after the phase I component revealed a lack of adequate efficacy. The planned enrollment target was 80 participants; however, only 34 subjects were enrolled before cessation due to the following reasons:

1. **Inefficacy of the Intervention:** The combination of plerixafor and azacitidine failed to demonstrate a statistically significant increase in overall response rates compared to azacitidine alone. Despite the hypothesized synergy, the initial dosing paradigm did not achieve the anticipated therapeutic benefit, leading to unacceptably low remission rates.

2. **Toxicity Profile:** A notable number of participants reported severe adverse events attributed to the combined regimen, including prolonged myelosuppression, necessitating dose modifications. The inability to maintain the planned administration schedule limited exposure to therapeutic doses.

3. **Suboptimal Patient Selection:** The inclusion criteria led to the enrollment of a population with substantial comorbidities, adversely affecting their response to treatment. Many patients presented with advanced age and pre-existing malignancies, which further compromised their treatment tolerance and outcomes.

4. **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** There were indications that azacitidine and plerixafor may engage in complex pharmacokinetic interactions. Patients exhibited decreased plasma levels of plerixafor, raising concerns regarding drug efficacy in improved stem cell mobilization.

5. **Lack of Adequate Mobilization:** Subsequent follow-up assessments indicated that the primary endpoint of achieving the designated target of 5 x 10^6 CD34+ cells/kg was not accomplished. Insufficient mobilization rates observed during leukapheresis further supported the conclusion that plerixafor did not provide the anticipated benefit when utilized in this cohort.

**Conclusion:**
The clinical trial elucidated critical obstacles associated with the prospective use of plerixafor in combination with azacitidine for AML treatment in an aging demographic. These challenges included poor response rates, unfavorable toxicity, and pharmacokinetic complexities. Consequently, the trial was closed early due to inadequate efficacy and safety concerns, precluding further exploration of this therapeutic strategy in the specified population.
