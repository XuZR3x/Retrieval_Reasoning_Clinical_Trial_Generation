**Title**: Efficacy of Ciprofloxacin in the Treatment of Complicated Intra-Abdominal Infections: A Multi-Centre Randomized Control Trial

**Background**: Complicated intra-abdominal infections (cIAIs) represent a significant surgical emergency, often requiring broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy. With the rise of multi-drug resistant pathogens in intra-abdominal infections, there is an urgent need to identify effective treatment options that address both efficacy and safety. This trial aims to evaluate the role of ciprofloxacin, considering its tailored approach based on microbial susceptibility and high bioavailability, which supports a seamless transition from intravenous to oral administration, while addressing the challenge of antibiotic resistance.

**Methods/Design**: This clinical trial is a multi-centre, randomized, open-label, active comparator-controlled study designed to assess the non-inferiority of ciprofloxacin in comparison to the current standard of care meropenem in the treatment of cIAIs caused by susceptible microorganisms. Patients aged 18 years and older with confirmed cIAIs based on clinical examination and imaging will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either ciprofloxacin 750 mg intravenously twice daily for 5 days followed by oral ciprofloxacin 750 mg twice daily for an additional 10 days, or meropenem 1 g intravenously three times daily for 14 days.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
1. Aged ≥ 18 years.
2. Confirmed diagnosis of cIAIs with microbiological isolation of pathogens sensitive to ciprofloxacin.
3. Ability and willingness to provide informed consent.
4. Patient must have a baseline clinical severity score of ≥ 5 based on the APACHE II criteria.

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
1. Patients with polymicrobial infections involving resistant pathogens.
2. Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
3. Presence of severe sepsis or septic shock.
4. Significant comorbidities that may interfere with adherence (e.g., severe renal impairment).
5. Patients with known allergies to ciprofloxacin or other fluoroquinolones.

**Endpoints**: The primary endpoint of the study is clinical success at 30 days post-treatment initiation, defined as the absence of fever, return of bowel function, and improved clinical symptoms without reoperation or need for continued intravenous antibiotics. Secondary endpoints include microbiological eradication rates, safety and adverse events monitoring, length of hospital stay, and cost-effectiveness analysis.

**Discussion**: The study leverages ciprofloxacin’s high bioavailability and effective targeting of pathogens shown to be susceptible, thereby providing compelling evidence for its use in complicated intra-abdominal infections. Furthermore, the cost-effectiveness of ciprofloxacin as compared to meropenem underscores its potential to not only enhance clinical outcomes but also to alleviate economic burdens associated with prolonged hospital stays.

**Number of Participants with Clinical Success**: The study aims to evaluate and compare the rates of clinical success between the ciprofloxacin arm and the meropenem arm, through disciplined follow-up assessments via clinical evaluations, patient-reported outcomes, and microbiological investigations.

**Conclusion**: This randomized control trial is envisioned to provide substantive data on the viability of ciprofloxacin as a frontline therapeutic option in the management of complicated intra-abdominal infections, thus contributing valuable insights that may influence clinical practice guidelines and antibiotic stewardship policies.
