<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Efficacy and Safety of Daclizumab in Combination with Mycophenolate Mofetil and Low-Dose Tacrolimus in Pediatric Kidney Transplant Recipients: A Multi-Center, Randomized, Controlled Study</Title>
    <Objective>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of daclizumab in conjunction with mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) and low-dose tacrolimus as an immunosuppressive regimen compared to standard immunosuppressive therapy in pediatric kidney transplant recipients. Secondary endpoints include the assessment of renal function, incidence of acute rejection episodes, and quality of life measures post-transplantation.</Objective>
    <Background>Kidney transplantation is a life-saving procedure for children with end-stage renal disease. Traditional immunosuppressive regimens, predominantly involving corticosteroids, have significant adverse effects, particularly in pediatric populations. Daclizumab's mechanism of action, as a monoclonal antibody targeting the IL-2 receptor, has demonstrated potential for enhancing graft survival and inducing tolerance mechanisms while minimizing corticosteroid use. This study was designed based on previously established success in reducing steroid-associated complications, promoting a favorable safety profile, and achieving robust renal function outcomes.</Background>
    <Design>This is a double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized trial involving pediatric patients aged 2 to 18 years who are undergoing their first kidney transplant. Enrolled participants will be randomized (1:1) to receive either the experimental regimen of daclizumab with MMF and low-dose tacrolimus or the control regimen of standard MMF and tacrolimus without daclizumab for a duration of 12 months.</Design>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Primary recipient of a kidney allograft.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Children and adolescents aged 2 to 18 years.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>PRA of ≤ 20%.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Written informed consent obtained from legal guardians.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>History of previous organ transplantation.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Active malignancy.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Acute or chronic infections at the time of transplantation.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Participation in another investigational drug study within 30 days prior to screening.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Inability to comply with study assessments or complete follow-up.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <Methods>Eligible participants will undergo randomization to receive either Group A: MMF plus low-dose tacrolimus, or Group B: daclizumab (administered within the first two months post-transplant) plus MMF and low-dose tacrolimus. The study will monitor renal function assessed through GFR at 12 months, alongside acute rejection rates, which will be determined through biopsies and clinical evaluations. Quality of life will be evaluated using validated pediatric scales.</Methods>
    <Endpoints>
        <PrimaryEndpoint>The rate of acute rejection episodes within the first 12 months post-transplant.</PrimaryEndpoint>
        <SecondaryEndpoints>
            <SecondaryEndpoint>Measurement of GFR at 12 months calculated via the Schwartz formula.</SecondaryEndpoint>
            <SecondaryEndpoint>Patient-reported quality of life assessed through PedsQL scale.</SecondaryEndpoint>
            <SecondaryEndpoint>Incidence of adverse events related to immunosuppression and graft function.</SecondaryEndpoint>
        </SecondaryEndpoints>
    </Endpoints>
    <StatisticalAnalysis>A sample size of 200 participants will provide 80% power to detect a significant difference in acute rejection rates with a two-sided alpha of 0.05. Analysis will be conducted using intention-to-treat principles. Outcomes will be assessed through the use of logistic regression to adjust for potential confounders.</StatisticalAnalysis>
    <Significance>This trial aims to demonstrate that the incorporation of daclizumab into pediatric renal transplant regimens not only reduces the incidence of acute rejection episodes due to enhanced immune tolerance mechanisms, but also optimizes renal function through a reduction in corticosteroid dependency, thereby promoting better long-term health outcomes in this vulnerable population.</Significance>
</ClinicalTrial>
