**Phase II Randomized Controlled Trial of LDE225 in Combination with Paclitaxel for Patients with Recurrent Ovarian Cancer**

**Study Overview:**
This Phase II randomized controlled trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of LDE225 administered in conjunction with paclitaxel in female patients diagnosed with recurrent platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. The mechanism of action for LDE225, as a potent inhibitor of the Hedgehog signaling pathway, is hypothesized to augment the cytotoxic effects of paclitaxel, thereby leading to enhanced therapeutic responses in this patient population.

**Background:**
Recurrent platinum-resistant ovarian cancer poses a significant challenge due to its aggressive nature and poor prognosis. The Hedgehog signaling pathway plays a critical role in tumorigenesis, especially in ovarian cancer. Prior investigations have demonstrated that the integration of LDE225 with paclitaxel may improve sensitivity in recurrent ovarian cancer cells, contributing to better clinical outcomes.

**Objective:**
The primary objective of this trial is to assess the overall response rate (ORR) of LDE225 combined with paclitaxel compared to paclitaxel monotherapy in patients with recurrent ovarian cancer. Secondary objectives include safety profile evaluation, progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) rates, as well as pharmacokinetic interactions between LDE225 and paclitaxel.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of recurrent epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal carcinoma
- Patients classified as platinum-resistant according to Gynecologic Oncology Group criteria
- Prior treatment history must include at least one platinum-based chemotherapy regimen and one taxane regimen
- Measurable disease confirmed by imaging studies, with appropriate RECIST criteria
- ECOG performance status of 0-2
- Adequate organ function as determined by standard laboratory assessments
- Signed informed consent from the participant

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Histological evidence of mucinous or carcinosarcoma subtype
- Active non-ovarian malignancies except for in situ cervical cancer
- Previous treatment with systemic Hedgehog pathway inhibitors
- History of significant cardiac or pulmonary disease
- Recent major surgery or active infection requiring therapy
- Patients unwilling to comply with effective contraceptive measures during and after the study period

**Methodology:**
This multicenter trial will enroll approximately 200 participants, randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either the combination therapy of LDE225 and paclitaxel or paclitaxel alone. The dosing regimen will consist of LDE225 administered orally once daily at the maximum tolerated dose determined in previous studies, while paclitaxel will be administered at a standard dosing of 80 mg/m² weekly. 

Patient follow-up will include monthly clinical assessments, imaging studies every two cycles, and regular laboratory evaluations to monitor overall health and response to treatment.

**Outcome Measures:**
The primary endpoint will be the overall response rate assessed via imaging studies after 4 cycles of treatment. Secondary outcomes will include the incidence of dose-limiting toxicities, evaluation of pharmacokinetic parameters, PFS, and OS.

**Statistical Analysis:**
Statistical analyses will be conducted using appropriate models for comparing treatment groups. The aim is to achieve a significance level of p < 0.05. Interim analyses will be performed to determine safety and efficacy, with pre-specified stopping rules in place.

**Rationale for Estimated Success:**
Several factors contribute to the anticipated success of this trial:
1. The hypothesized synergistic effect of LDE225 targeting the Hedgehog pathway, which underpins the biology of recurrent ovarian cancer, could significantly improve therapeutic responses when combined with paclitaxel.
2. A meticulously defined patient population ensures that findings are applicable to a homogenous cohort, maximizing statistical power to detect significant outcomes.
3. The established maximum tolerated dose will optimize efficacy while minimizing associated toxicities.
4. The multi-center approach promotes diversity among participants, enhancing the trial's generalizability across different patient demographics.
5. A robust pharmacokinetic framework has been established to assess drug-drug interactions that may affect treatment outcomes, building on previous studies highlighting LDE225's interactions with bupropion and warfarin.

In conclusion, the integration of LDE225 with paclitaxel holds great promise for advancing treatment modalities in recurrent ovarian cancer, with this Phase II trial poised to yield significant insights into its efficacy and safety profile.
