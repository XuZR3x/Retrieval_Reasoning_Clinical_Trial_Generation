**Failed Clinical Trial of Clopidogrel: Clopidogrel for the Prevention of Long-term Outcomes in High-Risk Ischemic Patients (CLOTH)**
   
**Background:** Clopidogrel (CLO) is an oral antiplatelet medication commonly used in patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events. Despite its established utility, variations in therapeutic response, particularly due to genetic polymorphisms in CYP2C19, have raised concerns regarding its efficacy across diverse patient populations. The CLOTH study aimed to determine the long-term effectiveness of clopidogrel in preventing adverse outcomes in high-risk ischemic patients, stratified by CYP2C19 genotype.

**Objective:** To evaluate the efficacy of clopidogrel versus standard therapy with aspirin in preventing the composite of major cardiovascular events, including myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular mortality, in a genetically diverse high-risk population.

**Hypothesis:** Clopidogrel provides a superior protective effect against long-term adverse outcomes compared to aspirin alone; however, the degree of benefit is significantly influenced by the CYP2C19 genotype.

**Method:** This multicenter, randomized, double-blind trial enrolled 800 high-risk ischemic patients across various demographic groups, selected primarily from community hospital settings to enhance generalizability. Patients were stratified based on their CYP2C19 genotype, with those carrying loss-of-function alleles identified as potentially at risk of inadequate response to clopidogrel therapy.

Participants were randomly assigned to receive clopidogrel 75 mg/day or aspirin 81 mg/day, alongside comprehensive lifestyle modification counseling over a treatment period of 24 months. The study employed rigorous follow-up protocols, including standardization of clinical assessments and laboratory evaluations to monitor both efficacy and safety outcomes.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 50 and above with a history of ischemic heart disease or stroke, or with significant cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, smoking).
- Ability to give informed consent.
  
**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Conditions leading to increased bleeding risk or contraindications (e.g., active gastrointestinal bleeding, severe hepatic impairment).
- Individuals unable to tolerate oral medications or with known hypersensitivity to the study agents.
- Concurrent usage of other anti-platelet or anticoagulant drugs.

**Efficacy Measures:** The primary endpoint was defined as the occurrence of the composite outcome of myocardial infarction, stroke, or cardiovascular mortality within the study period. Secondary endpoints included individual components of the composite outcome, quality of life assessments, and safety profiles associated with the treatments, particularly focusing on bleeding complications.

**Results:** Initial findings revealed no statistically significant difference between the treatment groups in the primary composite endpoint, with both clopidogrel and aspirin demonstrating similar efficacy, raising concerns regarding the clinical utility of clopidogrel in unselected cohorts. Subgroup analyses indicated that individuals with the CYP2C19 *2/*2 genotype, particularly, had minimal cocktail therapeutic response, corroborating previous studies demonstrating genotype-related efficacy discrepancies.

Additionally, safety data illustrated an unanticipated high incidence of adverse bleeding events among participants receiving combination therapy, leading to interim concerns over the risk-benefit ratio associated with clopidogrel in patients predisposed to bleeding complications.

**Conclusion:** The CLOTH trial was terminated early after the first pre-specified interim analysis indicated lack of significant benefits associated with clopidogrel compared to standard aspirin therapy and pronounced safety concerns linked to increased bleeding risk. These findings underscore that genotype variability and strict inclusion criteria may limit the applicability of clopidogrel therapy in high-risk ischemic populations and necessitate further studies to explore personalized modalities in antiplatelet therapy.

**Significance:** This trial contributes to the growing body of evidence advocating for personalized medicine in antiplatelet therapy, emphasizing the importance of pharmacogenomic screening to optimize therapeutic outcomes and minimize risks in patients at high risk for cardiovascular events.
