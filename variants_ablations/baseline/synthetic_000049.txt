**Successful Clinical Trial of Filgrastim: Combination Immunotherapy with Filgrastim in Treating Patients with Advanced Melanoma**

**RATIONALE:** Advanced melanoma remains a challenging condition with traditionally limited treatment options. Immunotherapy with checkpoint inhibitors has emerged as a promising avenue; however, these therapies can lead to significant immune-related adverse events and myelosuppression. Filgrastim, a granulocyte colony-stimulating factor, may enhance the proliferative capacity and reactivity of the immune system, promoting hematopoietic recovery in patients undergoing intensive immunotherapy. The application of filgrastim could potentially improve tolerability and effectiveness of combination therapies, aiming to augment overall survival and progression-free survival in patients with advanced melanoma.

**PURPOSE:** This phase III trial is designed to assess the efficacy and safety of the combination of pembrolizumab and local hypofractionated radiotherapy with filgrastim in patients with advanced melanoma.

**OBJECTIVES:**

- **Primary:** Determine the overall survival (OS) in patients with advanced melanoma treated with pembrolizumab and hypofractionated radiotherapy with or without filgrastim support.
- **Secondary:** Evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) rate at 12 months, the incidence of treatment-related adverse events, and the qualitative quality of life metrics utilizing validated questionnaires.
- **Exploratory:** Assess the immunological outcomes through biomarkers and the impact of filgrastim on immune-mediated adverse events.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind study. Eligible patients will be stratified based on Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status and disease stage.

- **Group A:** Patients receive pembrolizumab intravenously every 3 weeks for a total of 6 doses. Concurrently, patients undergo hypofractionated radiotherapy to a designated tumor site on days 1, 3, and 5 of the first treatment cycle.
- **Group B:** Patients receive the same treatment regimen as Group A, with the addition of filgrastim (5 mcg/kg/day) administered subcutaneously starting on day 7 following each dose of pembrolizumab and continuing until neutrophil counts recover.

Patients will be monitored for treatment-related adverse events, disease progression, and overall survival outcomes, with follow-up evaluations every 6 weeks for the first year, then every 3 months for the following 2 years.

**PROJECTED ACCRUAL:** A total of 200 patients (100 per treatment arm) will be accrued for this study over the course of 4 years.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed unresectable stage III or IV melanoma.
- Patients must have measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- No prior systemic therapy for melanoma permitted, except for limited use of BRAF/MEK inhibitors in BRAF-mutant patients.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: 18 and over.
- ECOG performance status: 0-2.
- Adequate organ function: 
  - Hematologic: Absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1,500/mm^3, hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL.
  - Hepatic: Bilirubin < 1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), AST/ALT < 2.5 times ULN.
  - Renal: Creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- History of autoimmune disease that requires systemic therapy.
- Treatment with anticoagulants or anticoagulation agents such as warfarin, which may interact with treatment regimens.
- Pregnant or nursing females, and fertile males and females not using effective contraception.

**EVENT-FREE SURVIVAL (EFS):** EFS is defined as the duration from the initiation of therapy until disease progression or death from any cause, and will be assessed using Kaplan-Meier estimates.

The methodological framework of this trial is engineered to substantiate the vital role of filgrastim as a supportive care agent aimed at enhancing the clinical outcomes for patients undergoing modern immunotherapeutic regimens. Given the compelling preliminary findings regarding filgrastim's impact on treatment response and quality of life, this study may provide significant insights into the integration of supportive therapies into immuno-oncology workflows.
