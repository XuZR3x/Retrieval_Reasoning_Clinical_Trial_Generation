**Failed Clinical Trial of Radiotherapy: Dose-Dense Radiotherapy With Concurrent Immunotherapy in Patients With Resectable Melanoma**  

**A Phase II Study of Dose-Dense Radiotherapy Combined With Nivolumab in Patients With Resectable Melanoma.**  

**RATIONALE:** The combination of dose-dense radiotherapy and immune checkpoint inhibition through nivolumab has been hypothesized to enhance anti-tumor immunity in patients with resectable melanoma. However, the potential benefits of this combined approach must be evaluated rigorously to ascertain its clinical utility.

**PURPOSE:** This phase II trial aimed to determine the efficacy and safety of dose-dense radiotherapy combined with nivolumab administration in achieving improved pathological complete response (pCR) rates among patients with resectable melanoma.

**OBJECTIVES:**  
**Primary:**  
- To assess the pathological complete response (pCR) rate at the time of surgery post-combined treatment with dose-dense radiotherapy and nivolumab in patients with resectable melanoma.  

**Secondary:**  
- To evaluate the overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) rates at 12 and 24 months post-treatment.  
- To assess the safety profile and incidence of adverse events associated with the intervention.  
- To explore biomarkers predictive of response to nivolumab in conjunction with radiotherapy.

**OUTLINE:**  
Eligible patients with histologically confirmed resectable melanoma received dose-dense radiotherapy (45 Gy in 15 fractions) to the primary tumor site, concurrently with nivolumab administered every two weeks for eight cycles. Treatment was initiated within four weeks of surgical resection. Patients underwent radiotherapy 48 hours after each nivolumab infusion to maximize immune activation within irradiated tissues.  

**Eligible Patients:**  
1. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of Stage IIIB or IIIC melanoma;  
2. Measurable primary and/or nodal lesions;  
3. Age ≥ 18 years;  
4. ECOG performance status of 0-1;  
5. Adequate organ function including liver, renal, and hematologic parameters.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Prior radiotherapy to the tumor site or systemic anticancer therapy;  
2. Active autoimmune disease or history of severe hypersensitivity to nivolumab;  
3. Presence of distant metastases;  
4. Pregnancy or breastfeeding;  
5. Significant comorbidities impacting safety.

**INTERVENTION DESCRIPTION:**  
Patients received radiotherapy five times a week for three weeks, with nivolumab concurrently administered 48 hours prior to each radiation treatment. The primary tumor lesion was monitored through imaging and histopathological assessment at surgery.  

The study followed a Simon two-stage design, enrolling up to 42 patients, with Stage 1 consisting of 20 patients. If six or more individuals demonstrated a pCR, the study would proceed to Stage 2.

**OUTCOME MEASURES:**  
Primary efficacy was determined by the proportion of patients achieving a pCR, assessed through pathological examination post-surgery. Safety was monitored via NCI-CTCAE v5.0 criteria, particularly focusing on immune-related adverse events.

**RESULTS:**  
This trial was halted during Stage 1 after 18 patients were enrolled due to inadequate pCR responses; only 2 of the patients exhibited a response. Contributing factors to this failure included:  
1. **Insufficient Power to Detect Efficacy:** The study was underpowered, leading to inconclusive efficacy data as optimal estimates were not met due to insufficient sample size.  
2. **Inadequate Patient Selection:** Many enrolled patients had prior comprehensive surgical excisions without concurrent systemic therapy, impacting their baseline immune status.  
3. **Suboptimal Therapeutic Regimen:** Patients experienced significant treatment interruptions due to adverse events, leading to reduced adherence and subsequent negative impact on treatment efficacy.  
4. **Limited Biological Rationale:** Insufficient preclinical studies on the combined effect of dose-dense radiotherapy and nivolumab raised concerns about biological synergy, ultimately compromising the expected immune-enhanced tumor response.  
5. **Inconsistent Outcome Measures:** Variability in response assessment methods at surgical time points introduced discrepancies in reporting overall efficacy, contributing to inconclusive results.

A follow-up study to refine patient selection criteria and explore alternative scheduling of immunotherapy in conjunction with radiotherapy is recommended to enhance future investigations into the synergistic potential of this combinatory strategy.
