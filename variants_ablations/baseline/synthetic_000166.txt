**Title:** A Phase II, Multicenter, Double-Blind, Randomized Study of Eribulin Mesylate in Combination with Pembrolizumab Versus Pembrolizumab Alone in Subjects with Locally Advanced or Metastatic Triple-Negative Breast Cancer

**Abstract:** This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of eribulin mesylate in combination with pembrolizumab, an anti-PD-1 therapy, compared to pembrolizumab monotherapy in patients with locally advanced or metastatic triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) who have not received prior systemic therapy for metastatic disease. The focus on a well-defined patient population enriched for therapeutic response, combined with a robust clinical design and stringent follow-up protocols, enhances the interpretability of results regarding the treatment's effects.

**Background:** Triple-negative breast cancer, characterized by the absence of ER, PR, and HER2 expression, represents a significant clinical challenge due to its aggressive nature and limited treatment options. Eribulin mesylate's mechanism of action, targeting microtubule dynamics, coupled with pembrolizumab’s immune checkpoint blockade, offers a compelling rationale for exploring this combination, particularly in a first-line setting to enhance therapeutic response.

**Study Design:** This is a multicenter, double-blind, randomized Phase II trial designed to assign patients in a 1:1 fashion to receive either eribulin mesylate (1.4 mg/m² intravenous infusion on Days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle) combined with pembrolizumab (200 mg intravenous infusion every 3 weeks) or pembrolizumab alone. A total of 120 adult female subjects, aged 18 years or older, will be enrolled to assess the objective response rate (ORR), safety profile, and overall survival.

**Key Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed locally advanced or metastatic TNBC
- At least one measurable lesion according to RECIST v1.1 criteria
- ECOG Performance Status of 0 to 1
- Adequate organ function as determined by specified laboratory criteria
- No prior systemic therapy for metastatic disease
- Informed consent provided

**Key Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with eribulin mesylate, pembrolizumab, or any other systemic anticancer therapies for metastatic disease
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy
- History of hypersensitivity to eribulin mesylate or components of the formulation
- Clinically significant cardiovascular disease
- Pregnancy or lactation

**Endpoints:**
- Primary Endpoint: Objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of participants with confirmed complete or partial responses per RECIST v1.1.
- Secondary Endpoints: Progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety assessments denoted by the incidence of adverse events categorized by CTCAE v5.0.

**Statistical Considerations:** Assuming a significance level of 0.05 and a power of 80%, a sample size of 120 patients is calculated using a two-sided Fisher’s exact test to compare response rates between treatment groups. Analyses will be conducted per the intention-to-treat principle.

**Follow-Up:** Patients will be followed up every 6 weeks for the first year post-treatment initiation and then every 3 months until progression or initiation of subsequent anti-cancer therapy.

**Importance of Design Elements:** 
This clinical trial leverages rigorous selection criteria to enhance the likelihood of observing an improved treatment response given the homogeneous nature of the enrolled subjects. The double-blind, randomized design mitigates bias and reinforces the validity of the conclusions drawn from the trial’s outcomes. Furthermore, the prospective follow-up designed to monitor both efficacy and safety assures prompt identification and management of any adverse effects that may arise, enhancing patient compliance and treatment adherence.

**Conclusion:** This Phase II trial seeks to build upon the foundational knowledge of eribulin mesylate’s efficacy in breast cancer treatment, examining its potential to enhance the response rates when combined with pembrolizumab in a population with significant unmet clinical need, while maintaining a strong emphasis on patient safety and monitoring throughout the intervention.

This clinical design aims at achieving meaningful results that may contribute to the management paradigms in triple-negative breast cancer by determining the superiority of the combination treatment over monotherapy.
