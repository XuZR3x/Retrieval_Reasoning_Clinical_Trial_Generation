**Failure Clinical Trial of Sorafenib Tosylate:**  
*Sorafenib Tosylate in Treating Patients with Advanced Cholangiocarcinoma*  
*A Multi-center Phase II Study of Sorafenib (BAY 43-9006) in Patients with Advanced Cholangiocarcinoma*

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To assess the response rate of sorafenib (BAY 43-9006) in patients with advanced cholangiocarcinoma, focusing on the overall clinical benefit as measured by objective response and progression-free survival.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**  
I. To evaluate time to progression and overall survival in this cohort of patients receiving BAY 43-9006.  
II. To characterize the safety profile and toxicity of sorafenib in patients with advanced cholangiocarcinoma.  
III. To investigate polymorphisms in relevant drug-metabolizing enzymes and assess their impact on the pharmacokinetics of BAY 43-9006.  
IV. To examine the expression of angiogenesis and apoptosis markers in tumor specimens and correlate these with treatment response.

**OUTLINE:**  
This is a multicenter, open-label trial. Eligible patients will receive oral sorafenib twice daily on days 1-28 of each 28-day cycle. Toxicity assessments and response evaluations will be conducted every 4 weeks. Patients will be followed for survival outcomes every 3 months.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced cholangiocarcinoma (intrahepatic, perihilar, or distal).  
- Patients must have measurable disease per RECIST criteria, assessed within 4 weeks before registration.  
- Age ≥ 18 years with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.  
- Patients must have received at least one prior chemotherapy regimen and have documented disease progression.  

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Previous treatment with sorafenib or other targeted agents that inhibit similar oncogenic signaling pathways.  
- Patients with significant comorbidities including unstable cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled hypertension.  
- Evidence of central nervous system involvement.  
- Concurrent treatment with investigational therapies or radiation therapy.

**PLANNED ACCRUAL:** 80  
**ACTUAL ACCRUAL:** 32  

**RESULTS:**  
The study’s primary endpoint inadequately addressed the mechanistic efficacy of sorafenib in cholangiocarcinoma, leading to a lack of significant therapeutic effect as a result of tumor heterogeneity. The cancer exhibited complex mutational profiles that rendered the inhibitory action of sorafenib on multi-kinase signaling ineffective. 

Moreover, extensive pharmacogenomic profiling indicated variable polymorphisms among participants that potentially contributed to suboptimal pharmacokinetic exposure and diminished clinical response. Patients’ enrollment was constrained by restrictive inclusion criteria, limiting the assessment of sorafenib's efficacy in populations with varying prognostic factors.

The toxicity profile noted considerable adverse events, including severe hypertension and unexpected dermatological responses, contributing to dropout rates and inadequate treatment adherence. This raised concerns regarding the overall tolerability of sorafenib in this patient population, further complicating the evaluation of its therapeutic potential.

In conclusion, the study reaffirmed the necessity for improved biomarker identification and validation to facilitate optimal patient stratification via genomics, particularly focusing on angiogenic and apoptotic pathways that were hypothesized as critical predictors of response to sorafenib. Unfortunately, this oversight masked potential efficacy and confirmed the anticipated disappointing outcomes delivered by this clinical trial.

**KEY METRICS:**  
- Overall Response Rate: 6 % (CR: 0%, PR: 6%)  
- 4-Month Progression-Free Survival Rate: 12%  
- Median Time to Progression: 6 weeks  
- Overall Survival (median): 9 months  

**CONCLUSION:**  
The Phase II trial of sorafenib tosylate in patients with advanced cholangiocarcinoma was terminated due to suboptimal efficacy attributed to insufficient mechanistic action and suboptimal patient stratification based on tumor genetic factors. Further investigations focusing on refined biomarker utilization and alternative therapeutic combinations would be warranted to enhance outcomes in this challenging malignancy.
