**Failed Clinical Trial of Rivaroxaban: Efficacy and Safety of Rivaroxaban for Stroke Prevention in Patients with Nonvalvular Atrial Fibrillation and Persistent Left Atrial Appendage Thrombus**

**Trial Identifier:** XAREX-002

**Background:** The management of stroke risk in patients with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation (AF) often necessitates effective anticoagulation strategies. Rivaroxaban has been established for stroke prevention in AF; however, its efficacy in patients presenting with persistent left atrial appendage (LAA) thrombus remains inadequately elucidated. Previous literature suggests that anticoagulation failure, specifically rivaroxaban, may result from pharmacodynamic limitations in this high-thrombotic risk population. Thus, this study aims to evaluate the impact of rivaroxaban on stroke prevention in patients with nonvalvular AF and confirmed LAA thrombus.

**Objectives:**
- **Primary Objective:** To evaluate the efficacy of rivaroxaban (20 mg once daily) for stroke prevention, measured by the incidence of thromboembolic events in patients with nonvalvular AF and persistent LAA thrombus over six months.
- **Secondary Objectives:** To assess the safety profile of rivaroxaban in this patient population, particularly regarding major bleeding events, and to explore the correlation between LAA thrombus characteristics on echocardiography and thromboembolic outcomes.

**Design:** This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving 120 patients with nonvalvular AF and ultrasound-confirmed persistent LAA thrombus, randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either rivaroxaban or placebo for a total of six months.

**Methods:**
- **Patient Population:** Eligible participants are adults aged 18-85 years with a diagnosis of nonvalvular atrial fibrillation and persistent thrombus in the left atrial appendage as demonstrated by transesophageal echocardiography.
- **Interventions:**
   - Experimental Group: Rivaroxaban 20 mg orally once daily.
   - Control Group: Placebo matched in appearance.
- **Outcome Measures:**
  - **Primary Outcome:** A composite of stroke, systemic embolism, and urgent revascularization or hospitalization for thromboembolic complications within 180 days.
  - **Secondary Outcomes:** Rate of major and clinically relevant bleeding events, assessed via predefined International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) criteria, and correlation between baseline thrombus characteristics and clinical outcomes.

**Eligibility Criteria:**
- **Inclusion:** Adults aged 18-85 with nonvalvular AF and confirmed persistent LAA thrombus.
- **Exclusion:** Contraindications to rivaroxaban or anticoagulation therapy, a history of significant bleeding complications, active malignancy with high bleeding risk, and elevated creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min.

**Results:** The study terminated early after enrolling 75 participants due to lack of efficacy. Rivaroxaban did not demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in the primary composite outcome compared to placebo, leading to increased incidence of thromboembolic events (e.g., ischemic stroke and system embolism) unlike anticipated. Additionally, the increased safety concerns emerged as an unexpected rise in major bleeding incidents was observed in the experimental group, warranting further inquiry into its safety profile in patients with elevated thrombotic risk due to LAA thrombus.

**Conclusions:** The trial did not support the hypothesis that rivaroxaban is effective for stroke prevention in patients with nonvalvular AF and persisting LAA thrombus, contradicting initial expectations. The increased incidence of thromboembolic events and adverse bleeding incidents necessitates a reconsideration of rivaroxaban's clinical application in such defined high-risk populations, as previously suggested by studies reflecting its limited efficacy and safety profile. Future investigations may need to explore alternative management strategies or anticoagulant agents tailored for this specific cohort.

**End of Study:** This clinical trial underscores the critical need for careful patient stratification in anticoagulation therapy for AF patients, particularly those exhibiting unique risk factors such as LAA thrombus, which may significantly influence clinical outcomes.
