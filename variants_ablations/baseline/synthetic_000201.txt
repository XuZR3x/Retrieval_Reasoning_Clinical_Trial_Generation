**Successful clinical trial of efavirenz: Pharmacokinetic Assessment of Efavirenz and Its Impact on Neurocognitive Function in HIV-1 Infected Adults**  
**Study Title:** Pharmacokinetics and Neurocognitive Outcomes of Efavirenz in HIV-1 Infected Adults  
**Protocol Number:** PNEURO-001  
**EudraCT Number:** 2019-000123-45  
**Name of Investigational Product:** Sustiva  
**Name of Active Ingredient:** Efavirenz  
**Phase of Study:** Phase II  

**Objectives:**  
- **Primary:** To evaluate the pharmacokinetics of efavirenz and assess its impact on neurocognitive function in stable HIV-1 infected adults.  
- **Secondary:** To determine the safety profile of efavirenz in this patient population and identify any correlations between pharmacokinetic parameters and neurocognitive outcomes.  

**Study Design:**  
Multi-center, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial lasting approximately 24 weeks, focusing on adults with HIV-1 who are stable on antiretroviral therapy.  

**Indication:** HIV-1 infection  

**Methodology:**  
This study will enroll adults aged 18-50 years, inclusive, who are confirmed HIV-1 positive and have been on tenofovir/emtricitabine, with efavirenz as part of their regimen for at least 6 months prior to study entry. Participants will undergo a series of neurocognitive assessments at baseline, Week 12, and Week 24, alongside pharmacokinetic evaluations of efavirenz at steady state as measured through trough concentrations (Ctrough) and area under the curve (AUC). 

**Planned Sample Size:**  
A total of 100 participants will be recruited, with 50 in the efavirenz group and 50 in the placebo group. This sample size will provide 80% power to detect significant differences in neurocognitive function based on efavirenz pharmacokinetics.

**Summary of Eligibility Criteria:**  
- **Inclusion:**  
    1. Confirmed HIV-1 infection.  
    2. Stable on tenofovir/emtricitabine/efavirenz for at least 6 months.  
    3. CD4 count ≥ 350 cells/mm³.  
    4. Undergoing routine treatment with undetectable viral load.  
    5. No history of central nervous system disorders.  

- **Exclusion:**  
    1. Use of CNS-active medications within 3 months prior to study entry.  
    2. Significant psychiatric illness.  
    3. History of drug or alcohol abuse impacting treatment adherence.  
    4. Pregnancy or breastfeeding.  
    5. Allergies to study medications.  

**Number of Study Centres:** Three (New York, Amsterdam, and Cape Town).  

**Duration of Treatment:** Approximately 24 weeks, including screening and follow-up.  

**Dose and Route of Administration:**  
All subjects will receive efavirenz 600 mg orally once daily and appropriate background therapy throughout the study period. 

**Criteria for Evaluation:**  
Evaluation will consist of neuropsychological assessments utilizing standardized tests measuring attention, memory, and executive function, in conjunction with pharmacokinetic analysis measuring efavirenz levels in plasma at baseline, Week 12, and Week 24.

**Primary Endpoint:**  
Pharmacokinetic parameters of efavirenz (Ctrough and AUC) and their correlation with neurocognitive assessment scores over 24 weeks.

**Secondary Endpoint:**  
1. Safety and tolerability of efavirenz as assessed by adverse event reporting and routine laboratory tests.  
2. Correlation between pharmacogenomic variants affecting drug metabolism and neurocognitive outcomes.

This clinical trial is predicated on the previously established pharmacokinetic profile of efavirenz, as seen in study SSAT063, demonstrating the drug's therapeutic implications during various physiological conditions such as pregnancy, highlighting its potential adaptability in diverse populations. Furthermore, parallel studies regarding treatment reinitiation in postpartum women underscore the resilience and enduring efficacy of efavirenz, illuminating its role in comprehensive HIV management [reference to clinical trial data]. 

This study aims to broaden the understanding of efavirenz's implications beyond viral suppression, potentially redefining best practice protocols in HIV treatment, with substantial relevance to neurocognitive health in this patient demographic. The anticipated outcomes could delineate novel therapeutic frameworks enhancing the quality of life for HIV-infected adults while minimizing adverse effects, thus paving the way for future patient-specific interventions.
