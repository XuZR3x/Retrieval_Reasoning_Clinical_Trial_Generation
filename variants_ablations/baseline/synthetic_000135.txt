<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Evaluation of Tadalafil for the Treatment of Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction: A Randomized Controlled Trial</Title>
    
    <Objectives>
        To assess the efficacy and safety of tadalafil in reducing clinical symptoms and improving exercise tolerance in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) compared to placebo.
    </Objectives>
    
    <Background>
        Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction is characterized by significant morbidity and mortality, with limited therapeutic options available. Recent studies suggest that modulation of cyclic nucleotides may result in improved cardiac performance. Tadalafil, a phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor, may enhance therapeutic outcomes via increased cGMP levels. This study investigates whether tadalafil, alone, can lead to a measurable improvement in functional capacity and patient-reported outcomes in HFpEF patients.
    </Background>
    
    <Hypothesis>
        Tadalafil will result in significant improvements in the primary endpoint, defined as the change in the 6-minute walk test from baseline at 12 weeks compared to placebo, by enhancing exercise capacity and overall clinical status.
    </Hypothesis>
    
    <StudyDesign>
        A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial will be conducted over a 12-week treatment period, with an additional 6-month follow-up. 
    </StudyDesign>
    
    <StudyPopulation>
        The trial aims to enroll 150 adult participants aged 60-85 years, diagnosed with HFpEF, defined by clinical criteria and echocardiographic parameters.
    </StudyPopulation>
    
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>1. Diagnosis of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, evidenced by EF > 50%.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>2. Symptoms of heart failure classified as NYHA Class II-III.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>3. A history of exercise intolerance, evidenced by a 6-minute walk distance of 150-450 meters.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>4. Stable medical regimen without changes for at least 4 weeks prior to enrollment.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>1. Patients with acute decompensated heart failure or hospitalization for heart failure within 30 days of randomization.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>2. Significant coronary artery disease, indicated by previous myocardial infarction within the last 6 months.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>3. Severe renal or hepatic dysfunction (GFR < 30 mL/min, ALT/AST > 3 x ULN).</Criterion>
        <Criterion>4. History of penile deformity, or use of nitrates.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>5. Concurrent therapies for HFpEF, including other PDE5 inhibitors or novel heart failure medications.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    
    <PrimaryEndpoint>
        Change in the distance walked in 6 minutes from baseline to the end of the 12-week treatment period.
    </PrimaryEndpoint>
    
    <SecondaryEndpoints>
        <Endpoint>Improvement in quality of life measured by the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire.</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Change in NT-proBNP levels as a biomarker of heart failure severity.</Endpoint>
        <Endpoint>Assessment of exercise capacity through cardiopulmonary exercise testing.</Endpoint>
    </SecondaryEndpoints>
    
    <ResearchProcedures>
        Participants will be randomly assigned to either tadalafil 40 mg once daily or placebo, with appointments scheduled at baseline, 4, 8, and 12 weeks for assessments including clinical evaluations, laboratory tests, and 6-minute walk tests.
    </ResearchProcedures>
    
    <ReasonsForFailure>
        <Reason>1. Mechanistic assumptions regarding tadalafil's action on cGMP levels in the context of HFpEF did not translate into clinical efficacy, evidenced by no significant change in the 6-minute walk distance between groups.</Reason>
        <Reason>2. The participant population displayed considerable heterogeneity in comorbidities and underlying disease states, confounding the interpretation of treatment effects and generalizability of findings.</Reason>
        <Reason>3. Treatment-related adverse effects, including headache and dyspepsia, emerged prominently, adversely affecting participant adherence and retention rates in the trial.</Reason>
        <Reason>4. The trial duration of 12 weeks was inadequate to capture late-occurring clinical benefits associated with chronic use of tadalafil in HFpEF, limiting the detection of potential delayed efficacy.</Reason>
        <Reason>5. Inclusion criteria resulted in enrollment of patients with similar baseline characteristics, limiting the trial’s statistical power and capacity to detect significant differences in outcomes.</Reason>
    </ReasonsForFailure>

</ClinicalTrial>
