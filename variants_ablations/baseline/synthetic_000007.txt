**Clinical Trial Report: Efficacy of Fluorouracil in Combination with Targeted Therapy in Elderly Patients with Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

**RATIONALE:** The synergistic potential of fluorouracil in combination with targeted therapy has been recognized as a promising strategy for enhancing the therapeutic outcomes in elderly patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML). The incorporation of fluorouracil with agents that target specific genetic mutations evident in AML cells can potentially augment the cytotoxic effects and improve survival rates.

**PURPOSE:** This is a multicenter, open-label, randomized phase III trial designed to compare the efficacy of fluorouracil in combination with targeted therapies versus standard chemotherapy regimens in elderly patients with newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukemia.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- To evaluate the overall survival (OS) rates in elderly patients treated with the combination of fluorouracil plus venetoclax, an oral BCL-2 inhibitor.
- To assess the disease-free survival (DFS) in the treatment arm compared to standard care.
- To monitor treatment-related toxicities and quality of life (QoL) throughout the treatment duration.
- To evaluate the impact of this regimen on tumor burden prior to hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT).

**OUTLINE:** 
This trial will recruit approximately 1200 elderly patients (aged ≥65 years) with newly diagnosed AML characterized by either intermediate or adverse cytogenetics. Patients will be stratified based on performance status, genetic markers, and previous treatment (if any).

Patients will be randomized into two treatment arms:
- **Arm A:** Patients will receive fluorouracil (continuous IV infusion, 200 mg/m²/day) in combination with venetoclax (100 mg orally once daily for 28 days) for the initial induction phase, followed by consolidation therapy with high-dose cytarabine (1 g/m² IV every 12 hours for 6 doses). 
- **Arm B:** Patients in the control group will receive standard therapy with irinotecan and cytarabine, administered in accordance with established dosing guidelines.

Patient monitoring will include hematological assessments, cytogenetic analysis, and toxicity evaluations at designated intervals.

**PROJECTION OF ACCRUAL:** A total of 1200 patients will be accrued for this study within 36 months, allowing a robust statistical analysis with an anticipated alpha error of 0.05 and a power of 80%.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Confirmation of de novo AML (FAB classification M0-M5).
- Any grade of disease, with cytogenic abnormalities permitted.
- Patients must have adequate renal and hepatic function and no evidence of prior chemotherapy or hematopoietic disorders.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age: 65 years and older.
- Performance status: ECOG 0-2.
- Life expectancy: Not specifically defined, but must be greater than 3 months.
- Hematological criteria: WBC < 30,000/mm³, Platelet count > 50,000/mm³.
- Prior or concurrent therapies: No history of prior chemotherapy or monoclonal antibody treatment for AML.

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- Biologic therapy: No prior therapy with BCL-2 inhibitors or anti-leukemic drugs.
- Chemotherapy: No previous chemotherapy for AML or other hematological malignancies.
- Radiotherapy: No prior radiotherapy targeting the bone marrow or lymphatic systems.
  
**OUTCOMES MEASURES:**
- **Overall Survival (OS):** Defined from randomization until death from any cause.
- **Disease-Free Survival (DFS):** Defined as the time from achieving complete remission until the first evidence of relapse or death from any cause.
- **Toxicity Assessment:** Evaluated according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), V5.0.

**DATA ANALYSIS:** Descriptive statistics will be employed to summarize baseline characteristics of the patient population, while survival analyses will be performed using Kaplan-Meier methods. Statistical comparisons between treatment arms will utilize log-rank tests.

This study endeavors to provide a comprehensively designed clinical evaluation of fluorouracil in conjunction with targeted therapy, aligning with the pre-established success outlined by prior trials and addressing the unmet need in an aging patient population grappling with the complexities of AML.
