**Failed Clinical Trial of Nintedanib: Efficacy and Safety of Nintedanib Combined With Avelumab for Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Study Title:** A Phase II, Open-label, Randomized Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Oral Nintedanib Administered in Combination With Avelumab versus Nintedanib Alone in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Post Platinum-Based Chemotherapy

**Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of nintedanib when co-administered with avelumab compared to nintedanib monotherapy in patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have relapsed after receiving at least one standard platinum-based chemotherapy regimen.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Male or female patients aged ≥ 18 years at visit 1.
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced (stage IV) NSCLC, according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) criteria, with progression following at least one prior platinum-based therapy.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
- At least one measurable lesion per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- Adequate organ function as defined by laboratory parameters (e.g., hepatic, renal, and hematologic).
- Written informed consent provided according to ICH-GCP and local regulations prior to any study-related procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with nintedanib or avelumab.
- History of severe adverse reactions to any component of the study drugs.
- Active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy within the past two years.
- Underlying severe comorbidities, including uncontrolled hypertension, significant cardiovascular events within the past 6 months, or severe chronic lung disease.
- Concurrent malignancies or any prior malignancy that may interfere with assessment of efficacy or safety.
- Known hypersensitivity to nintedanib, avelumab, or their excipients.

**Design:** This study adopts a randomized, open-label, parallel-group design and aims to compare two treatment arms: arm A consisting of nintedanib combined with avelumab and arm B receiving nintedanib alone. Recruitment was halted due to challenges arising from the following factors:

1. **Insufficient Patient Recruitment:** The stringent inclusion criteria significantly limited the pool of eligible participants, leading to prolonged enrollment periods. In particular, the high incidence of disqualifying comorbidities among potential candidates resulted in only a handful of patients meeting criteria throughout the trial period.

2. **Unfavorable Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** An analysis of pharmacokinetics indicated concerning interactions between nintedanib and avelumab, suggesting alterations in bioavailability which may have adversely affected the therapeutic impacts expected from this dual regimen. Subsequent blood tests exhibited elevated serum levels of both agents, raising concerns regarding their combined safety profile.

3. **Lack of Clinical Efficacy:** Preliminary data failed to demonstrate statistically significant improvements in primary efficacy endpoints, including progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS), suggesting that the combined treatment did not confer the anticipated additive benefits in managing disease progression in advanced NSCLC.

4. **Heightened Safety Concerns:** The trial revealed a marked increase in serious adverse events, particularly those related to immune-mediated reactions and gastrointestinal perforations. Elevated rates of adverse event-related discontinuations raised substantial safety concerns regarding the application of this combination therapy, impeding participant compliance and retention.

5. **Diminished Therapeutic Window in Advanced Disease Stages:** Many enrolled patients presented with advanced disease states and had undergone multiple lines of previous treatments, which may have resulted in a diminished responsiveness to nintedanib in conjunction with avelumab. This reduction in pharmacological efficacy potentially altered the anticipated clinical outcome of the intervention.

**Endpoints:**
- Primary endpoint: Change from baseline in progression-free survival (PFS) measured at 6 months post-randomization.
- Secondary endpoints: Overall survival (OS), safety profile including adverse events, and quality of life monitored through validated questionnaires (e.g., EORTC QLQ-C30).

**Conclusion:** Due to the factors outlined above, this clinical trial of nintedanib, in combination with avelumab for advanced NSCLC, was terminated early prior to final recruitment completion. The results illustrate the complexities faced in combining targeted therapies, especially in advanced disease settings, where pharmacokinetic compatibility and patient factors critically contribute to the success or failure of interventional strategies. Further investigations may focus on optimizing patient selection and exploring alternative therapeutic combinations to enhance efficacy while minimizing risks.
