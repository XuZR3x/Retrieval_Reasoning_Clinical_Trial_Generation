**Failed Clinical Trial of Pembrolizumab: A Phase II Study Evaluating Pembrolizumab in Combination With Cisplatin and Atezolizumab in Advanced Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Lung**

**Objective:**  
This Phase II clinical trial aims to assess the safety and efficacy of pembrolizumab in combination with cisplatin and atezolizumab in patients with advanced squamous cell carcinoma of the lung who have failed prior therapies. The hypothesis posits that the addition of atezolizumab—a programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitor—may enhance the anti-tumor response via dual checkpoint inhibition. 

**Background:**  
Existing literature suggests that the immunological landscape of squamous cell lung cancer may exhibit a unique intrinsic resistance to anti-PD-1 therapies, exemplified by variable levels of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes in the tumor microenvironment. Prior studies indicate that non-responders to pembrolizumab may harbor specific genetic mutations or epigenetic modifications affecting immune escape pathways. Furthermore, the combination with cisplatin introduces antitumor challenges, potentially inducing immunosuppression that compromises the efficacy of pembrolizumab, paralleling prior studies wherein chemotherapy detrimentally influenced immune response.

**Design:**  
This is a multi-center, randomized, open-label Phase II study wherein patients are assigned to receive either pembrolizumab in combination with cisplatin and standard of care versus cisplatin with standard of care alone. The primary endpoint is overall response rate (ORR) per RECIST 1.1 criteria. Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety profile assessment. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma of the lung.  
- Patients must have received at least one line of systemic therapy prior to enrollment.  
- Radiographically measurable disease.  
- An Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Documented disease progression on prior PD-1 or PD-L1 therapy.  
- Known history of central nervous system metastases or active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment.  
- Chronic steroid use or other forms of immunosuppressive therapy.  
- Significant comorbidities that preclude full participation in the study.

**Treatment Protocol:**  
- Patients will receive intravenous pembrolizumab (200 mg) every 3 weeks, combined with cisplatin (75 mg/m²) administered on day 1 of each cycle. Atezolizumab (1200 mg) will be administered concurrently every 3 weeks. Treatment will continue for up to 24 months or until disease progression.

**Assessment of Efficacy:**  
The overall response rate will be evaluated through imaging modalities, including CT or MRI, measuring complete response (CR) defined as total disappearance of lesions, and partial response (PR) as ≥30% decrease in total diameter of target lesions. The study will implement robust biomarker-driven selection criteria, emphasizing the necessity of adequate tumor samples for genomic profiling and PD-L1 expression analysis.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Efficacy assessments are anticipated to elucidate the effectivity of the dual immune checkpoint blockade but are confounded by the concurrent use of cisplatin. Previous data suggests that such chemotherapy regimens may evoke significant immunosuppression, thus undermining pembrolizumab's therapeutic potential. Notably, variability in patient population regarding previous cytotoxic treatments may yield heterogeneous outcomes, reflecting the limitations of biomarker stratification in this domain.

**Study Duration:**  
The study will enroll participants for a study duration of 36 months followed by an extended follow-up of 12 months post-therapy cessation for survival assessment.

**Conclusion:**  
This clinical trial is designed to critically evaluate the promising yet contentious interplay between dual immune checkpoint inhibition and chemotherapy in advanced squamous cell carcinoma of the lung. However, based on earlier failed trials, particularly those reflecting on immune efficacy adverse consequences tied to the concurrent administration of synergistic therapies, the risk of further disappointing results looms as a significant consideration. This underscores the current pressing requirement for optimally tailored treatment protocols that address individual tumor microenvironments and immune evasion mechanisms before advancing toward combination therapies.
