**Failed Clinical Trial of Enfuvirtide: Evaluation of Enfuvirtide for Treatment-Experienced Patients with Persistent HIV-1 Viremia**

**Title:** Efficacy and Safety of Enfuvirtide in Addition to Optimized Background Antiretroviral Therapy in HIV-1 Infected Treatment-Experienced Patients with Persistent Viral Load

**Study Design:** A multicenter, open-label, randomized, exploratory phase II trial assessing the efficacy and safety of enfuvirtide in treatment-experienced patients with persistent HIV-1 viral load.

**Objective:** To evaluate the added effects of enfuvirtide on virological and immunological outcomes when administered alongside an optimized background therapy (OBT) in HIV-1 infected individuals with a history of treatment failure.

**Background:** Prior trials have demonstrated that enfuvirtide may exhibit suboptimal efficacy in treatment-experienced populations, primarily due to pre-existing drug resistance mutations. Immunological restoration has been limited, as reflected in insufficient CD4 count improvement in severely immunosuppressed patients. Furthermore, the requirement for subcutaneous administration and potential drug interactions with OBT create barriers to patient adherence and therapeutic effectiveness.

**Study Population:** The trial aimed to enroll 300 participants (150 per group) aged 18-65 years with confirmed HIV-1 infection and documented treatment experience, including exposure to at least two classes of antiretrovirals. Participants must have had a persistent viral load of > 500 copies/mL despite the optimized background therapy for a minimum of 6 months. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18-65 with HIV-1 infection
- CD4 count < 200 cells/mm^3
- Documented previous exposure to two or more antiretroviral classes
- Baseline viral load > 500 copies/mL after at least 6 weeks of OBT

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Known coinfection with HIV-2 or other viral pathogens
- Previous treatment with enfuvirtide
- Active opportunistic infections requiring treatment
- Pregnancy or lactation
- Significant comorbidities potentially affecting study adherence (e.g., severe psychiatric disorders)

**Intervention and Control:** Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either enfuvirtide (Fuzeon) in conjunction with OBT or OBT alone for 24 weeks, followed by a follow-up phase lasting up to 48 weeks. Dosing of enfuvirtide was administered via subcutaneous injection as per approved protocols.

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary endpoint was the proportion of participants achieving viral suppression (defined as a plasma viral load < 50 copies/mL) at Week 24.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Changes in CD4 cell counts from baseline to Week 24
- Frequency of adverse events and treatment-related discontinuation rates
- Assessment of patient adherence to treatment regimen
   
**Results:** Results indicated no statistically significant difference in viral suppression rates between the two arms, with only 35% of the enfuvirtide group achieving the primary endpoint compared to 34% in the control arm receiving only OBT (p=0.78). Additionally, there was no significant increase in CD4 count, suggesting limited immunological restoration with enfuvirtide supplementation. Adherence assessments revealed that 40% of participants in the enfuvirtide group had issues with adherence due to the complexities associated with the injection route. 

**Conclusion:** The trial was terminated prematurely due to the prespecified futility analysis demonstrating a lack of significant benefit over OBT alone in achieving viral suppression and immunological improvement in a treatment-experienced population. The findings suggest that enfuvirtide demonstrates inadequate efficacy and poses challenges for patient compliance, reaffirming limitations observed in prior studies.

**Ethical Considerations:** The study was conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines and received approval from ethical review boards at all participating institutions. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. 

**Safety Monitoring:** An independent Data and Safety Monitoring Board oversaw the trial and recommended suspension due to adverse risk-benefit ratio after preliminary results.

**Future Directions:** Further research is warranted to explore alternative combination strategies that may optimize treatment efficacy for heavily treatment-experienced HIV-1 populations while considering the implications of resistance mutations and the practicality of treatment adherence.
