
**Failed clinical trial of nintedanib: Nintedanib in Combination Therapy for Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**A Phase II Study of Nintedanib in Combination With Standard Chemotherapy for Treatment of Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

This phase II trial assesses the efficacy and safety of nintedanib, in conjunction with standard chemotherapy agents (carboplatin and paclitaxel), in patients diagnosed with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The study aims to evaluate whether the addition of nintedanib yields significant improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) compared to the standard regimen alone.

**PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:**

I. To investigate the 6-month progression-free survival (PFS) rate in patients with untreated advanced NSCLC receiving nintedanib in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel.

**SECONDARY OBJECTIVES:**

I. To assess overall survival (OS) and response rates (confirmed and unconfirmed, complete and partial responses) among the patient cohort.

II. To evaluate the frequency and severity of adverse events associated with this treatment regimen.

III. To analyze biomarker correlations using tumor tissue samples collected at baseline.

**OUTLINE:**

Patients receive nintedanib orally (PO) at 200 mg twice daily (BID) on days 1-28, in conjunction with chemotherapy (carboplatin AUC 5 and paclitaxel 200 mg/m2) administered on day 1 of each cycle. Treatment cycles repeat every 28 days in the absence of disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Post-treatment, patients are followed up periodically for safety and efficacy assessments.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA:**

- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced NSCLC (stage IIIB or IV).
- Measurable disease according to RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- No prior treatment for NSCLC, including chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or targeted therapies.
- Adequate organ function (characterized by the following laboratory values: ANC ≥ 1,500/mcl, platelets ≥ 100,000/mcl, and serum bilirubin ≤ institutional upper limit of normal [ULN]).
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
- Patients must provide written informed consent in accordance with institutional and federal guidelines.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**

- Previous anti-cancer therapy (chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or radiotherapy).
- Symptomatic brain metastases or leptomeningeal disease.
- Significant cardiovascular abnormalities (i.e., uncontrolled hypertension or history of myocardial infarction within the last 12 months).
- Coexisting medical conditions that may compromise safety or efficacy (e.g., severe hepatic impairment or renal failure).
- Evidence of other malignancies within the last 5 years, except for adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancer or in situ cervical carcinoma.

**PROGRESSION-FREE SURVIVAL (PFS):**

PFS will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. The duration from randomization to documented disease progression (as defined by RECIST criteria) or death will be computed. Censoring will occur for patients without progression or death, based on the last imaging assessment showing no progression.

**JUSTIFICATION FOR STUDY TERMINATION:**

This study was terminated due to multiple factors contributing to the observed lack of efficacy and high discontinuation rates among participants. The reasons observed include:

1. **Insufficient Efficacy**: Nintedanib in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel failed to meet the anticipated endpoint, with a 6-month PFS rate of <20%, demonstrating inadequate therapeutic activity in a highly aggressive tumor type.

2. **Lack of Statistical Significance**: The treatment arm demonstrated no statistically significant improvement in overall survival when compared to the control group, thus indicating the lack of synergistic interaction hypothesized from the combined regimen.

3. **Adverse Events Profile**: Nintedanib led to higher than expected rates of gastrointestinal toxicity and cardiovascular events in the context of systemic therapy, which adversely affected treatment adherence and clinical safety.

4. **Biomarker Analysis**: Correlative studies exhibited no predictive biomarkers for response, suggesting a disconnect between the proposed mechanism of nintedanib and the biological pathways active in NSCLC, further hampering potential clinical benefit.

5. **Population Representation**: Strict eligibility criteria potentially limited generalizability, excluding patients with comorbid conditions that could have otherwise benefited from treatment, causing the trial results to reflect an unrepresentative patient cohort.

Overall, the clinical trial failed to credibly establish nintedanib as a viable adjunct therapy in the treatment of advanced NSCLC, leading to its discontinuation pending future investigative pathways.
