**Failed Clinical Trial of Regorafenib: A Phase III Study Evaluating Regorafenib in Combination with Standard Of Care in Patients with Advanced Colorectal Cancer**

**Study Title:** A Phase III Randomized Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Regorafenib Combined with Standard Chemotherapy in Patients with Previously Treated Metastatic Colorectal Cancer

**Overview**: This phase III trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy of regorafenib in combination with standard chemotherapy compared to standard chemotherapy alone in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) who have failed first-line therapy. 

**Primary Objective:** To assess the overall survival (OS) in patients receiving regorafenib in addition to standard chemotherapy versus those receiving standard chemotherapy alone.

**Secondary Objectives:** 
- To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) among treatment groups.
- To assess the safety and toxicity profile associated with the addition of regorafenib to standard chemotherapy.
- To analyze response rates (RR) defined by RECIST 1.1 criteria.

**Study Design and Methodology:**
Eligible patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either:  
1. **Experimental Arm:** Regorafenib (160 mg orally once daily) plus selected standard chemotherapy (e.g., FOLFIRI or FOLFOX). 
2. **Control Arm:** Standard chemotherapy alone.

Patients were treated in 28-day cycles for a maximum of 12 cycles, or until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurred. The study aimed to enroll 1,200 participants to provide adequate statistical power, targeting 600 deaths for OS outcome analysis.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed metastatic colorectal cancer refractory to at least one prior chemotherapy regimen.
- Measurable disease as per RECIST 1.1 criteria.
- ECOG Performance Status of 0-1.
- Adequate organ function, including hepatic and renal parameters assessed by laboratory tests.
- Ability to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with regorafenib or other multikinase inhibitors.
- Uncontrolled hypertension (systolic BP > 140 mm Hg or diastolic BP > 90 mm Hg).
- Significant cardiovascular comorbidities including NYHA Class III or IV heart failure, myocardial infarction within 6 months prior to enrollment, or unstable angina.
- Active or clinically significant coagulopathy.
- Evidence of secondary malignancies, symptomatic brain metastases, or any interstitial lung disease.

**Endpoints and Assessments:** 
- **Primary Endpoint:** Overall survival (OS) assessed from initiation of trial therapy until death from any cause.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** Progression-free survival (PFS), overall response rates (ORR), and safety as graded by NCI-CTCAE v4.0.

**Results:** 
Enrollment completion did not lead to expected clinical benefit in the experimental arm. Primary analyses showed no statistically significant difference in OS between the two groups. Of the 1,200 enrolled patients, 650 patients were randomized to receive regorafenib and 550 to the control arm. 

After an interim analysis conducted when 500 deaths had been recorded, it was found that:
1. **OS in Experimental Arm:** Median OS was 12.3 months.
2. **OS in Control Arm:** Median OS was 12.5 months.

Additionally, the expected improvement in progression-free survival was not observed, with a PFS of 4.5 months in the experimental arm compared to 4.7 months in the control arm. 

**Safety Profile:**
The addition of regorafenib was associated with a higher incidence of grade 3-4 toxicities, leading to diminished treatment adherence:
- Severe hypertension (31%)
- Grade 3 fatigue (28%)
- Gastrointestinal toxicities such as diarrhea and hepatic enzyme elevations were further deleterious, resulting in over 20% treatment discontinuation due to adverse effects.

**Conclusion:** 
The trial was terminated due to insufficient evidence supporting the combination therapy approach and the adverse impact of regorafenib on patient tolerability. These findings elucidate the inherent challenges associated with a heterogeneous population within advanced colorectal cancer, reiterating concerns regarding pharmacodynamic inadequacies and patient compliance due to toxicity burdens. This clinical trial highlights the necessity for more tailored therapeutic strategies to improve management outcomes in mCRC. Further studies with a more stratified approach considering molecular subtypes may be warranted for future regorafenib investigations.
