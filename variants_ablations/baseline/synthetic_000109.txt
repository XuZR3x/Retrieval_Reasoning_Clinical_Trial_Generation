**Failed clinical trial of laboratory biomarker analysis: Evaluation of Circulating Tumor DNA (ctDNA) as a Biomarker for Therapy-Related Adverse Events in Patients with Advanced Lung Cancer Receiving Pembrolizumab**

**RATIONALE:** The therapeutic landscape for advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) has shifted significantly with the advent of immune checkpoint inhibitors such as pembrolizumab. Determining the potential of ctDNA as a biomarker for predicting therapy-related adverse events is paramount to improving patient outcomes and tailoring personalized therapeutic strategies. 

**PURPOSE:** This Phase II trial aims to evaluate the correlation between ctDNA levels and the incidence of adverse events in patients with advanced NSCLC receiving pembrolizumab. By analyzing longitudinal ctDNA samples, this study seeks to elucidate potential biomarkers that could predict immune-related adverse effects associated with pembrolizumab treatment.

**OBJECTIVES:**
- **Primary:** To assess whether changes in circulating tumor DNA levels correlate with the development of therapy-related adverse events as measured by CTCAE v5.0.
- **Secondary:** To explore if specific genetic variants identified in ctDNA are associated with the exacerbation of adverse events and to evaluate the relationship between ctDNA levels and clinical outcomes, including progression-free survival and overall survival.

**OUTLINE:** Patients will be enrolled upon confirming histologically advanced NSCLC and initiating treatment with pembrolizumab. Blood samples will be collected at baseline and every cycle (3 weeks) until disease progression. ctDNA will be quantified using next-generation sequencing to identify variants. Clinical data regarding adverse events will be obtained from clinical assessments.

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Histologically confirmed non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)
- Stage III or IV disease, previously treated with first-line chemotherapy
- Scheduled to receive pembrolizumab as monotherapy 

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**
- Age ≥ 18 years
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2
- Able to provide informed consent 

**PRIOR CONCURRENT THERAPY:**
- Patients may have received prior treatment with chemotherapy or targeted therapy but must not have had prior immunotherapy.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**
- Patients with previously identified active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment or those on high-dose steroids (>10 mg/day of prednisone equivalent)
- Concurrent participation in another investigational drug study
- History of hypersensitivity to monoclonal antibodies

**PRIMARY OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- Correlation between changes in ctDNA levels and the occurrence of therapy-related adverse events over 12 months.

**SECONDARY OUTCOME MEASURES:**
- Relationships between specific detected variants in ctDNA and severity of adverse events.
- Progression-free survival (PFS) measured from the initiation of treatment until disease progression.
- Overall survival (OS) determined from the start of treatment until death from any cause.

**LIMITATIONS:** Several factors contributed to the cessation of this study. Inadequate patient selection criteria permitted variability in tumor types and comorbid conditions that diluted the focus on those likely to experience immune-related adverse effects. Additionally, inconsistencies in ctDNA assessment methodologies compromised the ability to draw meaningful correlations between ctDNA levels and patient-reported outcomes. Furthermore, the influence of concurrent medications on immune response mechanisms could not be adequately controlled. The complexity of the immune landscape of NSCLC likely obscured potential predictive power of ctDNA, making conclusions regarding its utility as a biomarker insufficiently robust. 

**CONCLUSION:** This trial reinforces the need for refined patient selection and standardized methodologies in biomarker analysis. An understanding of the multifaceted influences on immunity and cancer treatment responses is crucial for advancing the field of personalized oncology, particularly in efforts to mitigate treatment-associated adverse events. Future studies should strive for more stringent criteria and standardized outcome measurements to enhance the reliability of findings related to circulating biomarkers in oncology.
