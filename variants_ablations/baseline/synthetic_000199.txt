**Clinical Trial of Risperidone: Efficacy and Safety in Treatment of Agitation in Patients with Alzheimer’s Disease**

**Title:** A Multi-Center, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study of Risperidone in the Management of Agitation in Patients with Alzheimer’s Disease

**Objective:** To evaluate the safety and efficacy of risperidone in alleviating agitation symptoms in patients diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease.

Alzheimer’s disease is frequently associated with neuropsychiatric manifestations, including agitation, which significantly impact patient quality of life and caregiver burden. Traditional treatment methods have often proven inadequate, leading to a search for more effective therapeutic options. This study postulates that risperidone, with its dual action on serotonin 5-HT2A and dopamine D2 receptors, presents a significant advantage in addressing both the behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia, specifically agitation.

**Methodology:**
Conducted across 25 clinical sites, this was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial involving 200 patients aged 65 years and older diagnosed with mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease displaying symptoms of agitation. The primary goal was to examine the reduction in agitation as quantified by the Agitation Inventory Scale (AIS) over a 12-week study period.

**Intervention:**
Patients were administered either 1 mg/day of risperidone or an equivalent placebo. Doses could be titrated based on clinical response and tolerability, with a maximum dose of 3 mg/day, particularly considering the necessity for dosage adjustments to maximize patient comfort and minimize adverse effects. Regular follow-up visits were scheduled every two weeks throughout the study to ensure adherence and monitor adverse events.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age 65 years or older
- Clinical diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease (DSM-V criteria)
- Presence of moderate agitation (AIS score ≥ 15)
- Informed consent from legal guardians

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to risperidone
- Significant cardiovascular disease
- Active substance dependence or abuse
- Prior treatment with antipsychotic medications within 8 weeks
- Presence of neuroleptic malignant syndrome or tardive dyskinesia

**Outcome Measures:**
The primary efficacy endpoint was the change in AIS scores from baseline to 12 weeks. Secondary endpoints included the overall clinical improvement measured through the Clinical Global Impression Scale (CGI) and patient quality of life assessed through the Alzheimer’s Disease Cooperative Study-Activities of Daily Living (ADCS-ADL) scale.

**Results:**
The trial results indicated a statistically significant reduction in agitation scores among patients receiving risperidone compared to those on placebo, with a mean change in AIS scores of 7.2 points at 12 weeks (p < 0.001). The overall clinical improvement also mirrored this finding, with a higher percentage of risperidone-treated patients demonstrating marked improvements as assessed by CGI (45% versus 22%, p < 0.01).

**Safety Assessments:**
Adverse effects were monitored regularly, revealing that risperidone was generally well tolerated. The incidence of extrapyramidal symptoms was minimal and managed with supportive care, confirming the favorable safety profile of risperidone in this demographic.

**Conclusion:**
This clinical trial substantiates risperidone’s efficacy in managing agitation in patients with Alzheimer’s disease, showcasing a robust pharmacological profile conducive to improving patient outcomes. The rigorous design, selective inclusion criteria, and adaptive patient engagement reinforcement strategies employed throughout this study ensured the validity of the data collected, positioning risperidone as a viable treatment option to enhance quality of life in affected patients. Further investigations exploring long-term outcomes and comparative efficacy with other treatment modalities are warranted to consolidate these findings.
