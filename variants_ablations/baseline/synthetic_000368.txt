**Failed clinical trial of nbi-98854: Evaluation of NBI-98854 for Tourette Syndrome Management in Adults A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Safety and Efficacy of NBI-98854 in Adult Subjects With Tourette Syndrome**  

**Phase:** 2, double-blind, placebo-controlled study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of NBI-98854 administered twice daily (bid) for a total of 10 weeks of treatment. This study will enroll approximately 150 male and female subjects clinically diagnosed with Tourette Syndrome.  

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Have a clinical diagnosis of Tourette Syndrome (TS) based on DSM-V criteria  
2. Have a YGTSS Total Tic Score indicative of moderate to severe tic severity  
3. Experience persistent tic symptoms that significantly interfere with daily life, including academic, occupational, and social functioning  
4. If using treatment medications for TS or comorbid conditions (e.g., anxiety disorders, ADHD), must be on stable doses for at least 8 weeks prior to screening  
5. Be in good general health with no significant comorbidities  
6. Negative urine drug screen for illicit substances and approval from participating sites for all enrolled participants  
7. For female participants of childbearing potential, must agree to employ dual contraception methods consistently during the study period  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Have an active, clinically significant unstable medical condition (including cardiac or psychiatric conditions) within 1 month prior to screening  
2. Have a known history of long QT syndrome or ventricular arrhythmias  
3. Have had a diagnosis of neuroleptic malignant syndrome  
4. Any history of malignancy within 5 years prior to screening (except localized skin cancers)  
5. Hypersensitivity or adverse reactions to VMAT2 inhibitors or their components  
6. Any history of substance dependence or abuse within the past 12 months  
7. Have a predisposed risk of self-harm or violent behavior  
8. Currently pregnant or lactating  
9. Participation in another clinical trial within 30 days of screening  
10. Blood loss ≥500 mL or plasma donation within 14 days before screening  

**Primary Endpoint:** Change from baseline to Week 10 in the YGTSS Total Tic Score (TTS), a validated scale that measures the frequency, severity, and impairment associated with tic disorders. The maximum score is 50, representing severe tic symptoms.  

**Secondary Endpoints:**  
1. Incidence and severity of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs)  
2. Change in participant-reported outcomes using the TS Questionnaire (TSQ)  

**Results:**  

The trial observed a statistically significant failure to achieve the primary efficacy endpoint. Specifically, the change in YGTSS Total Tic Score from baseline to Week 10 did not reach clinically meaningful thresholds as determined by both statistical significance (p>0.05) and clinical relevance (minimal clinically important difference).   

Several contributory factors led to the unsuccessful outcomes of this clinical trial:  

1. **Insufficient Efficacy:** The intervention did not produce a statistically significant reduction in tic severity compared to the placebo, questioning the therapeutic impact of NBI-98854 in the studied population.  

2. **Adverse Event Profile:** The study reported a higher frequency of TEAEs among NBI-98854 recipients, particularly notable were neuropsychiatric manifestations, which ranged from anxiety to insomnia. This raised concerns about the overall safety profile in this demographic group.   

3. **Pharmacokinetic Variability:** Significant inter-subject variability in pharmacokinetics was noted, hindering optimal plasma concentration maintenance, which might contribute to the observed low efficacy among a subset of participants.  

4. **Participant Retention Challenges:** With an attrition rate exceeding 30%, attributed largely to intolerable side effects and perceived lack of benefit, the compromised sample size negatively impacted the study's power and results.   

5. **Inadequate Participant Cohort Definition:** The restricted inclusion criteria, aimed at ensuring homogeneity, may have inadvertently excluded participants with more varied expressions of Tourette Syndrome who might have demonstrated a more favorable response to treatment.  

In conclusion, this trial's findings indicate that NBI-98854 does not demonstrate sufficient safety and efficacy to warrant further development in this patient population. Therefore, the continuation of research into alternative treatment pathways for Tourette Syndrome is recommended.
