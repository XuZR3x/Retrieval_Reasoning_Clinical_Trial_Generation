**Success Clinical Trial of Ofatumumab:**

**Ofatumumab Plus Veneto­clax in Relapsed/Refractory Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL)**  
*A Phase II, Multi-Centre Study Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Ofatumumab in Combination with Venetoclax in Patients with Relapsed/Refractory Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia*

This Phase II, open-label, single-arm, multi-centre study aims to assess the safety and efficacy of ofatumumab in combination with venetoclax in adult patients diagnosed with relapsed or refractory chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL). The study recruits participants who have received at least one prior line of therapy, demonstrating a clinical need for further intervention.

Eligible subjects will be administered the combination therapy for a maximum of 12 treatment cycles, with treatment evaluations occurring at predetermined intervals to ascertain safety and response rates. The treatment regimen will begin with ofatumumab administered intravenously (Cycle 1: 300 mg on Day 1 followed by 1000 mg on Day 8; Cycle 2 onward: 1000 mg on Day 1 every 28 days) concurrently with daily oral administration of venetoclax (starting dose: 20 mg, titrated to a maximum dose of 400 mg).

The study's primary endpoint is the overall response rate (ORR), evaluated by independent investigators based on the IWCLL updated NCIWG criteria. Secondary endpoints focus on investigating the rates of complete remission (CR), the duration of response, progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS). Safety profiles will be meticulously monitored, with dose modifications and supportive care procedures established to manage any adverse events effectively.

Comprehensive participant screening will ensure a homogeneous population that specifically meets strict inclusion and exclusion criteria, reducing confounding variables impacting the observed efficacy of the therapy. Pharmacokinetic profiling during the study will enable real-time exploration of drug interactions, allowing for an optimized therapeutic index while maintaining patient safety.

Participants who demonstrate at least stable disease without significant toxicities after 6 cycles will be assessed for the potential continuation of therapy. Participants who progress after 3 cycles will exit the treatment phase and enter the follow-up period. Follow-up assessments will occur every 3 months post-treatment for a maximum duration of 3 years, allowing for comprehensive data collection on long-term safety and efficacy outcomes.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Confirmed diagnosis of relapsed or refractory CLL based on IWCLL criteria.
2. Prior treatment with at least one line of therapy (not including ofatumumab or venetoclax).
3. Age ≥18 years.
4. ECOG Performance Status of 0-2.
5. Adequate organ function as defined by the protocol.
6. Written informed consent obtained from the participant prior to any study-related procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Prior treatment with ofatumumab or venetoclax.
2. Known transformation of CLL to aggressive lymphoma or presence of Richter's syndrome.
3. Significant concurrent medical conditions assessed as contraindications by the investigator.
4. Active central nervous system involvement by CLL.
5. Documented hypersensitivity reactions to ofatumumab, venetoclax, or their constituents.

**Statistical Considerations**:
The trial will employ a one-sample binomial test to ascertain the response rates. Statistical significance is defined as a two-sided p-value <0.05. Interim analyses will guide further therapeutic decisions as needed, informed by both clinical response data and safety evaluations.

**Investigator Assessment of Response**:
Categorization of treatment response will follow the IWCLL updated NCIWG criteria, encompassing:
- **Complete remission (CR)**: Absence of detectable disease in blood, bone marrow, and lymphoid tissues.
- **Partial response (PR)**: ≥50% reduction in lymphocyte count and measurable disease.
- **Stable disease (SD)**: No significant clinical progression over assessment interval.

The anticipated outcomes of this study could substantiate the use of ofatumumab in a synergistic approach with venetoclax, demonstrating improved efficacy in the management of relapsed or refractory CLL, potentially establishing a new standard of care. 

This trial will conclusively enhance our understanding of ofatumumab’s role, particularly regarding its interaction with venetoclax, thereby potentially optimizing treatment outcomes for CLL patients exhibiting therapy-resistance characteristics.
