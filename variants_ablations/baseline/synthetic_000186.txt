**Title: Efficacy of Methotrexate in Inducing Remission in Steroid-Dependent Ulcerative Colitis: A Phase III Randomized Controlled Trial**

- **PHASE**: III 
- **TYPE OF STUDY**: Interventional, randomized, multicenter, double-blind study
- **OBJECTIVES**: To evaluate the superiority of methotrexate compared to a placebo in inducing steroid-free remission in steroid-dependent individuals suffering from ulcerative colitis (UC).

**STUDY DESIGN**: 
This clinical trial is designed to assess the safety and efficacy of methotrexate administered at a dose of 25 mg/week via intramuscular injection against placebo over a treatment period of 52 weeks, followed by an extended follow-up of 12 months. The study aims to recruit a well-defined patient population characterized by documented steroid-dependability, thereby providing a focused therapeutic target.

**INCLUSION CRITERIA**: 
- Patients aged 18 to 65 with a confirmed diagnosis of UC according to the Lennard-Jones criteria. 
- A Mayo Disease Activity Index score of 5 or less with sub-scores reflecting steroid dependency, indicated by a minimum oral prednisone dosage of 20 mg/day for at least three weeks within the last three months prior to inclusion. 
- Capability to provide informed consent. 
- Women of childbearing potential must be on reliable contraceptive measures.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA**: 
- Patients with a documented history of significant renal or hepatic impairment, including but not limited to creatinine levels exceeding the upper limit of normal and elevated transaminases.
- Current treatment with other immunosuppressive therapies or recent changes in mesalazine.

**STUDY TREATMENTS**: 
- **Group A**: Methotrexate 25 mg intramuscularly once per week. 
- **Group B**: Placebo administered at a parallel schedule.

**SAMPLE SIZE**: 
- A total of 300 patients will be enrolled, with 150 participants in each arm, thereby ensuring sufficient power to demonstrate statistically significant findings.

**STUDY DURATION**: 
- Total duration of the study is expected to be 64 weeks, encompassing a 52-week treatment phase and a subsequent 12-month follow-up period to monitor long-term outcomes.

**EVALUATION CRITERIA**: 
- Primary endpoint consists of the proportion of patients achieving steroid-free remission, evaluated at week 16 using a composite measure (absence of steroid administration, maintenance of clinical remission, and no need for colectomy).
- Secondary endpoints include assessment of disease activity, quality of life measures, and drug retention rates.

**BACKGROUND**: 
Ulcerative Colitis (UC) poses significant challenges in management, particularly in patients who demonstrate resistance or dependency upon corticosteroids, a commonly utilized treatment modality. Previous studies have indicated the inadequacy of standard therapeutic regimes, with only 41% of patients achieving lasting remission using Azathioprine. Methotrexate is a cost-effective therapeutic agent that has shown efficacy in other autoimmune conditions, including Crohn's disease. This study endeavors to investigate its potential benefits and generalizability in a neglected patient population with chronic steroid reliance, as preliminary evidence suggests a compelling rationale for its utilization at a higher intramuscular dosage of 25 mg/week.

The comprehensive and rigorous design of this multicenter study, characterized by clear inclusion criteria targeting a population at heightened risk for treatment failure, aims to furnish definitive insights into the therapeutic role of methotrexate in corticosteroid-dependent UC patients. The prolonged follow-up period will facilitate consideration of the durability and long-term safety profile of methotrexate usage, ultimately aiming to provide a robust evidence base justifying its incorporation into standard treatment paradigms for this challenging patient group. 

This trial is conducted under the auspices of the Gastroenterology Research Society (GRS) and with collaboration from leading gastroenterology units across multiple centers. The outcomes, if favorable, will substantially influence clinical practice and provide an efficacious, economical alternative for managing chronic ulcerative colitis in steroid-dependent individuals.
