**Failed clinical trial of asenapine: Efficacy and Safety of Asenapine in Major Depressive Episode Associated with Bipolar Disorder (P06544)**  
A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Asenapine in Adults Experiencing a Major Depressive Episode Associated with Bipolar Disorder 

The primary objective of this clinical trial was to evaluate the efficacy of asenapine compared to placebo in the treatment of patients diagnosed with a major depressive episode associated with bipolar disorder, type I. The hypothesis posited that asenapine would demonstrate superior efficacy in reducing depressive symptomatology, as measured by the Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS), relative to placebo over the course of 12 weeks.

In total, 200 patients aged 18-60 years, meeting the DSM-5 criteria for bipolar disorder type I with a current major depressive episode, were recruited across four study sites within an 18-month enrollment period. The study employed a double-blind design, wherein participants were randomly assigned to receive asenapine (starting dose of 5 mg bid) or matching placebo, with potential dose escalations to 10 mg bid based upon individual clinical response and tolerability.

Patient evaluations were conducted by trained assessors blind to treatment assignments at baseline (day 0) and follow-up visits at weeks 1, 2, 4, 8, and 12. Adverse events were documented, and changes in MADRS scores were the primary outcome measure.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Diagnosis of bipolar disorder type I as confirmed by MINI
- Age 18-60 years at time of screening
- Written informed consent obtained
- MADRS score ≥ 25 at baseline
- Active depressive episode lasting at least 4 weeks but not exceeding 6 months prior to screening

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnancy or lactation in females
- Presence of any clinically significant unstable medical conditions
- History of diabetes requiring pharmacological treatment
- Concurrent psychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia or major depressive disorder unrelated to bipolar disorder
- Non-compliance during screening or history of asenapine intolerance
- Suicidal risk as determined by a MADRS suicide score > 4

**Expected Outcomes:** 
The primary efficacy endpoint was the change in MADRS total scores from baseline to week 12. The MADRS response rate, defined as at least a 50% reduction in total score from baseline to endpoint, was additionally assessed.

**Result Summary:** 
The trial concluded with no statistically significant differences in MADRS score reductions between the asenapine and placebo groups at week 12, yielding a p-value > 0.05, indicating insufficient efficacy against placebo. Furthermore, high dropout rates attributed to tolerability issues were noted, raising concerns regarding adherence. Participant selection bias and restrictive inclusion criteria may have restricted generalizability and impacted the observed outcomes negatively. The treatment duration was deemed insufficient to capture potential therapeutic benefits in this population, leading to an inappropriate representation of asenapine's efficacy profile in real-world clinical settings. 

Ultimately, these factors collectively contributed to the trial's failure, aligning with previously documented concerns regarding asenapine's efficacy in treating mood disorders. As a result, asenapine remains unproven as an effective treatment for bipolar depression in the context of this trial.
