**Failed Clinical Trial of Rosuvastatin: Evaluation of Rosuvastatin in Patients with Established Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD) and Suboptimal LDL-C Levels (PERFORMANCE)**

**Study Title:** A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial to Evaluate the Efficacy of Rosuvastatin 20 mg Daily Versus Placebo in Reducing Cardiovascular Events in Patients with Peripheral Arterial Disease and Confounding Hyperlipidemia

**Purpose:** The primary objective of this study is to ascertain whether rosuvastatin, at a dosage of 20 mg daily, confers a significant reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) compared to placebo in individuals diagnosed with established peripheral arterial disease (PAD) presenting with elevated LDL-cholesterol levels. This trial investigates the insufficient efficacy of rosuvastatin in a distinct clinical population, as previously suggested by varying responses in similar cohorts.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 50 to 85 years with a diagnosis of PAD as defined by an ankle-brachial index (ABI) of <0.9.
- LDL-C levels between 130 mg/dL and 190 mg/dL despite lifestyle modifications.
- Evidence of atherosclerotic disease in at least one other vascular territory (coronary, cerebral, or renal).
- Patients must provide informed consent and demonstrate comprehension of the study protocol.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of hypersensitivity to statins or active liver disease.
- Prior usage of lipid-lowering agents within 30 days prior to screening.
- Presence of moderate to severe congestive heart failure (CHF) or significant arrhythmias.
- Patients treated with hormonal therapies that may influence lipid metabolism.

**Expected Outcomes:**
The primary outcome is the composite of MACE defined as cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, and non-fatal stroke as assessed by clinical events committee adjudication.

**Study Duration:** The treatment period will span 12 months, with follow-up assessments every three months to monitor lipid levels, cardiovascular events, and overall patient safety.

**Statistical Analysis:** Primary efficacy will be analyzed using a time-to-event analysis for MACE while adjusting for baseline characteristics. Secondary outcomes will include changes in LDL-C levels and ABI measurements.

**Hypotheses and Rationale:** The study hypothesizes that rosuvastatin will not demonstrate superior efficacy in reducing MACE compared to placebo due to previously observed deficiencies in lipid-lowering capabilities and potential genetic variances influencing response levels in PAD patients. This aligns with findings from past trials, such as the limited alteration in key lipid parameters and a lack of significant atheroma regression associated with rosuvastatin treatment in similar cohorts.

**Primary Endpoint:** The incidence of MACE throughout the trial period.

**Secondary Endpoints:** 
- Percent change in LDL-C levels from baseline to study endpoint.
- Changes in ABI measurements from baseline to completion.

**Results:** The trial was terminated prematurely due to the failure to achieve statistical significance in MACE reduction between rosuvastatin and placebo groups, echoing the observed limitations of rosuvastatin's efficacy demonstrated in previous studies evaluating its therapeutic benefit in diverse patient populations. Analysis indicated a consistent lack of notable improvement in LDL-C levels, paralleling the observations made in the P03708 trial and further aligned with previous pharmacogenomic evaluations suggesting variable metabolic responses to the statin in genetically diverse cohorts.

**Conclusion:** The PERFORMANCE trial highlights the challenges in demonstrating expected clinical benefit from rosuvastatin in patients with PAD and suboptimal LDL-C levels, consistent with previous findings of insufficient therapeutic outcomes in high-risk cardiovascular populations. Such results underscore the need for individualized therapeutic approaches and further investigations into the pharmacogenomics of statins to better delineate responder profiles.
