**Successful Clinical Trial of Azacitidine: A Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Azacitidine in Combination with Nivolumab for Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)**

**RATIONALE:** Azacitidine, a hypomethylating agent, has demonstrated efficacy in myelodysplastic syndromes and acute myeloid leukemia through the demethylation of oncogenes, ushering in differentiation and apoptosis of malignant cells. Nivolumab, a monoclonal antibody that inhibits the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), enhances the immune response against cancer cells. The proposed combination is hypothesized to capitalize on these synergistic mechanisms, collectively improving therapeutic outcomes in patients with relapsed or refractory AML who have exhibited resistance to standard therapies.

**PURPOSE:** This Phase II trial aims to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and optimal dosing regimen of azacitidine in combination with nivolumab in patients with relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia.

**OBJECTIVES:**  
**Primary:** Assess overall response rate (ORR) as defined by complete remission (CR) or complete remission with incomplete blood count recovery (CRi) according to the International Working Group (IWG) criteria.  
**Secondary:**  
- Evaluate progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in treated patients.  
- Monitor safety and tolerability profiles, reporting dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) and adverse events (AEs).  
- Assess changes in immune response biomarkers, including PD-L1 expression and cytotoxic T cell profiles pre- and post-treatment.

**OUTLINE:** This is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm Phase II study. Patients receiving azacitidine at a fixed dose of 75 mg/m² subcutaneously on days 1-7 will concurrently receive intravenous nivolumab at 3 mg/kg every 14 days, repeating every 28 days for up to 6 cycles unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. 

**DISEASE CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Diagnosis of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) according to the World Health Organization classification.  
- Patients must be relapsed or refractory to prior cytotoxic chemotherapy or targeted therapies.  
- No evidence of active central nervous system involvement.

**PATIENT CHARACTERISTICS:**  
- Adult males and females, ages ≥ 18 years.  
- An ECOG performance status of 0-2.  
- Adequate hematologic function: ANC ≥ 1,000/mm³, platelet count ≥ 50,000/mm³, hemoglobin ≥ 8 g/dL.  
- No serious or uncontrolled medical conditions that could compromise patient safety.

**EXCLUSION CRITERIA:**  
- Known hypersensitivity to azacitidine or nivolumab.  
- Pregnant or lactating females.  
- Active autoimmune disease or history of organ transplant.  
- Concurrent use of immunosuppressive therapy outside of study parameters.

**RESULTS:** This study is anticipated to provide pivotal data on the role of azacitidine in enhancing the efficacy of PD-1 inhibition in AML, thereby addressing the critical need for treatment alternatives in this challenging patient population. 

**DATA ANALYSIS:** Response rates will be analyzed using the binomial test, and survival outcomes will be evaluated utilizing Kaplan-Meier methodology with log-rank tests employed for group comparisons. Safety analysis will include descriptive statistics of AEs and laboratory abnormalities.

**CONCLUSION:** This Phase II trial aims to elucidate the therapeutic potential of azacitidine when combined with nivolumab, focusing on establishing a treatment paradigm that leverages dual mechanisms of action to improve response rates and survival outcomes in patients suffering from relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia. Through rigorous patient selection and monitoring protocols, the study strives to confirm the safety and efficacy of this innovative combination strategy, potentially setting a new standard in AML management.
