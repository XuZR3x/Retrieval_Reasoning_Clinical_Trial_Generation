<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>Evaluation of the Efficacy and Safety of Rosuvastatin in Combination with Omega-3 Fatty Acids for the Management of Hyperlipidemia in Adults: The ROSA-OMEGA Study</Title>
    <Phase>Phase III</Phase>
    <Design>
        <Type>Multi-center</Type>
        <Method>Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled</Method>
        <Comparators>Rosuvastatin combined with Omega-3 fatty acids versus Rosuvastatin with placebo</Comparators>
    </Design>
    
    <Objective>
        <Primary>To assess the efficacy of rosuvastatin in conjunction with omega-3 fatty acids in reducing LDL cholesterol levels and other cardiovascular risk markers in adults with hyperlipidemia.</Primary>
    </Objective>
    
    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Adults aged 30 to 75 years</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Documented diagnosis of hyperlipidemia based on current clinical guidelines (NCEP ATP III)</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients with baseline LDL cholesterol ≥ 3.0 mmol/L (116 mg/dL) and triglyceride levels ≤ 400 mg/dL</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Willing and able to provide written informed consent</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Agreed to adhere to a low-fat diet throughout the study</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>
    
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>History of myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, or hypersensitivity to statins or omega-3 fatty acids</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Significant hepatic impairment (AST or ALT > 2 times ULN)</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Severe renal insufficiency (creatinine ≥ 1.5 mg/dL)</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Active gastrointestinal diseases that may interfere with drug absorption</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Current use of other lipid-lowering therapies or participation in another clinical trial within the last 30 days</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Pregnancy or lactation without adequate contraceptive measures</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    
    <Endpoints>
        <PrimaryEndpoint>Percent change in LDL cholesterol from baseline at 12 weeks</PrimaryEndpoint>
        <SecondaryEndpoint>Changes in triglycerides, HDL cholesterol, and inflammatory biomarkers (hs-CRP and IL-6) at 12 weeks</SecondaryEndpoint>
    </Endpoints>
    
    <Rationale>
        <Reason>1. The clearly delineated inclusion criteria ensured the recruitment of a well-defined patient population, enhancing the internal validity of the findings.</Reason>
        <Reason>2. The trial's randomized, double-blinded design minimized the risk of bias and maximized objectivity in the assessment of efficacy and safety in lipid lowering.</Reason>
        <Reason>3. Utilizing a multi-center approach provided a diverse patient demographic, thereby supporting the generalizability of results across various populations.</Reason>
        <Reason>4. The focus on dual endpoints allowed for an expansive evaluation of rosuvastatin's profile, extending insights into the potential benefits of combination therapy with omega-3 fatty acids.</Reason>
        <Reason>5. The combination approach with omega-3 fatty acids was hypothesized to synergistically augment lipid-lowering effects, providing evidence of enhanced therapeutic strategies for hyperlipidemia management.</Reason>
    </Rationale>
    
    <ExpectedOutcomes>
        <Outcome>It is anticipated that patients receiving the combination will show a statistically significant greater reduction in LDL cholesterol levels compared to those receiving rosuvastatin alone.</Outcome>
        <Outcome>Furthermore, improvements in secondary lipid parameters and inflammatory markers are expected to substantiate the efficacy of the combination therapy.</Outcome>
    </ExpectedOutcomes>
    
    <Conclusion>
        <Statement>The ROSA-OMEGA Study aims to establish a clearer role for rosuvastatin and omega-3 fatty acids in hyperlipidemia management, potentially leading to enhanced guidelines on the multifaceted approach to cardiovascular disease prevention.</Statement>
    </Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
