**Title:** A Phase 2 Study of Ixazomib in Combination With Pembrolizumab in Patients With Relapsed/Refractory Multiple Myeloma 

**Study Identifier:** NCTXXXXXXX

**Study Status:** Completed

**Purpose:**
This multi-center, open-label Phase 2 study aimed to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of ixazomib in combination with pembrolizumab in subjects with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma. The integration of ixazomib—a second-generation proteasome inhibitor—with pembrolizumab—an anti-PD-1 therapy—was hypothesized to produce enhanced anti-myeloma activity due to their distinct mechanisms of action.

**Study Design:**
The investigation was conducted in two sequential phases: a safety assessment (Phase 1) and an efficacy evaluation (Phase 2). Phase 1 elucidated dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) and established the recommended Phase 2 dose (RP2D) for the combination therapy. Phase 2 assessed overall response rates (ORRs) and progression-free survival (PFS) in the context of safety profiles.

**Population:**
The study enrolled 100 adult patients diagnosed with relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, with a history of at least one prior line of therapy. Patients were assessed based on a stringent eligibility criterion which ensured a well-defined cohort, enabling a robust evaluation of ixazomib's therapeutic impact amid confounding variables that could affect treatment outcomes.

**Treatment Protocol:**
Participants received ixazomib at the identified RP2D of 4 mg orally on Days 1, 8, and 15 of a 28-day cycle in conjunction with pembrolizumab administered at 200 mg intravenously every three weeks. The treatment was continued until disease progression or intolerable toxicity occurred. Comprehensive safety assessments were conducted through regular monitoring, encompassing laboratory evaluations and clinical status updates.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Male or female participants aged 18 years or older with a diagnosis of multiple myeloma.
2. Patients must have measurable disease evidenced by serum or urine M-protein and must have received at least one prior line of therapy.
3. ECOG performance status of 0-2 with adequate organ function, including baseline laboratory values meeting inclusion thresholds.
4. Voluntary written informed consent obtained prior to the initiation of any trial-related procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Patients with a history of severe allergic reactions or hypersensitivity to ixazomib or pembrolizumab.
2. Prior exposure to immunotherapy targeting the PD-1 or PD-L1 pathway.
3. Presence of central nervous system involvement or other secondary malignancies that could compromise patient eligibility.
4. Conditions that could interfere with the administration or absorption of the study drugs, including but not limited to gastrointestinal abnormalities.

**End Points:**
The primary endpoint was overall response rate (ORR), defined according to the International Myeloma Working Group criteria. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS) and safety assessments as measured by treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) recorded throughout the study period.

**Results:**
A total of 100 participants were enrolled in the study, with a median follow-up of 12 months. The combination of ixazomib and pembrolizumab demonstrated a promising ORR of 65%, surpassing historical controls for relapsed and refractory multiple myeloma. The investigational regimen was also well tolerated, with manageable toxicities primarily classified as mild to moderate, including fatigue, nausea, and transient hematological abnormalities. 

Impressively, comprehensive pharmacokinetic data suggest favorable drug interactions and tolerated dosing regimens across diverse patient profiles, including those with varying renal function. This population-specific evaluation highlighted the potential for tailored dosing strategies, optimizing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing risks for adverse reactions.

**Conclusion:**
The study concluded that the ixazomib and pembrolizumab combination exhibits a favorable safety profile and significant antitumor activity in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma populations. These findings warrant further exploration in larger, pivotal trials, evaluating long-term outcomes. The structured multi-phase approach employed to gauge both safety and efficacy was deemed instrumental in the successful development of this therapeutic strategy.

**Ethical Considerations:**
The study was conducted in compliance with Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines and regulatory requirements. The Institutional Review Board (IRB) at each participating site approved the study protocol and the informed consent process prior to patient enrollment.
