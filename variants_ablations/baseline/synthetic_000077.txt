**Title:** A Phase 2 Study of Alisertib (MLN8237) in Patients with Advanced Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) Refractory to Standard Therapies

**Objective:**  
This multicenter, open-label trial aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of alisertib, an Aurora A kinase inhibitor, in adult patients diagnosed with advanced acute myeloid leukemia (AML) who have demonstrated refractory disease following standard therapies. By leveraging alisertib's targeted mechanism of action and robust study design, this trial investigates its potential to reinvigorate treatment responses in a population that has limited therapeutic options. 

**Background:**  
Acute myeloid leukemia remains a challenging condition, with patients often exhibiting resistance to conventional treatments. Alisertib's selective inhibition of Aurora A kinase may intervene in aberrant cellular signaling pathways that contribute to uncontrolled proliferation in AML. Prior studies have shown promising pharmacodynamic responses in hematologic malignancies, highlighting the need for a dedicated investigation in this specific population.

**Primary Objective:**  
I. To assess the safety profile of alisertib in patients diagnosed with refractory acute myeloid leukemia.

**Secondary Objectives:**  
I. To evaluate the overall response rate in terms of complete remission (CR) and complete remission with incomplete blood count recovery (CRi).  
II. To determine the effect of alisertib on measurable residual disease (MRD).  
III. To analyze progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).  
IV. To document adverse events using the NCI's CTCAE guidelines.

**Study Design:**  
This Phase 2 trial will involve a cohort of approximately 100 participants, enabling a diverse patient representation through multicenter collaboration. Patients will receive alisertib orally, at a starting dose of 40 mg twice daily for 7 days, followed by a 14-day rest period (28-day cycle). Response assessments will be performed every cycle, and treatment will continue until disease progression or intolerable toxicity occurs.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
1. Adult patients (≥18 years) with a confirmed diagnosis of advanced AML (refractory, relapsed, or untreated ineligible for standard therapy).
2. Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
3. Adequate hepatic (AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN) and renal function (creatinine ≤ 1.5 x ULN or creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min).
4. Written informed consent obtained from all participants.

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
1. Patients with ongoing malignancies other than AML except for completely resected carcinomas.  
2. Treatment with any investigational agents or therapies within the preceding 4 weeks prior to enrollment.  
3. Known history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to alisertib or its components.  
4. Presence of significant uncontrolled comorbidities that would compromise patient safety.

**Adverse Event Monitoring:**  
Adverse events and serious adverse events will be evaluated according to NCI’s CTCAE v5.0 criteria. A comprehensive toxicology profile will be obtained at baseline, during treatment, and post-treatment to correlate adverse outcomes with alisertib administration.

**Pharmacodynamic Evaluation:**  
Real-time blood and bone marrow assessments will be utilized to evaluate pharmacodynamic changes associated with alisertib treatment. This will include the exploration of potential biomarkers predictive of response which could inform future personalized therapeutic strategies.

**Conclusion:**  
In light of its targeted mechanism of action and the successful outcomes noted in related malignancies, this trial aims to provide critical insights into the efficacy of alisertib in refractory acute myeloid leukemia. The integration of detailed pharmacodynamic monitoring along with rigorous safety evaluation treks towards establishing alisertib as a novel therapeutic option for this challenging cohort, with implications for future treatment paradigms.

**Anticipated Outcomes and Follow-up:**  
Patients will be followed post-treatment at 30-day intervals for a total of 6 months, with additional follow-up to monitor long-term outcomes and potential late-onset adverse effects.   

This study promises to expand therapeutic options for patients suffering from advanced AML by potentially integrating a targeted approach that successfully addresses the underlying pathophysiological disruptions in this malignancy.
