<FailedClinicalTrial>
    <Title>A Phase II Study Evaluating Sirolimus for the Treatment of Chronic Rhinosinusitis with Nasal Polyps</Title>
    <Objective>
        The primary objective of this open-label, multi-center trial is to assess the efficacy and safety of sirolimus in patients diagnosed with chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP), particularly focusing on its immunomodulatory properties that may ameliorate the inflammatory responses associated with this condition.
    </Objective>
    <Background>
        Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps is a prevalent condition characterized by persistent inflammation of the sinonasal mucosa and the presence of bilateral nasal polyps. Current management strategies, including corticosteroids and surgery, provide limited long-term relief and are often associated with substantial adverse effects. Sirolimus, an immunosuppressive agent with antifibrotic properties, has shown promise in preliminary studies for targeting the underlying mechanisms of tissue inflammation and remodeling seen in CRSwNP. Nevertheless, concerns regarding its pharmacokinetic variability, potential adverse effects, and limited additive efficacy in isolation warrant further investigation.
    </Background>
    <StudyDesign>
        This study will enroll 150 adult participants with moderate to severe CRSwNP inadequately controlled by standard treatments. Participants will be randomized to receive either sirolimus (2 mg/day) or placebo for a duration of 12 weeks. Clinical assessments will include a modified Lund-Mackay score for sinonasal symptoms, nasal polyp grading, and adverse event reporting.
    </StudyDesign>
    <InclusionCriteria>
        - Adults aged 18-65 years.
        - Diagnosis of moderate to severe chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps according to the EPOS guidelines.
        - History of inadequate response to conventional treatment (nasal corticosteroids or prior sinus surgery).
        - Willingness to comply with study procedures and provide informed consent.
    </InclusionCriteria>
    <ExclusionCriteria>
        - History of malignancy or active infection.
        - Known hypersensitivity to sirolimus or any of its components.
        - Significant renal dysfunction (eGFR < 60 mL/min).
        - Prior treatment with immunosuppressive agents within the last 6 months.
        - Current use of medications that may interact substantially with sirolimus, such as strong CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers.
        - Ongoing systemic inflammatory conditions that may confound results.
    </ExclusionCriteria>
    <PrimaryOutcomeMeasures>
        - Change in total nasal symptom score from baseline to Week 12.
        - Change in the total Lund-Mackay score from baseline to Week 12.
    </PrimaryOutcomeMeasures>
    <SecondaryOutcomeMeasures>
        - Incidence of adverse events, including but not limited to hyperlipidemia, delayed wound healing, or renal toxicity.
        - Assessed patient-reported outcomes on quality of life through the SNOT-22 questionnaire.
    </SecondaryOutcomeMeasures>
    <Results>
        Initial data analysis revealed that sirolimus treatment did not result in statistically significant improvements in symptom scores compared to placebo (p=0.43). Furthermore, adverse effects such as altered lipid profiles and elevated serum creatinine levels were documented at alarming rates, leading to early discontinuation of therapy in numerous participants. The highly heterogeneous population contributed to confounding factors that further complicated outcome assessments, ultimately yielding inconclusive results regarding sirolimus's efficacy in managing CRSwNP.
    </Results>
    <Conclusion>
        The trial was terminated due to insufficient evidence supporting sirolimus as an effective treatment modality for chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, compounded by significant concerns regarding safety and adverse events. A multifaceted therapeutic strategy may be essential to address the complexities of this condition effectively, highlighting the limitations of sirolimus as a monotherapy.
    </Conclusion>
</FailedClinicalTrial>
