**Failed Clinical Trial of Oxaliplatin: Phase II Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Oxaliplatin in Combination with Gemcitabine and Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Urothelial Carcinoma**

**Title:** A Phase II Study to Investigate the Efficacy and Safety of Oxaliplatin in Combination with Gemcitabine and Pembrolizumab in Patients with Advanced Urothelial Carcinoma Previously Treated with Platinum-Based Chemotherapy

**Study Objective:** The primary objective of this study is to determine the objective response rate (ORR) as defined by the RECIST v1.1 criteria in patients with advanced urothelial carcinoma treated with an oxaliplatin, gemcitabine, and pembrolizumab regimen. Secondary objectives include progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and assessment of treatment-related adverse events.

**Background:** Prior investigative efforts have indicated a potential benefit of oxaliplatin in the treatment regimen for various solid tumors. However, resistance mechanisms inherent to the biological heterogeneity of advanced urothelial carcinoma and the lack of a robust biomarker-driven approach have provided significant challenges. This study seeks to evaluate whether the inclusion of oxaliplatin in conjunction with established agents can elicit a meaningful therapeutic effect.

**Treatment Plan:** This is an open-label, non-randomized Phase II trial intended for patients diagnosed with advanced urothelial carcinoma demonstrating progressive disease following prior treatment with platinum-based chemotherapy. Patients will receive up to 6 cycles of infusions consisting of oxaliplatin (85 mg/m²) and gemcitabine (1000 mg/m²) on days 1 and 8, coupled with pembrolizumab (200 mg) administered every 3 weeks.

**Expected Number of Patients:** The study anticipates enrolling 60 patients to secure 51 evaluable participants assessing the efficacy endpoints based on sample size calculations.

**Eligibility Criteria:**
*Inclusion Criteria:*
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed advanced urothelial carcinoma.
- Previously treated with one or more lines of platinum-based chemotherapy.
- Measurable disease as per RECIST v1.1 criteria.
- ECOG Performance Status of 0-2.
- Adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function.
  
*Exclusion Criteria:*
- Evidence of central nervous system metastases.
- Severe concurrent uncontrolled medical conditions.
- Active autoimmune diseases requiring systemic therapy.
- Known hypersensitivity to oxaliplatin or any of the components in the study regimen.

**Primary Endpoint:**
- Objective Response Rate (ORR) defined as the percentage of patients exhibiting complete or partial response to treatment per RECIST v1.1 criteria.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Progression-Free Survival (PFS) defined as the time from initiation of treatment to disease progression or death.
- Overall Survival (OS) as the time from start of treatment to death from any cause.
- Assessment of treatment tolerability and adverse events based on NCI CTCAE v5.0 criteria.

**Rationale for Failure:** 
1. **Suboptimal Pharmacological Synergy:** The combination of oxaliplatin with gemcitabine and pembrolizumab was anticipated to enhance therapeutic efficacy; however, inherent resistance pathways in urothelial carcinoma may have impeded synergistic effects.
  
2. **Inadequate Patient Population Selection:** The patient cohort exhibited substantial prior treatment exposure leading to cumulative toxicities and possibly altering tumor biology, which may have contributed to the reduced response rates noted throughout the trial.

3. **Disease Heterogeneity:** The biological complexity of urothelial carcinoma, characterized by diverse genetic alterations and pathways, may have further complicated the ability to predict responders, ultimately leading to suboptimal clinical outcomes.

4. **Insufficient Dosing Regimen:** The dosing regimen of oxaliplatin in combination with gemcitabine and pembrolizumab lacked optimization. Subtherapeutic exposure may have diminished the potential for achieving significant tumor response.

5. **Limited Biomarker-Driven Approach:** The study did not incorporate robust biomarker stratification to identify potential responders and non-responders effectively, thereby diminishing the overall efficacy of the combined treatment approach. 

Overall, the trial was folded due to an absence of notable therapeutic benefit observed in the enrolled patients, underscoring the challenges faced in treating advanced urothelial carcinoma with oxaliplatin-containing regimens.
