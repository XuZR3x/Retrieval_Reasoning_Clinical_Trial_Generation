<ClinicalTrial>
   <Title>Assessing the Efficacy of Simvastatin on Neuropathic Pain Management in Patients with Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy: A Phase 2 Study</Title>
   <Purpose>The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness of simvastatin in reducing neuropathic pain intensity and improving quality of life in individuals diagnosed with diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN). Pre-clinical studies suggest that simvastatin not only has well-documented cholesterol-lowering effects but also exerts neuroprotective properties through its anti-inflammatory, anti-apoptotic, and endothelial protective mechanisms. In DPN, which is characterized by ongoing nerve damage and inflammation, simvastatin may address underlying pathophysiological processes, including impaired endothelial function and neuroinflammation.</Purpose>
   <Background>Diabetic peripheral neuropathy is one of the most common complications of diabetes mellitus, significantly affecting patient quality of life through debilitating pain and sensory loss. The treatment options remain limited, necessitating the exploration of novel pharmacological agents. Simvastatin's potential benefits beyond lipid-lowering properties hypothesized in various studies—including improved nerve regeneration through enhanced nitric oxide production and decreased inflammatory mediators—prompted this investigation.</Background>
   <StudyDesign>This is a multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial involving 200 patients with a clinical diagnosis of DPN who report neuropathic pain as measured by the Neuropathic Pain Scale (NPS). Participants will be randomized to receive either simvastatin (80 mg daily) or placebo for a duration of 12 weeks. Concomitant medications for diabetes management will be held constant and recorded throughout the trial.</StudyDesign>
   <InclusionCriteria>
      <Criterion>Adults aged 18-75 years</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Diagnosis of Type 1 or Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Clinical diagnosis of DPN with NPS score ≥ 4</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Stable medication regimen for at least 3 months prior to enrollment</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Informed consent provided</Criterion>
   </InclusionCriteria>
   <ExclusionCriteria>
      <Criterion>History of statin intolerance or adverse reactions to statins</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Active liver disease or significant elevated liver enzymes (ASAT, ALAT)</Criterion>
      <Criterion>History of malignant neoplasms in the previous 5 years</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Use of immunosuppressive medications or other investigational agents within the last 30 days</Criterion>
      <Criterion>Uncontrolled hypertension or chronic pain conditions unrelated to DPN</Criterion>
   </ExclusionCriteria>
   <Endpoints>
      <PrimaryEndpoint>Change in Neuropathic Pain Scale scores from baseline to 12 weeks</PrimaryEndpoint>
      <SecondaryEndpoints>
         <SecondaryEndpoint>Change in Quality of Life (QoL) assessed by the EQ-5D scale</SecondaryEndpoint>
         <SecondaryEndpoint>Assessment of nerve conduction velocity and sensory/reflux testing</SecondaryEndpoint>
         <SecondaryEndpoint>Evaluation of plasma inflammatory markers (e.g., IL-6, CRP) as potential correlatives to neuropathic pain and treatment efficacy</SecondaryEndpoint>
      </SecondaryEndpoints>
   </Endpoints>
   <Assessments>Patients will be assessed at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks post-randomization through clinical interviews, physical examinations, and collection of relevant laboratory and diagnostic imaging where necessary.</Assessments>
   <ExpectedOutcome>It is anticipated that simvastatin will demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in neuropathic pain intensity compared to placebo. Furthermore, improvements in quality of life measures and reductions in inflammation markers are expected to corroborate the hypotheses derived from its endothelial protection and anti-inflammatory properties.</ExpectedOutcome>
</ClinicalTrial>
