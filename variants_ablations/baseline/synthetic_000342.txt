**Failed Clinical Trial of Teriparatide: Evaluating Teriparatide for Enhanced Bone Regeneration in Osteoporotic Femoral Fractures: The TERI-FEM Trial**

**Summary:** This study investigates the efficacy of teriparatide for enhancing bone regeneration post-surgical repair of femoral fractures in patients diagnosed with osteoporosis. The trial is designed as a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled approach at multi-center sites including Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic Foundation, and Stanford University Medical Center. The primary aim is to assess whether teriparatide therapy improves radiographic healing and functional recovery in comparison to placebo over a 12-month period.

**Hypothesis:** We hypothesize that the administration of teriparatide will significantly enhance healing outcomes such as bone union rates and functional mobility in patients following surgical intervention for femoral fractures compared to those receiving placebo.

**I. Introduction:** Osteoporosis significantly contributes to an increased incidence of fractures, particularly in the femoral region, where the potential for delayed healing exists. While teriparatide has shown promise in improving bone mineral density, its effects on fracture healing require thorough investigation. Recent animal studies suggest a positive association between teriparatide and accelerated bone regeneration post-fracture; however, its translational efficacy in humans remains uncertain.

**II. Objectives and Hypotheses:** The primary objective of this study is to determine the effect of teriparatide on bone union, as assessed by radiographic imaging and clinical outcomes in patients with osteoporosis undergoing femoral fracture repair. We aim to evaluate the following:

1. Time to radiographic union as determined by standardized X-ray evaluation.
2. Change in functional mobility assessed by the Timed Up and Go Test (TUG).
3. Bone mineral density changes measured at the femoral neck using DXA.

The hypotheses under consideration include:

1. The time to union will be reduced in the teriparatide group compared to the placebo group.
2. Significant improvement in functional mobility will be observed in the teriparatide group.
3. Bone mineral density at the femoral neck will be superior in patients receiving teriparatide.

**III. Statistical Analyses:** Statistical analyses will utilize an intention-to-treat approach. The primary outcome measure, time to radiographic union, will be analyzed using survival analysis methods with Kaplan-Meier estimators and log-rank tests performed to evaluate differences between treatment groups. Descriptive statistics will summarize baseline characteristics, while linear mixed models will be used to evaluate changes in mobility and bone density metrics. A p-value < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**IV. Sample Size Estimates:** Drawing from historical data on femoral fracture healing and anticipated effect sizes based on prior studies using teriparatide, this trial will require an estimated total of 120 participants (60 per group) to achieve an 80% power to detect differences in union rates at a significance level of 0.05.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Community-dwelling men and postmenopausal women aged 50 years and older with a diagnosis of osteoporosis (T-score ≤ -2.5).
2. Sustained a unilateral low-energy femoral fracture requiring surgical intervention.
3. Ability and willingness to provide informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of severe metabolic bone disease other than primary osteoporosis.
2. Recent treatment with any internal or external anabolic agents targeting bone metabolism in the past year.
3. Active malignancies affecting calcium homeostasis or metabolic bone diseases.
4. Maternal activity that would complicate bone recovery or teriparatide administration.

**V. Change From Baseline in Radiographic Healing**: Radiographic assessment will involve blind evaluation by two independent radiologists, focusing on evidence of bridging callus and cortical continuity at the fracture site. Time to achieving full radiographic union will be recorded in days post-surgery.

The study ultimately faced multiple challenges, leading to inconclusive outcomes. These included:

1. **Insufficient Mechanistic Understanding:** Predicated on a lack of clarity regarding the specific biological mechanisms of teriparatide on fracture healing in the target demographic, results were hindered. The relationship between teriparatide and specific healing endpoints was imprecisely defined, leading to an inability to establish a cause-effect correlation.

2. **Participant Heterogeneity:** Baseline variability among participants—including differing severities of osteoporosis and prior fracture history—complicated the data interpretation. Unexpected outcomes within subgroups obscured the overall efficacy of teriparatide.

3. **Inadequate Duration of Treatment:** The selected treatment period proved insufficient to fully evaluate the long-term benefits of teriparatide on the healing of femoral fractures. Healing in human subjects may extend beyond the 12-month evaluation period, diminishing observable treatment effects.

4. **Methodological Constraints:** Variability in radiographic imaging techniques resulted in disparate assessments of fracture healing. Variability in readouts from different imaging modalities inhibited an accurate appraisal of treatment efficacy.

5. **Pharmacological Interactions:** The concurrent use of various osteoporosis therapies among participants created a potential for pharmacodynamic interferences, complicating the isolation of teriparatide's specific impact on fracture healing.

The findings failed to demonstrate significant beneficial outcomes attributed to teriparatide administration compared to placebo, thus concluding the TERI-FEM Trial as a failure in demonstrating its utility for enhancing fracture healing in osteoporotic patients.
