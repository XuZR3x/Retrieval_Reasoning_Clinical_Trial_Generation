**Failed Clinical Trial of Mifepristone: Evaluation of Mifepristone in Combination with Antiretroviral Therapy for Management of HIV-1 Infection**

**Study Title:** Evaluation of the Anti-HIV Efficacy and Safety of Mifepristone in Conjunction with Standard Antiretroviral Therapy in HIV-1 Infected Adults: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Phase II Study 

**Study Purpose:** This study was conducted to evaluate the anti-HIV efficacy and safety profile of mifepristone when administered in combination with standard antiretroviral therapy (ART) among adults diagnosed with HIV-1 infection. The hypothesis was that mifepristone could potentiate the effects of ART through its glucocorticoid antagonistic properties, potentially enhancing viral suppression in individuals with stable HIV disease.

**Study Duration:** The trial was designed to last for a duration of 16 weeks.

**Study Design:** Participants were randomly assigned to one of two arms for a 12-week intervention phase, followed by a 4-week follow-up period. Arm A received mifepristone combined with ART, while Arm B received a placebo alongside ART. The primary endpoint was defined as the change in HIV-1 viral load from baseline at week 12.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 to 65 years with confirmed HIV-1 infection.
- Baseline CD4 count ≥ 350 cells/mm³ and plasma HIV-1 RNA ≥ 5000 copies/mL.
- Participants must have been on stable ART regimen for at least 12 weeks prior to enrollment.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of severe adverse reactions to mifepristone or related compounds.
- The presence of endocrine disorders which could be exacerbated by glucocorticoid antagonism.
- Current engagement in other investigational drug studies or received any HIV-related interventions within 30 days prior to randomization.
- Any severe concurrent medical conditions that would impair compliance or safety during the trial.

**Trial Details:** Participants were closely monitored throughout the intervention phase with regular assessments including physical examinations, laboratory evaluations of metabolic parameters, and psychological screening to detect potential adverse effects associated with mifepristone treatment. Additionally, assessments for target engagement were performed to evaluate the pharmacodynamics of the therapy.

**Primary Outcome Measure:** The primary outcome measure was the proportion of participants achieving at least a 1 log reduction in HIV-1 RNA levels from baseline to week 12, compared between the mifepristone group and the placebo group.

**Results Summary:** 

The trial encountered significant challenges leading to its termination due to the following contributing factors:

1. **Inadequate Target Engagement**: Compelling evidence revealed that systemic plasma concentrations of mifepristone were consistently subtherapeutic, failing to reach adequate levels for effective engagement with CD4+ T cells, which underpinned its proposed mechanism of action against HIV replication.

2. **Immunomodulatory Effects**: Unexpectedly, adverse immunomodulation was observed in the mifepristone cohort, reported as significant fluctuations in CD4 counts and immune dysregulation, which led to an increased incidence of opportunistic infections and further complications.

3. **Suboptimal Duration of Treatment**: The 12-week duration proved insufficient in chronic HIV-1 infection, where the need for sustained viral suppression was unmet due to intermittent efficacy and lack of a therapeutic cumulative effect.

4. **Patient Population Heterogeneity**: The diverse clinical profiles of participants introduced a layer of variability that diluted the clinical efficacy signal, creating statistically insignificant outcomes between the intervention arms.

5. **Safety and Tolerability Concerns**: The treatment group reported higher instances of adverse events related to metabolic and psychological expressions, which contributed to non-compliance and withdrawal from the study, raising concerns about the overall tolerability of mifepristone.

**Conclusions:** This Phase II clinical trial of mifepristone in conjunction with standard ART for HIV-1 treatment was ultimately terminated due to failure to demonstrate adequate efficacy and safety. The findings underscore the complexities associated with mifepristone as a therapeutic intervention in virology and illuminate the critical importance of pharmacokinetic optimization and safety assessments in drug development processes. Further investigations should consider these variables to derive informative insights into potential evaluation of mifepristone in appropriately delineated populations and more rigorously evaluating drug-drug interactions in the context of ART.
