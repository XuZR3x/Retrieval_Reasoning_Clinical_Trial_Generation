**Failed Clinical Trial of Dapagliflozin: Efficacy of Dapagliflozin on Glycemic Control in Hospitalized Patients with Severe Sepsis and Pre-existing Type 2 Diabetes**

**Study Overview**:  
This 12-week, multi-centre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase IV study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of dapagliflozin (10 mg once daily) in hospitalized patients with severe sepsis and a history of Type 2 diabetes mellitus. The primary objective was to assess the effect of dapagliflozin on glycemic control as determined by the change in HbA1c from baseline at week 12. 

**Study Design**:  
- **Intervention**: Dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily versus matching placebo.  
- **Duration**: 12 weeks.  
- **Randomization**: Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio.

**Inclusion Criteria**:  
1. Provision of signed informed consent prior to any study-specific procedures.  
2. Adults aged 18 to 85 years with a confirmed diagnosis of Type 2 diabetes mellitus (HbA1c ≥ 6.5% and ≤ 10.0%).  
3. Hospitalized for severe sepsis, defined as meeting the Sepsis-3 criteria.  
4. On a stable regimen of diabetes management for at least three months prior to enrollment.  
5. Evidence of adequate venous access for trial procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria**:  
1. Recent history of severe cardiovascular events within three months (acute coronary syndrome, cardiac revascularization).  
2. Signs of acute kidney injury with an estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) <60 mL/min.  
3. Concurrent treatment with SGLT-2 inhibitors or initiation of insulin therapy within the last 30 days.  
4. Severe hepatic impairment, malignancy, or any condition affecting survival.  
5. Pregnant or lactating women, or women of childbearing potential not using effective contraceptive measures.

**Primary Endpoint**:  
- Change in HbA1c from baseline to week 12. 

**Secondary Endpoints**:  
- Incidence of severe hypoglycemia.
- Safety profile assessed through adverse events and laboratory parameters.

**Results**:  
The trial was terminated prematurely after unfavorable interim analysis indicated a lack of statistically significant improvements in glycemic control in the dapagliflozin cohort compared to the placebo. Additionally, the trial encountered significant issues consistent with the following identified reasons for failure:

1. **Patient Selection Bias**: The specific inclusion criteria limited the patient demographics, resulting in a trial population that was significantly different from the broader population of patients with Type 2 diabetes and severe sepsis. This bias raised concerns regarding the external validity of the findings.

2. **Insufficient Sample Size**: The study was ultimately underpowered to detect meaningful differences in glycemic outcomes due to a lower-than-expected enrollment rate related to stringent eligibility criteria. Consequently, the anticipated statistical significance was not achieved.

3. **Variability in Treatment Compliance**: Preliminary analysis indicated disparities in adherence to the prescribed medication regimen. Participants exhibited inconsistent compliance largely attributed to the clinical complexity introduced by severe sepsis, thereby potentially diminishing dapagliflozin's effectiveness.

4. **Potential Adverse Effects**: Participants reported a higher-than-expected incidence of urinary tract infections and dehydration, leading to treatment discontinuation for over 15% of individuals receiving dapagliflozin. Such adverse effects complicated evaluations of the drug's safety profile.

5. **Lack of Clinically Meaningful Endpoints**: The reliance on HbA1c as the primary endpoint raised questions about its clinical relevance in the context of acute illness. It was noted that alternative outcomes such as mortality rates, sepsis resolution, or overall functionality might provide more informative insights.

**Conclusion**:  
This clinical trial evaluating dapagliflozin in hospitalized patients with severe sepsis and Type 2 diabetes encountered significant methodological challenges. The study highlighted critical considerations regarding patient selection, compliance, and endpoint relevance that ultimately contributed to its premature termination and low incidence of clinically meaningful findings. Further investigations are warranted to explore optimal treatment regimens and patient populations for SGLT-2 inhibitors in acute care settings.
