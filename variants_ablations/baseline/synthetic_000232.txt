**Failed Clinical Trial of OSI-906: Phase 2 Study of OSI-906 in Combination With Chemotherapy in Patients With Metastatic Colorectal Cancer**

A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Phase 2 Study of OSI-906 in Combination With Standard Chemotherapy in Patients With Metastatic Colorectal Cancer Plagued by Treatment Resistance

This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial was designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the investigational drug OSI-906 in combination with standard chemotherapy (FOLFIRI regimen) in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) who have previously failed first-line treatments. The primary objective was to assess progression-free survival (PFS) in the overall population, while secondary objectives included overall survival (OS), overall response rate (ORR), and safety profiles associated with the OSI-906 and chemotherapy combination.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed metastatic colorectal cancer.
- Previously treated with one line of systemic therapy, including fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin, and anti-VEGF or anti-EGFR agents.
- Measurable disease according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v1.1.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-1.
- Adequate hematological, renal, and hepatic functions as determined by laboratory tests.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Concurrent symptomatic brain metastases or history of significant cardiovascular disease.
- Prior treatment with OSI-906 or agents targeting the insulin-like growth factor receptor (IGF-1R).
- Uncontrolled diabetes or significant gastrointestinal disorders affecting drug absorption.

**Patient Stratification:**
Patients were stratified based on prior treatment experience (sensitivity versus resistance to standard therapies) and KRAS mutation status to optimize treatment outcomes based on pharmacogenomic variations.

**Objectives:**
- Primary: To compare PFS rates among patients treated with OSI-906 and chemotherapy versus placebo and chemotherapy.
- Secondary: To evaluate the ORR and assess toxicity profiles across the treatment groups.

**Study Design:**
- A total of 300 patients were randomized 1:1 to receive either OSI-906 in combination with the FOLFIRI regimen or placebo with the same chemotherapy.
- Treatment was administered until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**Results:**
The trial was terminated prematurely due to multiple factors contributing to its failure:

1. **Suboptimal Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Observational data indicated that the combined pharmacologic effects of OSI-906 and the FOLFIRI regimen led to diminished therapeutic efficacy. Patients exhibited negligible tumor shrinkage, resulting in poor ORR and prolonged disease stability not meeting the pre-defined clinical benefit criterion.

2. **Insufficient Patient Stratification:** The stratification based on KRAS mutation status did not accurately predict patient response to treatment. Many patients classified as treatment-sensitive were found to experience rapid disease progression, indicating a misalignment between molecular classification and actual clinical outcomes.

3. **Toxicity Profile:** The combination therapy was associated with a high incidence of severe adverse events, including significant gastrointestinal toxicities (grade 3 diarrhea and neutropenia). As a result, a substantial number of patients discontinued the regimen due to intolerable side effects, adversely impacting the integrity of the trial results.

4. **Delayed Biomarker Analysis:** Retrieving and analyzing KRAS mutation status prior to randomization took longer than anticipated. Consequently, many patients experienced disease progression during the enrollment period, contributing to skewed results and making it impossible to definitively assess treatment efficacy.

5. **Compliance and Adherence Issues:** Patients faced challenges adhering to complex treatment protocols, compounded by stringent eligibility criteria. An observational audit revealed significant deviations from the protocol, which likely influenced variability in treatment exposure and distorted efficacy assessments.

Given these failures, the primary endpoint of PFS could not be met, nor could sufficient evidence be generated to support the clinical utility of OSI-906 when administered across the patient population with mCRC. This trial emphasizes the critical importance of alignment between pharmacodynamic efficacy, patient selection, and the management of treatment-related toxicities in clinical study designs. Completion of a detailed analysis of the trial data is warranted to glean insights for future investigations involving OSI-906 and other therapeutic combinations in oncological settings.
