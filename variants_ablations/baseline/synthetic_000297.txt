**Phase II Trial Evaluating Efficacy and Safety of BIBW 2992 in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) After Platinum-based Chemotherapy**

**Study Title:** A Multicenter, Open-label, Phase II Trial to Determine the Efficacy and Safety of BIBW 2992 in Patients with Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer Following Platinum-based Chemotherapy

**Background:** BIBW 2992 (Afatinib) has shown potential therapeutic efficacy when utilized in patients exhibiting advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), particularly those who have progressed after the administration of platinum-based chemotherapy regimens. This trial aims to elucidate the anti-tumor effectiveness and safety profile of BIBW 2992 as a monotherapy in this population.

**Objectives:**

* Primary Objective: To assess the objective response rate (ORR) according to RECIST criteria in patients with advanced NSCLC treated with BIBW 2992 after failure of platinum-based chemotherapy.
* Secondary Objectives: To evaluate progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and the safety profile of BIBW 2992, as well as pharmacokinetic parameters pertaining to drug absorption and distribution.

**Inclusion Criteria:** 

1. Male or female patients aged 18 years or older with histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced or metastatic NSCLC.
2. Documentation of disease progression following at least one line of platinum-based chemotherapy.
3. An Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
4. Life expectancy of at least three (3) months.
5. Written informed consent adhering to ICH-GCP guidelines.

**Exclusion Criteria:**

1. Patients with disease progression while on or within three months of discontinuing last chemotherapy.
2. Active or uncontrolled infectious disease.
3. History of any other malignancy within the past three years, except for certain skin cancers.
4. Uncontrolled cardiac conditions or significant comorbidities deemed incompatible with the protocol.
5. Pregnant or breastfeeding females, or individuals unwilling to use effective contraception methods.

**Treatment Regimen:** 
Patients will receive BIBW 2992 at a starting dose of 40 mg once daily in a continuous fashion until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs. Rigorous monitoring of safety measures and treatment-related adverse effects will be performed throughout the trial duration.

**Endpoints:** 

- Primary Endpoint: Objective response rate (ORR) measured by complete and partial response as defined by RECIST criteria.
- Secondary Endpoints: 
  - Progression-free survival (PFS) evaluated from the initiation of treatment with BIBW 2992 until the date of disease progression or death from any cause.
  - Overall survival (OS) analyzed from treatment start until death from any cause.
  - Safety and tolerability assessed using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) grading.

**Statistical Analysis:** 
The sample size is projected at 120 participants, with analysis employing a significance level of 0.05 to determine the efficacy of BIBW 2992. ORR will be analyzed using confidence intervals, while median PFS and OS will be calculated using Kaplan-Meier methods.

**Discussion:** 
Recognizing the challenges posed by treatment resistance, the combination of a rigorously designed study with clear eligibility criteria aims to mitigate variability related to patient demographics and tumor biology. The pharmacokinetic evaluations will enhance our understanding of BIBW 2992 in this setting, thereby refining dosage guidelines to optimize therapeutic outcomes and minimize adverse effects. By utilizing established endpoints, this trial strives to contribute to the growing body of evidence affirming the role of BIBW 2992 in improving outcomes for patients with advanced NSCLC following initial chemotherapy failure.

**Conclusion:** 
This Phase II clinical trial seeks to provide insight into the potential efficacy and safety of BIBW 2992 in patients with advanced NSCLC. By adhering to stringent eligibility criteria and conducting thorough safety assessments, this study is poised to elucidate the viability of BIBW 2992 as a significant therapeutic option in the landscape of NSCLC treatment.
