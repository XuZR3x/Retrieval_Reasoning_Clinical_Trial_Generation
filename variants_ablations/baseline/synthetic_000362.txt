**Failed Clinical Trial of Irinotecan: Phase II Study of Irinotecan in Combination with Pembrolizumab in Patients with Recurrent ISG15-Positive Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

**Study Title**: A Phase II Clinical Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Irinotecan in Combination with Pembrolizumab for Patients with Recurrent ISG15-Positive Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer

**Objective**: This clinical trial aimed to explore the therapeutic potential of irinotecan, in conjunction with pembrolizumab, in achieving improved clinical outcomes in patients with recurrent ISG15-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). The study hypothesized that the combination would enhance immune response and exhibit synergistic activity in this defined patient cohort. 

**Design**: This was a non-randomized, open-label trial involving a multi-center approach to recruit eligible participants within North America. Patients received irinotecan intravenously every two weeks alongside pembrolizumab administered on a three-week cycle.

**Primary Endpoint**: The primary endpoint of the study was to evaluate the objective response rate (ORR) based on RECIST criteria, with a secondary analysis of progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).

**Secondary Endpoints**: Secondary endpoints included measures of overall survival, time to disease progression, quality of life assessments, and safety/tolerability profiles of the treatment regimen.

**Inclusion Criteria**: 
1. Histologically or cytologically confirmed ISG15-positive recurrent NSCLC.
2. Age ≥ 18 years.
3. Documented progression following at least one line of prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease.
4. ECOG performance status ≤ 2.
5. Measurable disease per RECIST criteria.
6. Adequate hematologic and organ function as specified in protocol criteria. 

**Exclusion Criteria**: 
1. Known hypersensitivity to irinotecan or pembrolizumab.
2. Prior treatment with irinotecan within 6 months.
3. Active central nervous system metastases not treated or controlled.
4. Severe comorbid conditions that the investigator deems incompatible with study participation.
5. Patients receiving immune-modulating therapy not mentioned in the protocol.

**Results**: Despite the rationale for trial initiation based on emerging data suggesting defined ISG15 expression as a predictive biomarker for treatment response, the trial was ultimately unsuccessful due to several contributing factors:

1. **Limited Efficacy in Selected Patient Populations**: The study population exhibited minimal objective response rates, with only 10% of patients achieving significant tumor reduction. The limited efficacy in the ISG15-positive cohort raised concerns about the viability of irinotecan in this specific genetic context, which mirrors historical findings regarding the inadequacy of traditional cytotoxic approaches against biologically distinct malignancies.

2. **Inadequate Drug Combination Synergy**: Evaluations of treatment responses suggested a lack of therapeutic synergy between irinotecan and pembrolizumab. The anticipated reinforcement of immune-mediated tumor control via pembrolizumab failed to observe cumulative effects with the cytotoxic actions of irinotecan, thus compromising overall treatment efficacy.

3. **Adverse Toxicity Profiles**: The combination therapy resulted in significant treatment-related adverse effects, notably grade 3 or higher diarrhea and febrile neutropenia, leading to a dose-reduction in over 30% of patients and treatment discontinuation in others. The safety profile of the combination regimen overshadowed clinical benefit, raising concerns of the risk-to-benefit ratio.

4. **Variability in Patient Response and Tumor Heterogeneity**: Remarkable heterogeneity in tumor biology was observed within the study population, evidenced by the variable treatment response rates. Insights drawn from comprehensive biomarker analyses post-trial indicated the presence of compensatory oncogenic pathways that rendered a substantial number of participants resistant to both agents.

5. **Inadequate Biomarker Development and Monitoring**: The trial suffered from a lack of a robust stratification strategy in selecting participants based on comprehensive tumor profiling. The reliance on ISG15 positivity alone without additional molecular characterization potentially contributed to the high variability in clinical responses observed.

**Conclusion**: The Phase II study of irinotecan in combination with pembrolizumab for patients with recurrent ISG15-positive NSCLC failed to meet its primary endpoints due to limited efficacy and an unfavorable safety profile. Future explorations should consider more targeted approaches incorporating advanced genomic profiling to identify more homogeneous patient clusters likely to benefit from specific therapeutic interventions. Further studies may also explore novel agents or combination therapies to surmount observed limitations. Additionally, the development of a more sophisticated biomarker selection strategy is essential to underpin the identification of suitable candidates for investigational therapeutic regimens.
