**Failed Clinical Trial of Cabozantinib: Cabozantinib in Combination With Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors for Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**  
**A Phase II Study of Cabozantinib Administered With Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**  

**Background**:  
Cabozantinib, a multi-receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor, has demonstrated efficacy in several malignancies; however, its clinical utility in advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains inadequately defined. This Phase II trial aimed to evaluate the safety and therapeutic potential of cabozantinib in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors amongst patients with advanced NSCLC. Prior preclinical studies have suggested potential synergy between cabozantinib's anti-angiogenic activity and immune checkpoint modulation. However, limitations in targeting specific tumor biology, as evidenced in prior trials, remain a major concern.

**Objectives**:  
To assess the objective response rate (ORR) of cabozantinib in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors among patients with advanced NSCLC and to evaluate treatment-related adverse events.

**Eligibility Criteria**:  
Inclusion:  
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced NSCLC (stage IV)  
- Measurable disease per RECIST v1.1 criteria  
- Prior treatment with at least one platinum-based regimen  
- ECOG performance status of 0-1  
- Adequate organ function  

Exclusion:  
- Prior treatment with cabozantinib or other multi-kinase inhibitors  
- Active central nervous system metastases  
- Concurrent therapy with strong CYP3A4 inducers or immunosuppressants  
- History of severe adverse events from immune-related therapies  

**Study Design**:  
The study aimed to enroll 100 patients, initiating treatment with cabozantinib at 60 mg daily in combination with standard immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., pembrolizumab or nivolumab) over 28-day cycles. Patient safety, tolerability, and efficacy were monitored through imaging studies and clinical evaluations. 

**Outcome Measures**:  
- Primary: Overall response rate (ORR) defined as the percentage of patients achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as assessed by RECIST v1.1.  
- Secondary: Progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and frequency of treatment-related adverse effects.

**Results**:  
Unfortunately, the trial demonstrated significant deficiencies leading to its cessation. Notably:  
1. **Limited Efficacy Against Specific Tumor Types**: The study yielded an ORR of only 10%, considerably lower than preclinical predictions. The lack of significant response in the cohort likely reflects cabozantinib's inadequate efficacy against molecular subtypes prevalent in advanced NSCLC.  
   
2. **Patient Population Heterogeneity**: The inclusion of patients with diverse genetic backgrounds underscored the variability in treatment responses. The presence of various tumor mutations and resistance profiles may have contributed to impaired therapeutic efficacy, mirroring challenges observed in previous studies.

3. **Inadequate Duration of Treatment Exposure**: Participants frequently expressed insufficient treatment duration due to early drug discontinuation driven by adverse events; many were unable to complete the minimum treatment cycles necessary to properly evaluate drug efficacy.

4. **Tumor Microenvironment Interference**: Altered tumor microenvironments in NSCLC, particularly those associated with desmoplastic reactions and immune cell infiltration, were not sufficiently addressed by the combination treatment, which limited drug efficacy as a result of altered pharmacokinetics.

5. **Adverse Event-Driven Drug Discontinuation**: High instances of grade 3 and 4 adverse events, including severe gastrointestinal complications and hepatotoxicity, led to withdrawal of numerous participants. The resultant impact on patient adherence likely undermined the assessment of long-term outcomes.

**Conclusion**:  
Given the disappointing outcomes characterized by limited efficacy, patient discontinuation, and adverse events, the trial resulted in failure to establish any clinically beneficial effect of cabozantinib in the treatment of advanced NSCLC in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors. Future studies should consider these factors, focusing on more homogeneous populations and optimizing treatment duration to better elucidate cabozantinib’s role in NSCLC therapeutics.
