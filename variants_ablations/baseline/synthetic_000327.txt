**Successful Clinical Trial of Aprepitant: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study of Aprepitant in Combination with Palonosetron for the Prevention of Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting in Patients Undergoing Carboplatin and Paclitaxel Therapy**

**Rationale:** The implementation of aprepitant in conjunction with palonosetron and dexamethasone may provide an enhanced protective effect against chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV) in patients scheduled to receive carboplatin and paclitaxel, which are known to induce higher levels of emesis due to their highly emetogenic profiles. 

**Purpose:** This phase III trial aims to investigate the efficacy and safety of aprepitant combined with standard antiemetic therapy in preventing CINV among patients with ovarian cancer undergoing chemotherapy involving carboplatin and paclitaxel.

**Objectives:**
- **Primary Objective:** Evaluate the percentage of participants with a complete response (defined as no vomiting and no use of rescue medication) during the first chemotherapy cycle.
- **Secondary Objectives:** Assess nausea control using a Visual Analog Scale, time to first emetic episode, quality of life indices, and the incidence of adverse events.

**Outline:** This is a multi-center, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial. Eligible patients will receive aprepitant or placebo daily on days 1-3, alongside palonosetron administered intravenously on day 1, and dexamethasone administered on days 1-4. Complete response will be monitored over a 120-hour observation period post-chemotherapy.

**Projected Accrual:** A total of 300 patients will be enrolled across multiple sites, with an estimated enrollment period of 24 months. 

**Participants:**
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  1. Female patients aged ≥ 18 years old.
  2. Histologically confirmed diagnosis of ovarian cancer.
  3. Scheduled to receive carboplatin and paclitaxel as first-line therapy.
  4. ECOG performance status of 0-1.
  5. Life expectancy of at least 6 months.
  6. Naïve to emetogenic chemotherapy.
  7. Adequate organ function parameters (e.g., ANC > 1500/mm³, creatinine ≤ 1.5 x ULN).

- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  1. Concurrent use of any highly emetogenic agents outside the chemotherapy regimen.
  2. Previous CINV prophylaxis with NK-1 antagonists.
  3. Patients receiving radiation therapy to the abdominal or pelvic area within a month of trial enrollment.
  4. Known hypersensitivity to aprepitant, palonosetron, or dexamethasone.
  5. Active gastrointestinal obstruction or uncontrolled infections.

**Primary Endpoint:** The number of participants achieving a complete response will be assessed during the acute phase post-chemotherapy (Day 1 through Day 5).

**Study Duration:** The duration of the study is estimated to be 30 months from recruitment to final analysis.

**Statistical Considerations:** The sample size is powered to detect a 15% increase in complete response rates with an anticipated complete response rate of 25% in the placebo group, assuming a significance level of 0.05 with 80% power.

**Expected Outcomes:** The findings from this trial are anticipated to demonstrate a significant improvement in the prevention of CINV in patients treated with aprepitant compared to the placebo. Given the established efficacy of palonosetron and dexamethasone, the inclusion of aprepitant is hypothesized to provide enhanced emesis control through a synergistic effect on the neurokinin-1 receptor pathway, corroborating previous studies that indicate aprepitant's value in similar treatment contexts. 

By utilizing a multi-modal approach of combining aprepitant with palonosetron and dexamethasone, this trial aims to solidify aprepitant as a cornerstone of routine CINV prophylaxis in ovarian cancer treatment regimens, potentially influencing practice guidelines and improving patient outcomes in chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting management.
