<clinical_trial>
  <title>Impact Assessment of Topiramate on Chronic Tension-Type Headaches: A Randomized, Controlled, Open-Label Study</title>
  <objective>The aim of this study is to evaluate the long-term safety and efficacy of topiramate as a prophylactic treatment for chronic tension-type headaches in adults.</objective>
  <background>Chronic tension-type headaches represent a common disabling condition, significantly affecting patients' quality of life and productivity. Topiramate, an established anti-epileptic drug, has shown promise in preventing various types of headaches beyond its primary indications. This study is designed in alignment with current efficacy findings to rigorously assess the extent of benefit topiramate can impart for patients suffering from chronic tension-type headaches.</background>
  <study_design>
    <phase>4-Week Blinded Dose-Ranging Phase and 12-Week Open-Label Maintenance Phase</phase>
    <description>This is a multicenter, outpatient, open-label, randomized controlled trial. Following a 4-week blinded dose-ranging phase where patients will be titrated to a starting dose of 50 milligrams daily, participants will then enter a 12-week open-label maintenance phase allowing individualized dose adjustments, with a maximum of 300 milligrams daily.</description>
  </study_design>
  <inclusion_criteria>
    <criteria>- Adults diagnosed with chronic tension-type headache according to ICHD-3 criteria</criteria>
    <criteria>- Ability to provide informed consent and comply with study procedures</criteria>
    <criteria>- In general good health with any concurrent medications meeting protocol standards</criteria>
    <criteria>- If female, using medically acceptable birth control methods</criteria>
  </inclusion_criteria>
  <exclusion_criteria>
    <criteria>- Presence of more painful conditions that could confound outcome measurements</criteria>
    <criteria>- Current use of medications contraindicated in the study by prior protocol</criteria>
    <criteria>- Uncontrolled medical comorbidities or significant hepatic dysfunction</criteria>
    <criteria>- Pregnant or nursing women</criteria>
  </exclusion_criteria>
  <evaluation_methods>
    <methods>Participants will maintain daily headache diaries documenting the frequency, duration, and severity of tension-type headache episodes, in addition to reporting associated symptoms and use of analgesics.</methods>
    <methods>Assessment tools will include the Tension-Type Headache Disability Questionnaire (TTHDQ), Migraine-Specific Quality of Life Scale (MSQ), and Global Impression of Change (GIC) metrics for both physicians and patients.</methods>
    <methods>Systematic evaluation of adverse events, neurological examinations, vital signs, and laboratory tests will provide comprehensive safety data.</methods>
  </evaluation_methods>
  <data_collection>
    <data_point>Primary efficacy endpoint will be the reduction in headache frequency recorded in daily diaries over the maintenance phase compared with baseline.</data_point>
    <data_point>Secondary endpoints will consist of changes in disability scores and quality of life assessments throughout the study duration.</data_point>
  </data_collection>
  <hypothesis>The primary hypothesis is that topiramate will significantly reduce the frequency and severity of chronic tension-type headaches compared to baseline, demonstrating its effectiveness as a prophylactic agent.</hypothesis>
  <outcomes>
    <outcome>The study anticipates a marked decrease in the mean headache frequency by at least 50% in the participant population, supporting the evaluation of topiramate as a viable option for long-term management of chronic tension-type headaches.</outcome>
    <outcome>Improvement in quality of life metrics, as measured by validated scales, will further establish the psychosocial benefits of topiramate therapy.</outcome>
  </outcomes>
</clinical_trial>
