**Title:** Efficacy of Exparel in Reducing Postoperative Pain Following Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy: A Randomized Controlled Trial

**Background:** The utilization of liposomal bupivacaine (Exparel) is hypothesized to provide enhanced postoperative analgesia following laparoscopic cholecystectomy through its sustained-release formulation. This trial seeks to evaluate the efficacy of Exparel compared to standard bupivacaine in managing pain after laparoscopic gallbladder removal.

**Study Design:** A Phase 3, multi-center, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group study.

**Objectives:** To determine if the intraoperative injection of Exparel leads to superior postoperative pain relief and decreased opioid consumption compared to the conventional injection of standard bupivacaine.

**Methods:**
- **Participants:** A total of 150 patients aged 18-70 years scheduled for elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy at three participating hospitals.
  
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  - Patients aged 18-70 who are candidates for elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy.
  - ASA Physical Status I or II.
  
- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Patients with a history of chronic pain disorders or those on chronic opioid therapy.
  - BMI < 18.5 or > 35 kg/m².
  - ASA Physical Status III or IV.
  - Known allergic reactions to local anesthetics.
  - Pregnancy or lactation.
  
- **Intervention:**
  Patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either:
  - Group A: Intraoperative administration of 266 mg Exparel at the conclusion of the procedure.
  - Group B: Intraoperative administration of 20 ml standard bupivacaine (0.25% solution).

**Outcome Measures:** 
- **Primary Endpoint:** The primary outcome will be the assessment of postoperative pain, quantified using the Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) at 24, 48, and 72 hours post-surgery.
- **Secondary Endpoints:** 
  - Total opioid consumption during the first 72 hours postoperatively measured.
  - Functional recovery evaluated through the Postoperative Quality of Recovery Scale (PQRS).
  - Incidence of adverse events.

**Statistical Analysis:** Descriptive statistics will characterize the patient population. Between-group comparisons for the NRS pain scores will be conducted using a mixed-model analysis of variance (ANOVA) approach. Opioid consumption will be analyzed using Mann-Whitney U tests due to non-normality of distribution.

**Expected Results:** The hypothesis asserts that patients receiving Exparel will exhibit significantly lower NRS pain scores and reduced opioid consumption compared to standard bupivacaine, thus supporting the use of Exparel in enhancing multimodal analgesia protocols for laparoscopic surgical procedures.

**Conclusion:** This trial is designed to ensure a homogenous patient population by employing strict inclusion and exclusion criteria to minimize confounding variables. The specificity of the surgical intervention alongside validated assessment tools enhances the reliability of outcome measures. Given Exparel's prolonged analgesic effects and its potential to significantly lower postoperative opioid needs, the findings from this investigation could substantiate the incorporation of liposomal bupivacaine into routine surgical practices, promoting improved patient recovery and pain management protocols.
