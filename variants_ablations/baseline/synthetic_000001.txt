**Failed Clinical Trial of Ranolazine: Efficacy of Ranolazine in Patients with Stable Ischemic Heart Disease and Chronic Renal Impairment**

**Study Title:** The Impact of Ranolazine on Angina Control and Quality of Life in Patients with Chronic Stable Ischemic Heart Disease and Chronic Renal Impairment

**Study Design:** Multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.

**Objective:** This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of ranolazine in reducing symptoms of chronic stable angina and improving quality of life in patients with chronic ischemic heart disease and concomitant chronic renal impairment, defined as an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of less than 60 mL/min.

**Setting:** Multi-center involvement across several cardiovascular units in tertiary care hospitals.

**Background:** Ranolazine has demonstrated cardiovascular benefits through the inhibition of late sodium currents; however, it remains unclear whether these benefits extend to patients with significant renal impairment, a subgroup often excluded from clinical trials. The pharmacokinetics of ranolazine can be adversely affected in this population due to altered metabolic profiles and potential drug interactions with commonly prescribed medications, posing a substantial concern for consistent therapeutic efficacy. 

**Enrollment Criteria:** Target population included a total of 1,200 patients presenting with symptomatic chronic stable angina secondary to ischemic heart disease and chronic renal impairment. 

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Acute coronary syndrome or acute myocardial infarction within the last month,
- History of severe hepatic dysfunction,
- Significant electrolyte imbalances,
- Patients requiring renal dialysis,
- Patients on regimens including strong CYP3A inhibitors,
- Known history of sensitivity or allergic reactions to ranolazine.

**Intervention:** Eligible patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either 1000 mg of ranolazine twice daily or matching placebo over a 12-week treatment period. Randomization was accomplished utilizing a centralized randomization service to ensure allocation concealment.

**Primary Endpoint:** The primary outcome measured was the change in frequency and severity of anginal episodes from baseline to 12 weeks, as assessed by the Seattle Angina Questionnaire (SAQ). Secondary endpoints included the change in quality of life scores and levels of myocardial biomarkers (troponin and CK-MB).

**Assessments:**
- Screening Included: Comprehensive assessment of clinical history, baseline kidney function (eGFR), and current medication regimen.
- Baseline Procedures: Assessment of angina frequency, quality of life questionnaires, and subsequent recommendations for medical regimens to control ischemic symptoms.

**Follow-Up:** Participants were scheduled for follow-up visits at weeks 4, 8, and 12, during which assessments of angina and quality of life were conducted, along with evaluations for adverse events and changes in renal function.

**Results:** The anticipated benefits were not realized, with analyses revealing no statistically significant difference in angina frequency or quality of life scores between the ranolazine and placebo arms. Subgroup analyses indicated that the pharmacokinetics of ranolazine were indeed compromised in the renal population; systemic drug levels were highly variable due to interactions with common medications for hypertension and other cardiac conditions. 

**Reasons for Failure:**
1. **Target Population Misalignment:** The primary mechanism of action of ranolazine did not adequately address the complex pathophysiological mechanisms prevalent in a chronically compromised renal population, impairing its efficacy in this crucial subset.
  
2. **Enrollment Bias:** High enrollment exclusions related to comorbid renal conditions led to a participant population lacking the breadth of individuals that may have demonstrated a more pronounced therapeutic effect, significantly limiting generalizability.

3. **Altered Pharmacokinetics:** The effects of concomitant medications on the cytochrome P450 system resulted in variances in ranolazine drug levels, undermining its therapeutic action within this cohort.

4. **Dosing Regimen Inefficacy:** The dosing regimen employed may not have been curated to maximize therapeutic effects for patients with impaired renal clearance, potentially failing to achieve optimal pharmacodynamic effects during the critical phase of treatment initiation.

5. **Endpoint Assessment Methodology:** Reliance on subjective outcome measures such as the SAQ was problematic as psychological factors and expectations related to chronic illness may have introduced variability in patient-reported outcomes, thereby complicating interpretations of treatment effectiveness.

**Conclusion:** The clinical trial ultimately failed to meet its primary endpoint, indicating that while ranolazine may provide value in select populations, its efficacy is highly contingent upon comprehensive inclusion criteria and patient metabolic profiles. Future investigations must consider targeted strategies and patient-tailored approaches to equip clinicians with better therapeutic options for individuals suffering from chronic ischemic heart disease and concurrent renal impairment.
