**Failed Clinical Trial of OSI-906: Combination of OSI-906 and Radiotherapy in Patients with Advanced Soft Tissue Sarcomas**

**A Phase II Study of OSI-906 in Combination with Radiotherapy for Patients with Advanced Soft Tissue Sarcomas**

This clinical trial aimed to investigate the efficacy and safety of OSI-906, an IGF-1R inhibitor, when administered in conjunction with localized radiotherapy in patients diagnosed with advanced soft tissue sarcomas. As previous trials have indicated adverse outcomes with combinations involving OSI-906, this study was particularly focused on addressing the potential pharmacokinetic interactions between OSI-906 and radiotherapy that might impact treatment response.

**Study Design:**
This was a multicenter, open-label, single-arm Phase II study. Enrolled participants received OSI-906 in conjunction with standard radiotherapy to assess progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and overall response rate (ORR).

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years or older.
- Histologically confirmed diagnosis of advanced soft tissue sarcoma not amenable to curative surgery.
- Measurable disease following Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) 1.1 criteria.
- ECOG Performance Status of 0-1.
- Adequate hematological, renal, and hepatic function as evidenced by laboratory tests performed within two weeks prior to enrollment:
  - ANC ≥ 1,500/mm³
  - Platelet count ≥ 100,000/mm³
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x ULN
  - AST and ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN
- Patients must provide written informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with IGF-1R inhibitors.
- Severe cardiovascular conditions or poorly controlled hypertension.
- Significant gastrointestinal disorders affecting drug absorption.
- Concurrent malignancies, except for adequately treated non-melanoma skin cancers.
- Pregnant or lactating women.

**Treatment Regimen:**
Patients were administered OSI-906 at a fixed dose of 200 mg orally once daily for 28 days, combined with localized radiotherapy to the primary tumor site. Radiotherapy was initiated in parallel with OSI-906 administration, with a typically employed fractional dose tailored to maximize tumor response while minimizing collateral damage to adjacent tissues.

**Endpoints:**
- The primary endpoint was the PFS, defined as the time from the initiation of treatment until disease progression or death from any cause.
- Secondary endpoints included OS and ORR based on RECIST criteria.

**Results:**
The trial was prematurely halted due to disappointing therapeutic outcomes. Analysis revealed a PFS of only 2.1 months and an ORR of 20%, significantly lower than anticipated, indicating a lack of synergy between OSI-906 and radiotherapy. 

Reasons for trial failure were identified as follows:

1. **Inadequate Pharmacokinetic Interaction:** The co-administration of OSI-906 and radiotherapy resulted in unclear pharmacokinetic effects that potentially diminished the systemic availability of OSI-906, leading to subtherapeutic levels during critical treatment windows. 

2. **Pre-existing Resistance Mechanisms:** The enrolled cohort exhibited a higher prevalence of tumors with inherent resistance pathways that were unaddressed by the combined therapy, thus abrogating efficacy.

3. **Tumor Heterogeneity:** Variability in molecular alterations across this heterogeneous sarcomatous population appeared to neutralize the efficacy of OSI-906, resulting in disparate responses to concurrent treatment.

4. **Insufficient Follow-up Duration:** While the initial follow-up period was established for the first evaluation of tumor response, a notable number of patients presented late-onset toxicities that were not adequately documented, which could obscure long-term outcomes and complications.

5. **Stringent Eligibility Criteria:** The trial's selection process led to a narrow demographic scope that did not reflect the broader soft tissue sarcoma patient population, potentially resulting in a bias that confounded outcome interpretations.

Overall, the study underscored the intricate interplay of IGF-1R signaling within the context of radiotherapy for sarcomas and prompted a reevaluation of combination strategies moving forward.

**Conclusion:**
The Phase II study of OSI-906 combined with radiotherapy ultimately failed to exhibit significant clinical benefit in patients bearing advanced soft tissue sarcomas. This trial highlights several important considerations for future research directions, particularly focusing on targeting heterogeneous tumor behaviors and optimizing pharmacological interactions for enhanced therapeutic efficacy.
