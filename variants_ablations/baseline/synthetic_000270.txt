**Failed clinical trial of rasagiline: Evaluation of Rasagiline's Impact on Cognitive Decline in Early Parkinson's Disease Patients**

A Multicenter, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Assess the Efficacy of Rasagiline on Cognitive Decline in Patients with Early Parkinson’s Disease

This study was designed as a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial aimed at determining the effectiveness of rasagiline in preventing cognitive decline in patients diagnosed with early-stage idiopathic Parkinson's disease (PD). The primary objective was to evaluate cognitive function over a 12-month period, assessing whether rasagiline could exhibit beneficial effects on neuropsychological outcomes relative to placebo.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Males and females aged 50 to 75 years with a clinical diagnosis of idiopathic Parkinson's disease according to the UK Parkinson’s Disease Society Brain Bank criteria.
2. Hoehn and Yahr stage 1 or 2 at enrollment.
3. Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) defined by scores of 26-30 on the MoCA.
4. Participants must be stable on dopaminergic medications for at least 8 weeks prior to enrollment.
5. Clinically stable with no significant co-morbid conditions impacting cognitive function.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. History of significant vascular disease or stroke.
2. Presence of significant psychiatric disorders, including major depression (BDI score > 10).
3. Current use of any other MAO inhibitors, cholinesterase inhibitors, or cognition-enhancing medications.
4. Evidence of other neurodegenerative disorders.
5. Pre-existing dementia (MMSE score < 24).
6. Renal or hepatic dysfunction.

**Primary Endpoint:**
The primary endpoint assessed the change in the Alzheimer’s Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive Subscale (ADAS-Cog) from baseline to 12 months. Scores on the ADAS-Cog range from 0 to 70, with higher scores indicating worse cognitive performance.

**Results:**
Findings revealed no statistically significant difference in ADAS-Cog scores between the rasagiline group and placebo over the 12-month period (mean change from baseline: rasagiline +0.4 ± 4.2 vs. placebo +0.3 ± 4.0; p = 0.85). Furthermore, exploratory analyses indicated that treatment with rasagiline failed to produce a meaningful effect on secondary cognitive measures, including the Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test (RAVLT) and the Trail Making Test A & B.

**Discussion:**
Contributing factors to the trial's failure include: 1) **Ceiling Effects** - Participants exhibited only mild cognitive impairment, resulting in a limited capacity for cognitive gains; 2) **Homogeneous Cohort** - Similar baseline cognitive profiles across the population that may have masked potential treatment effects; 3) **Insufficient Efficacy** - Rasagiline did not provide a clinically relevant impact on cognitive function, confirming previous findings of lack of cognitive improvement in early Parkinson's disease. This is compounded by: 4) **Failure to Address Disease Modification** - The treatment focus on symptomatic management rather than disease-modifying capabilities likely hindered efficacy; 5) **Confounding Variables** - Presence of co-morbid conditions that were not adequately accounted for, complicating assessment of cognitive outcomes.

This trial serves as a critical reminder of the importance of pre-specifying endpoints, accounting for patient heterogeneity, and ensuring that intervention approaches are optimally designed to examine the nuanced progression of cognitive impairment in Parkinson's disease.

**Conclusion:**
The results of this trial indicate that rasagiline fails to demonstrate significant cognitive enhancement in early-stage Parkinson's disease patients. Future studies should consider broader inclusion criteria to assess the full spectrum of cognitive profiles and utilize longer-term follow-up periods to better capture potential treatment effects.
