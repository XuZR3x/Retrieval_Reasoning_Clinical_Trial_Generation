Failed clinical trial of interferon beta-1a: A Phase II Trial Evaluating the Efficacy and Safety of Interferon Beta-1a in Patients With Early-Stage Parkinson's Disease  

This was a 48-week, multicentric, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase II clinical trial conducted across multiple centers to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and clinical efficacy of subcutaneously administered interferon beta-1a (Rebif® 22 micrograms [mcg], three times weekly) in patients diagnosed with early-stage Parkinson's disease (PD).  

Parkinson's disease is characterized by progressive neurodegeneration, specifically the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra attributable to various neuroinflammatory mechanisms. Interferon beta-1a was hypothesized to modulate these inflammatory pathways and provide neuroprotective effects through the suppression of proinflammatory cytokines and the promotion of neurotrophic factors. However, the study encountered significant challenges resulting in its termination due to failure to meet predefined efficacy endpoints.  

OBJECTIVES - To evaluate the safety, tolerability, and clinical efficacy of interferon beta-1a (Rebif® 22 mcg, tiw) in patients with early-stage Parkinson's disease.  

In this study, patients were randomized into two groups: the treatment arm (n=30) received Rebif® 22 mcg tiw, while the placebo arm (n=30) received saline. The treatment period lasted for 24 weeks, followed by an extended follow-up period of 24 weeks post-treatment. Efficacy was determined based on changes in the Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS), specifically examining motor and non-motor symptoms from baseline through Week 48.  

On Day 1 of the treatment period, subjects received instruction on self-administration techniques and were monitored during the initial dosage under clinical supervision. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Adult patients aged between 45 and 75 years  
- Diagnosis of early-stage Parkinson's disease according to the United Kingdom Parkinson's Disease Society Brain Bank clinical criteria  
- UPDRS motor score between 10 and 30 at screening  
- Patients who provided informed consent and were supervised by a caregiver  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients currently undergoing treatment for Parkinson's disease with dopaminergic medications or any disease-modifying treatments  
- Patients with significant comorbidities such as severe psychiatric disorders or uncontrolled systemic diseases  
- Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to interferons or any components of the drug formulation  
- Individuals with abnormal laboratory findings indicative of liver or renal dysfunction (transaminases > 2.0 times the upper limit of normal, estimated glomerular filtration rate < 60 mL/min)  
- Patients unable or unwilling to comply with study requirements or complete neuropsychological evaluations  

The primary efficacy endpoint was the change in UPDRS motor score from baseline to Week 48 between treatment and placebo groups. Additional efficacy assessments included patient-reported outcomes related to quality of life and functional status.  

However, the trial faced several critical issues resulting in its termination:  

1. **Suboptimal Mechanism of Action:** The proposed neuroprotective benefits of interferon beta-1a failed to align with the complex pathophysiology of Parkinson's disease, leading to insufficient modulation of neuroinflammation and dopamine preservation.  

2. **Inadequate Patient Selection:** The inclusion criteria applied were restrictive, inadvertently excluding a potentially heterogeneous population that could have exhibited varying responses to treatment, particularly those with mild cognitive impairments who may have benefitted from the immune-modulating effects.  

3. **Endpoint Selection Limitations:** Evaluative endpoints did not convincingly encapsulate clinically significant improvements in patient-centered outcomes, making it challenging to deduce any meaningful therapeutic impact from interferon beta-1a treatment.  

4. **Variability in Drug Administration:** The study design led to inconsistencies in treatment adherence, where varied patient compliance on self-administered injections potentially influenced pharmacokinetic profiles and patient responses in both trial arms.  

5. **Insufficient Study Duration and Follow-Up:** The clinical trial duration was inadequate to capture potential long-term effects of interferon beta-1a, failing to elucidate any delayed therapeutic benefits that may have emerged beyond the immediate treatment window.  

Overall, this trial illustrated significant challenges associated with the testing of interferon beta-1a in conditions of neurodegeneration, emphasizing the need for refined therapeutic strategies targeting inflammatory pathways in Parkinson's disease, as well as the reevaluation of eligibility criteria and endpoint selections in future clinical studies.
