**Phase II Trial of Oxaliplatin, Gemcitabine, and Abraxane for Patients with Unresectable Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma** 

**Study Overview:**
This phase II clinical trial aims to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of the combination therapy using oxaliplatin, gemcitabine, and nab-paclitaxel (Abraxane) in patients diagnosed with unresectable pancreatic adenocarcinoma. The study will investigate the primary endpoint of progression-free survival (PFS) and assess overall response rates among the patient cohort.

**Background:**
Pancreatic adenocarcinoma often presents at an advanced stage, limiting curative surgical options and leading to poor prognosis. The current standard of care with gemcitabine monotherapy or its combinations yields suboptimal results, with notable resistance developing in a sizable fraction of the population. Incorporating oxaliplatin into the chemotherapeutic regimen is anticipated to augment tumor response through its mechanism of inducing DNA crosslinking, thereby enhancing cytotoxicity of gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel. This synergistic effect aims to target biologically active tumor cells more effectively, hypothesizing improved treatment outcomes.

**Study Design:**
The trial will utilize a single-arm design enrolling 100 adult patients aged 18 years or older, with histologically confirmed unresectable pancreatic adenocarcinoma. The treatment regimen will consist of oxaliplatin administered at a dose of 85 mg/m² on day 1, gemcitabine at a dose of 1000 mg/m² administered over 30 minutes on days 1 and 8, and nab-paclitaxel at 125 mg/m² on days 1, 8, and 15 of a 28-day cycle, for a total of 6 cycles.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of unresectable pancreatic adenocarcinoma.
- Measurable disease per RECIST criteria.
- ECOG performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate hematologic function: Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³, Platelets ≥ 100,000/mm³, Hemoglobin ≥ 9 g/dL.
- Normal organ function parameters: Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 x upper limit of normal (ULN), Serum Creatinine ≤ 1.5 x ULN.
- Informed consent must be obtained prior to participation.
 
**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous chemotherapy for pancreatic adenocarcinoma.
- Presence of any active, untreated malignancy.
- Patients with significant comorbidities that would interfere with protocol compliance.
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the study drugs.

**Primary Endpoint:**
The primary endpoint of the study will be the overall response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) under the RECIST criteria, as well as the evaluation of progression-free survival (PFS) after completion of the treatment regimen.

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Assessment of overall survival (OS).
- Evaluation of quality of life during treatment, measured by the EORTC QLQ-C30 scale.
- Safety and tolerability as assessed by the frequency and severity of dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) and adverse events throughout the treatment course.

**Statistical Considerations:**
A total of 100 patients will be enrolled to ensure adequate power to detect significant differences in both primary and secondary outcomes. Continuous data will be summarized as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical data will be reported as frequencies and percentages. Progression-free survival and overall survival will be analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method.

**Conclusion:**
This trial represents an innovative approach to salvage therapy in patients with unresectable pancreatic adenocarcinoma by leveraging oxaliplatin's diverse mechanisms of action alongside the established paradigm of gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel. The integration of metabolic and molecular profiling may further guide treatment decisions and stratifications, fostering enhanced outcomes in this challenging clinical setting. The successful implementation of this study could potentially address the unmet needs within this patient population, warranting further exploration into the role of combination chemotherapies in pancreatic cancer management.
