<clinical_trial>
    <title>Evaluation of Daclizumab in Combination With Reduced-Dose Tacrolimus for Maintenance Immunosuppression in De Novo Renal Transplant Recipients: A Multi-Center, Open-Label Study</title>
    <objective>The primary objective of this study is to assess the efficacy of daclizumab administered during the initial two months post-transplantation in combination with a reduced-dose regimen of tacrolimus in preventing acute rejection episodes and maintaining renal function in patients receiving a de novo renal allograft.</objective>
    <study_design>This is a prospective, multi-center, open-label study with a randomized allocation of participants to either the experimental arm, receiving daclizumab and reduced-dose tacrolimus, or the control arm, receiving standard immunosuppression with tacrolimus at normal dosing. The study will be conducted over 12 months with a follow-up period extending to 24 months post-transplantation.</study_design>
    <inclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>Men and women aged 18-75 years.</criterion>
        <criterion>Recipients of a de novo single-organ renal transplant from living or deceased donors.</criterion>
        <criterion>Patients who provide written informed consent.</criterion>
        <criterion>Calculated Panel Reactive Antibody (PRA) less than 20% at screening.</criterion>
    </inclusion_criteria>
    <exclusion_criteria>
        <criterion>History of previous organ transplantation.</criterion>
        <criterion>Presence of active malignancy (excluding adequately treated basal cell carcinoma).</criterion>
        <criterion>Severe renal dysfunction pre-transplantation, defined as estimated GFR <30 mL/min.</criterion>
        <criterion>Patients who have received systemic corticosteroids within three months prior to transplantation.</criterion>
        <criterion>Evidence of acute or chronic infection at the time of enrollment.</criterion>
    </exclusion_criteria>
    <intervention>
        <group>
            <name>Experimental Group</name>
            <description>Participants in the experimental group will receive daclizumab (1 mg/kg intravenously) on Day 0 and Day 14 post-transplantation, combined with a low-dose regimen of tacrolimus (target trough levels of 4-6 ng/mL).</description>
        </group>
        <group>
            <name>Control Group</name>
            <description>Participants in the control group will receive standard maintenance immunosuppression, consisting of tacrolimus at normal dosing (target trough levels of 8-10 ng/mL) and mycophenolate mofetil (MMF).</description>
        </group>
    </intervention>
    <primary_outcome>
        <description>Incidence of acute rejection episodes within the first 12 months post-transplantation, defined by biopsy-confirmed acute rejection (Banff classification) or treated acute rejection episodes.</description>
    </primary_outcome>
    <secondary_outcomes>
        <outcome>Renal function as assessed by the estimated GFR at 12 and 24 months post-transplantation.</outcome>
        <outcome>Incidence of adverse events and serious adverse events related to the immunosuppressive regimen.</outcome>
        <outcome>Changes in quality of life as measured by the Kidney Disease Quality of Life Short Form (KDQOL-SF) at baseline, 12, and 24 months.</outcome>
    </secondary_outcomes>
    <rationale>The study leverages the existing evidence regarding the efficacy of daclizumab as an induction agent in renal transplantation, as documented in previous trials demonstrating enhanced graft function and reduced acute rejection episodes. Furthermore, the risk mitigation associated with utilizing daclizumab in combination with a reduced tacrolimus regimen aligns with contemporary approaches to personalized immunosuppressive therapy, minimizing both steroid-related complications and the overall burden of immunosuppression.</rationale>
    <significance>This study is expected to contribute valuable data on the safety and efficacy of a daclizumab-tacrolimus combination approach, potentially informing future immunosuppressive protocols in kidney transplantation and enhancing graft survival and long-term patient outcomes.</significance>
</clinical_trial>
