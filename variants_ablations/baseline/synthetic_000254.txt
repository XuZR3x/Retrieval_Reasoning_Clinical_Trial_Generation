**Failed Clinical Trial of Vinflunine: Phase III Trial of Vinflunine Plus Pembrolizumab Versus Pembrolizumab Alone in Patients With Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**

**Background:** The treatment options for patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer who have progressed after platinum-based chemotherapy are severely limited. Pembrolizumab has emerged as a standard treatment in this setting. Given the potential for vinflunine to possess synergistic antitumor properties in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors, a Phase III trial was initiated to investigate the efficacy and safety of vinflunine plus pembrolizumab compared to pembrolizumab monotherapy. 

**Primary Objective:** 
To compare the efficacy of vinflunine in combination with pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab alone in terms of overall survival in patients with previously treated advanced NSCLC.

**Secondary Objectives:**
- To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) in both arms.
- To assess the objective response rate (ORR) and duration of response in both treatment arms.
- To analyze the safety profiles and incidence of adverse events associated with both regimens.
- To examine quality of life metrics and symptom control as measured by validated tools.

**Methodology:** 
This multicenter, open-label, randomized Phase III trial enrolled a total of 452 patients diagnosed with stage IIIB or IV NSCLC who had previously been treated with a platinum-containing regimen. Patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive vinflunine in conjunction with pembrolizumab (Arm A) or pembrolizumab alone (Arm B).

**Inclusion Criteria:**
1. Written informed consent.
2. Histologically or cytologically confirmed NSCLC without actionable mutations.
3. Documented locally advanced or metastatic disease not amenable to curative surgery or radiotherapy.
4. Documented disease progression after at least one prior chemotherapy regimen.
5. ECOG performance status of 0 or 1.
6. Adequate organ function defined by specified laboratory values.
7. Age ≥ 18 years at the time of screening.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
1. Prior systemic therapy with vinflunine or other investigational agents.
2. Spinal cord compression or symptomatic brain metastasis requiring treatment.
3. Significant comorbid conditions that might interfere with treatment.
4. Active autoimmune disease requiring treatment or immunosuppressive therapy.
5. Previous treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors multiple times.
6. Any contraindications to vinflunine or pembrolizumab.

**Endpoints:**
The primary endpoint was overall survival, calculated from the time of randomization to death from any cause. Secondary endpoints included PFS assessed via RECIST 1.1 criteria, ORR, duration of response, and treatment-related adverse events graded by CTCAE version 5.0.

**Results:**
The trial unfortunately failed to meet its primary endpoint. The combination of vinflunine and pembrolizumab did not demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in overall survival compared to pembrolizumab alone, with median survival times of 9.8 months (Arm A) and 10.2 months (Arm B). The progression-free survival was similar in both arms, with no statistically remarkable benefits attributed to the introduction of vinflunine. Response rates were 18% in Arm A and 22% in Arm B, lacking sufficient differentiation. 

**Adverse Events:**
Notably, the addition of vinflunine led to an increased incidence of severe toxicities, including peripheral neuropathy and hematologic adverse events, which resulted in higher rates of treatment discontinuation and a significant detrimental impact on patient quality of life.

**Conclusions:**
This Phase III trial of vinflunine in combination with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced NSCLC did not demonstrate the anticipated improvement in overall survival or significant clinical benefits, leading to its premature termination. These results suggest that inherent resistance mechanisms to vinflunine, coupled with the high toxicity profile and suboptimal patient selection, contributed to the failure of this combination therapy in the intended patient population. Future studies must further clarify the role of vinflunine within multi-modal treatment strategies and improve patient stratification based on molecular profiling to enhance therapeutic outcomes.
