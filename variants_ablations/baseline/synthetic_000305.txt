<ClinicalTrial>
    <Title>LDK378 in Patients With ALK-Positive NSCLC: A Phase II, Randomized, Controlled Multicenter Study</Title>
    <Design>A Phase II, randomized, controlled, open-label trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of oral LDK378 in adult patients with ALK-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have received prior treatment with chemotherapy and are crizotinib-resistant. This trial investigated outcomes in a pre-defined population to capture the most accurate data on the drug’s therapeutic impact.</Design>

    <Background>The rising incidence of ALK-positive NSCLC necessitates the development of targeted therapies that can effectively combat resistance to previous treatments such as crizotinib. Building upon the previously demonstrated efficacy of LDK378, this study aimed to provide robust data regarding its efficacy in a heterogenous group of patients with ALK rearrangements.</Background>

    <InterventionDetails>
        <Drug>LDK378</Drug>
        <Dosage>750 mg orally, administered once daily</Dosage>
        <ContinuationCriteria>Patients were permitted to continue treatment beyond the progression of disease as assessed by RECIST criteria, provided that the investigator observed clinical benefit.</ContinuationCriteria>
    </InterventionDetails>

    <InclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Histologically or cytologically confirmed diagnosis of stage IIIB or IV NSCLC with an ALK rearrangement verified by the FDA-approved FISH assay.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Age 18 years or older at the time of informed consent.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients must have documented progression after treatment with crizotinib and one prior line of cytotoxic chemotherapy.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Evidence of a tumor tissue sample collected within the past 12 months prior to the first dose of LDK378.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Recovery from grade 3 or 4 toxicities related to prior anticancer treatment to grade ≤ 2.</Criterion>
    </InclusionCriteria>

    <ExclusionCriteria>
        <Criterion>Patients with known hypersensitivity to any components of LDK378.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Presence of symptomatic central nervous system metastases requiring corticosteroid treatment or those with significantly deteriorating neurologic status.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Patients with a history of interstitial lung disease.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Previous treatment with any other ALK inhibitor or investigational agents aside from crizotinib.</Criterion>
        <Criterion>Clinically significant, uncontrolled cardiovascular disease.</Criterion>
    </ExclusionCriteria>

    <Objective>The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the overall response rate (ORR) in patients treated with LDK378 as assessed by the investigators using RECIST v1.1 criteria. Secondary objectives included assessing progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).</Objective>

    <OutcomeMeasures>
        <PrimaryOutcome>Overall Response Rate (ORR) defined as the percentage of participants with a confirmed complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as per RECIST v1.1.</PrimaryOutcome>
        <SecondaryOutcome>Progression-Free Survival (PFS) measured from the start of treatment until documented progression or death, whichever occurred first.</SecondaryOutcome>
        <SecondaryOutcome>Overall Survival (OS) defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause.</SecondaryOutcome>
    </OutcomeMeasures>

    <StatisticalAnalysis>Sample size estimation aimed for a total of 200 patients to achieve sufficient power to detect a significant difference in response rates. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline characteristics and outcomes, while Kaplan-Meier methods were utilized for survival analyses.</StatisticalAnalysis>

    <Conclusion>This trial successfully demonstrated that LDK378, when administered as a continuous therapy to a carefully selected patient population, resulted in a visually and statistically significant increase in the overall response rate. Furthermore, continuation of treatment beyond progression based on clinical benefit further emphasized the trial's patient-centric approach. As a result of rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria, alongside a multi-center design, the findings may serve to enhance the therapeutic avenues available for patients with ALK-positive NSCLC.</Conclusion>
</ClinicalTrial>
