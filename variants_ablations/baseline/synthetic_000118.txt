**Title: A Double-Blind, Randomized Study of Mifepristone as an Adjunct Therapy to Control Weight Gain in Patients Undergoing Treatment with Olanzapine** 

**Objective:**  
This clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of mifepristone as an adjunct therapy to mitigate olanzapine-induced weight gain in patients diagnosed with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder.

**Study Design:**  
This is a two-month, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial involving adult patients aged 18 to 50 years who are receiving stable doses of olanzapine. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either mifepristone at 600 mg daily or matching placebo. The primary endpoint will be the change in body weight and BMI from baseline at Week 8. Secondary endpoints will assess metabolic parameters, quality of life, and changes in appetite.

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Patients aged 18 to 50 years with a confirmed diagnosis of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder as per DSM-5 criteria.  
- All patients must be on a stable dose of olanzapine for a minimum of 6 weeks prior to enrollment.  
- BMI between 18.5 and 34.9.  
- Provide written informed consent.  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- History of Cushing's syndrome or current corticosteroid use.  
- Known psychiatric co-morbidities severely affecting compliance.  
- Active substance abuse or proposed concurrent use of other investigational drugs.  
- Significant medical conditions (e.g., cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension) that may increase risk or affect study compliance.  
- Pregnant or nursing women.  

**Hypotheses and Rationale:**  
Based on prior studies establishing mifepristone’s antagonistic activity on glucocorticoid receptors, it is hypothesized that its integration into olanzapine therapy will significantly attenuate hyperglycemic responses and subsequent weight gain, as illustrated in mechanisms analyzed during cumulative clinical observations. The stringent selection criteria will ensure a homogeneous patient population, thereby enhancing the reliability of outcomes. Additionally, rigor in monitoring adverse events will provide valuable data regarding the safety profile of mifepristone in this psychological cohort.

**Endpoints:**  
- **Primary Endpoint:**  
   - Change in body weight (measured in kilograms) from baseline to Week 8.
- **Secondary Endpoints:**  
   - Change in BMI from baseline to Week 8.  
   - Changes in metabolic parameters (lipid profiles, fasting glucose).  
   - Assessment of quality of life using standardized questionnaires (e.g., QoL-AGHDA Scale) 
   - Changes in appetite assessments via validated scales (e.g., Eating Disorder Examination Questionnaire).

**Timeline:**  
Enrollment is expected to take 3 months, with patient observations extending over 8 weeks, followed by an additional 1-month follow-up for safety assessments post-intervention.

**Statistical Analysis:**  
Data will be analyzed using appropriate statistical methods to compare means and assess the significance of mifepristone's impact versus placebo. A p-value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

**Discussion:**  
This clinical trial seeks to elucidate the role of mifepristone in controlling side effects related to antipsychotic therapy. Employing mifepristone as an adjunct could potentially offer a dual benefit of managing psychiatric symptoms while concurrently mitigating adverse metabolic effects as demonstrated in thorough subgroup analyses of previous studies. The outcomes may pave the way for integrated pharmacological strategies enhancing long-term patient adherence and overall wellbeing. 

**Conclusion:**  
The proposed trial aims to provide robust evidence which may substantiate mifepristone's utility in weight management for patients undergoing treatment with olanzapine, thereby contributing significant advancements in personalized psychiatric care. A successful outcome could translate into substantial improvements in not only clinical efficacy but also patients' quality of life and adherence rates in long-term therapeutic regimens.
