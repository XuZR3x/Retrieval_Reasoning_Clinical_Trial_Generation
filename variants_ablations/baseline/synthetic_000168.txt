**Failed Clinical Trial of Interleukin-2: Phase III Study of Interleukin-2 Combined with Chemotherapy in Advanced Renal Cell Carcinoma**

**A Phase III Study of Interleukin-2 Combined with Chemotherapy in Patients with Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma**

Objective: This clinical trial aimed to investigate whether the combination of interleukin-2 (IL-2) with chemotherapy would yield improved efficacy compared to chemotherapy alone in patients with advanced renal cell carcinoma (RCC). The hypothesis posited that IL-2 would augment the immune response to renal cell carcinoma, leading to enhanced tumor regression in both treated and non-treated lesions.

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Histologically confirmed metastatic renal cell carcinoma.
- Measurable disease as defined by RECIST criteria.
- Age ≥ 18 years.
- Life expectancy of ≥ 3 months.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0-2.
- Adequate organ function as evidenced by:
  - Serum creatinine ≤ 1.5 mg/dL.
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1,500/mm³.
  - Total bilirubin ≤ 1.5 mg/dL.
- Informed consent obtained prior to any study-specific procedures.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of severe hypersensitivity to interleukin-2 or any of the components of the chemotherapy regimen.
- Prior systemic treatment for metastatic RCC within the last 6 months.
- Known presence of central nervous system metastases.
- Active autoimmune disease or chronic infections including HIV, Hepatitis B, or Hepatitis C.
- Pregnancy or lactation.

**Study Design:**
This was a multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled Phase III trial. Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive either the combination of IL-2 and standard chemotherapy regimen (sunitinib) or chemotherapy alone. 

**Intervention Details:**
- Drug: Interleukin-2 administered intravenously at a dose of 600,000 IU/m² for 5 days every 28 days.
- Chemotherapy: Sunitinib administered at the standard dosing schedule (50mg/day for 4 weeks followed by 2 weeks off).

**Primary Outcomes:**
- The primary endpoint was overall response rate (ORR) as evaluated by independent radiologic review at 12 weeks from the initiation of therapy.

**Secondary Outcomes:**
- Progression-free survival (PFS).
- Overall survival (OS).
- Incidence of adverse events evaluated per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE v4.0).

**Estimated Enrollment:** 400

**Study Start Date:** June 2018  
**Estimated Study Completion Date:** June 2022

**Results:**
The trial failed to meet its primary endpoint of improved ORR with the combination therapy. The response rates were similar across both arms: 25% in the combination group versus 23% in the chemotherapy-alone group. Moreover, no significant improvement in either PFS or OS was observed, reinforcing the theory that supplemental immune activation through IL-2 may not yield anticipated therapeutic benefits against advanced RCC.

Failure Analysis:
1. **Insufficient Patient Selection Criteria:** The protocol did not adequately address heterogeneity in RCC subtypes. Grouping all RCC into a single category likely masked the efficacy in potentially responsive populations.

2. **Suboptimal Dosing Regimens:** The dosing of IL-2 may have been insufficient to elicit meaningful immune responses. Previous studies indicated a threshold required for effective T-cell activation that was not met in this protocol.

3. **Lack of Efficacy in Distant Tumor Response:** The trial found no significant difference in the reduction of distant metastases, indicating that local IL-2 administration has not translated into systemic efficacy for comprehensive tumor control.

4. **Inadequate Immune Activation:** The anticipated enhancement of cellular immune response from IL-2 may not have been validated in this context and potentially disrupted by the immunosuppressive environment created by accompanying chemotherapy.

5. **High Relapse Rates and Adverse Events:** The combination therapy resulted in a higher incidence of serious adverse events, including severe fatigue, renal impairment, and cardiovascular events, leading to treatment discontinuations, thus undermining safety profiles and limiting continuation among patients.

In conclusion, the integration of interleukin-2 with standard chemotherapy for advanced renal cell carcinoma failed, highlighting critical insights into patient selection, dosing, and the need for further investigations on optimal strategies that leverage IL-2's immunomodulatory effects.
