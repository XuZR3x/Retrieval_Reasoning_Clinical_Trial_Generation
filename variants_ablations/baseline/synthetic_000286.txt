**Failed Clinical Trial of Metformin: Efficacy of Metformin in Chronic Kidney Disease**

**Title**: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study Evaluating the Efficacy of Metformin in Patients with Stage 3-4 Chronic Kidney Disease

**ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier**: NCT02345678

**Background**: Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) is characterized by a progressive loss of renal function and is associated with metabolic alterations, including insulin resistance and dyslipidemia. Metformin has shown potential in altering serum creatinine levels and improving metabolic dysfunction. This study aimed to assess the efficacy of metformin to improve renal outcomes and metabolic status in individuals with mildly to moderately impaired renal function.

**Objectives**: This trial's primary objective was to evaluate the change in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) from baseline after 24 weeks of treatment with metformin compared to placebo.

**Study Design**: A multicenter, 1:1 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Subjects were treated with either metformin (850 mg bid) or placebo for 24 weeks.

**Inclusion Criteria**:
1. Adults aged 50-80 years.
2. Confirmed diagnosis of Stage 3-4 Chronic Kidney Disease based on KDOQI criteria.
3. Serum creatinine levels between 1.5–3.0 mg/dL.
4. HbA1c ≤ 6.5%.
5. Signed informed consent.

**Exclusion Criteria**:
1. Active malignancy.
2. Uncontrolled diabetes (FBS ≥ 126 mg/dL).
3. History of lactic acidosis or metabolic disorders that affect renal function.
4. Concurrent use of insulin or sulfonylureas.
5. Pregnant or lactating women.
6. Severe concomitant illnesses or conditions that would impair trial participation.

**Endpoints**: 
- Primary Endpoint: Change in eGFR (mL/min/1.73m²) from baseline to 24 weeks.
- Secondary Endpoint: Changes in metabolic parameters including fasting glucose, HbA1c, and lipid profiles.

**Results**: 
The trial enrolled 250 participants but was terminated prematurely after an interim analysis. Key findings included:

1. **Insufficient Metabolic Modulation**: It was identified that metformin did not activate the metabolic pathways sufficiently in CKD patients, where underlying renal disorders override therapeutic effects on insulin sensitivity, leading to lack of change in eGFR. 

2. **Suboptimal Patient Selection**: The trial population became homogeneous due to strict inclusion criteria, resulting in limited variability in the expected renal response, ignoring candidates with rather advanced stages of kidney impairment who might have benefited from metformin's pleiotropic effects.

3. **Adverse Interactions with Concurrent Treatments**: Pharmacokinetic assessment indicated unexpected interactions between metformin and concomitant medications utilized by the subjects (particularly antihypertensive agents), diminishing the drug's efficacy and thus affecting renal function outcomes.

4. **Inadequate Duration of Intervention**: Following the 24-week duration established for the trial, it was apparent that the chronic nature of CKD needed a longer intervention period to attain possible beneficial outcomes on renal function.

5. **Variability in Individual Responses**: Genetic polymorphisms influencing drug metabolism among participants were not accounted, leading to variable renal responses that diluted the overall therapeutic effects observed in the cohort.

**Conclusion**: The trial was terminated due to failure to demonstrate significant efficacy of metformin in improving renal function within the specified intervention period. Further studies should focus on refining selection criteria and extending the duration of treatment to allow for a comprehensive assessment of metformin's renal benefits, particularly in more complex patient profiles.
