**Clinical Trial Report: Selinexor in Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Acute Myeloid Leukemia**

**Title:** A Phase II Study of Selinexor for Patients with Relapsed or Refractory Acute Myeloid Leukemia Not Eligible for Intensive Chemotherapy  

**Study Identifier:** NCTXXXXXXXX

**Study Type:** Randomized, Open-Label, Phase II

**Introduction:**  
Selinexor is a selective inhibitor of nuclear export (SINE) targeting exportin-1 (XPO1). This study aims to assess the efficacy and safety of selinexor in patients aged ≥ 60 years with relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia (AML) who are ineligible for intensive chemotherapy. Previous trials exhibited significant challenges that may include insufficient target engagement, limited patient cohort response, and a high toxicity profile, which merit thorough investigation in this context.

**Objectives:**  
The primary objective of this study is to determine the overall survival (OS) rate in patients treated with selinexor compared to a physician's choice of standard care. Secondary objectives include assessment of treatment response rates, safety profile, and tolerability of selinexor in the target population.

**Study Design:**
This is a randomized, open-label, multicenter Phase II trial, designed to distribute patients randomly into two cohorts: one receiving selinexor and the other receiving physician's choice treatment. 

**Inclusion Criteria:**  
- Age ≥ 60 years with a diagnosis of relapsed or refractory AML (excluding acute promyelocytic leukemia)
- One prior therapy for AML and ineligible for stem cell transplantation  
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2  
- Availability of an archival or recently obtained bone marrow sample for central review  

**Exclusion Criteria:**  
- Central nervous system (CNS) leukemia  
- Significant concurrent illness that may affect study compliance  
- Known active viral infections (e.g., HIV, HBV, HCV)  
- Previous treatment with selinexor  

**Intervention:**  
Patients in the selinexor arm will receive the drug orally once weekly, with treatment continuing until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal.

**Results and Analysis Plan:**  
The primary endpoint will be OS defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause, analyzed using Kaplan-Meier survival estimates. Statistical comparisons will be made using the log-rank test for survival analysis. Secondary endpoints will include treatment response rates, defined by complete response (CR) or partial response (PR), and adverse event reports analyzed as per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE). 

**Estimated Sample Size:**  
Approximately 200 participants will be recruited to ensure adequate power to detect a significant difference in OS between treatment arms.

**Study Challenges:**
The rationale for this trial stems from observations in earlier studies which revealed:  
1. **Insufficient Target Engagement:** Data indicated that selinexor may not adequately engage with its target XPO1, resulting in suboptimal pharmacodynamics that limited cytotoxic efficacy against leukemic cells.
2. **Limited Patient Cohort Response:** The study will address patient heterogeneity, acknowledging that the complexity of relapsed or refractory AML may lead to varied treatment responsiveness.
3. **High Toxicity Profile:** Concerns regarding the safety profile have prompted stringent monitoring protocols to manage and evaluate adverse events associated with selinexor administration, particularly when considering the age and fragility of the patient population.
4. **Inefficient Drug Distribution:** As previously noted, challenges with the distribution and bioavailability of the drug could hinder therapeutic outcomes, thereby necessitating close pharmacokinetic monitoring.
5. **Inadequate Combination Synergy:** The exploration of selinexor as a monotherapy distinct from current combination treatment paradigms aims to elucidate its standalone efficacy, refining treatment strategies for a challenging patient cohort.

**Expected Outcomes:**  
Failure to demonstrate superior OS or acceptable safety margins with selinexor may indicate a need to retreat from development pathways and emphasize the necessity for more granular exploratory studies focusing on biological underpinnings of AML resistance to XPO1 inhibition.

**Conclusion:**  
The study will provide critical insights into the therapeutic viability of selinexor in a targeted AML population. Findings from this trial are expected to inform future research directions, including potential combinations or alternative treatment strategies tailored to patient-specific molecular characteristics. 

**Current Status:**  
Pending institutional review board approval with projected enrollment to commence in the first half of 2023.
