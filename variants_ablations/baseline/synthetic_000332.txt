Successful clinical trial of exemestane: Exemestane in Combination with Radiotherapy and Chemotherapy for Advanced Squamous Cell Lung Cancer 

A Phase III Study of Exemestane with Concurrent Radiotherapy and Chemotherapy in Advanced Squamous Cell Lung Cancer Patients 

RATIONALE: The premise of this trial is predicated on the documented efficacy of exemestane as an aromatase inhibitor, which significantly reduces estrogen levels and has shown synergistic effects when combined with chemotherapeutic agents. Emerging evidence suggests that the reduction in estrogenic stimulation may enhance tumor radiosensitivity, particularly in hormone-responsive neoplasms. This trial aims to evaluate the combined therapeutic benefits of exemestane, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy for patients diagnosed with advanced squamous cell lung cancer (SCC).

PURPOSE: This study will assess whether the addition of exemestane to a standard chemoradiation regimen improves overall survival and disease progression in patients with locally advanced SCC of the lung, relative to those receiving chemoradiation alone.

OBJECTIVES: 

PRIMARY:
I. To determine the overall survival (OS) rate of patients receiving exemestane in combination with chemotherapy and radiotherapy compared to those receiving chemotherapy and radiotherapy alone.

SECONDARY:
I. To evaluate the progression-free survival (PFS) and disease control rate (DCR) at 6 months.
II. To assess the objective response rate (ORR) utilizing RECIST version 1.1 criteria.
III. To analyze quality of life (QoL) metrics pre- and post-treatment using the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire (EORTC QLQ-C30).
IV. To investigate the pharmacokinetics of exemestane in conjunction with standard chemotherapeutics.

OUTLINE: This phase III study employs a multicenter, randomized, double-blind design. Patients will be stratified based on baseline performance status, presence of distant metastasis, and prior treatment history. Eligible patients will be administered exemestane orally (PO) once daily (QD) during treatment cycles. Concurrent chemotherapy, consisting of cisplatin and etoposide, will be given intravenously on days 1-3, followed by radiotherapy doses daily over 5 weeks.

Inclusion Criteria:
- Histologically confirmed squamous cell lung cancer (stage III-IV), not amenable to surgery or definitive radiotherapy.
- Measurable disease per RECIST criteria, with no prior systemic therapy for metastatic disease.
- Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≤ 2.
- Patients must be postmenopausal, defined as either being aged ≥ 60 years or having no menses for ≥ 12 months with an FSH level in the postmenopausal range.

Exclusion Criteria:
- Previous treatment with an aromatase inhibitor or hormonal therapy within 12 months prior to randomization.
- Presence of symptomatic central nervous system metastasis or active major medical conditions (e.g., cardiovascular, renal).
- Concurrent use of investigational agents or participation in other clinical trials within 30 days of enrollment.

Projected Accrual: A total of 800 patients are anticipated to be enrolled across multiple sites in the United States and Europe.

PATIENT TREATMENT: 
- Patients randomized to the experimental arm will receive exemestane (25 mg PO QD) continuously throughout the chemoradiation period.
- The control arm will receive a placebo identical in appearance to the study drug.

OUTCOME MEASURES: 
The primary endpoint will be assessed at a minimum follow-up of two years, measuring overall survival using Kaplan-Meier survival curves and Cox proportional-hazards models to analyze the impact of the intervention.

SAFETY ASSESSMENT: Adverse events (AEs) will be tracked according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0, allowing for a comprehensive safety profile assessment.

This innovative clinical trial design integrates a robust mechanistic understanding of exemestane’s role in oncogenesis and disease management, leveraging the strengths of concurrent chemoradiotherapy with targeted hormonal manipulation. The outcomes expected from this trial will potentially define new therapeutic strategies for patients suffering from advanced SCC, reinforcing the hypothesis of exemestane’s multifaceted impact in oncology.
